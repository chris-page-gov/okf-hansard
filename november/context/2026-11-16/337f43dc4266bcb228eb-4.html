Turn 25 · Baroness Antrobus (Lab)

On reparations, as has been said several times, our position on financial reparations has not changed. That is not where we need to be; it is with work towards a formal apology. On the question posed, going forward, there is structured and careful dialogue planned over the next year that is to be jointly designed with our Caribbean and African co-chairs, which is an important point. This is not about the UK driving this. We are responding to requests from Commonwealth nations to get more actively involved in resolving this issue. The way to do it will be led by them and not by us.

Turn 27 · Baroness Antrobus (Lab)

I thank my noble friend Lady Alexander for that question, which is much appreciated. Yes, it is a revived CHOGM; it looked and felt different. For the UK and that attitude of being outward-looking, reliable and respectful, the tone set ahead of CHOGM by the Prime Minister then read into what was a very successful CHOGM—I am thinking not just of our country but of our allies around the Commonwealth. The Commonwealth covers 2.7 billion people and five continents, and it has the capacity to be a really influential forum for shared values and interests, as well as feeding into discussions around debt relief, as has been mentioned. I assure my noble friend that both the Prime Minister and the Foreign Secretary are committed to this future conception of the Commonwealth, as is the current Commonwealth secretary-general.

Turn 29 · Baroness Antrobus (Lab)
