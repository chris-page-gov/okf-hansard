Turn 105 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government absolutely recognise that defendants, including those remanded in custody, are entitled to a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time. Delays that prevent a hearing taking place or result in proceedings being adjourned can contribute to individuals waiting longer for their cases to be resolved, which is why we are working across the criminal justice system to reduce delays and improve timely production of defendants at court.

Turn 106 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government are working with local councils to grant prison vans access to bus lanes to help defendants to reach court on time and prevent avoidable disruptions. One route, I am pleased to say, is already operating between HMP Forest Bank and Manchester Crown Court and has cut journey times by up to one-third. Following other successful rollouts in Bristol, Salford and Nottingham, the Government have written to other local authorities to push for wider adoption of the scheme nationwide.

Turn 108 · Speaker unlabelled

My noble friend asked two specific questions on monitoring and metrics. On publicly available data regarding the performance of the prisoner escort and custody service—PECS—key performance indicators are published quarterly via the Cabinet Office for transparency and return on KPIs for government contracts. The quarterly performance reporting for PECS includes three priority KPIs, one of which is a narrative assessment of supplier performance and courtroom delays.

Turn 109 · Speaker unlabelled

On metrics and the 1% that my noble friend suggests, I reassure her that PECS contracts are already actively managed through a range of contractual and operational performance measures. The Government do not consider it appropriate to require statutory review based on a single performance metric. Contract management should continue to take account of a range of factors and the full range of performance information. Given this action, and some of the reassurances that I hope I have just provided, I hope that, when the time comes, she will not move her amendment and that the noble Baroness, Lady Brinton, will withdraw hers.
