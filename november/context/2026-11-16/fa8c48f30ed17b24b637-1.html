Turn 3 · The Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government (Angela Rayner)

I beg to move, That the Bill be now read a Second time. This Government are committed to good growth in every postcode, not just as a matter of fairness but because Britain is at its best when every area of the country can play its part. The Visitor Levy (England) Bill is the next major step towards that, and will give strategic authorities new powers to raise money and invest in their area. Every mayor will soon be able to choose whether an overnight visitor levy is right for their region. If they do implement one, they will be able to use what they raise to invest in local priorities, which will improve the visitor experience and support wider economic growth. Turn 6 · Deirdre Costigan (Ealing Southall) (Lab)

I very much welcome the overnight visitor levy, which is the next step in devolution. However, the Secretary of State will know that London has a different form of devolution to the rest of the country, as we do not have a combined authority, and there is no requirement to include local authorities in decisions. While we can be sure that the current Mayor of London has the best interests of London at heart, that might not always be the case with future mayors—perhaps they will be from the Conservative party. Will the Secretary of State consider whether we need a formal role for London boroughs in the design and governance of this funding to future-proof London governance?

Turn 7 · Angela Rayner

As my hon. Friend acknowledges, the Greater London Authority has its own set of governance arrangements. The Bill gives London Assembly members a formal scrutiny role and a vote on the mayor’s proposals at the approval stage, while the final decision on whether to proceed with the levy rests with the mayor. We are bringing forward this Bill because we believe that this country needs a new model of government.
