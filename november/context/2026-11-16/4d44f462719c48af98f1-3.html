Turn 17 · Mr Frith

This is the first time I have been thanked for a performance I have yet to make, so I appreciate the hon. Gentleman’s remarks. The benefits are clear. In Greater Manchester, hospital teams book cancer tests, so patients can choose to be tested sooner at another hospital. In the midlands, we are testing regional systems and monitoring for winter performances as we head from autumn to winter. In Northampton, transfers between hospitals cut surgery waits. These are the benefits that we should be judging the system on. On the hon. Gentleman’s point about the SPR, I can confirm that interoperability will be baked in by design. The system will be secure by design and interoperable by design, in line with the new codes that he has rightly championed for so long with colleagues across the House. As to the funding, it will be at the current annual rate for the duration of the extension.

Turn 23 · Mr Frith

I thank my hon. Friend for her excellent question. I can confirm exactly that: the data will be transferred, and the exit and transition provisions are part of the contract. In getting my head around the issue of a federated data platform and speaking to others about it, it has helped to think of it as fetching. It fetches data, presents it to the user, and then puts it back where it was. It is, by its design as a federated model, a safer option than having one honeypot, as it were, where information and data are kept. As with the FDP as it is now, the design of any future iteration is about ensuring that that data is stored. [Interruption.] And I can see that Madam Deputy Speaker wishes for me to fetch myself and sit down!
