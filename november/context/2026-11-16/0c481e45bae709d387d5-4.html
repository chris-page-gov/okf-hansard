I beg to move, That the Committee has considered the draft Persistent Organic Pollutants (Amendment) Regulations 2026. The draft instrument was laid before the House on 14 October 2026. The Minister of State, Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, my hon. Friend the Member for Kingston upon Hull West and Haltemprice (Emma Hardy), sends her apologies; I am standing in for her. This affirmative statutory instrument makes amendments to the existing persistent organic pollutants—POPs—regulation, as permitted under powers in article 15 of the regulation. It will prohibit the manufacturing, placing on the market and use of five recently adopted POPs substances. Turn 26 · Robbie Moore (Keighley and Ilkley) (Con)

It is a pleasure to serve with you in the Chair, Sir Alec. Persistent organic pollutants are, as the name suggests, chemicals that do not go away. They are persistent and toxic, they build up in living things, and they travel long distances in the environment. They end up in our soil, our rivers and our food chain. That matters to every farmer and every family in this country, and that is why the Opposition support the ability to control them. The United Kingdom is a party to the Stockholm convention. In May 2023 and May 2025, the conference of the parties agreed to list five further substances for global elimination: UV-328; Dechlorane Plus; medium-chain chlorinated paraffins; long-chain perfluorocarboxylic acids; and the pesticide chlorpyrifos.
