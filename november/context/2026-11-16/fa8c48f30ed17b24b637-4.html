Turn 25 · Angela Rayner

The Bill gives mayors powers, which they can use if they wish. It is not for me as Secretary of State to decide whether a mayor wants the levy or not; the powers will exist, and if a mayor wants to take advantage of them, that is up to them locally. That is the whole point of devolution—I do not understand why Members do not understand that point. It is not for me to dictate; it is for me to work with local leaders to provide the framework for productivity in their local area.

Turn 27 · Angela Rayner

I agree with my hon. Friend that mayors know their area best. I think 10 mayoral areas have confirmed that they would not go above 5%, because they know their areas and are working with their local hospitality industry and other local businesses. The consultation I have described forms part of a broader process that means strategic authorities must publish a prospectus and an impact assessment before proceeding. The public will expect that these decisions are not made off the cuff. We are also introducing key aspects of the administrative framework for the levy through this Bill, including annual reporting requirements and information-sharing provisions. Together, these mechanisms will allow for transparency and local scrutiny while also minimising administrative burdens on businesses.

Turn 31 · Angela Rayner

Again, we have been looking at a national framework, but local levies can contain exemptions. That will be for local areas to determine. Through the Bill, we are also bringing forward provisions on enforcement, appeals and anti-avoidance powers. These include tax-style compliance and inspection powers, civil penalties, rights of appeal to tribunals, and offences for fraudulent evasion or deliberate misstatement. Subject to the passage of the Bill, local leaders will be able to set out plans by March 2028for how levy revenues will be invested, and then introduce a levy before the end of this Parliament.
