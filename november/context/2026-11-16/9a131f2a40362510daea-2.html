Turn 9 · Lord Ahmad of Wimbledon (Con)

My Lords, as an island nation, our vulnerabilities go much further. The main challenge for the United Kingdom remains data resilience and the vulnerability of undersea cables. Bearing in mind that almost 96% of our data goes through those cables, what added security measures, in light of not just the outages but the threat from shadow fleets to our shores, is the United Kingdom taking to ensure that not only our data but our coastline are safe?

Turn 10 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

My Lords, the noble Lord is absolutely right about the importance of our connectivity through subsea cables. The UK has quite good connectivity with good redundancy—a good ability to switch between cables where there are incidents—and a relatively rapid ability to repair those cables where breakages are found, for whatever reason. We have recently set out a range of measures that we are looking at taking to further strengthen our subsea cable infrastructure.

Turn 11 · Baroness Teather (LD)

My Lords, I declare an interest as a member of the Barts Health NHS Trust board. I listened to the Minister’s response to the noble Baroness, Lady Kidron, where she reassured us that front-line services continued to work last week, but it is undeniable that the impact was huge. In June, the Minister told my colleague, my noble friend Lord Clement-Jones, that the sovereign AI fund was “not designed to replace foreign cloud providers”.—[Official Report, 1/6/26; col. 627.] I am not sure how her answer today moves us further forward. Will the Government now reconsider this approach, treat reliance on a few US tech giants as a risk to our nation’s resilience and use our buying power to back British alternatives, so that our front-line NHS and police services can keep working if a supplier fails or cuts us off?
