Turn 9 · Lucy Powell

These issues are important, and we should be debating and discussing them, but I find it a bit irresponsible of the Conservatives to use baseless claims for party political point scoring. The evidence actually shows the opposite; some 95% of teachers in primary schools say that less than an hour a day is spent on screens in schools. However, more needs to be done, and that is why we have taken steps, in relation to statutory guidance on phones in schools. We are bringing in ed tech guidance, so that safety comes first. We have a social media ban for under-16s, and there is more to come.

Turn 10 · Laura Trott

I am absolutely astonished by that response. Either more needs to be done, or I am scaremongering—the Secretary of State needs to make up her mind. The point is that looking at a screen for hours a day is harmful to children, regardless of the content on it. I have little hope of this now, but will she back the guidance from Health Professionals for Safer Screens—from doctors—that was published today? It would limit screen time to two hours a day at secondary school, and would ensure minimal screen time at primary schools.

Turn 11 · Lucy Powell

The Conservatives were responsible for the Online Safety Act 2023, which they watered down at the last minute. How much screen time guidance did they introduce when they were in government? None at all. Were they the Government who used artificial intelligence to give children their GCSE and A-level results during covid? Yes, I think they were. Labour will take action to keep children safe, put child development first and make sure that no child is left behind.
