Political donations and housing developers

Commons · 2026-11-16 · Turns 9–13 of 13 in this section.

Hansard speaking order. A reviewed question or reply has its own link. Open Hansard for the full wording of any excerpt.

Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

Under the Government’s reforms, companies wishing to make political donations will be required to demonstrate that they are carrying out genuine business activity, have sufficient profit to fund any donation, are headquartered in the UK, and are majority owned or controlled by UK electors or eligible citizens. These measures are intended to prevent shell or dormant companies from being used to channel funds into UK politics and to ensure that donations reflect genuine UK-based interests.

Turn 11 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government do not believe that risks associated with political donations are confined to any single industry. Instead, our approach has been to introduce a robust, proportionate and consistent framework that applies across all sectors. By focusing on the legitimacy of the donor, the source of the funds and the donor’s connection to the UK, our reforms address the underlying risks that can arise regardless of the donor’s area of business.

These changes sit alongside enhanced “know your donor” requirements, and stronger enforcement powers for the Electoral Commission. Together, these measures will make it significantly more difficult for malign actors to exploit the political finance system while allowing legitimate political participation and fundraising to continue.

Turn 13 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government therefore have no plans to introduce a sector-specific ban on donations from housing developers. Instead, we remain focused on implementing a comprehensive package of reforms that close known loopholes, increase transparency, protect the integrity of our democratic system and reinforce public trust in political finance arrangements.
