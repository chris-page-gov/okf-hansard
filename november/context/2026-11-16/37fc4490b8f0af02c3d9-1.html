Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Baroness Hayman of Ullock) (Lab)

My Lords, in Farming Roadmap 2050, the Government set out their vision for a profitable farming sector. Since publication, we have already taken targeted action, including strengthening fairness in agricultural supply chains, expanding access to domestic and export markets and removing planning and regulatory barriers to investment. The Government are also investing in productivity, equipment and innovation, supporting farmers to reduce input costs through SFI and working with the sector to unlock investment and growth.

Turn 5 · Lord Blencathra (Con)

My Lords. After the family farm tax, the SFI shambles, uncertainty over new agricultural technology like gene editing, which may be sold out to the EU, and a relentless growth in regulatory burdens, farmers have had quite enough government intervention. Farmers do not need another strategy, a road map or a working group. Is not the best way to improve farm profitability to stop overtaxing them, stop overregulating them, stop undercutting them with low-standard imports and stop changing the rules every year? I know the Minister gets it, but will she please try to get the message across to the rest of this Government that farmers are not a problem to be eradicated but the solution to food security, rural prosperity and environmental stewardship?

Turn 6 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

It is important that we have a long-term plan for farming in order to establish farming profitability. The farming road map sets a direction for agriculture that involves clearer, fairer routes to market and better access to the tools, technology, skills and supply chains that farmers need to run sustainable businesses. It also sets out clear roles for the state and for farmers. It looks at the wider sector to make the vision a reality and gives the sector confidence to grow their businesses. I suggest that, under the previous Government, farmers were not as profitable as they would have liked to have been.

Turn 7 · Baroness Grender (LD)

Does the Minister agree that farm profitability cannot be fixed on the farm alone when the groceries code still covers only the 14 retailers with turnover above £1 billion, which misses out the processors and middlemen that most farmers sell to? Now that both adjudicators sit in Defra, will the Government combine them into one regulator and let bodies such as the NFU refer complaints without farmers fearing reprisals?
