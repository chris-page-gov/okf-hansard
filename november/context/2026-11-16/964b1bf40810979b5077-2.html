Turn 9 · Lord West of Spithead (Lab)

My Lords, we need to be clear exactly what we mean by a drone threat. Between June 1944 and the end of the war, 10,000 V-1s were fired against us—2,500 into London, each with a 2,000-pound warhead about the same size as that Bench—as well as thousands of V-2s. They killed something like 25,000 people and injured something like 60,000. They were fired at us from another part of Europe. With the threat from drones to us now, we have to separate out those coming from somewhere else—we are at the very periphery of western Europe—and those being used by people inside this country who are already a threat. My noble friend the Minister knows that well, and I know that this is being looked at. We must be very careful not to overinvest in something where we do not need lots of investment, although I have to say I think our population, the way it is at the moment, could not take the sort of damage …

Turn 10 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

My noble friend speaks with great authority on these matters. I say to him that we need to examine both the potential for drone use in the United Kingdom by hostile forces against sites of critical national infrastructure and also by criminals who can scope out particular isolated sites. We need to do that in the context of ensuring that we do not damage the drone industry that we have in the United Kingdom. The points he mentioned about drones launched by foreign powers external to the United Kingdom is extremely important. The £750 million that the MoD has invested will look at the counterdrone strategy in a battlefield context as opposed to a UK context. We have to look at a range of powers. We continue to do that and to ensure that we identify threats and vulnerabilities, particularly for critical national infrastructure.

Turn 12 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

At UK ministerial level, we are looking at both the Ministry of Defence and the Home Office co-ordinating policy on those matters. In the incident this weekend at Lakenheath, for example, the Ministry of Defence Police and Suffolk Constabulary worked closely with the United States Air Force police. All were deployed to assist, and the situation there was quickly resolved. We need to have a cross-government policy on the offensive nature of drones from a crime, terrorism and state actor perspective, and that is what we are trying to do.

Turn 14 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

As I have mentioned a couple of times in response to questions, the MoD has put £750 million into the examination of counterdrone policy; a significant sum—£450 million—has gone into security; and £470 million has gone into the security at military bases. We are taking a concerted approach across government. For example, the Department for Transport is currently examining how we can have identification numbers for drones in the sky, and that is being developed. We are looking at working with the police on a general counterdrone strategy. We are trying to do that in a way where technology is still advancing, but we recognise that there are challenges and things that the Government need to do in a much more effective way.
