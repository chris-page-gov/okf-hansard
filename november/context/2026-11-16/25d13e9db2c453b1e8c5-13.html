Turn 97 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 20 is explicitly drafted to specify certain matters but also provide enough flexibility to determine matters via the terms of reference with the reviewer who is appointed. There is a balance to be struck between certainty of inclusion and timely deliverability of the review. One could argue that if you continued to add in a number of different demographic factors, when would you be able to make sense of it all and deliver a timely review? The independent reviewer can expand the review in their terms of reference, and they are independent, but the review itself is purposefully narrow.

Turn 98 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 56 seeks to assess the impact of court reforms on the use of out-of-court resolutions and diversion. However, the purpose of the independent review is to provide focused scrutiny on the impact of court reform measures in the Bill, which are directed at criminal court processes rather than pre-court disposal decisions, including the use of out-of-court resolutions and diversion.

Turn 99 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 57 would require the review to examine rates of legal representation in the magistrates’ courts. The Government recognise the importance of ensuring that legal aid is available for those who need it most and that the financial eligibility threshold should reflect this. We have, in principle, accepted the recommendation from the noble and learned Lord, Lord Leveson, to increase the legal aid income thresholds at magistrates’ courts, with final decisions subject to future funding decisions and operational considerations.

I draw your Lordships’ attention to the measures in place that will support post-legislative scrutiny. Alongside the independent review, we will undertake wider evaluation of the operation and impact of the reforms, in line with the Government’s best practice on monitoring and evaluation. Turn 102 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 60 is concerned with data collection and seeks to ensure that the reviewer will have access to the information they need to carry out the independent review. As noble Lords will be aware, much data is already collected in relation to the criminal justice system. The noble Baroness, Lady Hamwee, will be reassured to hear that data already exists to allow the reviewer to investigate any differences in treatment or outcomes between defendants, as specified in Clause 20. Broader questions about the operation and effectiveness of the reforms will continue to be considered through wider evaluation work in line with government best practice on monitoring and evaluation to support post-legislative scrutiny.

Finally, Amendment 62, tabled by my noble friend Lady Goudie, would require the Secretary of State to publish and lay before Parliament a monthly statement of delays in transporting persons in custody to criminal court hearings in England and Wales.
