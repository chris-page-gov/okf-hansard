Turn 9 · Mr Frith

The hon. Member and I agree on the opportunity given to us by a new FDP procurement process. I repeat that part of the opportunity is about fully articulating additional capabilities to realise the productivity benefits and efficiency savings that the FDP, as it exists, is already proving. The decision taken today is based on independent measures of use by 137 trusts. This is not about the politics of executives of suppliers, nor is it about the territories or customers of one company or one supplier. To repeat what I said in response to the opposition spokesperson, the right hon. Member for East Hampshire (Damian Hinds), the procurement process will be open, fair and transparent, with UK sovereign capability—SMEs in constituencies across the land—encouraged to tender, and any international capability also invited to tender.

Turn 13 · Mr Frith

In the common standards framework, we would expect there to be an explicit appreciation of the lineage or ownership of any particular company. The point is that the product—the FDP—is NHS-owned, as is the intellectual property and the data. Who provides the product is subject to open, robust, free procurement processes. The distinction we are making is that we recognise—not least through the appetite of those using the FDP—that the FDP can do more. I will be making the case as to why—not just now in this period of continuity of care, but in the future iteration—more trusts should sign up and take it up, and why those using it should do so fully. I look forward to using their voices to ensure that the case is made to their peers.
