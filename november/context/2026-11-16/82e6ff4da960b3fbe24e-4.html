Turn 26 · Munira Wilson (Twickenham) (LD)

Every child, regardless of their background, should have the right and the choice to study the subjects that they are interested in. Does the Education Secretary not recognise that history has taught us that sorting children and young people from the age of 14 risks creating a divide where the most disadvantaged pupils are funnelled into vocational routes, while more affluent pupils get to follow academic pathways? We Liberal Democrats believe that young people should have more time to discover their strengths and talents through a broader curriculum up to 18. Does the Education Secretary really believe that narrowing choices at 14 is the best way to promote social mobility?

Turn 27 · Lucy Powell

We are absolutely not narrowing choices; we are expanding choices. For the first time, we are making GCSEs equivalent, whether they are in applied, vocational, technical or creative subjects or academic subjects, so that all children—including mine and those of the hon. Lady and others in this House—will be attracted to those subjects and want to choose them. This is about giving all our young people the opportunity to have a broad, engaging and inclusive education and to take the pathway that they want to take.
