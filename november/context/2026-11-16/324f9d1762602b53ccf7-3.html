Turn 18 · Lord Mann (Lab)

My Lords, since 2006 we have had a system—I negotiated it with Prime Minister Blair—that, on a cross-government basis, has been an outstanding success. It is not fail-proof, but many lives have been saved and many attacks have been stopped by the way in which we, cross-party, have had the state fund security for the Jewish community. We should recognise that. If there is a weakness, it is that it is very much an English system, with Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland perhaps not being as advanced in what is needed. Can the Minister, with the Home Office, look at pulling together those three nations to see what more can be done to ensure that we have consistency of security for Jewish communal buildings across the whole of the United Kingdom?

Turn 21 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

I do not accept that the Jewish community in the United Kingdom has brought these attacks on itself, under any circumstance whatever. People of the Jewish faith in the United Kingdom have a perfect right to enjoy their faith, live their lives, go to their schools and enjoy their community centres without any threat at all from an external body, be it an individual or an organisation led by a state threat. There are complex issues, and individuals have views on the State of Israel and the State of Palestine and the Middle East situation. Those views should be exercised and discussed in a way that does not put a threat on any part of any community. That is my policy objective, so I am afraid that I cannot agree with the noble Lord in all he has said today.
