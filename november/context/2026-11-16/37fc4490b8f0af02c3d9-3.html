Turn 19 · Baroness Rock (Con)

My Lords, I declare my interest as a tenant farmer. Tenant farmers do not own their own land. They pay commercial rent to farm and are often unable to enter environmental schemes. Short-term agreements, restrictive tenancy conditions and difficult landlord and tenant relationships are all barriers that prevent tenant farmers investing and building viable businesses. Can the Minister tell me what steps the Government are taking to identify the changes needed to create a resilient, profitable and productive agricultural tenanted sector, including new land-use opportunities?

Turn 20 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

The noble Baroness puts her finger right on this when she talks about the length of tenancies. Longer-term tenancies are clearly extremely important if tenant farmers are to have security in their investments, because why would you invest if your tenancy is going to come to an end in two years? That is an important part of increasing productivity on tenanted farms. To give her some examples, Defra has been working with the National Estate for Nature group to capture and disseminate best practice models for longer-term tenancies. From that, some of its members, such as the Crown Estate and the Duchy of Cornwall, are already moving to 15-year-plus farm business tenancies. That is the absolute direction of travel we need to go in if we are to get that security and profitability for tenant farmers.
