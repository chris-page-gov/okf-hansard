Turn 4 · The Minister of State, Home Office (Lord Hanson of Flint) (Lab)

The potential for drones to be used in hostile action against the UK is, sadly, increasing, driven by rapid advances in technology and tactics. Drones can be used for surveillance, for disruption and as weapons. As we have seen, there has also been a significant rise in their use to facilitate crime. The Home Office is leading work across government to strengthen the UK’s resilience, ensuring that we continue to have the capabilities, powers and legislation needed to respond.

Turn 5 · Lord Farmer (Con)

I thank the Minister for his reply. The Americans have removed planes from RAF Fairford due to unauthorised drone activity; RAF Mildenhall has received threats from drones; and drones were spotted over RAF Lakenheath last week, I believe. What assessment have the Government made of the vulnerability of civilians surrounding those military installations and any political buildings that may be targeted? Do they consider that our defence systems have sufficient capability to detect and neutralise a surge in hostile drones, before they can cause harm?

Turn 6 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

The Government keep the drone threat under continuous review, including the very important point that the noble Lord mentioned about the risk to communities near sensitive sites. Our protection is based on risk, and we are working across government, police and security to ensure that sites understand vulnerabilities and have appropriate security measures in place. It may also be of help to the noble Lord to know that the defence investment plan includes £470 million to enhance security around and at military bases. It is an ongoing challenge, but I hope the Government are addressing it positively.

Turn 8 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

The noble and gallant Lord makes an extremely important point. In the defence investment plan, which is obviously a Ministry of Defence capability, we have supported £750 million for counterdrone capabilities, and as I mentioned to the noble Lord, Lord Farmer, a further £470 million to enhance military site security. But it is absolutely right that we need to have intelligence-led operations that ensure we know when any potential hostile actor is trying to undertake activity and that we continually keep under review our skills, capability and resilience. That is an important point not just for the Home Office but also for the MoD.
