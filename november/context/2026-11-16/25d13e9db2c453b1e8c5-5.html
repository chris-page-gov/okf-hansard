Turn 35 · Speaker unlabelled

The presumption is the starting point for teachers who notice changes in a child’s well-being, behaviour or attendance following family breakdown, perhaps if a parent is wholly absent. It influences early health practitioners and social workers to encourage constructive and safe shared parenting. Family hub workers are required to support parents and children upstream of court proceedings. A change in the legal framework would subtly shift the messages they gave to parents that both of them were important. So if this review becomes a fallback option, it must consider the whole of the separating population and not ignore the majority of parents who do not use the courts. They are the ones who will arguably be most affected by the presumptions of appeal.

Turn 38 · Speaker unlabelled

I can say with absolute certainty that in all the years that I did family law—probably 15 at the Bar and about 30 as a judge—the issue of whether both parents were suitable was absolutely up front; every judge considered that both parents mattered, until you had to consider whether, for one reason or another, one parent might not be suitable. I can tell your Lordships that there were cases where I thought the mother was quite unsuitable and had no difficulty in handing the child to the father. I understand that psychologically, and probably politically, there are concerns that the press will pick up on presumptions, but if this is properly sold to the public—forgive me for using the word “sold”—it really does not need to sound as significant as I think it really is not.
