Turn 2 · The Secretary of State for Education (Lucy Powell)

We will ensure that no child is left behind and no child is held back by where they grow up. The report was right to focus on stronger local leadership, and we are bringing together local leaders to help shape the future of childhood in their areas. We are taking cross-Government action, bringing together public services through our rewiring of the state programme, and our 10-year plan will focus on childhood.

Turn 3 · Lauren Edwards

Medway was one of the lowest-scoring areas in the Children’s Commissioner’s recent report, but, on closer inspection, we see that ranking was heavily based on its 2024 survey, which identified Medway as one of the local authorities whose sample was not representative, with just a few hundred respondents. A lot of good work is happening in Medway to create a brighter future for our young people, as is reflected in a more recent children and young people survey, which had thousands of young respondents, 81% of whom said they “liked” or “strongly liked” their area, up from 68% in 2021. Does the Secretary of State agree that it is much better for us to work with places to build better services around children and family rather than to criticise them? Will she consider visiting Medway to see the good work being done?

Turn 4 · Lucy Powell

I agree with my hon. Friend that we need to be talking areas up, not putting them down. She is also right to say that Medway is offering many of its young people a brighter future and has some fantastic local leaders who are working really hard for young people in their area. She has four Best Start family hubs in Medway, to take just one example. I look forward to us working together and I am sure I will be visiting Medway very soon.

Turn 5 · Sir Ashley Fox (Bridgwater) (Con)

The report by the Children’s Commissioner is a damning indictment of the Liberal Democrats’ record of running Somerset council. To be ranked among the worst places in England for children to grow up is appalling. At the time of the report, Somerset was the third worst out of 148 local authorities for issuing education, health and care plans within the statutory 20-week framework, with just 6% completed on time. I am now told that figure has fallen to 0%. What steps will the Secretary of State take to ensure that the Liberal Democrats do not continue to fail children in Somerset?

Turn 6 · Lucy Powell

I thank the hon. Gentleman for raising issues relating to his constituency. I might add that the report also reflects on previous Governments, in terms of the support they brought in for children, but he is right to say that we need to take further action on supporting children with special educational needs. We are bringing forward our generational reform plans so that we can get more support in earlier, for more children. It is also why we are working with local authorities to ensure that they have a SEND plan that they are able to deliver, and that we can hold them to account for that.
