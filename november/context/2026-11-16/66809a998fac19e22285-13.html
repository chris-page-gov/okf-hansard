Turn 102 · Lord Marks of Henley-on-Thames (LD)

That is a matter for the conscience of individual Members of Parliament and individual Members of this House. The amendment is before the House. If the amendment is right—some noble Lords have said firmly that it is, and others have said that we should wait—then noble Lords will make the decision on that basis, and that is right. I believe that jury equity is a cardinal principle of law; that it is right, and that it is about conscience trumping strict law at the wish of the jury. I do not believe that it should be for the court to decide whether a defendant or counsel should be found guilty or not guilty of contempt at any particular time, and I do not believe that the Committee should delay a decision on the basis that those proceedings are pending, but those are the points made by the noble Lords who took a different opinion after the noble Lord, Lord Pannick, voiced his.
