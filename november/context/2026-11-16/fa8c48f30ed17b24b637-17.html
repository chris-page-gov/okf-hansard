Turn 131 · Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown

The logical impact of what the hon. Gentleman suggests is that we clobber poor people and discourage foreign visitors from coming to this country by imposing a tax on them. The reverse should be the case. We should want businesses to thrive and make a profit, but they have been clobbered by this Government: they have been clobbered by extra taxes on jobs, by inheritance taxes on their businesses and by employment regulations. They have been clobbered by all sorts of things by this Government. The tourism, leisure and hospitality sector is internationally sensitive. Whether people come to an area is very price-sensitive, and if we start making our hospitality more expensive, foreign visitors at the margin will simply not come and our tourist business will go down and down—it is happening already.
