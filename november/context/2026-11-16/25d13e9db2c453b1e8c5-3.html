Turn 21 · Speaker unlabelled

That said, I am interested in what the Government have to say about this. The history of divisions of the High Court is odd: we used to have the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division, known famously as “wills, wives and wrecks”. If we look at criminal law, we see that there are other anomalies out there. I will take 45 seconds to highlight just one. At the moment, if you appeal from the Court of Appeal Criminal Division to the Supreme Court, the Court of Appeal Criminal Division has to certify a point of law of general importance. It can give you permission, but, if it does not, you can get it from the Supreme Court. However, if it does not certify, you are stuck. That has always seemed to be a real problem to me, because it means that, if the Court of Appeal Criminal Division decides a point of law and declines to certify, in effect it shuts out the Supreme Court from looking at it.
