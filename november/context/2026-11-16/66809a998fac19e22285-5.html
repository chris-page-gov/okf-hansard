At present, you have a full rehearing. That is not simply an attempt to deal with the miscarriage of justice; it is a full rerun of the case—another bite of the cherry. Miscarriages of justice are perfectly adequately taken care of in a proper appeals system, which is what we are seeking to bring in. Turn 38 · Speaker unlabelled

The Minister’s final remarks suggested that the answer to there being no oral renewal hearing was that there could be a judicial review or appeal by way of case stated. This line is perhaps somewhat startling, because it is not as if the Administrative Court, or indeed the King’s Bench Division, has plenty of spare capacity to hear lots of renewals against appeals from the magistrates. Further, there does not appear to be any provision for legal aid for those sorts of appeals, and one can anticipate a good deal of chaos. We shall see, perhaps on Report, where those matters go.

Turn 39 · Speaker unlabelled

I also thank the noble Viscount, Lord Hailsham, for his support for the withdrawal and, in particular, the point that he made about the impact on justice. The overall thrust of the Committee’s contributions is that there is something special about the importance of appeals from magistrates’ courts, given the nature of lay justice. Perhaps that is the answer that the noble Lord, Lord Pannick, did not discern when he was considering why this is the only form of appeal that is without a permission requirement. Perhaps it is because there is an opportunity for rough justice by non-lawyers. The House will have to think deeply about that on Report. For now, I am prepared to withdraw my opposition to the clause standing part.
