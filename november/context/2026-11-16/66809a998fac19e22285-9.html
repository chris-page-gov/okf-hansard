Turn 70 · Speaker unlabelled

Section 28 of the Youth Justice and Criminal Evidence Act 1999 introduces pre-trial video-recorded cross-examination as a special measure. This measure has been rolled out only to the Crown Courts; when it is used, eligible victims are cross-examined via a video recording at an earlier date before trial outside the courtroom setting. Only the judge, lawyers and defendant are present in the courtroom on the day of the Section 28 hearing and screens can be used to prevent the defendant seeing the victim. If Section 28 is granted, the questions that will be asked during cross-examination have to be approved by the judge at the ground rules hearing, so any improper or vague questions are removed. The recording is then played back on the trial date to the jury. This prevents any need for the victim to attend the trial.

Turn 71 · Speaker unlabelled

Following a Section 28 hearing, victims are not usually required to give evidence again. However, there are certain circumstances in which they may be called back. There are barriers to granting a Section 28 hearing at the moment, because access is not automatic and remains subject to judicial discretion. There are also factors beyond the victim’s needs that appear to be influencing access, including concerns about conviction rates, that court delays can work against victims, that Section 28 can be granted and later withdrawn and that eligible victims currently face different thresholds for accessing Section 28. These factors create uncertainty and inconsistency for victims. That is why this amendment would help, as it would ensure parity of service for victims.
