Turn 4 · The Secretary of State for Education (Lucy Powell)

Alan Milburn’s report on youth opportunity is a clarion call for us all. He is clear that although college and training create strong pathways into work and success, the journey begins much earlier. We know the scale of the challenge we face, with almost 1 million young people not in employment, education or training. I will ensure that no child is left behind and that education faces the future, not the past, and engages every young person, giving them a pathway to success.

Turn 5 · Andrew Ranger

The Education and Skills Act 2008 saw one of the biggest changes to our education system, requiring a young person to be in some form of education or training until they are 18. Yet 18 years on, thousands are still falling through the gaps as we still have no national database that tracks the situation and provides alerts. Instead, we rely on a patchwork of multiple organisations with no comprehensive overall picture of the true circumstances facing these young people. Can my right hon. Friend confirm whether she and the Government are looking at addressing this issue so that we never leave a young person behind?

Turn 6 · Lucy Powell

My hon. Friend is absolutely right. This generation of young people has been let down over the last 15 years—there is no way of sugarcoating it. He is absolutely right that one of the most appalling statistics is that there are nearly 200,000 young people—16, 17 and 18-year-olds—who should be in college or education. He is also right to say that we need the data sharing, the transition points and the accountability to ensure that all our young people are in education, training or employment, and that is something we will embark on.
