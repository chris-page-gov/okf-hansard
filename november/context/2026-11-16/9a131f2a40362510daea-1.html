Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Baroness Lloyd of Effra) (Lab)

My Lords, the Government recognise the widespread disruption and inconvenience caused by the Microsoft outage on Thursday evening and Friday. This was a serious incident affecting individuals, businesses, the health sector and other public services. However, there were no major critical service impacts, and the public sector resilience plans were effectively implemented. Key services, such as 999, remained operational. Accident and emergency departments remained open. Transport services were unaffected. Borders remained operational. Our mobile, broadband and landline connectivity remained working. As soon as this incident emerged, we were in touch with Microsoft to discuss the issue. The first duty of any Government is to ensure national security and the protection of citizens. We are undertaking a significant programme of work to improve the digital resilience of our nation.

Turn 6 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

My Lords, the Government indeed need secure and resilient digital infrastructure, greater choice and stronger UK capability. That is the focus of our work—to understand and manage supplier concentration and strategic dependencies. Through, for example, the national cloud infrastructure programme, we are looking at how the Government’s significant cloud demand can be used more strategically to strengthen competition and resilience, increase choice and manage the risks associated with reliance on a small number of providers. That is the way in which we are taking forward the balance of getting the right technologies for the Government and the country to ensure that the Government are a strong customer of the whole cloud market, while supporting greater portability, interoperability and opportunities.

Turn 7 · Baroness Coussins (CB)

My Lords, I declare an interest as chair of the National Resilience Committee, whose report is published today. In relation to technology and communications issues, will the Minister persuade the Government to abandon the planned switch-off of digital terrestrial television?
