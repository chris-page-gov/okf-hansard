Turn 5 · The Parliamentary Secretary, HM Treasury (Lord Pitt-Watson) (Lab)

I welcome this Question. The Government are closely monitoring developments following the Binance security breach and are engaging with the Financial Conduct Authority and other authorities to assess the implications for the UK. Binance is not authorised or registered to provide crypto asset services to the UK. However, it is possible that UK customers may still have been affected, and the Government are currently working to understand the associated impacts and risks.

Turn 7 · Lord Pitt-Watson (Lab)

We are looking to learn from what happened at Binance, but I am not clear that it makes sense to put in an interim arrangement. By October next year, we will have a comprehensive FCA regime for crypto assets, including disclosure, market abuse and resilience. To put that in place requires changes by the firms, and it then requires authorisation. There will be an iterative process there, and I think it might not be helpful to have an interim regime in the middle of that. It would perhaps slow things down.

Turn 8 · Baroness Kramer (LD)

My Lords, the hacking of Binance demonstrates that large-scale theft is far more possible in a crypto world. At present, UK banks are resisting providing accounts, financial services and credit lines to crypto service providers, but that is expected to change in the next year when the FCA’s crypto providers authorisation scheme kicks in. What would be the consequence to bank stability of such a heist happening after that authorisation and the relationship created between the banks and the crypto world? Would the banks be covering the losses? It is certainly unclear who is covering the losses now.
