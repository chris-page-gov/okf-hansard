Turn 145 · Speaker unlabelled

In the year to July 2026, just 3,427 small boat arrivals were returned by any route—fewer than one in 10 of last year’s arrivals. Any smuggler can do the maths. We know that certainty of removal works. When we agreed a returns agreement with Albania in late 2022, Albanian small boat arrivals fell from 12,658 in 2022 to 927 in 2023—a fall of 93%. When people know that they will be sent back, they stop coming.

Turn 146 · Speaker unlabelled

We know what failure looks like. Let me start close to home: in October 2023, Ahmed Alid tried to murder his housemate in Hartlepool, then went out into the street and stabbed to death Terence Carney, a 70-year-old man out for an early morning walk. Alid was sentenced just up the road from my constituency at Teesside Crown court. He came to this country without permission in 2020, having already tried and failed to get asylum in several other European countries. He was arrested, and only then did he claim asylum. His claim was refused, but three years later he was still here in Home Office accommodation. Under new clause 42, he would have been subject to a duty to remove, and under new clause 43, the claim he made after arrest could not have stopped that.

Turn 147 · Speaker unlabelled

New clause 43 also deals with claims that stall removal. The Home Office’s own analysis found that among small boat arrivals detained for return, the proportion referred to the modern slavery system rose from 52% in 2020 to 73% in 2021. Figures in this area have been contested for foreign offenders, but the pattern is plain, and the “bad faith” power that this House gave Ministers was used to disqualify just six people in the whole of 2025.

Turn 148 · Speaker unlabelled

New clauses 55 and 56 would make the consequence permanent. Today, someone could come here illegally, be removed and later apply through a legal route as if nothing had happened or stay, sit out the backlog and eventually get status and citizenship. Settlement grants hit 199,628 in the year to June 2026—the highest for 16 years. The Government took the statutory citizenship ban out of the law in the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act and put a weaker version in guidance instead. Since February 2025, Home Office guidance has said that people who came here illegally will “normally” be refused citizenship, but guidance can be changed with a written statement on a Thursday afternoon, and “normally” leaves the door ajar.

Turn 150 · Speaker unlabelled

We need to be clear about our motivation, because it is not cruelty, and anyone who says so has not been listening; it is the opposite. Every crossing is a dangerous journey in an overloaded dinghy, organised by criminal gangs who treat human beings as cargo. People have died in the Channel; children have died in the Channel. The single most compassionate thing that this House can do is to end the incentive that puts them in those boats. The only thing that ends that incentive is certainty, the certainty that anyone who comes that way will not be allowed to stay.

Turn 151 · Speaker unlabelled

This is also about fairness to those who play by the rules—the nurse who came on the right visa, the family who waited for years for a resettlement place or the refugee who came through a safe and legal route. Every time someone jumps the queue by paying a smuggler, it is those people who are let down. A system that rewards breaking the rules is unfair to everyone who keeps them. This is also about the capacity of the country—every hotel room and every pound spent on support for someone with no right to be here is a room and a pound not available to those we have chosen to help.
