Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

This creates a significant accountability gap. Jewish patients are therefore largely invisible in ethnicity-based assessments, as their religious identity is rarely captured in patient data. If we cannot identify where discrimination is occurring, how can we reasonably expect the NHS bodies to address it? That is precisely why Amendment 258 would require the annual report to include data broken down by religion as well as ethnicity. It is also why I hope the Government will act on the recommendation of the noble Lord, Lord Mann, to introduce Jewish and Sikh categories, which they say they are still considering. These are necessary steps towards ensuring that antisemitism is properly identified, measured and addressed across the health service.

Turn 11 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 29 concerns the education and training duty this Bill transfers from NHS England to the Secretary of State. It would require that training to include recognising, preventing and responding to antisemitism and other forms of racism, as recommendations 32 to 36 of the review set out. Some of this already sits in the NHS core skills guidance, but that guidance is owned by the body this Bill is winding down. If the duty moves, the commitment should move with it.

Turn 12 · Speaker unlabelled

I want to say a word about what training should look like. Too much equality training has left antisemitism out altogether, or, worse, has placed Jews outside the protection it claims to offer. A tick-box module completed alone at a screen, in my experience, is unlikely to change behaviour. Again, in my experience of caring for doctors, attitudes change with time, and the space and time to reflect with one’s peers is needed. That is why the noble Lord, Lord Mann, called for quality assured content developed with subject experts and for its impact to be monitored and reported publicly.

Turn 14 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, Amendment 259 touches a part of my own profession directly. Every GP in England, myself included, must be on a performers list to practise. At present, conditions can be placed on a practitioner’s inclusion on that list on efficiency grounds but not on suitability grounds. Recommendation 26 of the review asked the Government to change that so it can address racist and extremist conduct directly. This is a modest amendment: it asks only that the Government tell Parliament within six months whether they intend to amend the regulations, and if not, why not.

Turn 15 · Speaker unlabelled

These are probing amendments and I do not intend to press them to a vote, but I have four questions for the Minister. First, I welcome the Government’s first progress update, which was published on 27 October. However, it describes next June’s report as the final one. Will the Minister commit to continuing to report to Parliament every year after that, as Amendment 258 would require? Secondly, will the Government ensure that the NHS staff survey data on discrimination is published by religion as well as by ethnicity? Thirdly, will the Minister confirm that the consultation on protected characteristics data, planned for early 2027, will include Jewish and Sikh categories, as the noble Lord, Lord Mann, recommended? Fourthly, when will the Government amend the performers list regulations, as recommended in the noble Lord’s recommendation 26?
