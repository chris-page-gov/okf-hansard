Turn 100 · Gareth Bacon

I beg to move, That the clause be read a Second time. The most recent data available to us shows that the waiting list for social housing stands at a shocking and shameful 1.34 million people. However, that data, which comes from a Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government publication from March 2025, is now well out of date. To understand one of the fundamental problems that we are here to rectify, which is how to get people off the streets, out of temporary accommodation and into social housing, we must understand the full scale of the issue before us. My new clause 52 would therefore require housing register applications to be reviewed at least every three years to ensure that the waiting list remains accurate and up to date. Turn 101 · Matthew Pennycook

I thank the shadow Minister for tabling and speaking to this new clause. I am enjoying the irony of a shadow Minister who bemoans this Government’s, in his words, zealous overcentralisation and penchant for regulation, but who proposes a new clause that would impose on local authorities a statutory requirement to review their housing register at least once every three years—a new duty that would create additional administrative burdens—without any evidence that it would improve allocation decisions. Local authorities take different approaches to managing their housing register. It is right that they have the flexibility to do what works best for their own local area. In addition, councils already reassess housing need both before a household joins a waiting list and again before allocating a social home, and thereby ensure that social homes go to those most in need.

Turn 102 · Speaker unlabelled

In August, as I have mentioned a number of times in Committee, we launched a call for evidence for our review of the statutory guidance on social housing allocations. That review gives us an opportunity to consider whether clearer expectations would help local authorities to maintain accurate registers, including how they review applications and respond to changes in circumstances, but our clear starting point is that this is a process that local authorities are best placed to manage themselves. We intend to address expectations through statutory guidance if necessary.
