Turn 66 · Speaker unlabelled

I am a very proud and long-standing member of the All-Party Parliamentary Group on HIV, AIDS and Sexual Health. The Mildmay Mission and the Sussex Beacon were set up a long time ago to be palliative care systems. Luckily for that community, the purpose for which they were set up changed because of treatment plans. But repurposing those buildings into centres of excellent care and treatment for people who are getting old, often with comorbidities, is a battle for those organisations. Why? Because there have been so many NHS reorganisations that there is no institutional memory about provision of care. Bear in mind that this was 40 years ago. It was the HIV sector that really shook up what was then considered to be the classic model of palliative care.

Turn 67 · Speaker unlabelled

The noble Lord, Lord Patel, is absolutely right: co-ordination is needed above all else when a patient and their family are in that state. A key part of it is advance choice documents—personally, I would make them mandatory, but I know that we cannot. They are the single transformative thing for patients and their families. I am very lucky; I knew exactly what my wife wanted and did not want. I have talked to other carers in the same position I was in; they do not. That is extremely difficult for them. In our case, it made it so much easier to talk and agree with doctors about what was going to happen and when.
