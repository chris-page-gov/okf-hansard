Turn 33 · Lord Mawson (CB)

My Lords, I too support Amendment 22 and am encouraged by this debate, which definitely needs to happen. Indeed, the GPs I work with in the East End of London would definitely want to open up these very choices to patients from many different backgrounds, because sometimes one can get a sense on the ground, certainly in challenging communities, that the NHS can be quite condescending to people in assuming they do not want these kinds of choices. Yet if you go into East End housing estates you will find lots of different people living there from lots of different backgrounds who do want choice. This is a really important message and needs to be grappled with and taken seriously.

Turn 34 · Speaker unlabelled

On a practical level when we as a country and the NHS are in such a difficult financial position, I mentioned at Second Reading that the NHS currently spends £90 million a year on unused rooms in health buildings, many of them primary care buildings. Indeed, we have unused rooms sitting there that are costing the NHS all this money. There are deals to be done here with the independent, social and charitable sectors that make financial sense and health sense. I raised this question at Second Reading but got no reply: what are the Government going to do about that opportunity? How do we grasp that nettle of £90 million and turn empty rooms into opportunities for patients in the NHS?
