Turn 5 · The Earl of Clancarty (CB)

My Lords, we hear the legal and nationalistic arguments for return, but surely the key argument is the aesthetic one. If, for example, Picasso’s “Guernica” were to be cut into pieces and displayed in different museums, there would be an almighty clamour to get those pieces reunited. Should not the same consideration be given to Phidias’s masterwork? Secondly, the Government say that this is a matter for the British Museum, except that its hands are tied by the British Museum Act. Will the Government amend the Act in a limited way so that a decision can be made?

Turn 6 · Baroness Twycross (Lab)

The British Museum and the Government recognise the strength of feeling on this. The British Museum has consistently stated that it would consider a loan request for the sculptures on the condition that the British Museum’s ownership is acknowledged and that a binding undertaking is given to return them. In relation to the Charities Act 2022, which I think is what the noble Earl was alluding to, the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport has committed to review the implementation of Sections 15 and 16 as part of the post-implementation review of the whole of that Act. This review is due to report in February 2027.
