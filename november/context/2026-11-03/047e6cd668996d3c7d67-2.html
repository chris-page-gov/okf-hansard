Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

Identifying carers, recognising them as partners in care, involving them properly in hospital discharge planning, and ensuring that their own health and well-being are considered should not be optional extras. The issue of respite and breaks from caring in my noble friend Lady Tyler’s Amendments 175 and 176 is really important. A meaningful break is not a luxury; it is often the intervention that enables carers to continue to care, year in, year out, yet too many carers cannot access the support they need and too many face a postcode lottery in provision. If we are serious about prevention, supporting carers before they reach that crisis point must become a priority. The cost of failing to do so is borne not only by the carers but by the wider health and care system when caring arrangements break down.

Turn 11 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendments 21, 173 and 174, from the noble Lord, Lord Hunt, look to enshrine in law the right of an individual to have access to in-person support from a health and care partner. A legal right to nominate a health and care partner would ensure that people’s chosen representatives are welcomed as an essential part of the care team. This would make it easier for health and care staff to understand who is so important to the person that they are not just a visitor. It would rebalance the power in care settings and provide the comfort of knowing that the person you have chosen as your eyes, ears and voice can be there for you when you need them.
