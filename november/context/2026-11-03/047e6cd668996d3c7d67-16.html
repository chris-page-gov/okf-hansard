Turn 121 · Speaker unlabelled

I emphasise that this amendment applies across all healthcare professionals. The principle should not depend on which professional regulator happens to have jurisdiction. Patients do not distinguish between different regulatory structures. They simply expect that those who abuse them sexually while occupying positions of professional trust will no longer be fit to practise. I believe the vast majority of healthcare professionals will strongly support this amendment. I am amazed that we have had to table it and that this is not the existing law.

Turn 122 · Speaker unlabelled

The amendment is about patient safety. It is about maintaining confidence in professional regulation, supporting victims who have the courage to come forward and recognising that sexual misconduct by a healthcare professional is not simply another disciplinary offence; it is a fundamental violation of the trust that underpins healthcare itself. When that trust has been abused in such a serious way, the privilege of professional registration should be lost permanently. I look forward to the Minister’s response, and I hope she will look favourably on this amendment or tell me it is not necessary because the Government are already going to do it.

Turn 126 · Speaker unlabelled

I am not criticising GPs for the choices they make and the pressures they are under. I am simply saying that if you are going to have a workforce plan that looks at resilient primary care, you have to get under the skin of this level of detail. Head-in-the-sand wishful thinking of “get the training numbers up and all will be well” has not worked. That is despite the fact that, between 2015 and 2019-20, the proportion of the NHS budget spent on primary medical care deliberately increased—although it has fallen back since. That was not principally a problem of resource input on to the training. This is why we need a holistic view of the workforce, not just the training pipeline, to answer these sorts of questions. Those are part of the reasons why Amendment 253, which draws attention specifically to primary care, is so important.
