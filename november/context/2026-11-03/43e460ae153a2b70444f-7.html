Turn 49 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to new clauses 47 and 61, tabled by the shadow Minister and the hon. Member for Newbury respectively. New clause 47 would require the Secretary of State to publish guidance on the social responsibilities of registered providers of social housing, and new clause 61 would require the regulator to publish a home and place standard, alongside a methodology for assessing providers against that standard.

Turn 50 · Speaker unlabelled

All registered providers of social housing must deliver the outcomes set out in the regulatory standards that are set by the independent regulator. Those standards specify a range of outcomes, including those relating to the safety and quality of homes, where providers must have a good understanding of the condition of their homes and meet the decent homes standard. They require providers to treat their tenants with fairness and respect and to take action to deliver fair and equitable outcomes for them. The standards also set outcomes relating to the role that social housing providers must play to support social, environmental and economic wellbeing within the areas in which they operate, and they require registered providers to work in partnership with the local authority, the police and other relevant organisations to deter and tackle antisocial behaviour.

Turn 51 · Speaker unlabelled

The regulator seeks assurance from registered providers that the outcomes set out in those standards are being met, including through routine inspections of large registered providers, and it has a range of enforcement powers at its disposal, where required. Taking all of that into account, I am of the view that neither an additional quality standard nor further guidance on the social responsibilities of registered providers is necessary.

Turn 52 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to new clauses 48 and 49. New clause 48 would require the Secretary of State to undertake a review of the governance arrangements of registered providers of social housing, while new clause 49 would require the Secretary of State to publish guidance establishing minimum governance standards for registered providers. I agree that strong governance, transparency and accountability are fundamental to a well-functioning social housing sector, but it is my view that strong governance mechanisms are already in place.

Turn 53 · Speaker unlabelled

Private registered providers are already subject to the governance and financial viability standard set by the regulator. The standard requires providers to have effective governance arrangements that deliver their aims and intended outcomes for tenants in an effective, transparent and accountable manner. It also requires them to be accountable to tenants, the regulator and relevant stakeholders.

Turn 54 · Speaker unlabelled

All registered providers must also meet the transparency, influence and accountability standard, which requires providers to give tenants meaningful opportunities to influence and scrutinise their strategies, policies and services. Providers are required to publish information about their performance through the tenant satisfaction measures. The regulator is currently considering how its approach to economic regulation should evolve, through its “More and better and social homes” discussion document, including how regulation of governance can be strengthened. In that light, I do not think that either a separate statutory review or further guidance from the Secretary of State is necessary.

Turn 55 · Speaker unlabelled

New clause 50, which was also tabled by the shadow Minister, would require senior officers of registered providers to attend local authority overview and scrutiny committees in certain circumstances. The Government recognise the importance of strong relationships between PRPs and local authorities, and we are committed to giving local leaders more influence over housing in their local areas. Social housing providers have an important role to play in their communities. We expect them to work constructively with local authorities and to respond openly and transparently where concerns arise. In that context, I should hope that senior officers of registered providers would be willing to engage with their local authority in whatever form was most suitable to the specific circumstance.

Turn 56 · Speaker unlabelled

Although oversight of registered providers is important, it is my view that it would be disproportionate to require a senior officer of a registered provider to attend a local authority’s overview and scrutiny committee meeting. We are bringing forward an enhanced scrutiny regime through schedules 4 and 5 to the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026, which makes provision for local scrutiny committees for mayoral strategic authorities. Those will have the power to require a key person to attend meetings or provide information where required. A “key person” can mean the mayor, deputy mayor, mayoral commissioners, officers of the authority and members of the authority with policy responsibilities, as well as others who will be specified in regulations.
