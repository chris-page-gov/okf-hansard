Turn 105 · Speaker unlabelled

The Royal College of Psychiatrists advises that investing in the psychiatric workforce will be essential for effective preventive mental health care, staff well-being and successful NHS service delivery. Vacancy rates are just one stark reminder. The most recent Royal College of Psychiatrists census found that around one in seven consultant psychiatrists posts in England was vacant or unfilled—this was in 2025—but the true vacancy rate, it suggests, was 27% because more than one-quarter of consultant psychiatrist posts across England were vacant or filled by locum or non-substantive consultants. In the quarter ending June 2026, the medical and nursing vacancy rates in English mental health trusts were 10.8% and 8.1% respectively, much higher than those in acute trusts, which were 5.1% for medical vacancies and 4.8% for nursing vacancies.

Turn 112 · Speaker unlabelled

Let us look at the people on whom we rely for those services. The Royal College of Nursing found that the NHS in England employed 5,368 learning disability nurses in May 2010. By July 2024, the figure was 3,034—a fall of 43%. In 2024, no university in the south-east offered a degree for people wishing to do nursing with learning disabilities as their main specialty. Nearly one in three NHS trusts have no acute liaison service with a registered learning disability nurse. Reports last year quoted a senior source as saying that England could have no domestic supply of these nurses by 2028.
