Turn 133 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn briefly to Amendment 240, from my noble friend Lord Blencathra, concerning sexual misconduct by healthcare professionals. Patient safety and public confidence must remain paramount. No patient should have to fear exploitation or abuse at the hands of someone they trust with their care. There are important questions about proportionality and the appropriate role of professional regulators, but the underlying concern raised by this amendment is entirely legitimate. Can the Minister explain whether the Government are satisfied with the current regulatory framework and whether it deals robustly and consistently with proven sexual misconduct? Will the Government consider whether further action is indeed needed to ensure that professionals who pose a serious risk to patients cannot simply return to clinical practice, perhaps to repeat the same offences?

Turn 134 · Speaker unlabelled

I return to workforce planning and will make two or three points. We have heard much about the Government’s ambitions to shift care from hospitals to the community—something that all noble Lords would support. It is therefore appropriate to highlight that primary care is also suffering under this reorganisation. The Royal College of General Practitioners notes that GPs receive the lowest share of the NHS budget, estimated at around 8.3%, but GP workload continues to rise, albeit with some of the demographic challenges we see as we shift to a more feminised workforce, and some of the challenges women GPs face as they balance work and life challenges. We know that, as we shift more from hospital to community, primary care will face even more pressure. Do the Government intend to implement a commensurate shift of resources?
