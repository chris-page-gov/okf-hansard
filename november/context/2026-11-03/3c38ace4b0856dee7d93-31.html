Turn 241 · Speaker unlabelled

Even if the intention is for appeal rights to continue, new clause 54 would not just affect protection and human rights claims. Decisions made on student or worker applications, or any other type of immigration decision against which there is no right of appeal, would have no means of judicial oversight. That would be a draconian step that would undermine the rule of law and go against the values the UK has always proudly sought to uphold. For those reasons, I cannot support new clause 54.

Turn 248 · Matt Vickers

I beg to move, That the clause be read a Second time. New clause 47 is one line long, and would abolish legal aid for applications or claims about a person’s immigration status or right to be in the UK. Immigration and asylum legal aid cost £59 million in 2024-25, which paid for more than 64,000 pieces of work. The Government have since added a £20 million-a-year uplift to the fees. Under this Government, more taxpayer money goes to immigration litigation, not less. Let me put it simply: a family in Stockton who cannot get legal aid for a dispute with their employer or for most family court cases are entitled to ask why it is there for someone who came here illegally to fight their removal.
