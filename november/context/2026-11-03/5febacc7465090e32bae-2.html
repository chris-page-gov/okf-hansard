Turn 10 · Lord Leong (Lab)

I totally agree with my noble friend. The labour market is changing, and the provisional ONS figures for the year to September 2026 show that the payroll has increased by 66,000 in administrative and support services, by 20,000 in education and by 19,000 in health and social care. The wholesale and retail sector lost 71,000, accommodation and food services lost 46,000 and manufacturing 32,000. We are backing investment into skills and sector jobs plans to help businesses recruit and people move into secure long-term employment.

Turn 14 · Lord Leong (Lab)

Employers, employees and suppliers are part of the ecosystem, and we need to support the employers and suppliers as well. The way to tackle unemployment is by working with employers and looking at pathways into growing industries. Professional and business services firms have pledged to create 22,400 apprenticeships for young people under the age of 25 over the next five years. Construction aims to create 10,000 job opportunities each year, while digital and technology employers are targeting 20,000 entry-level jobs. These sector jobs plans will link investment to skills and recruitment, and our focus is on turning those commitments into lasting careers.

Turn 16 · Lord Leong (Lab)

My Lords, we are determined to tackle youth unemployment and look forward to receiving Alan Milburn’s recommendations. In addition, we have committed a further £2.5 billion to the youth guarantee and growth and skills levy, helping nearly 1 million young people aged 16 to 24 move into jobs, education and training. This will create 200,000 jobs, provide £3,000 for youth jobs grants and £2,000 in apprenticeship hiring payments and guarantee jobs for young people claiming universal credit who have been unemployed long term.
