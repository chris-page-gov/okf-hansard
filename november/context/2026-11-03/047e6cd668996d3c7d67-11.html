Turn 82 · Speaker unlabelled

I am grateful to the Minister for her comprehensive overview—I am afraid that I am less confident in ICBs going on to commission. I want to make it clear that specialist palliative care and hospices are not synonymous: the one does not mean the other. They are part of a spectrum of care delivered, and you have NHS specialist teams and those that are funded by the charitable sector. The problem I have been highlighting is the deficit across the piece in providing care universally. Different models are important because there are different societies and different ways that people live.

Turn 83 · Speaker unlabelled

The other problem with the hospice construct and the building of it is that hospices have tended to grow up in wealthier areas. In very poor areas and rural areas, access is much reduced, so teams must operate very flexibly. It is the specialist teams that upskill the generalists, and it was in specialist services that we learned the basics of morphine and antiemetic prescribing. That was not happening anywhere in general services, so to think that you can have one without the other is deeply misleading. Specialist services will look after only a small number of complex problems and will be there intermittently as people need support. As my noble friend Lord Patel pointed out, the consultant was available; fortunately, they were not needed, but that support had been there. It is that framework that I was seeking to get into the Bill.

Turn 84 · Speaker unlabelled

Other noble Lords have made important points, and I wish that I had time to answer every one of them. On advance care planning, there is no difference between what is happening and the amendment tabled by the noble Baroness, Lady Pidgeon. My concern is that if you put that in the Bill, you do not have flexibility and run the risk of people saying, “Have we had the conversation? Yes, we have”, and it being ticked off on the single patient record. Advance care planning has evolved through specialist palliative care. They have been the leaders nationally in developing it and running the conferences on it. Knowing what people want, listening and allowing flexibility is crucial. Those conversations must happen.

Turn 85 · Speaker unlabelled

Following on from this, I hope we can have a meeting with the Minister and noble Lords who have contributed to this important debate to find a way forward.
