Turn 9 · Earl Russell (LD)

My Lords, as the Minister rightly identified, grid upgrades are essential for our future electrification. On that note, the National Audit Office reported in September that up to £70 billion of transition investment may be needed by 2031 and that any delays could lead to higher energy bills. NESO has identified eight projects that could cut constraint costs if accelerated, and the NAO has called for stronger oversight and transparent monitoring of delivery. Will the Government act on these recommendations, and how will they ensure that the grid is ready for future demand?

Turn 15 · Baroness Boycott (CB)

My Lords, at the moment, 23% of Ireland’s power is going to data centres. This caused a €360 increase in household bills between 2015 and 2023—this is a cautionary tale. Will the Minister commit that the data centres we build here will be run as much as possible from renewables, and that consumer bills will not be affected by helping these giant American tech companies? In other words, will the renewables pay for the infrastructure they need? Will she also ensure that Drax, which is opening an AI centre, phases out using biomass, which can no longer be regarded as a sustainable fuel?

Turn 16 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

Again, there is a substantial amount in that question. On the general point, we understand that local communities, and indeed people more generally, have concerns about data centres—that perhaps they dominate unfairly. We regard them as vital components of our future, but they have to be managed properly. We have introduced a Connections Accelerator Service, and data centres going through that are required to meet five tests: lower electricity bills, investing in local communities, high environmental standards, and generally maintaining British security. That addresses a number of the noble Baroness’s questions, and I can reassure her that we have that issue in mind.
