Turn 18 · Pat McFadden

My hon. Friend is absolutely right that this is a problem throughout the United Kingdom—no part of the United Kingdom can look at itself and say, “We do not have a problem here.” The Prime Minister, the Chancellor and I attended a meeting with the Scottish and Welsh First Ministers in Glasgow a couple of weeks ago where this was a top item on the agenda. Turn 22 · Pat McFadden

I thank my right hon. Friend for his question, and I appreciate his concern. The truth is that we are putting forward this new settlement because we believe that it is a better deal for young people. Let me give him a figure from another review—this one conducted by Charlie Mayfield, on keeping people in work. He pointed out that a young person who falls out of work and on to long-term benefits can lose up to £1 million in lifetime earnings, with it costing the state a similar sum to support that person. Let us get this support to young people so that they have the experience of earning and making their way. That is what this settlement offers.

Turn 24 · Pat McFadden

The personalised local support that we are speaking of will have to be available throughout the UK, whether or not people have a strategic mayor. That is the way that many of our neighbouring countries and partners do it, and it is the way that we should proceed. There is a good reason to do so, because local travel to work areas and labour markets differ around the country. This is about local authorities as well as mayors getting support to young people in the right place to help them in their local area.
