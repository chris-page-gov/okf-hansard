Turn 23 · Speaker unlabelled

The difficulty is that NHS decisions bind only the NHS, and there is still a bit of a Wild West. A 16 year-old or 17 year-old refused hormones by the NHS can still obtain them from a private clinic or on a prescription from a doctor elsewhere in Europe that can then be dispensed here. Without reference to, or supervision by, adequate health providers, doctors or others, this is simply not safe. We have heard all the evidence why these very powerful hormones can cause lasting change and damage to young people.

Turn 24 · Speaker unlabelled

I do not suggest that this is necessarily the correct amendment or the correct wording; that is for exploration in the Committee. I tabled the amendment because, while supporting all the steps taken and the banning within the NHS of the prescribing of cross-sex hormones, I hope to explore with the Minister how to close the current loopholes. This would make a much safer environment for young people, who, as we have heard multiple times and know from the evidence, in a majority desist and resolve their gender dysphoria as they get older. I understand that there is currently a review into this matter. That has been happening for some time, but the work has stalled through successive changes of Secretary of State, and so I want to use the opportunity of the Health Bill and the debate now to ask the Minister what the Government propose to address these very worrying issues.
