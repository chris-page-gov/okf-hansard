Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero (Martin McCluskey)

The Government are committed to delivering the warm homes plan. We are investing billions of pounds to support households to upgrade their homes with technologies to lower bills, cut fuel poverty and create skilled jobs. We have taken difficult decisions to fund the defence investment plan, but the vast majority of funding for low-income and social housing will be protected.

Turn 3 · Sarah Olney

The autumn Budget confirmed a £1 billion reduction in capital grant funding for the warm homes plan. While the Government insist the majority of funding for low-income households and social housing is protected, the cuts suggest otherwise. The £25 million pilot scheme, which would have given free plug-in solar panels to low-income households, has been scrapped, while the 0% VAT rate on solar panels, batteries and heat pumps is also due to end. As the roll-out of the warm homes plan is already significantly behind the Government’s own ambitions, with fewer than 6,000 homes receiving upgrades, what assessment has the Minister made of the impact of these cuts on the delivery of the programme?

Turn 4 · Martin McCluskey

Tens of thousands of homes have received upgrades through Government-funded schemes in recent years. Our ambition for the warm homes plan remains: we will upgrade 5 million homes by the end of this Parliament and, while difficult decisions have been made as part of the defence investment plan, we still have many billions of pounds of spending for home upgrades. We also have the future homes standard and the minimum energy efficiency standards, which will come into play and create upgrades across the housing sector.

Turn 6 · Bill Esterson (Sefton Central) (Lab)

As the Minister knows, not every home is suitable for a heat pump, solar or battery. I encourage him to look at the work in this space of York University, which has identified significant savings for people in fuel poverty through the adoption of appliances including air fryers, dehumidifiers and clothing racks, and indeed heated wearables and electric blankets. Alongside the warm homes plan, this is a great way of enabling people to take advantage of electrification, which is consistent with the Government’s agenda.
