Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero (Ms Polly Billington)

I welcome the hon. Member’s advocacy for people living with a terminal illness. No one facing a terminal illness should have to worry about their energy bills. I have seen reports in the media about Marie Curie and its campaign on fuel poverty and people who are experiencing a terminal illness. One of the first things I did when I took this job was to invite representatives from disability and age-related health charities—including Scope, Mencap, Sense, Age UK, Fair by Design—along with Citizens Advice and Martin Lewis, to speak to me about how we tackle fuel poverty among those people, given their exposure to high energy bills. I will meet Marie Curie in a couple of weeks to hear directly about those pressures, and discuss how we can best support those in need.

Turn 3 · John Milne

I thank the Minister for that reply. Energy costs for people with a terminal illness often increase after diagnosis because of an increased need for heating or to run medical devices. As the Minister said, research from Marie Curie has found that since 2023 nearly 400,000 people have died in fuel poverty, which equates to about one person every four minutes. Will the Minister consider introducing a social tariff for energy, specifically for people living with terminal illness, to provide at least a 50% reduction in their energy bill to help them meet the extra cost? We could, for example, use the recently published Energy Independence Bill.

Turn 4 · Ms Billington

It is extremely important for us to consider that issue, and how we want to reform the overall energy retail market. There is consensus, particularly on the Labour Benches, that the energy retail market is broken, and that is why we are looking to reform gas standing charges, and electricity standing charges over time. As we do that, we will increasingly need to work out how we target help for people who have unavoidably high energy usage because of medical need and so on. We must ensure that support for one group does not increase costs on others, which is why we are looking carefully at who should be supported, how that support should be funded, and how it can be delivered fairly. I would welcome support from across the House for us to establish proper data matching, so that we can get people the help they need without them having to apply for it.
