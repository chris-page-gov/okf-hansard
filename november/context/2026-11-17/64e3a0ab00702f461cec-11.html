Turn 83 · Speaker unlabelled

However, from my visits and discussions with women’s centres, charities and their advocates over the years, it is clear that there are still two fundamental problems. First, there needs to be capacity to ensure that all women have access to the services, and this may be compounded by the welcome increase in the number of community sentences. Secondly, there is a lack of long-term funding for the centres, as so clearly identified by my noble friend Lord Hunt. Across the country, women’s centres are often anxious about their future, relying on short-term grants, charitable contributions and short-term commissioning arrangements, often with a lack of a local commissioning plan to guarantee the resilience of services required in the much longer term.

Turn 84 · Speaker unlabelled

I strongly believe that the immense value of women’s centres should be clearly identified in this Bill, to ensure that essential programmes are consistently commissioned in every ICB area to improve prevention, diagnosis, treatment, awareness of services and, crucially, access to them. If we are serious about reducing the number of women entering the criminal justice system, we must further develop upstream services for early intervention, such as liaison and diversion services, and community-based services supporting rehabilitation and resettlement, with women’s centres at their heart. This ambition must be embedded in this Health Bill if its mission to reduce health inequalities in every community is to be realised.
