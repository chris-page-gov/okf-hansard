Turn 89 · Lord Scriven (LD)

The noble Lord makes my point. A person does not necessarily have one doctor or one service; they have multiple needs. Therefore, accountability is not brought about by greater fragmentation; it is about thinking about accountability for the whole pathway, rather than different bits of it. That doctor will have accountability for only part of potentially myriad services that the patient requires. Nobody intends it, nobody designs it and nobody is accountable for the whole. That is what we have to think about. The amendment from the noble Lord, Lord Hunt, requiring a report on the duties to co-operate under Sections 72 and 82 of the 2006 Act, is indeed very sensible. These duties have been on the statute book for a long time, so it is fair to ask a simple question: have they made any measurable difference?
