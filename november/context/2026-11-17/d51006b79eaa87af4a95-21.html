Turn 166 · Florence Eshalomi

Clause 28 will establish co-operation requirements with the Regulator of Social Housing, and clauses 29 to 31 will establish the duty for co-operation where there is more than one responsible entity for a relevant building’s external walls. Social housing residents deserve the same urgency and protections as residents of privately owned buildings. That is why the Bill places clear legal duties on social landlords to assess and remediate unsafe buildings. At the same time, we recognise the unique pressures within which social landlords operate. Clause 28 therefore provides a proportionate safeguard for social housing providers. Turn 167 · Speaker unlabelled

The clause is carefully balanced. It makes it clear that enforcing authorities need not consult when urgent safety action is needed—for example, when a local authority is seeking to issue an emergency prohibition order under the Housing Act 2004. It also prevents consultation from delaying enforcement action indefinitely. If the Regulator of Social Housing does not respond within 28 days, the enforcement authority can proceed.

Turn 168 · Speaker unlabelled

Remediation should not be delayed just because responsibility for a building is split between different parties, or when different regulatory regimes are not working together effectively. At more complex buildings, responsibility may be split between different parties. There may be more than one responsible entity under chapter 1 of the Bill, because different parties hold repairing obligations for different parts of the building. The responsible entity may also be different from the responsible person under the fire safety order or the accountable person under the Building Safety Act. They have overlapping duties to assess and manage fire safety risks.
