Turn 97 · Speaker unlabelled

On integrated care boards, as the noble Lord, Lord Scriven, said, “integrated” is in their name, yet do we think they have done much to really integrate? I do not mean having papers that say, basically, “integration of rationing”; I mean real integration, where you would notice the difference if you were a patient or a social care client. The problem is that I do not think they know how to do it. I do not think they have the capability to do it, because it is hard work. In the health service, every attempt to set up colleges for leadership has failed. We simply do not invest enough in the people we are expecting to take on these jobs.

Turn 98 · Speaker unlabelled

My noble friend the Minister accepts that integration is important. She is right about the Bill: it will allow you to do many things. If the Government wish to further devolve, they can easily do it under the Bill. The question is: can we hold local bodies accountable for starting to integrate? The jury is out on that, because the reality is that ICBs are judged on two things: keeping the finances under control and waiting time targets. Everything else is secondary. We have to make integration one of the top three issues. Having said that, I beg leave to withdraw my amendment.
