Turn 82 · Justin Madders

The shadow Minister makes some interesting points. There are a number of different ways for a toll operator to respond. I do not think it is appropriate for us to put that in legislation, because it is effectively an operational matter. If the technology fails, journeys may not have been recorded, and the systems in place will obviously have appeal mechanisms and could allow more time for people to pay. If the internet went down, as in the example last week, and people were not able to log their payment, operators should have a bit of discretion to allow more time to pay. There are a number of ways that a reasonable and sensible operator could deal with that, and there are, of course, avenues to challenge them where they act capriciously. That, however, is not something we want to cater for in the Bill.

Turn 86 · Justin Madders

Clauses 95 to 97 complete part 7 of the Bill. Each is quite different, so I shall speak to them separately. The group includes Government amendment 21, which follows on from Government amendment 20, which the Committee considered as part of a previous group. Clause 95 will help to ensure that tolling equipment meets minimum standards. Clause 96 provides for the independent adjudication of appeals against penalty charges and includes a power for the Lord Chancellor to make regulations to appoint adjudicators and make provision for appeals against their decisions. Clause 97 provides interpretations for important terms used throughout part 7 of the Bill. Clause 95 creates a mechanism for ensuring that tolling equipment meets minimum standards. That could be important to ensure the interoperability of equipment between free-flow tolling and road-user charging operations.
