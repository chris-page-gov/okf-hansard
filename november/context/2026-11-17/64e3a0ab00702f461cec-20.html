Turn 157 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, I turn very briefly to my Amendment 98, which addresses the wider purposes of care. Again, I thank the noble Baroness, Lady Pitkeathley, for adding her name to it. The Care Act well-being principle recognises that good care is about more than treatment or avoiding hospital admissions; it is also about independence, dignity, relationships, participation and a feeling of control over everyday life. These purposes need to be visible in neighbourhood health planning, not overshadowed by NHS activity measures.

Turn 158 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 98 asks statutory partners to have regard to the Care Act’s well-being purposes when preparing their neighbourhood health plans. Again, this is not about creating a new individual assessment regime or transferring those duties to integrated care boards but rather making the well-being purpose of care a key consideration in joint planning.
