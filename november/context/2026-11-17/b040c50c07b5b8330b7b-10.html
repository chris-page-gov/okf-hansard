Turn 75 · Justin Madders

I never doubted the shadow Minister for a minute, but he is obviously future-proofing himself by seeking to amend the legislation. The shadow Minister spoke as if what we are proposing—that liability should sit with the vehicle keeper—is an entirely novel concept. Of course, that is already the existing provision for tolls, and it is important because the keeper record is the official information about who is responsible for a vehicle. That does not prevent other people from paying a toll or a penalty charge remotely, which is important, but it is even more important that liability remains with the person legally responsible for the vehicle, unless there are very clear reasons to take a different approach.

Turn 79 · Justin Madders

Clause 89 makes certain actions an offence when people intentionally seek to avoid paying a toll or penalty charge, or are identified as having failed to do so. This includes interfering with tolling equipment and, crucially for automatic number plate recognition systems, obscuring the registration plate of a vehicle. The clause sets out sanctions for these offences, including a fine, and, for the more serious offences of making false representations and interfering with equipment, the possibility of imprisonment. For clarity’s sake, the references to equipment do include number plates. I hope that Members will understand why it is important that that terminology is used. Turn 80 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, clause 94 creates a mechanism for the Secretary of State to recover the costs of making collection and enforcement regulations from the applicant. It is a fairly standard arrangement, ensuring that companies that benefit from the making of regulations contribute to the costs to the public purse of providing that benefit. The costs will be limited to reasonable cost recovery only, and the fees charged will be set by, or in accordance with, regulations to be made under clause 94(2).
