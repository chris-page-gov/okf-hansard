Turn 9 · Baroness Blackwood of North Oxford (Con)

My Lords, I declare my interest as chair of Genomics England and the Health Data Research Service. Cyber incidents affecting health data are particularly grave. The 2024 ransomware attack on Synnovis delayed appointments for more than 11,000 patients. In a statement to this House, the Minister acknowledged that the NHS has more than 80,000 suppliers and limited visibility of dependencies. The NHS has set out proposals to increase resilience through the cyber improvement programme. Can the Minister please set out the progress that has been made in that regard, and the lessons that have been learned from Synnovis?

Turn 10 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

The Government have learned from many of the cyber incidents that have happened in recent years. One of the ways we are taking forward lessons from previous incidents is through the cyber security and resilience Bill, which focuses on the leadership needed at all levels of the sectors within the scope of the Bill—the health service is within that scope—and increases the power of the regulators to enforce the standards set under it. Through the security and resilience requirements we will also set standards for data, including on the issues around data protection and data minimisation, which were a feature of the Synnovis attack.

Turn 12 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

The national cloud infrastructure programme that I referenced a moment ago is in recognition of the fact that the UK Government have considerable spending power, but we need to think carefully about how we use that. The intention and purpose of that programme is to use the Government’s spending power more strategically to strengthen competition, diversify supplier choice and support UK technology businesses. As I mentioned to the noble Lord, Lord Clement-Jones, formal procurement will start by the end of the year. Then, after a period of engagement, we will see the result of that and what it does to the statistics that the noble Lord referred to.

Turn 14 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

As I set out yesterday, there were impacts on parts of the public sector, but all key operational services—for example, 999 services—were maintained. We saw the Government responding as they should, with a co-ordinated approach. We saw evidence of alternative communication channels being used, but there was disruption to communication. Even this House was disrupted, but the business of the House was not affected. We will learn the lessons, and we expect Microsoft to share its lessons learned report with us. We will continue to learn those lessons and put them into practice to improve our resilience.
