Turn 46 · Pat McFadden

The report makes it clear that help will be there for those with severe long-lasting disabilities that make work not a realistic possibility and those who will have extra costs. It also makes it clear that we can do better for a number of young people. I remind my hon. Friend of the statistic I quoted in my opening statement: a young person signing on for long-term sickness benefits now, at 20, is more likely to still be on them at 30 than to have had a steady job in the intervening years. Our ambition should revolve not just around someone’s monthly income but around whether or not they have a good future. That is why I say that the exam question that the system has to ask has to change—and it has to change in that direction.
