Turn 56 · Speaker unlabelled

It should also be noted that the crime itself is often driven by unmet clinical need. A UCL evaluation of the multi-agency stalking intervention programme, MASIP, found that perpetrators who completed health interventions in Cheshire demonstrated a reoffending rate of just 17.6%, which is extraordinary. The evidence from MASIP is clear: where health services become active partners, risk is managed, victims are better supported and money is saved. Preventing a single high-risk stalking case escalating can save enormous costs across health, policing, criminal justice and social care. With this amendment, the NHS would become a fully commissioned partner in tackling one of the most harmful forms of abuse and victimisation, enabling the truly multi-agency response that victims deserve.
