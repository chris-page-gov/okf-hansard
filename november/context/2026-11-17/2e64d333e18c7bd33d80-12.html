Turn 94 · Neil Duncan-Jordan (Poole) (Lab)

I rise to support new clause 62, which is in my name. Not only does it have the support of a number of MPs from a wide range of parties, but it has the backing of 10 of the country’s trade unions. That is because it goes to the heart of their understanding of what a fair and decent society looks like. I begin by welcoming the recent announcement by the Home Secretary that many of those working in the care sector and other health and educational settings will continue to be eligible for the five-year route to settled status. I pay tribute to Unison and its general secretary Andrea Egan for the campaign they have been running on this issue. This decision recognises the importance of this group of workers, but I believe that the same arguments that are used for this group can quite easily be applied to those currently on the skilled worker visa as well.

Turn 96 · Neil Duncan-Jordan

I do not accept my hon. Friend’s analysis of what my new clause would do. For many of those affected, it is not just about waiting longer for settlement. They have made significant career, financial and family decisions based on the existing immigration rules. The plan to double the qualifying period for settled status risks alienating skilled workers who benefit the UK every single day. Retrospection is wrong, and my new clause would give voice to that. I urge every hon. Member across the House who disagrees with retrospection to back it.
