Turn 84 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to Amendment 275 on gender services and the disclosure of data for research. The proposed new clause seeks to turn the Government’s expectation that gender clinics will share data into a legal duty. It would place a legal duty on every NHS-funded gender service, for children and adults, to disclose all data to ethically approved outcome studies and would make compliance a condition of contract. It would remove the practical barriers: it would protect disclosure under Section 22 of the Gender Recognition Act and would allow records to be linked where an NHS number has changed. It would require a published timetable for the Cass review data linkage study, covering everyone treated before the age of 25, with findings laid before Parliament. It would require six-monthly reports to Parliament naming any non-compliant provider.

Turn 85 · Speaker unlabelled

This is needed because the data linkage study was meant to follow around 9,000 former Tavistock GIDS patients into adulthood. It was not completed because adult clinics would not share data, despite approvals being in place, as the Minister confirmed in Written Answers in January and March this year. A 2022 order already gave clinics legal protection to share data, and the data still did not come. The 2026 order makes disclosure lawful but compels no one. This is not good enough given healthy bodies are at stake. A legal duty also protects clinicians who want to co-operate, which is essential given the well- documented chilling effect that we know about.

Turn 86 · Speaker unlabelled

Other countries can answer these questions. Finland, for example, has linked national records for over 2,000 young patients and 16,000 controls. It found no evidence that medical transition improved their mental health. The need for specialist psychiatric care did not fall after treatment. It rose from around one in 10 to six in 10 for feminising treatment, and from one in five to over half for masculinising treatment. In other words, other countries can do this, so I do not understand why we do not or we cannot. Here, by contrast, practices run ahead of evidence.
