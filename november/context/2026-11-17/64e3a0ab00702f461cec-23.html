Turn 177 · Speaker unlabelled

At the heart of this is the concern raised by my noble friend Lady Manzoor about blurred accountability. The neighbourhood health plan is put together by a health and well-being board, which sits under the umbrella of a local authority, which is the democratically elected commissioner of social care services in an area. I am not clear about how the respective functions, roles and responsibilities of the NHS, on the one hand, and elected local authorities, on the other, are intended to interrelate, especially bearing in mind the wider responsibilities of local authorities for public health and the wider determinants of health more generally.

Turn 183 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, I turn to Amendment 100 in the name of the noble Baroness, Lady Gerada. Reducing inequalities is absolutely a core objective of neighbourhood health. Both the neighbourhood health framework and the strategic commissioning framework for ICBs already emphasise the need and requirement for engagement with seldom heard and underserved people and communities. The Bill requires local authorities and their partner ICBs to involve local people and the communities that they serve when they are preparing plans.
