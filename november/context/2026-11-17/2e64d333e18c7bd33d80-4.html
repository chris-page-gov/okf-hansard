Turn 25 · Speaker unlabelled

New clause 10 will mean that people who have exhausted their appeal rights, as well as people whose removal failed outside the Home Office control, or who have had a judicial review outcome saying that they are removable, may be removed within six months without a further notice period. This amendment completes the new authority framework, reinforcing the single appeal and late claims processes by ensuring that claims withheld for tactical reasons made either from detention or after an individual has become “appeal rights exhausted” no longer routinely frustrate removal.

Turn 26 · Speaker unlabelled

Let me now deal with amendments relating to modern slavery. Amendments 15 to 18 clarify the circumstances in which the duty to disqualify on public order or bad faith grounds will apply. The provisions in sections 63 and 63A of the Nationality and Borders Act 2022, inserted and amended through this Bill, will enable individuals to be disqualified from the protections and support offered by the national referral mechanism if they are deemed to be a threat to public order or national security, or if they are found to have made a claim in bad faith. In both cases, a disqualification means that the individual will not benefit from the protections associated with the recovery period, including protection from removal.

Turn 27 · Speaker unlabelled

The amendments make it clear that the duties to disqualify in sections 63 and 63A will not apply following the expiry of the recovery period or expiry of the further recovery period, if one is granted as a result of subsequent exploitation. This reflects the practical effect of both disqualification duties. Indeed, bad faith can operate prior to the reasonable grounds decision and during the recovery period, while public order disqualification decisions operate only during the recovery period. Amendments 15 to 18 therefore ensure that the Secretary of State is not required to make a determination when that determination would have no substantive legal effect.

Turn 28 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 19 explicitly enables the Government to publish modern slavery statements or links to statements that are required under the transparency in supply chain provisions in the Bill. Transparency is at the heart of the modern slavery reporting regime. By making it clear that submitted statements may be published, the amendment leaves organisations in no doubt that the information they provide is intended to be publicly available and open to scrutiny.
