Turn 171 · Florence Eshalomi

I thank my hon. Friend for that point. This issue does come up, and many right hon. and hon. Members see it in their casework. I think it is clear that the new duty to co-operate does not transfer any statutory responsibilities between parties. It is important that each person remains responsible for complying with the relevant legislation. The purpose of the clause is to ensure that those separate responsibilities work together effectively where action or information from one party or another is needed for them to effectively support each other. Clause 30 gives clause 29’s duty to co-operate meaningful consequences by making it a criminal offence to intentionally fail to comply with that duty. The threat of prosecution should drive compliance in most cases. However, where a person deliberately ignores the duty to co-operate, prosecution will be available.
