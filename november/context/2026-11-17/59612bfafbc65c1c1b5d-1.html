Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Baroness Lloyd of Effra) (Lab)

Large technology vendors bring benefits through their ability to scale and invest in platform capability and security. However, last week’s Microsoft outage showed that supplier concentration can create resilience risks. The Government Cyber Action Plan sets out how this Government are improving cyber security and digital resilience across the public sector. The Government are taking action to diversify our cloud infrastructure. The national cloud infrastructure programme is intended to reshape the UK cloud market, building a stronger, more diverse supply base.

Turn 5 · The Lord Bishop of Hereford

I thank the Minister for that Answer and for referring to the Microsoft outage last week—which I am sure will have inconvenienced many of us and cost many UK businesses a large amount of money, reinforcing our vulnerability given the small number of providers, largely international, of computer services. However, cyber resilience depends not solely on technology but on people. Given the excellent work being undertaken through programmes such as the Cyber Quarter in Hereford to develop expertise in this country, what steps are the Government taking to strengthen the UK’s cyber resilience by cultivating and retaining domestic cyber talent?

Turn 6 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

The right reverend Prelate is absolutely right to focus on the many aspects needed to strengthen our cyber resilience, from infrastructure to understanding, board and senior management leadership and, crucially, having the right skill set—the right-skilled professionals and the pipeline. Through TechFirst the Government are investing in developing skills from school-aged pupils to undergraduates and graduates, so that we have the capabilities needed in this country to support our cyber resilience.

Turn 7 · Lord Clement-Jones (LD)

My Lords, yesterday the Minister told the House that the Government are “encouraging departments to look at the full range of cloud providers”.—[Official Report, 16/11/26; col. 459.] But encouragement has left 46 of the UK’s 48 police forces relying on Microsoft not just for email but to hold much of their data, despite their own national risk assessment warning of exactly this in 2017. Encouragement is clearly not enough. Will the national cloud infrastructure programme set firm targets for reducing that dependence, and when will it report?
