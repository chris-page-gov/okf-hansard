Can the Secretary of State tell us what steps the Government will take to ensure that employers are accommodating of the needs of young people?

Turn 34 · Pat McFadden

My hon. Friend is right to say that employers are critical. We really want to work with employers, because this should be more than a policy; it should be a cause. We can only really crack it if Government and employers work together.

Turn 35 · Sir Oliver Dowden (Hertsmere) (Con)

The crisis in youth unemployment is at its heart a crisis in demand. I have lost count of the number of small businesses in my constituency who are asking what the point is of employing a less experienced young person when they will end up paying about the same amount to employ them, thanks to increases in the national minimum wage and the Government hiking national insurance contributions for those over 21. Businesses fear that if it does not work out, thanks to the Employment Rights Act 2025, they will not be able to move them on. That is acknowledged throughout the report, for example in paragraphs 267, 284 and 304. Will the Secretary of State unconditionally accept those recommendations and update the House on what he is going to do?

Turn 39 · Lara Bird (Arbroath and Broughty Ferry) (SNP)

We welcome many of the recommendations in this review, especially as so many of the calls are already embedded in Scotland. However, it does let the Labour party off the hook for its own economic record. We want to see the welfare bill come down, but the idea that the reason for the numbers of young people out of work is that they are lazy or unmotivated is just fantasy. Instead, they have been failed by a Labour Government who have increased job taxes and have overseen a catastrophic decline in vacancies. Is it not true that the Labour Government can only solve this crisis if they accept that the number of young people out of work is a direct consequence of their own stagnating economy?
