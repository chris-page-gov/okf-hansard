Turn 3 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero (Ms Polly Billington)

Off-grid homes in council tax bands A to D are eligible for the £100 household energy payment and will also benefit from the zero rate of VAT on electricity bills that we have extended to July 2027. In the Glastonbury and Somerton constituency, around 33,000 households will receive the energy payment. In the Chichester constituency, around 37,000 households will receive payment. This is on top of the £53 million of targeted support for low-income heating oil users via the crisis and resilience fund, announced in March.

One Henstridge household now pays more than double for 500 litres of heating oil compared with this time last year. They are not alone. The way off of oil is by switching to a heat pump, powered by clean, home-grown renewables, but some older homes are harder to switch. The off-grid grant uplift ends in March, adding around £2,200 to the cost of switching. Will the Minister extend the uplift and back alternatives for homes where heat pumps will not work?

Turn 6 · Jess Brown-Fuller

More than 8,000 households in my constituency, because of their reliance on heating oil, have been left exposed to the price volatility that the Minister talks about. A specific issue is the minimum order requirement, which currently stands at 500 litres. It means that households are having to stump up hundreds of pounds at a time when they cannot afford to, especially those that do not receive financial support. The Competition and Markets Authority report into the heating oil market recognised this specific issue and called for a review. Will the Minister outline what steps the Government are taking to reduce the required minimum order volume?

Turn 7 · Ms Billington

The hon. Lady is right. I am delighted that the Government decided to respond to the Competition and Markets Authority recommendations and to finally regulate the heating oil market. It is appalling that people have been so exposed to those prices and that behaviour for so long. We will explore how minimum payments will fit into regulation. In the interim—we need to ensure that regulation is right—I am talking to the UK and Ireland Fuel Distributors Association and other trade bodies to ensure that the voluntary standards and codes of conduct are enforced and upheld this winter, including on minimum payments. I am concerned about people ending up out of pocket. It is also worth while pointing out that heating oil users in council tax bands A to D will benefit from the household energy payment, which will give people some of the breathing space they are looking for.
