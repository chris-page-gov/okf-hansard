Turn 5 · Speaker unlabelled

The Minister assured the Committee that the Bill is not intended to regulate legitimate medical care or interfere with ordinary parenting. I accept that assurance as sincerely given, but the courts have to give effect to the words enacted by Parliament. Hence, ministerial assurances are at their strongest when the legislation itself clearly reflects them. We should protect the abused, and if the law falls short we should strengthen it, but we should also ensure that clinicians can undertake careful, evidence-based assessments, and that parents can continue to guide and support their children without fear that ordinary conversations may later be misconstrued. For these reasons, I strongly support Amendment 279QP.
