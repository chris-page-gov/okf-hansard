Turn 11 · Pat McFadden

I thank the hon. Member for his questions. He said that economic growth was important. I entirely agree, and I hope he will welcome the latest figures showing that growth is up by 0.7%, once again defying the forecasts that it would not go up by as much. I thought he might also welcome today’s labour market figures showing that unemployment has fallen and that employment is up. He is quite right to say that devolution is crucial in this, and that the voices of young people are important. He will be aware that no employer national insurance is payable for workers under the age of 21 unless they earn more than £50,000 a year, so there is already a tax break in place in the system. Of course, we know that employment costs matter, and we will consider all these things as we move forward.

Turn 16 · Pat McFadden

I thank the right hon. Gentleman for his question. He talks about consequences and responsibilities, which I covered in my statement. A new settlement should offer opportunity and hope to young people, and that does come with responsibility. However, I do not see that as a threat. Alan Milburn was clear in his interim report that 84% of young people in this position want to work if they get a chance. I do not believe in the narrative that young people do not want to work—what they lack is a chance to do so. That is where this new settlement can really make a difference.
