Turn 65 · Pat McFadden

I am an optimist. I spoke, together with others, in a meeting with the Scottish First Minister a few weeks ago. I think there was acknowledgment that this is a common problem and challenge throughout the UK, so I think we will work together. Crucially, I do not believe that this can all be done at the level of the Scottish Government. We are going to have to get beneath that to get more localised support in local labour markets. That is a challenge for us all and if we believe that personalised support is the right way to go, it should happen all over the country.

Turn 66 · Helen Hayes (Dulwich and West Norwood) (Lab)

I thank the Secretary of State for his statement and welcome the focus on support for young people who across our country are ambitious, creative and full of potential. I welcome the whole-system approach set out by Alan Milburn, from the early years to post-16 opportunities. Those of us on the Education Committee look forward to playing our part in scrutinising the role of the education system in reducing the number of young people who are outside education, employment and training. Apprenticeships are an important part of Alan Milburn’s recommendations, yet just 5% to 6% of young people are able to participate in an apprenticeship. How does the Secretary of State intend to ensure that colleges, training providers and employers work together to increase the number of apprenticeship opportunities available to young people?

Turn 67 · Pat McFadden

My hon. Friend is right about the critical role of education. That is why there has to be a whole-Government response. On apprenticeships, we are already changing the funding to try to get 50,000 more youth apprenticeship starts over the next few years. I will do everything I can to get more apprenticeship opportunities for young people. They are a great start in life for many and the job retention rate for those who complete their apprenticeship course is well north of 90%. They are a great opportunity and I will do everything I can to create more of them.

Turn 71 · Pat McFadden

The problems can often start in the early years, and that is why we have an ambitious target to ensure that children are school-ready. Alan Milburn, in his report, has asked us to go further still and make that more ambitious. It is stretching, but I have worked very closely with the Education Secretary on this and I know that she shares the ambition that, where possible, every child should be ready for school when they start. If they are not, that can have a lasting impact on the child’s fortunes for the rest of their life.
