Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Ministry of Justice (Lord Lemos) (Lab)

My Lords, I will speak also to the draft Administrative Court (Recording and Broadcasting) and Court of Appeal (Recording and Broadcasting) (Amendment) Order 2026. Taken together, these orders extend the broadcasting regime that already successfully operates in the courts. Specifically, they remove the prohibitions on recording in three areas: first, proceedings in the Administrative Court, which is a part of the High Court; secondly, proceedings involving litigants in person in the Court of Appeal; and thirdly, the magistrates’ court, to enable recording and broadcasting of the sentencing remarks delivered only by the Chief Magistrate in open court. My Lords, I start by thanking the Minister for bringing forward these orders for scrutiny and for the explanations he has provided. The Official Opposition support them both. The instruments before us expand the existing framework for recording and broadcasting in court, and we support them. These orders continue in the same direction and improve transparency further. The first order permits sentencing remarks from the senior district judge in the magistrates’ court to be recorded and broadcast.
