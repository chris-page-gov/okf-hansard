Turn 63 · Gareth Bacon

No, of course that is not what we are saying. The building owner may not know that there is remediation that needs to take place; that is the point we are trying to make. If there is no dispute and remediation needs to take place, that remediation should take place. If they know that the building is unsafe and that remediation is required, but they are not doing it, that should be a criminal offence. It is not about whether they know what the law is; it is about whether they know that there is stuff that needs to be done to their building.

Turn 64 · Gideon Amos

I am grateful for that further explanation. I still find it difficult to understand how the abuse of such a provision could be avoided. The remediation orders will surely make it clear where there is a duty to remediate. The fact that anyone could say, “Sorry, I just didn’t know about it,” simply cannot be a defence. Amendment 31 would provide a non-exclusive definition of what would count as a reasonable excuse. Similarly, I do not think we should be legislating in the Bill for excuses. We need to get remediation done, and it needs to happen in a timely way.
