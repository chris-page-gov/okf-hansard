Turn 55 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 94A, tabled by the noble Baroness, Lady Cass, I assure her that the statutory guidance that requires ICBs to have a named executive board lead with responsibility for children and young people will be maintained and will be reissued by the Secretary of State. I heard what the noble Baroness said: it might be in place, but is it happening? The intention of reissuing and maintaining that statutory guidance is to ensure that ICBs have appropriate executive leads for children and young people on their boards. In addition, the guidance will be explicit that ICB websites should clearly identify the executive board member who has lead responsibility for children and young people up to the age of 25.

Turn 56 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, I will touch briefly on Amendment 241, tabled by the noble Lord, Lord Patel. It is important that the Secretary of State ensures that the health service is working effectively, of course. It is our view that adding a requirement for a regular independent review of public health into primary legislation would increase bureaucracy. Our priority is to use existing evidence and accountability mechanisms. For example, we will hold ICBs to account through the NHS outcomes framework. The annual assessment of ICBs will also look at how they have met their statutory duties. In using the existing mechanisms, we will work to understand and improve that performance as necessary to protect the public’s health.
