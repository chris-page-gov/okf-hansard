Turn 30 · Speaker unlabelled

This is not a radical departure from established public health practice. We have the Yellow Card scheme, through which health professionals can report suspected adverse reactions to medicines without having to prove causation. Those reports are brought together and analysed to identify emerging patterns of harm. This amendment would apply the same principle to the digital environment. Crucially, it would allow health professionals to report suspected harms without first having to establish that digital technology caused them. That is the whole point of an early warning system: to identify risks that warrant investigation, not to wait until the harm is conclusively proven.

Turn 31 · Speaker unlabelled

As my noble friend Lady Cass made clear, reporting is of little value unless someone is analysing the evidence and acting on it. That is why this amendment goes further: it would require a designated body to identify emerging patterns of harm, recommend research and alert both the Secretary of State and Ofcom. Where a credible risk is identified, the Government must take proportionate protective measures. Crucially, a lack of full scientific certainty cannot be used as an excuse for delay. We do not have to wait until harm is proven beyond doubt before taking steps to protect children. We cannot allow the technology sector to be treated as an exception to the standards of safety and surveillance that we already expect in other areas of public health.

Turn 32 · Speaker unlabelled

This amendment is not a silver bullet. We still need Ofcom to take children’s lived experience more seriously, to use its information powers as Parliament intended and to ensure meaningful independent research access. We cannot afford a system in which warnings are observed but never brought together or where they are identified but never acted on. Amendment 279QG would create a route from clinical observation to investigation and proportionate protective action. It would require the Secretary of State to report annually to Parliament, including explaining decisions not to act on the advice received. I hope that the Minister will work with us to ensure that this critical gap in our arrangements for protecting children is addressed.
