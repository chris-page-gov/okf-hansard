Turn 118 · Speaker unlabelled

Where a responsible entity is a company, it can be difficult to see who in the organisation is accountable for compliance or where responsibility sits in a wider corporate group. Clause 14 will therefore require greater corporate transparency. The plan must name a relevant senior individual, explain their compliance responsibilities and identify the entity’s beneficial owners. Where relevant, it must also set out the wider group’s structure and beneficial ownership. All chapter 1 buildings that the entity or members of its group are responsible for should be listed. That will give residents and enforcement authorities a clear picture of who is responsible for compliance and who owns the entity and the buildings across the wider portfolio.

Turn 120 · Speaker unlabelled

There will be cases where new risks or issues emerge after work begins and a remediation compliance plan has been published, and the plan must be updated to remain accurate and to reflect how remediation will be delivered and the expected timescales. Clause 15 will support clause 9 by ensuring that when recommendations change, the remediation compliance plan also changes. It will require the responsible entity to revise and disseminate the updated plan within three months of receiving the written report containing the alternative recommendations. That will ensure that leaseholders and residents have an up-to-date plan that reflects the works required, improving transparency and accountability and ensuring that enforcement authorities can see the updated route to proper remediation.
