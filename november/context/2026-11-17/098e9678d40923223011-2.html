Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

Between these two poles, we have young people with diabetes, renal impairment, rheumatological conditions, epilepsy and a range of other conditions who need to make a smooth transition to adult services, yet all too often this is not well managed or orchestrated, precipitating setbacks in physical or mental health. Simple measures, such as having a hospital transition lead, can improve outcomes, yet very few hospitals support time for this in consultant job plans. I call on the Secretary of State to take these issues seriously and develop a strategic plan and implementation framework specifically for these services.

Turn 11 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, I will mention two other amendments that I support. The first is Amendment 161 in the name of the right reverend Prelate the Bishop of Leicester, on retention of the NHS number following adoption, which I fully support. From my work in gender services, I saw tangible examples of the risks when parts of a child’s medical history were lost due to changes in the NHS number. Adopted children should not be subject to the same risks. Secondly, Amendment 243 in the name of my noble friend Lady Finlay pulls together a series of crucial measures around communication when a child is close to death, which, if implemented, would help to avert end-of-life court cases and make a lifelong difference to parental grief. I beg to move.
