Turn 209 · Pat McFadden

I thank my hon. Friend for the many conversations that we have had and for how much he has taught me over the past year about parents coping with these issues. He is right to show belief in young people with neurodiverse conditions—I think we owe them belief—and to point out that worklessness can sometimes be intergenerational and difficult to crack. That is why getting one person into work can make a difference for the whole family.

Turn 210 · Josh Dean (Hertford and Stortford) (Lab)

This report recognises something that a generation of young people—my generation—has felt for a long time: that too often, this place has forgotten them. I agree with the Secretary of State that this issue requires a new settlement, but it is so important that we bring young people with us, because, for too long, previous Governments have not. What will he do to engage young people, particularly those who are neurodivergent or living with a mental health condition, to shape the next steps following this review?

Turn 211 · Pat McFadden

Given the chance, they will want to work, learn or participate in a broader way, and that will help us to close the stubborn disability employment gap that there has been for so many years.

Turn 212 · Perran Moon (Camborne and Redruth) (Ind)

I thoroughly welcome this statement and congratulate the Secretary of State on the sensitive and collegiate way in which he has carried himself to achieve such a groundbreaking proposal. In his report, Alan Milburn proposes that strategic authorities should lead local youth opportunity services and area participation plans, with an extension of their responsibilities to cover 18 to 24-year-olds. Can the Secretary of State confirm that foundation strategic authorities such as Cornwall will have all those powers and funding arrangements? Will he also talk to us about buses?

Turn 213 · Pat McFadden
