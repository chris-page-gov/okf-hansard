Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

We on these Benches recognise wholeheartedly that our fishing sector is crucial to many areas, and we do not want to see this industry lost; hence the need to work collaboratively towards improved practices. More broadly, under the previous Government, we began the work of replacing the EU common fisheries policy with a new framework, consulting on six fisheries management plans. In 2021, £100 million was allocated to support the sector’s future. In 2024, we negotiated quotas of 750,000 tonnes of fish, an 80,000-tonne increase compared with 2023.

Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

However, this Labour Government signed away 12 years of access for EU vessels to UK waters. Agreeing not to increase the UK share of fish from UK waters over the next 12 years is costing our fishing industry in excess of £500 million per annum, in impoverished coastal communities where that money means a great deal. There has been a 15% decrease in the number of full-time jobs that the sector provides since 2024. How will the Government seek to prevent a further decrease in the number of these jobs?
