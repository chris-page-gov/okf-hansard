Turn 122 · Speaker unlabelled

Responsible entities need clarity about when they are expected to meet their duties to mitigate and remediate relevant fire safety risks and publish a remediation compliance plan. That is why clause 33 will allow the Secretary of State to issue statutory guidance to provide the necessary details of how these duties may be carried out in practice. The intent is to use the power to set out the steps that responsible entities should take to progress remediation, with indicative timescales to clarify what “as soon as reasonably practicable” might mean. This power should provide a practical road map to compliance, giving responsible entities and residents greater certainty and support about what is expected. It is a key tool to help responsible entities meet their new duties.

Turn 123 · Speaker unlabelled

The guidance will make it clear that all responsible entities, including RMCs and RTMs, should not face enforcement where they can demonstrate that they have taken reasonable steps to comply, including where they have followed the guidance but have been unable to progress because of circumstances outside their control. It will give enforcing authorities a clearer benchmark for assessing progress, challenging unjustified delays and deciding whether enforcement action is necessary and proportionate to accelerate remediation.

The guidance will also have evidential weight. When an enforcing authority is investigating non-compliance with the chapter 1 duties, evidence that the guidance was or was not followed can be used to establish whether the relevant duty was met. Guidance is the right approach because it provides practical detail and may be adapted if needed without placing every detailed requirement in primary legislation.
