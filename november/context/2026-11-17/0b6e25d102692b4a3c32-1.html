Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (Lord Wood of Anfield) (Lab)

My Lords, the Government do not intend to change the bilateral official development assistance allocations. The allocations reflect the UK’s modernised approach to international development, focusing resources where we can have greatest impact. Respecting established multi-year budget allocations provides the predictability needed to support better planning for FCDO and FCDO’s partners, and more effective delivery. ODA alone is insufficient. We are drawing on the full breadth of UK expertise to deliver the development that our partners want and need.

Turn 8 · Lord Wood of Anfield (Lab)

The noble Lord is right to raise education and girls, and a broader set of issues to do with violence against women and girls, a priority which was set by the previous Government and has been continued by this Government. In the course of the cuts to bilateral aid and the reprofiling, we have continued to prioritise humanitarian need, health, climate and nature, underpinned by economic development and with women and girls at the centre of development efforts, as well as maintaining country allocations in areas that suffer the greatest humanitarian outrages, particularly Ukraine, Sudan and Palestine. Again, this is a matter of responding to an overall cut in the budget. I am not going to walk away from the fact that those cuts have consequences, but maintaining priorities, including in the area that the noble Lord referred to, remains a priority in the development policy.
