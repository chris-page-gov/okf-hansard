Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Baroness Hayman of Ullock) (Lab)

My Lords, these regulations were laid before this House on 14 October 2026. This affirmative SI makes amendments to the existing persistent organic pollutants—POPs—regulation, as permitted by the use of powers available within Article 15 of the POPs regulation. It will prohibit the manufacturing, placing on the market and use of five recently adopted POPs. POPs are substances that have been agreed internationally by the United Nations Stockholm convention to be extremely dangerous to human health and the environment. So far, 37 POPs have been adopted for global prohibition under the convention. They account for a very small proportion of chemicals on the UK market. The identification and adoption of new POPs is based on thorough scientific analysis. Turn 6 · Baroness Redfern (Con)

My Lords, regarding the amendment we are debating, my response is, first, to support it. It is important to protect the environment and control continued organic pollutants, as well as protecting the human health of the nation. From my past experience of working with my late husband, running our small agricultural and chemical enterprises, I know how important it is for small businesses’ responses to be heard as well as the input from very large organisations. I question whether adequate responses to this amendment were received from the small and medium-sized business sectors. It is interesting to note the number of responses His Majesty’s Government received.
