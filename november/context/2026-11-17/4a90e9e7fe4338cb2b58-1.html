Turn 2 · The Minister for Climate Transition (Katie White)

I thank my hon. Friend for asking this incredibly important question. Young people should be involved in the issue of climate change because the window of opportunity to act is now; we cannot kick the can down the road. That is why I launched the youth climate and nature panel. It is why I have engaged with campaigners like Ava Langridge and Unjaded Jade. Last week at COP, I was lucky enough to meet the UK Youth Climate Coalition and the Department for Education’s youth sustainability champions.

Turn 4 · Katie White

I totally agree with my hon. Friend, who made this point eloquently in the Westminster Hall debate yesterday. We cannot act with our youth campaigners in a tokenistic way; we need to ensure that they are fully involved. That is why, when I met the final 25 of the 1,000 applicants we had, we agreed that we would co-create the process that we will work on over the next two years. That is really important. We are also exploring how we can involve youth campaigners throughout our G20 presidency. I want to give them the space to do it in their own way, so that they can flourish and bring their best ideas to the forefront.

Turn 6 · Katie White

We obviously pursue various elements of the “polluter pays” principle through the energy profits levy. The COP negotiations are live, and the hon. Lady will have seen that the UK has been at the forefront of those. I was hugely proud that the Prime Minister was out there with the Foreign Secretary, and the Secretary of State is out there this week. She is pursuing a range of issues, including clean electricity, the Tropical Forest Forever Facility and various other issues. I am happy to keep the hon. Member updated on those.
