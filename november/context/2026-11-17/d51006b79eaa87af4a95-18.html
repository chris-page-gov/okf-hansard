Turn 137 · Sarah Gibson

Amendment 15 would place a duty on the Secretary of State to provide ringfenced funding to enforcement authorities to cover the full cost of enforcement and step-in remediation. Those of us who have had a long career in the building industry will know that a lack of enforcement, not a lack of legislation, is quite often the issue that causes delays, problems and a lack of clarity. The Minister mentioned earlier that the prospect of fines should be a deterrent, but I beg to mention that the prospect of fines would act as such only if enforcement is enacted and properly funded. Residents left without that option can be left with long legal action, which I want to avoid.

I thank the hon. Members for Orpington and for Taunton and Wellington for their engagement on clauses 22 and 23. Turn 141 · Florence Eshalomi

As with any new burden, the Government will ensure that we carry out a full and proper assessment, in co-operation with local authorities and the Local Government Association. As I said, the Bill allows the Secretary of State to provide financial assistance to remediation authorities carrying out backstop work. The arrangements provide an appropriate mechanism for assessing and meeting a cost arising from the new regime, without creating an inflexible statutory ringfence. I hope my explanation has given the hon. Member the assurance that he needs. I ask him to withdraw the amendment, and I commend clauses 22 and 23 to the Committee.

Turn 142 · Gareth Bacon

I thank the Minister for her response on amendment 38. Unfortunately, we are in the same predicament as when I moved the amendment: we simply do not believe that the Bill makes clear what would happen in the event of an impasse between the various regulators.
