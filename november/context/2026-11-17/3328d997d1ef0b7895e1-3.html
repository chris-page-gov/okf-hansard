Turn 18 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

My Lords, how can I resist that invitation? The noble Lord is absolutely right, and particularly in this time of COP, when we have seen the evidence with our own eyes. In all public policy and all government work, we should follow the evidence, and we have the evidence: the droughts and wildfires, the summers we have had, and a recent very influential report telling us about the impact this can have on the nation’s health. This is not the time to break that consensus; it is the time to recognise the challenge of climate change and to do all we can to meet that challenge. It is deeply disappointing that the Opposition Benches have abdicated from the leadership required and reneged on the political consensus that was built up by that leadership, often from the Conservative Party.

Turn 21 · Lord Moynihan (Con)

My Lords, I declare my interests as chair of Amey, Acteon and Buckthorn. Given that some 60% of the operating costs of data centres are electricity costs, which of the following Government policies hastens the dawn of low-cost electrification in the UK? Is it NESO paying up to £4 billion a year for capacity payments to the wind farm operators to switch off? Is it Ofgem’s price cap for January next year, which is expected to increase the cost of electricity by between 16% and 31%? Or is it Ed Miliband’s view that approving Rosebank in the North Atlantic would be “climate vandalism”—although he has now personally approved the Sea Lion oil field in the South Atlantic?

Turn 22 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

The noble Lord never fails to disappoint. Every time I have stood here, he has mentioned most of those points, although perhaps not all of them. I will not be tempted into passing comment about Rosebank, but I will come back to the main point. Our future lies in clean, home-grown energy; thereby, we will get energy security.
