Turn 36 · Justin Madders

The clause amends the Highways Act 1980 so that a highway to be constructed under a licence may be directed by the Secretary of State to become a trunk road. The change is needed because the licensing regime applies only to trunk roads in England, or roads that are to become trunk roads. A new highway delivered under a licence must therefore be capable of joining the trunk road network. The clause uses the existing statutory framework for trunk road directions. It adds highways to be constructed under a licence to the categories of proposed highway that may be covered by an order under section 10 of the Highways Act 1980. The clause therefore ensures that a highway delivered through the licensing regime can be incorporated into the trunk road network under the section 10 powers if the Secretary of State considers that appropriate. Turn 37 · Justin Madders

Clause 82 establishes the main appeals route for specified regulatory decisions made under the Bill. Decisions that can be appealed include important regulatory decisions such as licence refusals, certain licence modifications and penalty decisions. Those decisions are signposted in the relevant sections of the Bill. Appeals will be heard by the Competition Appeal Tribunal as the appropriate body with expertise in economic regulation and competition matters. Appeal must be brought by submitting a notice to the tribunal setting out the provisions under which the decision being challenged was made and the grounds of appeal. The tribunal may dismiss the appeal or quash all or part of the decision. When a decision is quashed, the tribunal may send the matter back to the decision maker for reconsideration.
