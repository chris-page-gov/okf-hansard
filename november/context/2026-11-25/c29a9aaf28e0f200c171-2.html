Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

The Secondary Legislation Scrutiny Committee has drawn these regulations to the special attention of the House, and the treatment of perishable goods is the reason. Under Regulation 15, a consumer who cancels a meal kit or fresh food subscription during the cooling-off period is entitled to a full refund, including delivery, for any order not yet supplied, which in practice means not yet delivered. By then, the order has been selected by the customer, picked, packed and is often on the road. It cannot be resold—it will be thrown away. The committee was concerned that the Government may not have considered sufficiently the impact of treating perishable and non-perishable goods alike. In a sector the Government themselves describe as booming, one leading provider estimates the effect on its business alone at some 2.6 million meals and 1,450 tonnes of food wasted each year. UK consumer law has long recognised that perishable goods are different. A one-off purchase of the same food carries no cooling-off right. Nobody suggests that subscribers should lose their right to cancel the subscription and stop future orders. The industry’s proposal is modest: that regulations set a fixed cut-off, say three or five days before scheduled delivery, after which that order is not refundable. The Government’s objection is that this would let businesses set their own cooling-off period. But a period fixed in regulations, the same for every trader, does no such thing; it gives consumers a clear and predictable rule. Turn 15 · Speaker unlabelled

Last December, the Minister said that the Government would work closely with charities as the secondary legislation was finalised. Regulation 40 is the result. It inserts a new paragraph 14 in Schedule 22, excluding charity contracts for viewing property, performances or films. We welcome it. It will cover the great heritage garden and wildlife charities, but it is not the test that the sector asked for. The exclusion is framed around viewing, whereas HMRC’s membership rules allow gift aid where members also take part in activities forming part of the charity’s objectives.
