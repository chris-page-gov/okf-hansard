Turn 33 · Ann Davies (Caerfyrddin) (PC) · Question 902257

Q3. Diolch yn fawr, Mr Llefarydd. When he came to office in July, the Prime Minister promised to end Whitehall’s resistance to devolution and to listen to Wales. How disappointing then, that he has overruled the mandate given by the people of Wales for more responsibility. He has dismissed the calls of all 22 Welsh councils, TUC Cymru and two former Welsh Labour First Ministers, Carwyn Jones and Mark Drakeford. Last week, the Prime Minister was not persuaded and said no. When exactly did he decide that Wales is no longer worth listening to?

Turn 34 · The Prime Minister

I am listening to Wales, and I have met the First Minister a number of times. I put an offer on the table of deeper devolution around employment support. Let us first work where we have got a clear case and common ground, so that we can get more young people in Wales into work and good jobs. I will never completely rule things out, but at this moment in time, that is the issue that we should work on, where we can agree that there is a case to go further and get deeper devolution down into all parts of Wales.

Turn 38 · The Prime Minister

Our guidance is clear: schools and local authorities should consider whether further support is more appropriate than a fine, as well as considering a pupil’s particular circumstances. I agree with the hon. Lady that fines really should not be used in these circumstances. Perhaps in the most exceptional cases they should, but even then I would be with her in wanting to send that message to all schools in England.

Turn 40 · The Prime Minister

I am one of those who believe that we cannot get enough sport and sporting facilities, so I am quite happy to back my hon. Friend’s campaign. I hope that Glasgow city council will listen to what she is saying. These are essential facilities—critical infrastructure at the heart of our communities. It was wonderful news that the UK was confirmed as the host of the 2035 FIFA women’s world cup, to be held in Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and England. This gives us a huge opportunity over the next decade to build women’s and girls’ sport in this country on a cross-party basis, and I hope the whole House will take it. I know that the news will have excited young women across the country this week.
