Turn 43 · Bridget Phillipson

I am pleased to hear about the work that Safer Places is doing in Harlow to ensure that women can access timely support when they are experiencing abuse. My hon. Friend is right that it is often at the point of choosing to leave that women are at gravest risk. That is why it is important that we provide routes and avenues for women to seek help, including through universal services such as health provision, whether it is at the midwife or the GP, because those are often the only places where women can be seen. Such routes and avenues mean that we do not just have to wait for women to come forward. I am really pleased that the Health Secretary is leading further work in this area to ensure that health services can identify where women are experiencing abuse, rather than leaving it to the women to find that help for themselves.

Turn 48 · Iqbal Mohamed (Dewsbury and Batley) (Ind)

Emotional, physical and sexual violence against women and girls is abhorrent, and must be condemned by all, regardless of the ethnicity of the perpetrators or victims. All those who are politicising the pain and trauma of the victims and survivors by focusing on perpetrators from selected ethnicities, are harming not helping the survivors, and should be ashamed. Such despicable crimes against women and girls are widely prevalent across our country and must be a top priority for Government. In my constituency, the most common crimes are violence and sexual offences. What additional funding and resources have been offered to local police forces in places like Dewsbury and Batley, and what more can we do to ensure that victims who come forward, as well as those who are too afraid to do so, are supported after these horrific crimes are committed?
