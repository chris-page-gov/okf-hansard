Turn 1 · The Prime Minister (Andy Burnham)

With permission, Mr Speaker, I would like to make a statement on nuclear test veterans. This day has taken far too long in coming. The only reason it has arrived is the sheer courage of thousands of ex-servicemen and their families who refused to give in. Our nuclear test veterans stand with all other veterans in displaying all the typical characteristics that we so rightly commend in the British armed forces: service, bravery, dignity and determination. Yet these veterans have been left to stand apart for decades, in a wilderness and with no choice but to fight their own country for recognition and answers. It is hard to know how we can even begin to put that right today, but it does mark the day when we make a start in earnest. Next year will mark the 75th anniversary of Britain’s first nuclear test. Turn 6 · Mr Tanmanjeet Singh Dhesi (Slough) (Lab)

Having raised this issue on numerous occasions over the years, including in our Defence Committee deliberations, I welcome, thank and commend the Prime Minister on fulfilling his promise about this long-awaited independent inquiry for the long-suffering nuclear test veterans. The courageous campaigning that has been happening behind the scenes to obtain complete medical records and clear answers must also be commended. After decades of delay, we need this non-statutory process to lead to resolution. Will the Prime Minister commit today to a clear timetable for considering and implementing the panel’s recommendations next year?
