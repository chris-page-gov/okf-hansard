Turn 17 · Speaker unlabelled

Under those circumstances, why, in the Minister’s letter of Sunday evening—this famous letter—is it felt that the principle of the common-law duty to confidentiality should be disapplied, for instance, where data is provided by providers from the devolved Administrations? Are there not other ways to deal specifically with those few limited circumstances where there is a belief that this duty might in some way offer an impediment or inhibition? The overriding principle is retained as part of the amendment of the NHS Act 2006. It would be a very dangerous move—one which would ultimately attend this attempt at a single patient record to failure—if it were ever suggested that confidentiality was not in some way at the core of, and in every aspect of participation in, provision of and use of, a single patient record.

Turn 20 · Speaker unlabelled

In this important matter, just as with the question of the common-law duty of confidentiality, the reality is that acting in a way that secures confidence is probably as important as the measures we take. To be able to reassure that proper scrutiny has been conducted and a full parliamentary process has been applied means that, ultimately, when the single patient record emerges and the legislation attending it—be it primary or secondary legislation—is available, all will understand, both experts in these matters and the public more generally, that every question has been addressed, every stone has been turned and, on this occasion, no compromise has been made and, therefore, there should be absolute and total confidence in the single patient record that is ultimately delivered.
