Turn 27 · Bridget Phillipson

My hon. Friend makes an important point. Those who have survived domestic abuse or are still experiencing it can come from any background and walk of life, including older women. I know from my own personal experience of running a women’s refuge that older women often feel limited in coming forward. They have often been in a relationship for a very long time, and wonder whether there is any support available and whether they could really make such a change. My message to anyone living today with domestic abuse is that there is support available, and the police will take it seriously. I will of course look carefully at my hon. Friend’s suggestions about ensuring that we have a full picture of what is going on across the country.

Turn 29 · Bridget Phillipson

I can only repeat what I said to the right hon. Gentleman, which is that we will be providing a full update a year on, in response to our violence against women and girls strategy. We intend to use British crime survey data to understand the full picture on domestic abuse and sexual violence, given the under-reporting that we know is often represented in the police crime data. Alongside that, we intend to publish a metric to better understand attitudes towards women. That work is under way and is connected very closely to the tackling misogyny taskforce, which will ensure that we do not just deal with the consequences of male violence, but address its root causes.

Turn 32 · Jess Brown-Fuller (Chichester) (LD)

I am pleased to hear the Minister acknowledge that we all need to meet the moment on tackling violence against women and girls in all areas on this White Ribbon Day. My hon. Friend the Member for Chelmsford (Marie Goldman) asked about strategic lawsuits against public participation. It is not only NDAs that are being weaponised by rich and powerful men, but SLAPPs and the threat of legal action. The amount of time, energy and money that individuals must expend to fight SLAPPs is immensely prohibitive and keeps instances of abuse against women and girls in the dark for years—if they ever come to light. Will the Government therefore commit to bringing forward universal anti-SLAPP legislation that will enable such things to be dismissed at an early stage of proceedings and fully compensate targeted defendants, so that the truth can come out and justice can be served?
