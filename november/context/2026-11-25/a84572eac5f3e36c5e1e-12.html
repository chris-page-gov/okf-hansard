Turn 89 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 278 from the noble Baroness, Lady Kidron, calls for a strategy for the Health Data Research Service to tackle researcher access while maintaining privacy and public trust, and we strongly support that. Finally, we strongly support Amendment 279Q, also tabled by the noble Baroness. Innovation and public trust are not mutually exclusive, but they can coexist only if our data architecture is transparent, sovereign, publicly controlled and anchored in the public interest. I look forward to the Minister answering the questions raised by these amendments.

Turn 96 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 172C addresses something I suspect many noble Lords will recognise, which is the messiness—and possibly the risk—of a piecemeal, trust-by-trust approach to adopting AI. It proposes a national framework for evaluating, procuring and monitoring AI tools. It would require a public record of the AI used in clinical care and the reporting of adverse incidents to the MHRA. I am quite sure that this idea would serve patient safety. It could also make the NHS a more effective customer for British innovators. The noble Lord, Lord Tarassenko, gave us some first-class examples of how AI is already being used with data from mammograms as a predictive tool for cardiovascular disease.
