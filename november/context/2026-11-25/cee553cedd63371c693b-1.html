Turn 2 · Jim Shannon (Strangford) (DUP)

I commend the hon. Lady for bringing this debate forward. It is very much in my mind, and I suspect in the minds of other hon. Lady agree that before driverless vehicles are rolled out more widely, we need cast-iron guarantees on how this technology handles unpredictable rural roads, poor weather and slow-moving agricultural machinery, as well as absolute clarity on legal liability when things go wrong? Turn 3 · Siân Berry

Those are all important questions that I hope the Minister will consider and give us some thoughts on. I am concerned about not only nervous potential travellers and risk appetite from them; the people on the streets who are not in these cars are most at risk from any errors. On a strategic level, the assembly report points out that London has set a clear Vision Zero target for traffic fatalities and serious injuries and is pushing up standards across the board. Potentially, greater automation could help improve safety in terms of speed limit compliance, leaving safe stopping distances or braking to avoid collisions. It could clearly help more disabled people to travel independently with adapted vehicles. We can all agree on those goals, but if we are to have no qualified driver at all in public service vehicles, that carries clear risks and worries among the public.

Turn 4 · Dr Peter Prinsley (Bury St Edmunds and Stowmarket) (Lab)

I am grateful to the hon. Member for bringing this interesting subject to Parliament. Does she agree that self-driving vehicles have the potential to solve the problem of rural isolation? For instance, elderly people who are unable to drive depend on one bus a week or less. If they dial up a car that will come to their house and take them to the pharmacy or the hospital, that will solve the problem of rural isolation and make rural living, particularly for elderly individuals, much more straightforward. We must recognise the massive advantages that will come with self-driving vehicles.

Turn 5 · Siân Berry

I thank the hon. Member for those examples, which have been talked about for a long time. Shared transport, demand-responsive transport and public transport could fill some of the gaps. I cannot quite see the difference between that and a hire taxi, but that is something we might think about. I have some examples to talk about later as well. On public opinion, the YouGov survey that comes with the report shows that Londoners are currently more likely to oppose than support the use of robotaxis by 42% to 29%. Concerns about safety are higher among women and disabled Londoners. The assembly is very concerned that the data being used to back up dramatically high safety claims from the companies comes now from the USA, whose street designs, traffic rules and, importantly, number and behaviour of pedestrians are very different compared with our towns and cities.

Turn 8 · Dr Prinsley

The hon. Member just said that the benefits are purely commercial. Can she not imagine the benefits to the general population?
