Turn 26 · Sir Iain Duncan Smith (Chingford and Woodford Green) (Con)

I commend the Prime Minister on taking this decision and making it very clear to Whitehall that the usual obfuscations and ways of hiding information cannot and shall not operate. Turn 29 · The Prime Minister

I thank my hon. Friend and all members of the Hillsborough Law Now campaign. They have given the nuclear test veteran community incredible support, and we would not be here without the work that he and others have done. The Hillsborough campaign has helped other campaigns move forward. It is a real movement for change, and it is one that is changing the country for the better. I understand what my hon. Friend says. I am very conscious of this issue, and I am not a person who does half a job; I would never forgive myself. The reason for the interim report in October next year is to give at least some truth to people while they can see it. There will be a further period of work beyond that to ensure the full job is done. I think it is right to construct the inquiry in this way, but I encourage him and other hon. Members to watch it at every stage to ensure this is the right process.

Turn 31 · Ian Roome (North Devon) (LD)

Last night, I saw a very moving “Newsnight” programme, on which the Defence Secretary appeared. There was one veteran who had fought to get his medical records and was told he would get them in 2069, by which time he will most probably have passed. Those medical records exist today, so will the Prime Minister commit to giving the nuclear test veterans and their families access to those records now rather than having to wait until way down the line in October 2027? That would go a long way towards helping our veterans.
