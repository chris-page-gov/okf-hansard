Turn 2 · The Prime Minister (Andy Burnham)

With your permission, Mr Speaker, I will make a statement after Prime Minister’s questions on our nuclear test veterans. Today is International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women and Girls. Recognising the growing concern in the country and the need to strengthen all possible measures to protect victims, we are today confirming that we will bring forward the date to January by which non-disclosure agreements can no longer be used to silence a victim of sexual harassment. This morning I had meetings with ministerial colleagues and others. In accordance with my duties in this House, I shall have further such meetings later today.

Turn 4 · The Prime Minister

My hon. Friend is right to say that Greater Manchester had some good policies under the previous mayor, as it does under the current one, too. One of those was the provision of free transport to care leavers up to the age of 25. I hope other areas around the country would look to do the same. We do need to do more to support our young people who have left care and who do not have a mum and dad to fall back on. That is our collective responsibility, and I can promise him today that I will go further as Prime Minister.

Turn 7 · The Prime Minister

I think the right hon. Lady has answered her own question. The deal on tuition fees is not done. As the President of the European Commission said, it is on “a separate piece of paper”. It is a separate deal, and we will take that conversation forward, always rightfully mindful of the challenges faced by higher education. But let us be clear about what this deal does do. It gives new opportunities to everyone in the country under the age of 31. It puts more hope on the horizon of young people—hope that was taken away by the Brexit deal, the flawed Brexit deal, that the Conservatives negotiated.
