Turn 15 · Speaker unlabelled

The common-law duty of confidentiality applies not only to confidential healthcare data but more broadly, across the sharing of any confidential information. It is a very strong and simple principle that can be easily understood by the public, patient and professional. To suggest that there may be circumstances that warrant—and therefore a necessity to provide the opportunity for—that common-law duty of confidentiality to be suspended, at the very least creates a dangerous perception that confidentiality is no longer at the heart of the single patient record.

Turn 16 · Speaker unlabelled

Why take a well-recognised, well-respected common-law duty where there are clear opportunities for its suspension? If, for instance, consent is given, the common-law duty is disapplied in that the confidential information may be shared on a consented basis. If legal authority is provided, the common-law duty to confidentiality may be suspended. If there is an overriding public interest, that duty may be overridden.
