Turn 47 · Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown

I am grateful to the Leader of the House for giving way. I had not really intended to intervene, but I have been listening carefully to the debate, much of which I agree with: I think the costs are too high, the governance is far too complicated, and too much about the whole matter has been discussed behind closed doors, in secret. I would like to get one concession from the Leader of the House today. I have looked at his motion very carefully. One of the key recommendations of the PAC, which I have the privilege to chair, was that this Parliament, although it cannot bind its successor, recommend to the next Parliament to have an early vote, in 2030, which would give the authorities plenty of time to see if they could get the costs down, and sort out the governance and a way forward. Would he give serious consideration to that before he puts his motion before the House?

Turn 48 · Sir Alan Campbell

It may not be a decision for me, but that will become self-evident when we get into the initial programme of seven years. There is initial work that needs to be done when that option is put before the House in mid-2030. One of the issues to look at will be governance and another will be financial costs. We will have an annual debate, every year. The hon. Gentleman knows that because we have talked about the role that the PAC might want to play in all this. Although I would be very surprised if I was in the job that I am doing now when we get to that time, I would find it extraordinary if, when the House had a view on the costed proposals or anything else, there would not be an opportunity to have a debate on those matters. It is not about shutting MPs out of this process; it is about getting on with it and making sure that the right people are there to drive it.
