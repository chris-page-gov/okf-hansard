Turn 105 · Baroness Sugg (Con)

My Lords, I will come to the amendments on the maternity commissioner shortly, but I will speak first to my Amendment 265, co-signed by the noble Baronesses, Lady Watkins, Lady Thornton and Lady Barker. It would require the Secretary of State to publish a women’s health implementation plan within 90 days of the Act receiving Royal Assent. The purpose of the amendment is pretty straightforward: to ensure that the ambitions set out in the renewed women’s health strategy are matched by a clear route to delivery, measurable objectives and public accountability. I appreciate very much the Minister’s efforts in improving women’s healthcare, but there is widespread recognition that too many women continue to face far too long waits for treatment, delayed diagnosis and difficulty navigating services.

Turn 106 · Speaker unlabelled

The renewed women’s health strategy sets out a positive vision and contains a significant number of commitments designed to address those challenges. It sets out actions to improve health outcomes for women and girls, including in menstrual health, infertility, menopause, mental health, cancer and reducing disparities. However, as noble Lords will be well aware, strategies alone do not improve outcomes; progress depends on their implementation, transparency and sustained focus. That renewed strategy contains 117 actions, each, welcomely, with an associated timetable. We have had six months since the publication of the strategy and 36 of those actions are due to be completed within this first year. This amendment simply asks the Government to set out how delivery will be achieved, and how Parliament and the public will be able to judge that progress.

Turn 107 · Speaker unlabelled

One area where this is particularly important is in gynaecology care. The strategy includes a commitment to bring gynaecology waiting lists down to 18 weeks by the end of this Parliament. That is a really welcome objective. However, hundreds of thousands of women are currently waiting for care and a long-term commitment really must be accompanied by a credible plan for action now. The latest figures, which are not even complete, indicate that well over 578,000 women in England are waiting for treatment, while the number waiting for more than a year has continued to rise. We are still seeing far too many women spending years seeking answers before receiving appropriate care.

Turn 108 · Speaker unlabelled

The amendment is also about ensuring that women’s health does not become lost in the wider reforms. We discussed previously the opportunities presented by neighbourhood health services and community-based care. Those reforms may well deliver benefits, but women need clarity about where they can access the specialist support that they require. Women’s health hubs have already demonstrated their value in bringing together services and expertise, and many stakeholders are concerned that their future role is not sufficiently clear.

Turn 109 · Speaker unlabelled

Ultimately, the amendment does not seek to reopen the women’s health strategy or add new commitments to it; rather, it seeks to ensure that the commitments already made are backed by clear milestones, transparent reporting and meaningful accountability. Women have waited too long for these issues to receive the attention they deserve, and an implementation plan would provide confidence that the Government’s ambitions will be translated into real improvements in care, experience and outcomes. I very much hope that the Minister will happily accept the amendment, but, in anticipation that she may not, can she lay out when we will receive an update from the Government on progress in the implementation of the women’s health strategy and can she give any guarantees on transparency on achievements within that first year?
