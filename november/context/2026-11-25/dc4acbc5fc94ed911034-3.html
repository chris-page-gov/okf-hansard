Turn 17 · Speaker unlabelled

This code is a reasonable first draft. It gets the architecture right: registered providers only, at the citizen’s request, with minimal and accurate data. However, the safeguards that matter most—transparency, oversight, security and redress—are still largely left to good practice and good will. We will be watching closely how this gateway works in practice, but we expect a much stronger second edition.

Turn 18 · Lord Markham (Con)

My Lords, we thank the Minister for introducing this secondary legislation and all noble Lords for their contributions. From our side, we see this as a sensible set of measures and are supportive of the endeavour to save time and money for individuals and companies. We want to be sure that, by increasing the provision of data, we are not unnecessarily increasing the cyber security risks involved. In particular, when we talk about verification, that should be, wherever possible, a yes or no. Going back to the example of asking whether someone is over 18, that should be a yes or no. They do not need to provide their date of birth as a response. I should like to be sure in all this, and would be glad to hear the Minister’s assurance that, wherever technically possible, the verification service is one of just verification—yes or no answers—rather than disclosure of underlying personal data. …
