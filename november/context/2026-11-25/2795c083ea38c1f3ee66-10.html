Turn 73 · Baroness Finlay of Llandaff (CB)

My Lords, on Monday we asked about the review of the compensation process to make sure that the money paid out in compensation when things go wrong is used to the best advantage not only of the person but of society overall and the NHS, and that it recognises that often there is system failure at many levels rather than one person who is necessarily culpable. I realise that the Minister will not be able to give us an answer over publication of the report today. I stand up simply to say that the extension and the new HSSIB that we envisage doing an even better job should be able to ensure that the compensation system matches the concept that things may go wrong across the whole organisation rather than blame being attributed to one person, and that therefore there could be lessons learned without some of the acrimony.

Turn 75 · Speaker unlabelled

Nothing in my amendment touches on HSSIB’s safe space, but we have to be honest about what happens after a report is published. The Health and Care Act 2022 gave HSSIB the power to make recommendations. Anyone who receives one must reply in writing to say what they propose to do. That is where the law stops. There is no duty to act and no duty to finish what was promised. No one is responsible for checking that the promised action has changed anything on the ward, in the community or in the care home.

Turn 76 · Speaker unlabelled

HSSIB’s own figures show the results. In August this year, Professor Ted Baker set out the record. Since April 2024, HSSIB has made 56 safety recommendations. Each one has had a formal response, yet only 11 recommendations from HSSIB and its predecessor have been fully implemented and closed. I repeat that this is not to cast aspersions on the work that HSSIB does, but there is a gap in the law to make sure that what it finds needs to be done is done. Its report on in-patient mental health care found the same things: critical recommendations are too often not implemented, and the opportunity to prevent them harms patients and staff if the recommendations are not put into place.

Turn 78 · Speaker unlabelled

Secondly, proposed new Section 116B deals with the department itself. Many of HSSIB’s most important recommendations are made to national bodies, including the Department of Health and Social Care. The amendment would require the Secretary of State to lay a plan before Parliament within three months. If a recommendation is not to be implemented, the Secretary of State must say why. There must then be an annual report on progress and on the reasons for any delay. That way, the department answers to this House, where we can question it.
