Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Northern Ireland (Sarah Owen)

I thank my hon. Friend for the chance to highlight the work that we are doing on this. I am committed to bringing investment to Northern Ireland, and to promoting its unique dual access to the UK and EU markets. That is why in the Budget we announced £1 million to fund an NI investment summit, which will highlight its world-class sectors, such as cyber-security, advanced manufacturing and the creative industries, some of which I have seen at first hand, including at Catagen, AVEVA and Collins Aerospace. This is a whole-Government approach, and as we heard, we recently welcomed the Minister for Trade to Northern Ireland, during International Trade Week, to meet businesses from across Northern Ireland.

Turn 5 · Jim Allister (North Antrim) (TUV)

May I suggest to the Minister that the much-vaunted dual-market access is a fraud and a deceit? It has not produced one instance of foreign direct investment, for the very simple reason that if a foreign company comes to manufacture in Northern Ireland, its inputs are restricted by the Irish sea border, and it has to go through customs declarations and much more besides. That is the reason why foreign direct investment is failing to take off in Northern Ireland. Is not the answer to restore Northern Ireland’s economy to what it was—a fully integrated part of the United Kingdom economy?

Turn 6 · Sarah Owen

I thought that the hon. and learned Member would welcome the new SPS agreement, given the concerns that he has outlined. This agreement will provide certainty for Northern Ireland cross-border traders, and will free UK businesses from financial and administrative burdens when exporting energy-intensive goods to the EU. While routine SPS checks will be removed, the facilities for NI ports will remain in place to cover checks for goods from the rest of the world. Those checks actively protect UK biosecurity. Stakeholders and businesses that I have met tell us that supply-chain simplification will make it easier to offer a wider range of products to consumers in Northern Ireland. I know that there is no blue moon, but he could have at least welcomed that.
