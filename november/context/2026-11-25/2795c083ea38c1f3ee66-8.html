Turn 60 · Earl Howe (Con)

My Lords, in opposing Clause 65, I will also speak to the related stand-part notices on Schedule 9 and Clauses 66 and 67, and to the other amendments in this group. I begin with something that an Opposition spokesman does not get to say very often—a thank you to the Minister and the Government, for their decision to retain HSSIB. The Bill as introduced would have abolished the Health Services Safety Investigations Body and moved its investigative functions to the Care Quality Commission. The Government have now decided to step back from that and keep HSSIB as an independent body. This is the right decision. It shows that Ministers have listened to the concerns raised in this House and the other place and by many patient safety experts, clinicians, families and organisations outside Parliament. That model depends on two things. The first is the statutory safe space. It protects material gathered during an investigation so that staff, patients and families can give full and candid accounts without fear of how their words might be used. The safe space works only if people believe it is safe. In patient safety, how independent an investigation is seen to be matters as much as how independent it actually is.

Turn 62 · Speaker unlabelled

The second factor is separation from the regulator. The CQC registers providers, inspects them, rates them and takes enforcement action against them. It cannot also be seen as a neutral investigator of the system that it regulates. When the causes of harm include failures of regulation or inspection, an investigator inside the regulator would, in effect, be investigating itself. That conflict was obvious from the start, and it is why so many voices warned against the merger.
