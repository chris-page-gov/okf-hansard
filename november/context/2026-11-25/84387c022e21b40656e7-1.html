Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department of Health and Social Care (Baroness Merron) (Lab)

My Lords, we are delivering some key ADHD taskforce recommendations, including improving national and local data, strengthening commissioning and payment guidance, and developing workforce capability. The work of the taskforce also informed the independent Fonagy review into the prevalence of and support for mental health conditions, ADHD and autism. We will build on this progress through the forthcoming mental health strategy so that we can move towards earlier needs-led support while improving access to high-quality assessment and treatment when required.

Turn 6 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I cannot predict the final outcome of the mental health strategy, as I hope the noble Baroness will understand, but it will be available soon and she will be able to make her judgment. In respect of responding directly to the taskforce, we have sought to progress the recommendations, as I have already described. NHS England, for example, is publishing an ADHD data improvement plan and technical guidance for recording referrals, assessments and diagnosis in national datasets—exactly the kind of thing that the taskforce was looking for. The taskforce has informed the Fonagy review, will inform the mental health strategy and has informed the work of NHS England.

Turn 7 · Baroness McIntosh of Hudnall (Lab)

My Lords, my noble friend will be aware that, once people with ADHD have a diagnosis, it is apparent that they are vulnerable to a number of other risks, more than the general population. For example, as I mentioned in a question yesterday, they are much more vulnerable to eating disorders—they show up in that population more than other groups—and to other kinds of mental health disorder. Unfortunately, treatment for those disorders is very restricted and, in some cases, absolutely unavailable. Will my noble friend ensure that, in looking at ADHD, she also looks at the things to which people with ADHD are liable to fall victim and makes sure that those treatments are available for them?

Turn 8 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

Yes, indeed. My noble friend makes the very good point that ADHD so frequently co-occurs with other conditions. For example, some 21% of those with ADHD are also autistic, and ADHD rates were at 39% among people with a common mental health condition, compared with 7% among those without one. It would be quite wrong to see ADHD in isolation, as my noble friend says. The work that NHS England has undertaken has moved us forward massively, not least because it had no national programme on ADHD prior to 2024.
