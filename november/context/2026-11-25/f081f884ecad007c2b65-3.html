Turn 17 · Bridget Phillipson

We are approaching a year on from the publication of our violence against women and girls strategy, and we will provide an update to the House on that shortly. Given that we know reporting is often not as we would like it—there is significant under-reporting when it comes to domestic abuse and sexual violence—we follow the work of the British crime survey to understand the real rates we are seeing. That is in part because it would obviously be perverse if we encouraged reporting and areas felt disproportionately affected for having done a good job in raising awareness and encouraging victims to come forward. In parallel, we intend to set out further steps on a metric to track attitudes, because we know that violence against women seen in the experiences of women follow from attitudes that often start young and develop over time. That is why our focus on prevention is also important.

Turn 18 · Josh Fenton-Glynn (Calder Valley) (Lab)

I am incredibly proud that White Ribbon UK was founded in my Calder Valley constituency, and I am proud to wear a white ribbon every day, not just on White Ribbon Day. I recently re-read the Casey review of group-based child sexual exploitation, and it struck me that many of the older teenage victims were not considered victims of child sexual exploitation, just by dint of being over 16. While grooming is not a specific offence, if teenagers are over 16, there is very little that grooming gangs can be prosecuted for. Could we undertake to look at the idea of grooming as a specific offence, but also to look at what more we can do to change the consent laws, so there is a recognition that younger girls should not be targeted and preyed on by older men?

Turn 21 · Bridget Phillipson

The hon. Gentleman makes a compelling case. We all have a responsibility to tackle violence against women and girls, wherever it occurs and whoever is responsible. We see a consistent pattern of men putting themselves into positions of trust and authority in order to abuse and exploit women. That happens in politics, sadly; it happens in the creative industries; it happens in business; and it happens in the Church. It happens right across society. The sooner and the more quickly we confront that reality and talk openly about it, the better the place we will be in to support women to come forward, share their experiences and not feel that there should be any stigma or shame in seeking support.
