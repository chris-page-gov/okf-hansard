Turn 9 · The Prime Minister

Tomorrow we will introduce legislation in the House that will set the date for the implementation of the deals on food, on emissions, and indeed on the wider youth experience scheme. We will continue discussions with the European Union on the question of tuition fees, but it is what we have said all along, and the Conservative party needs to decide where it stands. Will it stand against a deal that will remove mountains of paperwork from British businesses and British exporters—a deal that the vast majority of Britain’s major supermarkets support because, they say, it can bring down pressure on the weekly shop. The Conservatives were once the party that opposed the imposition of red tape; they are now the red tape Tories standing against a deal that will be good for British businesses, good for British consumers, and good for young people in Britain.

Turn 15 · The Prime Minister

I remember when I first came to this House and made a statement about my approach to this whole question. The right hon. Lady said that I had revealed a negotiating position and that we would not get a deal. Well, we did get a deal. We actually got a deal that removed the threat from “Made in Europe” to the British car industry, the British steel industry and the aluminium industry. She minimises those things because she looks for the negative in everything. She has not got the ability to see the bigger picture and what this deal will do for the economy, but also in building a more constructive relationship with our nearest neighbours. The Conservatives burned those bridges, and they damaged good will with our nearest market. We are repairing it, and we are right to do so.
