Turn 105 · Dr Tidball

No, I would describe them as a gateway to an inclusive democracy. This building, in which laws are made and where we ensure power is fully exercised in a way that reflects the vibrance and diversity of our proud British democracy, should be accessible and inclusive to our citizens. This Parliament is a House where all British people should feel at home: it belongs to them, too. Thanks to the state education I had in Penistone, which helped me catch up on three years of surgeries that I needed, I was able to go on to study at the University of Oxford, where I did a law degree to understand how disabled people could have a greater role in making and changing the law. It is the honour of my life to sit on these green Benches and speak in this Chamber as the voice for the place where I grew up.
