Turn 26 · The Prime Minister

If I could first go back to energy bills, Mr Speaker, the right hon. Gentleman is right to push for more and I will do more. Next year, we will remove the standing charge from gas bills, and I will seek to do that as quickly as possible, hopefully with the support of the House. On the steel industry, particularly in Wales, he will know that the Government have invested heavily in the electric arc furnace at Tata Steel in Port Talbot. Again, there is more to be done, and I will always meet if he asks me to do so, but he should recognise that we have, as a Government, invested heavily in steel transition in south Wales.

Turn 30 · The Prime Minister

The hon. Gentleman is right that the Good Friday agreement is based on the principle of consent, and that is very clearly laid out within it. The important thing, as we approach its 30th anniversary, is that we all remind ourselves of what precisely is in the Good Friday agreement and work very hard to uphold its values. More broadly, the priority now is for all parties in Northern Ireland to come together and urgently agree a balanced budget to protect public services. I am concerned that this has not happened, and along with the Northern Ireland Secretary, I urge all parties to use the additional funding that this Government have offered to agree a budget as a matter of urgency. We can then sure up the arrangements in Northern Ireland and mark the 30th anniversary of the Good Friday agreement in two years’ time.
