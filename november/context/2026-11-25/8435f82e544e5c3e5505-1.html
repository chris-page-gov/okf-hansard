Turn 1 · Rushanara Ali (Bethnal Green and Stepney) (Lab)

I beg to move, That this House has considered Government support for the protection of the rights of the Rohingya. It is a pleasure to serve under your chairmanship, Sir Jeremy. On 19 November, the International Court of Justice found Myanmar responsible for genocide against the Rohingya. It is 80 years since the International Court of Justice was established in the shadow of the second world war and the horrors of the Holocaust, but it is the first time since 1946 that the United Nations’ highest court has found a state guilty of genocide. As Philippe Sands KC has said, the ICJ verdict is “right up there with the great moments of international justice”. Turn 3 · Rushanara Ali

I absolutely agree and will come on to what we need to do and what we need to see our Government do. I thank my hon. Friend for the work he has done with us on this important issue. As I said, the signs were there long before 2017 when colleagues across the House started the campaign with me and others. In 2013, when I visited Rakhine state with the support of Burma Campaign UK and Refugees International, the Burmese military had already committed horrific crimes and displaced 140,000 people, but it took the rape, torture and murder of thousands more before the world really took notice. It took years before the British Government backed our campaign to support the Gambia’s case against the Myanmar military at the International Court of Justice.

Turn 4 · Sir Jeremy Hunt (Godalming and Ash) (Con)

I commend the hon. Member’s long-standing advocacy and the powerful voice in our Parliament she has exercised for the Rohingyas. Does she agree that this is a moment of choice for humanity? If we get this right, it could be as powerful as the conviction of Radovan Karadžić in 2016 for war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide, but if we get it wrong, it could be as shameful as the way the world turned a blind eye to the genocide in Rwanda in 1994. For that reason, it is imperative that the UK Government lead an international coalition to ensure that genocide will never be forgotten, never be ignored and never ever be swept under the carpet.

Turn 6 · Tahir Ali (Birmingham Hall Green and Moseley) (Lab)

My hon. Friend is making an important speech. Does she agree that the Rohingya in Myanmar were repeatedly branded as Bengali illegal immigrants, despite living there for centuries? They were portrayed as a threat to Buddhism, as outbreeding the majority, as a danger to women and as a fifth column; those narratives came before the genocide. Given that we now hear the same echoes in claims about British Muslims from extremists and in public debates in the UK, does she agree that we need to address the issue of anti-Muslim hatred seriously and urgently before it gets out of hand?
