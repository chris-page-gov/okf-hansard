Turn 25 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs (Chris Elmore)

I beg to move, That the Committee has considered the draft International Claims Commission for Ukraine (Immunities and Privileges) Order 2026. May I say what a pleasure it is to serve under your chairship for the first time, Ms Lewell? This order is required before the UK can ratify the convention establishing an International Claims Commission for Ukraine. The UK signed the convention on 16 December 2025. The International Claims Commission builds on the register of damage for Ukraine established in 2023, which collects and records claims from individuals, organisations and public bodies in Ukraine for damage, loss and injury inflicted by Russia. Once the commission has been established, the register and its works will be transferred to the commission. Turn 27 · Edward Morello (West Dorset) (LD)

It is a pleasure to serve with you in the Chair, Ms Lewell. The International Claims Commission for Ukraine has been established to assess the claims and award compensation for damage caused by Russia’s illegal war against Ukraine. The statutory instrument gives the commission the legal status and protection it needs to carry out its work independently. The instrument allows it to enter contracts and hold assets in the UK. It protects property, archives, bank accounts and premises from lawsuits, searches or seizure by UK authorities. Commissioners, officials and experts are given immunity from legal proceedings for actions carried out in their official duties, and commissioner salaries are exempt from UK income tax. Turn 28 · Sadik Al-Hassan (North Somerset) (Lab)

It is a pleasure to serve under your chairship, Ms Lewell. This order is practical, but it matters. It gives legal effect in the UK to the privileges and immunities that the International Claims Commission for Ukraine needs to do its job. The commission will deliver justice and compensation to Ukrainians for the harm caused by Russia’s invasion. In my constituency, people have shown what solidarity looks like: residents have opened their homes to Ukrainian families, volunteers and community groups have collected donations, and our council, libraries and employers have helped new arrivals to learn English and find work. Those same people want to see accountability for Russia’s actions, and this order helps to deliver it.

Turn 30 · Taiwo Owatemi (Coventry North West) (Lab)

I thank the Minister for bringing the order forward. I would like to highlight three key things. First, there is an immediate task here in Britain to give councils and Ukrainian organisations a reusable referral route for claimants. Secondly, we must use our influence over the rules so that people can remedy gaps in their evidence before a final decision. Thirdly, Ministers should bring to Parliament an account of progress towards a fund, including how existing loan commitments will be respected and urgent personal claims prioritised when money becomes available. We can protect the evidence entrusted to us, help to establish an independent body capable of deciding what Russia owes and use Britain’s influence to secure payment. I welcome the progress that the Minister has made.
