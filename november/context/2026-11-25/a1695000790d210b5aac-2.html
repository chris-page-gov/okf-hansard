Turn 12 · Baroness Wolf of Dulwich (CB)

My Lords, as recruiters of international students, universities use rather different English language tests from the Home Office. As a King’s College London academic who currently does admissions for a very competitive MSc, I am all too aware of the changes being wrought at speed by AI. We do online interviews of marginal students to see if they can cope with the course. One can see how instant translation software is being used by more and more people as they are interviewed and talking to us. Many noble Lords will have noted the recent meltdown in the university entrance examinations in Korea because of AI-enabled cheating. Can the Minister tell me how the Government intend to monitor these ongoing threats to online testing in the years ahead?

Turn 15 · Lord Collins of Highbury (Lab)

The noble Baroness is talking about a potential future threat. For the last 14 years we have had a system that could have been open to abuse. Net immigration has fallen under this Government. We are absolutely determined to address this issue. Let me say what I have said again and again: every time someone applies for a visa to come to this country, they have to go through some of these tests. Some of the testing centres that have been in existence for the last 20 years, under the last Government, could be open to the sort of abuse the noble Baroness is talking about. We are determined to stop that abuse and to have a system that is fair to the individual but also very fair to the taxpayer.
