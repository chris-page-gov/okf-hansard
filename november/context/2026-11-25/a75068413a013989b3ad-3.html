Turn 18 · The Prime Minister

I am glad that the right hon. Gentleman mentioned John Baron, whom I knew well and worked very closely with in this House. I am conscious that other former Members and other people have helped this cause over the years; I cannot name them all today, but I know that this has been a joint effort by many people across the House. I absolutely agree with the right hon. Gentleman’s specific point. I mentioned the Hillsborough law and the criminal sanctions in it. That will take a while to commence, but it will commence while this panel is doing its work. I ask every civil servant involved in this work and responding to the panel’s request to be mindful of it at all times. That is not to threaten anybody; it is simply to say: do the humane, right and decent thing. Those should be the values with which all Government Departments work, all the time.

Turn 20 · The Prime Minister

I thank my hon. Friend for his very kind words, and I agree with him strongly. That was part of my reason for leaving this House 10 years ago; I could not understand the way that Parliament, rather than being a check on the Executive, is often just a rubber stamp on long-held departmental positions. That says something about the need to change our democracy. On the specific question, the First Secretary of State is looking at a national oversight mechanism, which is precisely the kind of body that my hon. Friend suggests, and we will involve him in that discussion. Let me come back to the point that the pattern is too entrenched here; we have had too many injustices going on for too long, and so many people have been left in the wilderness and traumatised by that. We cannot keep seeing that and not act to fundamentally change the balance of the relationship between citizen and state.

Turn 21 · Helen Maguire (Epsom and Ewell) (LD)

Since being elected in 2024, I have been working closely with Alan Owen, John Morris and Steve Purse from LABRATS, as well as Susie Boniface and Members from across the House, to support our nuclear test veterans. Justice for our veterans is important, and I pay full tribute to all those who have campaigned on their behalf. I very much welcome the independent panel, because it is long overdue. The average age of a nuclear test veteran is 88, and around two of them pass away every single week. However, the panel will be effective only if it has real power; the truth must not be hidden behind national security. Will the Prime Minister commit to providing the panel with access to all national security data, and give a commitment that nothing will be withheld, whether it is from Porton Down, the Atomic Weapons Establishment or Five Eyes?

Turn 22 · The Prime Minister

I thank the hon. Lady for her work on this matter. Let me say again that Bishop James Jones is exactly the right person to carry out this work, because he is known in the Whitehall world and can be trusted to see all material. We accept that some material will not be able to be put into the public domain, given the nature of the issue that we are discussing, but I do not want to see information withheld. Where necessary, I will intervene, with the clearance that I have, to look at it myself and see whether it is justifiable for that information to be withheld. Sometimes in government, the phrase “national security” is thrown around and used perhaps at times when it should not be used, and we must not have a situation like that here. I will never compromise national security—nothing that I would ever do would do that—but at the same time, it cannot be used as an excuse to hide the truth.

Turn 24 · Becky Gittins (Clwyd East) (Lab)

I welcome the announcement from the Prime Minister, which comes after years of campaigning from my constituent Steve Purse. Steve’s father was a nuclear test victim, and Steve was born with disabilities and health conditions that he believes are a direct result of his father being exposed to radiation while serving in the RAF. It has been an absolute privilege and really heart-warming to hear Steve’s name spoken so many times in this Chamber today, and to have seen him sat with the Prime Minister, talking about this experience. As well as paying tribute to the tireless work of Steve, his family and the formidable nuclear test veteran community, will the Prime Minister confirm that this work will begin straightaway? My constituent and many others have already had to wait too long for the truth.
