Turn 66 · Jim Shannon (Strangford) (DUP)

First of all, after decades of no progress, today we have small steps in the right direction. I personally thank the Prime Minister and our Government for the initiative that they have taken today. Across the United Kingdom, it does not go unnoticed among all of us who look for guidance and progress. Our nuclear test veterans across the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland served Queen and country in the 1950s and 1960s. They have waited for too long for justice, with too many of them sadly passing away before seeing accountability. Alongside the welcome announcement of Bishop James Jones’s panel, what immediate steps are the Government taking to ensure timely recognition and fair compensation for the 2,000 remaining veterans and their bereaved families before the 75th anniversary?

Turn 67 · The Prime Minister

The hon. Gentleman asks an important question. To answer it, the best thing I can say is that we need to have some tracks moving concurrently through this process. I will have to wait for the interim report before we can make those decisions, but I do not think we should delay all the work. With the First Secretary of State, I will look at every possible way to move things forward together. I thank the hon. Gentleman for his comments, as indeed I thank all Members for their comments in relation to this statement. I want the whole of Whitehall to see the complete unanimity across both sides of this House and from all parties. It sends the strongest of messages that we need to get on. We have got to do this. We have to treat people properly at all times, not only in this instance, but in every instance in relation to how the state works with people who have suffered injustice, bereavement …
