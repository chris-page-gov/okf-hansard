Turn 10 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

As we develop, for example, the technical education routes that I have outlined, we are also working with schools and with teachers’ representatives to make sure not only that the right number of teachers are in place to deliver these courses but that we are thinking about the professional development that will be necessary for those teachers. Having taught both academic and vocational subjects alongside each other, I think it is key to have qualified teachers in front of young people, to provide the tenets of good teaching—many of which spread across technical and vocational and academic teaching—to as many teachers as possible, and to meet our target for more qualified teachers in front of students, as we are successfully doing at the moment.

Turn 12 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

It is important that the new Prime Minister has emphasised—for example, with his proposal for new technical routes for young people from 14 to 16—the significance he places on those qualifications. In developing new vocational GCSEs, we will also boost the status of these more technical and vocational qualifications by ensuring that they are based on national standards and national content—something that does not happen at the moment with the technical awards that students take. We will ensure, particularly as we develop work with strategic authorities, that it is clear that the routes young people take through technical and vocational qualifications will lead them to high-quality destinations, whether higher education, skilled work or apprenticeships. That is what will make this education valuable in the eyes of young people and their parents.

Turn 15 · Baroness Cash (Con)

My Lords, the Minister knows that I share her desire to ensure that as many young people as possible have a future route through their education. The Government have said that technical awards vary by provider, have no nationally set content and do not consistently support progression to further study. There will be a problem for one cohort in 2028: the technical pathways that start in September 2028 will use the same technical awards that the Government recognise are problematic, because the new vocational GCSEs will not be ready until 2029 at the earliest. How can schools be expected to promote qualifications that the Government themselves have called inconsistent? Will the Minister ensure that no child of the 2028 cohort is pushed into qualifications that will not lead to a post-16 route?

Turn 16 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

I share the noble Baroness’s objective that we need to keep improving the system in order to make sure that the routes available to young people are of the highest possible quality. As the noble Baroness said, we will introduce new vocational GCSEs from 2029, with nationally set content that we think will be of a higher quality than existing technical awards. We think that there are opportunities, particularly in mayoral strategic authorities, to develop routes using existing technical awards, which have brought alongside them high-quality work experience and links to employers, that local strategic authorities will be able to facilitate. That is an improvement on the existing position whereby lots of young people are taking technical awards and, to be fair, are succeeding. This strengthens the technical awards as we develop the new vocational GCSEs.
