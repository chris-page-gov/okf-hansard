Turn 19 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 279QW is also in the name of the noble Lord, Lord Jamieson. As we have already discussed in Committee, effective care and support depend on strong co-operation between local authorities, the NHS and their wider partners. I am confident that this principle of mutual co-operation between the NHS and local government is already embedded in statute. In addition to the duties in the National Health Service Act 2006, the Care Act 2014 places co-operation firmly within its statutory framework, including through those sections that promote integration, strategic co-operation and, importantly, co-operation on individual cases. These duties are fundamental to ensuring that services work together around the individual.

Turn 20 · Speaker unlabelled

At the risk of repeating myself, noble Lords may be aware that I was the lead member for health and adult social care in a local authority, in Islington, until May this year, so I am more aware than most of the pressures on local authorities and the consistent reduction in their core grant between 2010 and 2024. I understand very acutely the pressures facing local authorities with regard to adult social care. I hope I have reassured noble Lords that, while the Government remain committed to a new vision for adult social care, we believe that decisions on future arrangements should be considered as part of the wider reforms on an agenda championed not just by the Government but by the Prime Minister himself, rather than in isolation.
