Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department of Health and Social Care (Baroness Merron) (Lab)

My Lords, in the world’s largest trial, the NIHR-funded early detection using information technology and health—otherwise known as EDITH—trial will test whether AI can safely help NHS experts detect breast cancer earlier. It will assess whether AI could free up specialist time, enable more women to be screened and perform effectively across different groups of women. Its findings will provide vital evidence to inform decisions about the future use of AI in NHS breast screening.

Turn 5 · Baroness Nargund (Lab)

My Lords, I thank the Minister for her reply. I congratulate our Government on their investment in this landmark, multicentre breast-screening trial: the world’s largest. It has huge potential to improve early breast cancer detection and will have a significant positive impact on women’s health. Given that breast cancer rates are rising among young women, and they tend to be more aggressive types that are diagnosed late, leading to poorer outcomes, I ask the Minister what consideration has been given to including younger women in the trial. Also, given the existing health inequalities and potential risk of AI bias, can the Minister assure the House that women from low socioeconomic regions, as well as from ethnic-minority backgrounds, are sufficiently included, and that the sub-group outcomes will be published to ensure that AI-assisted innovation is not only safe but equitable?

Turn 6 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I certainly can assure my noble friend that the EDITH trial will run, first of all, within the existing screening programme, and will assess whether AI performs equally across age, ethnicity and socioeconomic groups. On the point about younger women, as my noble friend will be aware, routine screening does begin at 50, which is the independent recommendation by the National Screening Committee. That is because mammograms are less reliable in younger women, due to denser breast tissue. However, I should say that women who are at very high risk can be offered earlier checks. Also, the AgeX trial is currently examining the benefits of screening at a younger age and will report in a few years’ time.

Turn 7 · Baroness Laing of Elderslie (Con)

My Lords, although we would all welcome the progress that the Minister has just described, is she aware that women, especially young women, who have been identified as carrying the BRCA gene often have to wait for more than 18 months to have the necessary surgery? During this 18-month delay, cancer often develops which had not been there in the beginning. Will the Minister take action to have the surgery that is necessary because of the identification of the BRCA gene put on the cancer surgery pathway, rather than the ordinary surgery waiting list, in order that these operations can be done much more quickly than 18 months and specific cancer development can be prevented.
