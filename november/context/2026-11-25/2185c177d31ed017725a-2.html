Turn 9 · Baroness Hussein-Ece (LD)

My Lords, this trial is very welcome and I am particularly encouraged by the Minister’s response to the noble Baroness, Lady Nargund, on how health inequalities will certainly be taken into account. The trial uses opt-out consent for 660,000 women. What assurance can the Minister give that their mammogram data will not be used to train commercial models without proper governance, and that a qualified human being will remain accountable for every diagnosis?

Turn 10 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I can give the assurance that all the usual safeguards apply. The EDITH trial is supporting mammogram interpretation and comparing standard double-reading with two assisted pathways. One pathway is using AI after an NHS expert—a human being—has been involved. Another is using AI first, to inform the review by one or two experts: in other words, one or two human beings. AI will not be working alone; it will be working with experts. I hope that is reassuring.

Turn 11 · Lord Tarassenko (CB)

My Lords, I declare an interest as senior adviser to the Alan Turing Institute and as the holder of an honorary researcher contract with my local NHS trust. I welcome the fact that the EDITH trial is starting to recruit patients this week. It will help to answer some fundamental questions about the use of AI in radiology: for example, what happens when the AI disagrees with the radiologist when it is one of the two readers of the image? Will the second radiologist, brought in to arbitrate, be told what the decisions of the AI and the first radiologist were? So I ask the Minister: will the full trial protocol be published by the NIHR, the funders, or the co-investigators in an academic journal?

Turn 12 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I am glad that the noble Lord is as enthusiastic as I believe I am for this EDITH trial. I emphasise that it is the largest trial of this nature in the world, and it is originating in the UK. The answer to his question is yes, the protocol will be published. I can also confirm that, after the trial, the findings will be published, presented and shared with decision-makers, charities, clinics and, indeed, the public. The results will certainly inform the Government as well as the screening services and the UK National Screening Committee. This is a hugely significant trial.

Turn 14 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I very much understand the point. The EDITH trial in particular will span some 30 centres across four nations, which will ensure that we are comparing its performance by ethnicity, age and socioeconomic status, as well as breast density. On coming forward for mammograms—which I think my noble friend is referring to—this is one of the reasons that we have, for the first time, established a partnership with Breast Cancer Now. We accept the point that there are a number of women who may check their breasts regularly but do not have the confidence to come forward; that is not acceptable, and it will apply to particular groups more than others. By working with Breast Cancer Now, our information will be targeted and appropriate to those groups. I mention that as just one example.
