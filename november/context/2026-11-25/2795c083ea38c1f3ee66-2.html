Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

I have some questions for the Minister. What will the national care service do? Will it take responsibilities or delivery actions from local authorities, or will it be an additional service? How will this then interact with local authorities? How will responsibilities be split and what will the governance arrangements be? Will there be changes to local authorities’ statutory duties? What does free personal care mean, and what will its scope be? How will personal circumstances be taken into account? Coming from a rural area, I know that it is far more difficult to support someone in a house in a rural village than someone living in their own apartment as part of a community in the centre of a town.

Turn 12 · Speaker unlabelled

There are also far too many older people who are suffering and need extra support while awaiting operations such as a replacement knee or hip. We see a similar situation for young people with the backlog of mental health support for children. These delays mean that their underlying health deteriorates. For some, that delay may mean that they will no longer regain true independence. Fixing this would be a win-win. It is often said that the health system cannot be fixed until social care is fixed, but without the right support from the health system, the social care system will never be fixed. That means there needs to be true co-operation and a greater focus on prevention and community support. That is the reason why there needs to be a mutual duty of co-operation.

The existing charging framework contains financial protections through the minimum income guarantee and personal expenses allowance, which ensures that people are left with a protected level of income after contributing towards their care costs. We have recently strengthened these protections through increases to social care allowances, including an uplift of up to 7% to the minimum income guarantee. This is the first above-inflation increase in over a decade.
