Turn 66 · Speaker unlabelled

We should also be clear about what has been kept, because HSSIB’s record is a strong argument for its independence. Since it began operating, HSSIB’s recommendations have changed practice and saved lives. Its recommendations to the Royal College of Emergency Medicine and the Royal College of Radiologists on aortic dissection have led to around 300 more people a year receiving life-saving treatment. Its recommendations to NICE changed national guidance on detecting jaundice in newborn babies. Its work on out-of-area mental health placements directly influenced what is now the Mental Health Act 2025, strengthening safeguards for some of the most vulnerable patients in our health system. Turn 67 · Speaker unlabelled

HSSIB’s influence also goes well beyond individual reports. In its first two years, there were more than 40,000 enrolments on its courses for local investigators. It has helped change the conversation across the NHS from blame to understanding human factors, safety science and systems thinking. In 2023, it founded the International Patient Safety Organisations Network, which brings together countries that have, or want to build, an organisation like HSSIB. Other countries look to that model as an example, so this would have been a strange moment to dismantle it.

Turn 69 · Speaker unlabelled

That brings me to one or two questions for the Minister. First, now that the Government have decided to keep HSSIB, what is their vision for it? HSSIB itself has suggested several areas where it could do a lot more. One is investigations in adult social care, which is part of the content of the amendment tabled by the noble Lord, Lord Scriven. Another is helping to build local capability so that investigation findings turn into lasting improvements, and perhaps also independently overseeing whether the recommendations of major national inquiries are implemented after those inquiries close. It has also suggested that it is well placed to lead selected national inquiries into major patient safety concerns. Are the Government willing to look at proposals of that kind? And if so, will they do it through this Bill or through future legislation?

Turn 70 · Speaker unlabelled

Lastly, HSSIB staff have lived with uncertainty about their future since at least the summer of 2025, so I hope that the Minister will take this opportunity to reassure them that their work is valued and that the organisation has a secure future. As we often say, patient safety rests on the trust that when something goes wrong, people can speak freely, the causes will be understood and lessons will be learnt rather than buried. HSSIB was created to earn that trust and its record shows that it has. By keeping it independent, the Government have protected something that I think is very precious. We welcome that decision. We thank the Minister for it, and we look forward to working with her to make sure HSSIB can do even more in the years ahead.
