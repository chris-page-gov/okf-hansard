Turn 10 · Jesse Norman

I am afraid to say that I did not think that the Public Accounts Committee showed itself at its best in that report. We did not see, for example, anything like the detailed scrutiny of individual costs that I would have expected. I will come on in a second to the costings, then the hon. Lady can decide whether the PAC has done its job in relation to those. It is easy to get lost in such enormous numbers, but what do they mean in real terms? By way of comparison, the cost of the restoration of Notre-Dame was about £600 million. Yes, it has just a quarter of the footprint of the Palace of Westminster and was not occupied during its restoration, but as one commentator remarked, we could restore a Notre-Dame every single week over a year for the cost of one R and R and still have enough cash left over for a nuclear-powered aircraft carrier.

Turn 13 · Sir Julian Lewis (New Forest East) (Con)

One thing I think we need to get clear is what is the primary purpose of this restoration. Is it fundamentally just to make the building safe or is it to try and in some way transform the building? According to the National Audit Office’s June 2026 update report, it is the former.
