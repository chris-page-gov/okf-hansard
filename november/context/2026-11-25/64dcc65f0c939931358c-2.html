Turn 9 · Earl Russell (LD)

My Lords, the latest price cap increase is almost entirely accounted for by wholesale costs: £404 out of £443, according to reliable estimates. It is our dependence on fossil fuels that continues to drive up our energy bills. That said, too little is being done to protect those most in need, partly because the Government lack a data infrastructure to effectively support them. Where support is provided, it is often poorly targeted and expensive. Given that the kick-starter project has been running since January, can the Minister update the House on progress and say what other data-matching initiatives are being undertaken?

Turn 10 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

That is a very important question. As I am sure noble Lords will know, the Government put a great deal of effort into making sure that interventions are targeted effectively, because we recognise the scale of fuel poverty. I could list a range of them, but the Chief Whip has told me not to give such long answers; on another day, I would be happy to go through them all in great detail. The kick-starter programme is important. Noble Lords will know about the household energy payment. Thinking has emerged from that and we are working hard to ensure that data is developed across a range of programmes. We are doing that across government, because it affects a number of other departments. I will be happy to update the House with details of that.

Turn 13 · Lord Vaux of Harrowden (CB)

The main reason why electricity prices are so high is because the wholesale price is set at the marginal cost of each half hour, which is usually gas. Even if 99% of it is renewable, if 1% is gas then the whole 100% is set at the gas price. When are we going to sort that out and decouple the electricity price from the marginal gas price?

Turn 14 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

That is a fundamental point and something that the Government are working very hard on. I am sure that the noble Lord is aware that, in 2020, gas set the price of electricity 90% of the time. It is coming down and it is now 60% of the time. Developing the renewable industry and making sure that we develop the grid is critical to delinking. I can guarantee that delinking is a priority for this Government.
