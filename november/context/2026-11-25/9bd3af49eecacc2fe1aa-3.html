Turn 19 · Mr Paul Kohler (Wimbledon) (LD)

Clause 13 of the troubles Bill states that “reconciliation should be promoted”, but it fails to define the term. As one witness said to the Northern Ireland Affairs Committee today, we need a definition, and we need agreement on what components are included in reconciliation. That is a point made by many in Northern Ireland civil society, including the WAVE trauma centre and the Community Relations Council. The failure to create a budget is just one of many manifestations of the failure to reconcile. When will the Secretary of State give reconciliation the legal framework that Northern Ireland needs?

Turn 20 · Chris Bryant

I agree with the hon. Gentleman about reconciliation. As I said to the Northern Ireland Affairs Committee, I have always hated this idea that we want a tolerant society, because that always feels like we are putting up with people we do not particularly like. It is much better to have a society based on respect—respect for difference, and respect for different political opinions and views on the future of Ireland. That is precisely what we should achieve.

Turn 22 · Chris Bryant

I am grateful for the dedication that the right hon. Gentleman has shown on this issue. As he knows, I met families at Omagh on Remembrance Sunday, and I know that the pain is very, very raw for very many of them. I am very keen to ensure they get all the answers they need. As I have said, and as the right hon. Gentleman referred to, it is not for me to decide on the basis of the evidence that has already been brought forward to the inquiry; it is for the inquiry to decide how credible it is and how to measure it all. To be absolutely clear, though, I think it is inconceivable that there will not be an inquiry in Ireland, and I know the families think it would be very helpful if the Irish Government now laid out the terms of reference for such an inquiry.
