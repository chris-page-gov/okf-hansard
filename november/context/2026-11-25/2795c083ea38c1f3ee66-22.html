Turn 170 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 208 would widen the focus to the performance of transplant services, variation between centres, organ utilisation and whether there is enough funding and staffing for 24/7 on-call teams. Behind every transplant is a team that must be ready at any hour of the day or night. If an organ is offered but cannot be used because a centre lacks the capacity, that is a tragedy twice over: for the donor family who said yes and for the patient still waiting. Can the Minister say how many organs offered last year went unused and what is being done about variation between centres? I also welcome her response to the Organ Donation Joint Working Group’s call for a bolder, braver approach, including embedding specialist nurses for organ donation in hospital teams, as my noble friend Lord Lansley rightly called for.

Turn 171 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 209 raises the most delicate question in this group: how the Human Tissue Authority’s guidance treats deemed consent and how far a family’s objection should be decisive. I understand the noble Lord’s intention to make sure that the law Parliament passed is fully reflected in practice, but I am sure he would agree that we need to proceed with some care. Clinicians will understandably be reluctant to proceed against the wishes of a grieving family, and the trust families place in the donation system is precious. Much can be achieved, as has been said, by phrasing the questions to the families in the right way, but we have to be careful how we do that because damaging trust could, in the end, cost more lives than it saves.

Turn 172 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to Amendments 279K and 279L in the name of the noble Baroness, Lady Finlay. It is striking that Scotland has legislated for statutory oversight of the funeral director sector under the Burial and Cremation (Scotland) Act 2016, including a statutory code of practice and powers relating to inspection, and has been developing a licensing regime for funeral directors, while England has no equivalent comprehensive statutory framework. I think many of us were surprised to read that that was the case.

Turn 174 · Speaker unlabelled

The noble Baroness, Lady Finlay, has given us two possible approaches. Amendment 279K is an enabling power with consultation and the affirmative procedure, which would give Ministers flexibility in designing the regime. Amendment 279L is more prescriptive. It would extend the Human Tissue Authority’s remit within six months. The HTA already has expertise in regulating premises that store bodies, so there is a logic to that, but regulating every funeral director, private mortuary and direct cremation provider in England within six months would, I am sure she would acknowledge, be a very large task indeed. We would want to be sure that the authority has the resources and capacity to do that properly.
