Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

I finish on the three issues I would like the Minister to consider. Can transitional arrangements not be put in place through a very tightly drafted exemption that we proposed during the passage of the Medical Training (Prioritisation) Act? Secondly, what about these vacancies? Can a way not be found for foundation posts that are unfilled or fall vacant mid-year to be offered to these graduates? Finally, can the Minister not change the priority groups by regulation? That power should be used to include British citizens with degrees from UK universities who become a priority for foundation training.

Turn 12 · Baroness in Waiting/Government Whip (Baroness Hyde of Bemerton) (Lab)

My Lords, I thank noble Lords for their engagement with this important issue, but the Government cannot support the amendment. While we recognise the high quality of training of graduates from overseas campuses of UK universities, what matters is where the doctor trained. Data shows that domestic graduates are more likely to stay in the country they trained in than those trained internationally. Doctors trained in the UK are also likely to be better equipped to deliver healthcare tailored to the UK’s population because they have a stronger understanding of UK-specific epidemiology and NHS practice.
