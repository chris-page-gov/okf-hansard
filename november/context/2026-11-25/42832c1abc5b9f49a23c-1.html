Turn 5 · Baroness Stedman-Scott (Con)

My Lords, I thank the Minister for introducing these regulations and setting out their purpose. We on these Benches support them. Their principle is sound. The core issue being addressed is clear: someone convicted of a serious violent or sexual offence and sent to prison cannot claim benefits, but someone convicted of the same offence and detained in a secure hospital under a hospital order can, under the present rules, receive more than £800 a month in universal credit. The taxpayer is already paying for their bed, board and treatment, so we are paying twice: once for their care, and again for an income they do not need. No one outside this building would think that fair. These regulations treat offenders sent to hospital in the same way as offenders sent to prison and remove an advantage that should never have existed. Turn 8 · Speaker unlabelled

In conclusion, the benefits system provides an important safety net for millions of people. The purpose of this change is to ensure fairness and remove the duplication of public funding. This has been a long-standing loophole and, thanks again to campaigners and the families of victims, the Government are acting to remove benefit entitlement from individuals convicted of a serious offence and detained under hospital orders. I am grateful for the support of the Committee.
