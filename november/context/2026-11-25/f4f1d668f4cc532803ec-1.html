Turn 3 · Speaker unlabelled

The petitioners therefore request that the House of Commons urge the Government to introduce urgent reforms, known as “Tim’s Law”, to strengthen the response to drink, drug and dangerous driving, including the immediate suspension of driving licences for those suspected of serious driving offences, the introduction of rapid evidential roadside saliva testing for drug driving, stronger measures to tackle repeat offending by dangerous drivers, and improved monitoring and enforcement of high-risk drivers.

Turn 7 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government published their road safety strategy alongside a consultation on proposed changes to penalties for motoring offences. That consultation has now closed and responses are being analysed. It would not be right to pre-empt the outcome or commit to specific measures before that analysis is complete, but the consultation sought views on issues at the heart of this petition, including powers to suspend licences for those suspected of drink and drug driving pending court or forensic analysis, and for those under investigation for the most serious motoring offences involving death or serious injury, and on the use of oral fluid, saliva or other samples in drug-driving forensic analysis.

The consultation also sought views on whether the drink-drive limit in England and Wales should be lowered, whether a lower limit should apply to novice drivers, and whether penalties and preventive measures should be strengthened. The Government recognise that enforcement is central to road safety. We will continue to work with the police, the Home Office, the Ministry of Justice, Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency and other partners to ensure that the police have the appropriate tools to detect, deter and prosecute those who put others at risk.
