Turn 49 · Joe Powell (Kensington and Bayswater) (Lab)

I join others in thanking the Prime Minister, particularly for his clarification that the duty of candour applies and for the appointment of Bishop James Jones. He was absolutely right about “the patronising disposition of unaccountable power”, and I thank him for his support for the Grenfell families, too. It is so important that people have confidence that these panels, inquests, investigations and inquiries will lead to change. As such, I join my hon. Friend the Member for Eltham and Chislehurst (Clive Efford) in encouraging the set-up of a national oversight mechanism, so that we have something long-lasting that gives the campaigners reassurance that they will not have to be the ones who track all these things for years to come.

Turn 51 · Steff Aquarone (North Norfolk) (LD)

My constituent was a member of 543 Squadron, who flew through the nuclear test clouds of foreign states to take samples. They have long been left out of the conversation about nuclear test veterans. My constituent is also worried about the ground crews, particularly those who were required to clean the returning aircraft. Can the Prime Minister give us an assurance that these people, who were clearly involved, will be part of the scope and will be made to feel welcome, part of this conversation, and part of the panel’s terms of reference?

Turn 53 · Alison Hume (Scarborough and Whitby) (Lab)

I thank the Prime Minister for his commitment to truth and justice for our nuclear test veterans. My constituent Edward Luther was stationed on Christmas Island in 1957. Aged just 20, he was told on the way over that he would not make it to 50. At 89 years old, he is still waiting for answers to medical issues that both he and his family have suffered. He told me that he feels abandoned and dismissed. Today, I hope that he feels heard. Edward received his medal through the post. Can the Prime Minister ensure that surviving veterans such as Edward are invited to next year’s national memorial day, so that they can be properly seen and honoured?

Turn 55 · Sorcha Eastwood (Lagan Valley) (Alliance)

I really welcome the Prime Minister’s statement today, and I know the families and the LABRATS will as well. I have to mention another case on behalf of my constituents—families in my constituency of Lagan Valley. Chinook Zulu Delta 576 crashed on 2 June 1994. There were no survivors. It took 31 years for those families to have a meeting with Defence Ministers. That meeting took place last year, but nothing came from it. Today, I am again asking the Prime Minister to meet the Chinook families and ensure that they—as well as the LABRATS and everyone else failed by the state—get justice.
