Turn 12 · Chris Bryant

I want to say two things. First, the Bill is primarily about families getting answers. We need do no more than scratch the surface of Northern Ireland politics to discover problems that still exist from the troubles era. For instance, on 9 May 1997, 100 people were gathered in the Parliament bar in Belfast—a gay bar, as it happens. In came a terrorist with a gun, who shot dead a 24-year-old officer of the Royal Ulster Constabulary, Constable Darren Bradshaw. Nobody has ever been held to account for that murder. We need—the families need—answers on precisely that kind of event in Northern Ireland, and that is what our Bill will deliver.

Turn 15 · Chris Bryant

As the shadow Minister probably knows, I spoke directly with my counterpart in Dublin last week. I made it very clear that we have to have full co-operation from the Irish Government, not only with the ICRIR, but with the Omagh inquiry, as well as all the different ongoing inquiries that need to proceed. People in Northern Ireland will get answers only if the Irish Government fully comply. I am very confident that we will not only work in lockstep, but dovetail our legislation to ensure that we get those answers.
