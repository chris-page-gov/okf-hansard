Turn 11 · Lord Bruce of Bennachie (LD)

My Lords, when Paddy Ashdown published his Humanitarian Emergency Response Review, he concluded that the people who were best able to cope with disasters were those who had been through them before and learned from them. In my own area of north-east Scotland, we have got used to sustained power cuts, and one of the worst storms we had did not just cut off power but all communications—digital, telephones, data and everything else—for days. What are the Government doing to co-ordinate the knowledge that we gain, at home and abroad, and ensure that all the relevant bodies in the private and public sector have access to all that and can give their training on the basis of experience?

Turn 12 · Lord Coaker (Lab)

It is a very important question, and I hope the noble Lord heard some of the answer that I gave to the noble and gallant Lord, which was about working with the devolved Administrations. With Scotland, there has been a lot of consultation with the Scottish Government, but also with local organisations. The British Red Cross, for example, has been involved. Many of the authorities that are responsible for water and those that are responsible for some of the emergency services have been utilised. Alongside that, the noble Lord is right to point out the need to use volunteers and to use those who have the knowledge and experience that he speaks about. As part of that, we are engaging with local communities to build on that, so that they can be part of the conversation as well.
