Turn 17 · Speaker unlabelled

Furthermore, decisions about green belt land should be informed by an overall application of the relevant policies in the NPPF, including policies in the “natural environment” chapter that concern areas of importance for their landscape, geodiversity or biodiversity value. The NPPF is also clear that development proposals should consider the environmental qualities of land proposed for development, including habitats, landscape character and the natural beauty of the countryside, and identify opportunities for those qualities to be conserved or enhanced. If significant harm to biodiversity resulting from a development cannot be avoided, adequately mitigated or, as a last resort, compensated for, then the development should be refused.

Turn 18 · Speaker unlabelled

Each local authority determines planning applications in the light of the local plan and all other material considerations, including policies in the framework. It is for the decision-maker to consider which facts or circumstances are relevant, and how much weight to give them. Where inappropriate development is proposed on the green belt, it is up to the decision maker to judge whether very special circumstances outweigh the potential harm to the green belt.
