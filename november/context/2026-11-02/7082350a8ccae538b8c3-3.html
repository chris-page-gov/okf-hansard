Turn 22 · Speaker unlabelled

Electoral registration officers are responsible for determining eligibility for direct registration. They must be satisfied that a person is entitled to be registered before adding them to the register. If the available evidence is insufficient, an ERO should not issue a notice of direct registration and the ERO will be held responsible for that. We are considering carefully with the election administration sector which data sources electoral registration officers will be able to use to provide evidence of entitlement. Further detail will be set out in secondary legislation under the affirmative procedure in guidance.

Turn 24 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 48 would require an electoral registration officer to verify a person’s existence through three separate datasets before they could be registered without an application. I share the noble Baroness’s and the noble Lord’s commitment to ensuring that only those who are eligible are added to the electoral register. However, I do not believe that this amendment is necessary. Under the provisions in the Bill, an ERO must directly register someone only where they are satisfied that the person is entitled to be registered. The Government do not consider it appropriate to specify in legislation a minimum number of datasets that should be used to determine someone’s existence.
