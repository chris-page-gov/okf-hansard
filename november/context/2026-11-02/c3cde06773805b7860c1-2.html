Turn 9 · Baroness Caine of Kentish Town (Lab)

My Lords, the aim of the Young Creatives Commission—I declare an interest as a member—is to promote and rebuild youth access to the arts and creativity, on a par with sport. It will publish its final report in January. Does my noble friend the Minister agree that its recommendations will be helpful, and will she agree to a meeting between the commission, DfE and DCMS to discuss progressing that agenda?

Turn 12 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

The most effective education in both arts and sports will need to use the broadest range of organisations and facilities, as the noble Earl says. We need to make sure we are building as many links as possible into schools, and I know that councils will be considering this when thinking about the use of their assets. Equally, of course, schools facilities should be used by others who can access them for sports and arts. Unless we work effectively together, we will not be using the resource that we have as well as possible to open up those opportunities to the most people, including both schoolchildren and those of us getting a bit older who also benefit from those activities.

Turn 13 · The Lord Bishop of Leicester

My Lords, the holiday activities and food programme is a shining example of the approach described by the noble Lord, Lord Addington. It offers a proven way of linking up children from disadvantaged backgrounds with a wide range of enriching activities during the school holidays. However, the current eligibility criteria result in more than 500,000 children, mostly from poorer working families, being unable to access the programme. Will the Minister commit to rectifying this by aligning the programme’s eligibility criteria with those that are attached to term-time free school meals?

Turn 14 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

The right reverend Prelate is right that the HAF programme has provided both food and activities for very many children and young people. While we have provided 21 million days to children and young people in England, which have been delivered across 153 local authorities, notwithstanding the £600 million that has been committed to it, we need to look carefully at how we get that to as many children as possible. I cannot commit to changing the entitlement rules but I will take back the points that the right reverend Prelate has made on that programme.
