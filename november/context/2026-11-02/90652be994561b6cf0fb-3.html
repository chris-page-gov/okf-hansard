Turn 18 · Baroness Watkins of Tavistock (CB)

My Lords, will the Minister explain how, as we get people back into work, which the majority of people want to do, we will ensure that they do not have to work from home? I fully agree that for many it is a huge advantage, but the latest figures from the well-being survey at Bupa show how lonely young people are and how easy it is to lose employment if you have no social connection with the people you are working with. Can we ensure that the Government carefully consider how employers will be responsible for bringing people into work as well, so that it does not become cheaper to employ people who are disabled at home?

Turn 21 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My noble friend raises an excellent point. Successive Governments have looked very hard at finding ways to do this. The reason that we asked Alan Milburn to look carefully at young people and the drivers behind inactivity is that we need to understand why people are not in work, not just tell them that they should be and cut money. We need to find a way to make sure that the social security bill is sustainable over time, but we also need to make sure it is doing its job of supporting people to get into work and, crucially, supporting them to stay there and to flourish when they do.
