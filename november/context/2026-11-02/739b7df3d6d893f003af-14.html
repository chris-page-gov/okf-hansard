Turn 105 · Speaker unlabelled

As my noble friend said, Britain’s arrangements are anomalous. Only Germany and Austria have the ability to vary the voting age, and they are very strict, as he said, concerning in particular the capacity of foreign voters to cast their ballots in elections. That is important. If one looks at the situation in Wales, we have heard before about the ability of Commonwealth and Irish citizens to vote. Scotland permits legally resident foreign nationals to vote in Scottish Parliament and local elections. In December last year, 198,000 qualifying foreign nationals were registered to vote in Scottish Parliament and local elections. That is not far off 5% of the electorate. That is a significant issue.

Turn 106 · Speaker unlabelled

No one is casting aspersions on the bona fides of those people—they live in Scotland—but if you are in the business of protecting the integrity of our voting system, and the long-standing link between voting and having a vested interest in your community and nationality, then at your peril you undermine that by allowing people who do not have the same investment, perhaps, constitutionally or historically, in the country in which they are voting. These days, we need to be thinking about the trust people have in the system. There should be consistency between devolved elections, national referenda and parliamentary elections concerning the eligibility of foreign nationals to vote. The devolved Administrations should be brought in line with the national qualifying standard of eligibility to vote.

Turn 107 · Speaker unlabelled

When we are discussing the self-interest of some parties, as my noble friend said, there is the egregious example of the SNP in 2011, I think, using its devolved competence to affect the franchise for its own narrow electoral self-interest. If anything else, were this amendment to be adopted, it would act as a check on devolved power by limiting the devolved Administrations’ ability to amend their rules further—we heard about asylum seekers earlier—encouraged by the dramatic change under this Bill of lowering the voting age to 16. This would cap the Administrations’ abilities to expand the franchise even further—the slippery slope argument.

Turn 108 · Speaker unlabelled

I support my noble friend’s amendments. I think they are sensible. They do not resile from support for the concept of devolution, but they try to bring some coherence and commonality to our electoral systems in this country. That would be good, because people would then understand what they are voting for and would be more likely to vote and exercise their franchise. It seems common sense and sensible so to do. On that basis, I strongly support the amendment.
