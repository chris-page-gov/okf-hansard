Turn 98 · Speaker unlabelled

Those are the problems, and they possibly explain why other European countries have been much more reticent in this regard than we have in the UK. Switzerland, which is an explicitly federal state with very highly devolved powers, even now, is the only one that gives similar rights to its cantons as those that Scotland has. In Germany and Austria, the Länder can vary the voting age within constraints that are set centrally, but they are explicitly prohibited from granting voting rights to foreign residents. We will come on to this question, but granting voting rights to foreign residents at all is extremely rare in Europe. Crucially, in those countries the rights are the same for all territories. There are no asymmetric arrangements as we have in the UK, and that asymmetry produces part of the problem.

Turn 101 · Speaker unlabelled

For reasons of normal constitutional logic and international comparability, and to stop politicising something so fundamental as the electoral mandate, we need to look at this again. The national Government have a responsibility to look at the issues and consequences for the wider country. Devolution allows different parts of the UK to govern themselves differently. It does not follow that each must have an independent power to determine the basic qualifications of those who are entitled to elect a legislature. I contend that those qualifications ought to be settled nationally through this Parliament and that we can and should preserve different Governments and policies without dispersing responsibility for the democratic foundations of the state.

Turn 104 · Speaker unlabelled

I have said it before and I will say it again: we are a unitary state. It seems to me odd that, if you live in Monmouthshire, you have a completely different voting system and a way of electing people who matter and make decisions about your life from those five miles across the border in Herefordshire—ditto in Cumbria or Northumberland vis-à-vis Argyll or Dumfriesshire or East Lothian. It seems strange for such a small country. We have the single transferable vote, the additional member system, the regional list, the alternative vote, first past the post and so on, and I have mentioned the d’Hondt system before. Voting rights, as my noble friend said, should not change simply because of someone moving across the United Kingdom between different counties, towns, cities and villages.
