Turn 66 · Speaker unlabelled

I appreciate the interest of the noble Baroness, Lady Bennett of Manor Castle, in the right of young people to vote. However, it is right that those convicted of a crime and serving a sentence in custody cannot vote. We believe that extending the franchise should bring the same rights and responsibilities as apply to other electors, and that must include the existing restriction on voting for those convicted of an offence and serving a custodial sentence. The clause ensures that this applies consistently across the different forms of youth detention accommodation. I will also take this opportunity to reassure my noble friend Lady Lister, who has tabled Amendment 6 on this point; I think she recognises that the drafting does not affect young people in secure children’s homes who are not convicted prisoners.

Turn 67 · Speaker unlabelled

It is very unusual for young people under 18 to receive a custodial sentence, so those that do have usually committed very serious offences. These provisions concern elections for which the UK Government are responsible; devolved elections in Scotland and Wales are subject to different arrangements. The provisions in the Bill simply reflect the well-established position on prisoner voting and ensure that 16 and 17 year-olds are treated the same as all other electors and the Government will not be revisiting this position.
