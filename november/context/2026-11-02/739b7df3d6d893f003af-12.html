Turn 89 · Speaker unlabelled

5: Clause 2, page 2, line 19, leave out “in England” Member’s explanatory statement This amendment, and others in the name of Lord Frost, seeks to make the Representation of the People Act 1983 franchise, as amended by this Act, the franchise for all statutory elections, referendums and polls in the United Kingdom, except City of London ward elections. It would abolish the Scotland-only and Wales-only franchise variants and make the franchise a wholly reserved matter.

Turn 94 · Speaker unlabelled

The second issue is one of constitutional logic. This is one country, and in modern democracies voters have the same rights. We may agree or disagree with exactly what those rights should be and how we extend them. As I have said, this is a point not about the substance but about how things are done in devolved Administrations. It is worth highlighting that it cuts both ways. If, for example, Reform UK had won the elections in Wales earlier this year, it might have introduced its policy to end Commonwealth voting in Wales—a policy that I happen to agree with, and we will come on to that—and a huge change to the system, once again in only one part of the country. I contend that this is not a satisfactory situation.

Turn 96 · Speaker unlabelled

Thirdly, the most pernicious consequence that we have seen is the use of franchise changes in a highly political way, in very charged environments. As I noted, the original extension of votes at 16 in Scotland—since we have allowed ourselves to break the taboo that these changes might be made for other than objective reasons—was a transparently political gesture aimed at giving the SNP the best chance of winning its referendum. The extension to all foreign residents in Scotland and Wales, wherever they come from, is, again, a deliberate attempt to skew the potential support for different parties and ensure that any repeated referendum on any national question would be slanted by large numbers of non-British citizens on the register.
