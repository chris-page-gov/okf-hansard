Turn 98 · Speaker unlabelled

This brings me to a broader point. It has been a long-standing policy under Governments of all political stripes to use the registered electorate rather than population data as the basis for constituencies. Can the Minister say whether this will cut across boundaries? It could also mean asymmetric electoral rolls, distorting the operation of boundary reviews. Turn 99 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 44B in the name of the noble Lord, Lord Bird, seeks to extend the franchise to convicted criminals serving a custodial sentence not exceeding 12 months. While I appreciate the work he has done in this area, we must remember that those who are imprisoned have been convicted of serious crimes and therefore lose their civic rights for the duration of their time in prison. In December 2018, the Council of Europe’s Committee of Ministers upheld the previous Government’s ban on voting for convicted prisoners in jail. This judgment extends across the UK, and the ECHR consideration is now closed.

Turn 101 · Speaker unlabelled

This brings me to Amendment 45 in the name of my noble friend Lord Fuller, which would retain individual voter registration for EU and Commonwealth citizens. Automatic voter registration would risk relying on outdated entries on other databases, which could mean that EU and Commonwealth citizens who are residents but not eligible to vote could be registered. Such instances would not be minor mishaps but would undermine the rules of our electoral system. It could also lead to unsolicited poll cards being sent to those households. Can the Minister say what assessment has been made of these specific risks?

Turn 102 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 277 in my name and that of my noble friend Lord Jamieson would require the Government to publish an independent review of the steps necessary to avoid non­qualifying EU or Commonwealth voters being incorrectly automatically added to the electoral roll. The Government may have done their homework already but if so, we would like to see it. Individual voter registration was implemented to stop fraudulent electoral registration, to ensure a more accurate register with fewer errors, and to remove the outdated concept that only a “head of household” could decide who should be on the electoral roll. That is why we believe individual voter registration should be retained as a whole.

Turn 103 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, the remaining amendments in my name and that of my noble friend Lord Jamieson seek to strengthen the provisions in the Bill as drafted, probing what checks and processes will be in place to ensure someone is definitely of voting age before registering them. Amendment 55 also probes how individuals will be notified that they are registered, and Amendments 56 and 57 probe how electoral registration records are kept and handled once the officer responsible is no longer in post. These are important issues concerning people’s data, as well as the accurate maintenance of electoral registers. I hope the Minister can provide some clarity and reassurance on these points, and I look forward to her response.
