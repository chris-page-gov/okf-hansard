Wales: Constitutional and Policy Divergence

Lords · 2026-11-04 · Turns 17–23 of 23 in this section.

Turn 17 · Lord Katz (Lab)

I agree with my noble friend. As I have previously said, we want to see full regional empowerment and power passed down not just from Westminster to Cardiff Bay but through there to reach all parts of Wales. On the specifics, we are supporting the Welsh Government in investing across the whole of Wales. I point to the Gwyndod investment in Ynys Môn for nuclear energy, and urge Plaid Cymru to be clear about how it supports the ambitions we have for nuclear energy to provide both energy security and lots of good, high-skilled jobs in Wales and across the rest of the UK. We are also talking about improving transport links through a new station in Deeside. The UK Government are supporting lots of investment in Wales, but we also want to see the Welsh Government supporting regional empowerment throughout the whole country of Wales.

Turn 18 · The Earl of Kinnoull (CB)

My Lords, intergovernmental relations are set out in a quarterly report. In the first six months of this year, the Northern Ireland Executive had 29 interministerial group meetings, the Scottish Government had 23 and the Welsh Government had only 21. When one looks by department, one sees that the Wales Office managed to have only one in the first six months. The interministerial group structure is one of the key things for making our United Kingdom work properly. Does the Minister feel satisfied with the number of meetings that I have just described? What plans are there to increase that number for Wales?

Turn 19 · Lord Katz (Lab)

I thank the noble Earl for that observation; I am not keeping quite as close a tally as he is on the number of meetings. I point out that, just this weekend, we had the first meeting of the new National Economic Council, and it was positive to see the Prime Minister convene it. He made a very clear offer to the leaders of all the devolved Governments to put politics aside and to work in partnership with the UK Government. That is the spirit in which he wishes to work with the Heads of the devolved Governments; I hope that it will be taken up by them in the same spirit, and I am sure it will be.

Turn 20 · Baroness Bloomfield of Hinton Waldrist (Con)

My Lords, both the Minister and the noble Baroness, Lady Wilcox, whom I congratulate on raising this issue, have spoken about outcomes from devolution. The Minister will understand how personally upsetting I find it to see Wales falling behind in so many devolved areas. The latest PISA results were shockingly bad, highlighting the challenges facing Welsh education, yet the Welsh Government continue to seek further powers, including the devolution of law and policing. Will the Minister rule out further constitutional divergence, where it risks undermining the integrity of our United Kingdom? A quick, one-word answer will suffice.

Turn 23 · Lord Katz (Lab)

Personally, I get advice from officials in the Wales Office. Part of the reason the Prime Minister is convening the National Economic Council is to have frank, open and positive conversations with the leaders of the devolved Governments, including the Plaid Cymru Administration in Cardiff, to ensure that we have an open dialogue about Wales’s challenges. They need to come to the table—I am sure they will—in that spirit of openness and with a willingness to work in partnership on what matters to people in Wales, which I would say is improving public services.
