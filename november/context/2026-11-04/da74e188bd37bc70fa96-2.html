Turn 12 · Baroness Humphreys (LD)

As the Minister already said, devolution in Wales has given us the ability to do things differently from the rest of the UK if we feel it meets the needs of the people of Wales. That inevitably means divergence. Wales needs to move on and to travel further on its devolution journey. We face a number of choices highlighted in the Senedd Commission’s report: the status quo, Plaid’s independence or the Lib Dem preference for a federal future. What steps do the Government intend to take to progress further powers to Wales?

Turn 13 · Lord Katz (Lab)

I agree with the sentiment that the noble Baroness has expressed. Last week at the National Economic Council, the Prime Minister set out an offer on empowering local regions to deliver good growth and thus develop better public services to prevent Wales maintaining its position as highly centralised. This involves all sorts of things, such as devolving more employment support on the condition that it reaches down into Wales’s regions and communities and exploring how we can improve city region deals. Constitutionally, we need to carry on talking respectfully to all our partners in Governments across the United Kingdom. The Prime Minister is looking forward to Cardiff hosting the next meeting of the National Economic Council next spring. I am sure that these discussions will continue.

Turn 14 · Baroness Goldie (Con)

My Lords, the noble Baroness, Lady Wilcox, is absolutely right in advancing the test, as she does, of the quality of public services delivered. The experience in Scotland is sad and instructive. After 21 years of SNP incumbency, we have had a relentless prosecution of the case for independence—rejected by the Scottish electorate—and a cataclysmic decline in public services, while being the highest-taxed part of the United Kingdom. Can the Minister reassure this House that his Government will take steps to ensure that Administrations in the devolved parts of this nation are concentrating exclusively on the services they are supposed to deliver for their electorates?
