Turn 4 · The Minister of State, Department for Work and Pensions (Baroness Sherlock) (Lab)

My Lords, this Government are spending tens of billions of pounds on housing support each year. We are also investing over £40 billion over 10 years in the social and affordable homes programme. We know the causes of homelessness are complex, both personally and structurally. My department is working with MHCLG on the national plan to end homelessness. The Government’s investment in homelessness and rough sleeping services is now over £4 billion over the next three years, building on the actions set out in that strategy.

Turn 5 · Baroness Lister of Burtersett (Lab)

My Lords, poverty and housing charities and the LGA are very disappointed that the local housing allowance freeze, made under austerity, was not lifted in the Budget, despite the Government’s welcome commitment to tackling homelessness and child poverty. The shortfall for low-income private renters has reached an all-time high, with consequences for poverty, destitution and homelessness, and the need for temporary accommodation and food banks. The Prime Minister himself told the Commons recently: “The freeze does not save money, because it just creates an unfunded pressure in council budgets”.—[Official Report, Commons, 1/9/26; col. 63.] Will the Government therefore now consider classifying the LHA as preventive spending, with a view to restoring its original annual uprating from April?

Turn 7 · Baroness Teather (LD)

My Lords, LHA levels have now fallen so far behind market rent levels that fewer than 2% of properties are affordable, leaving hundreds of thousands of families stuck—stuck on social housing waiting lists with no chance of housing; stuck in temporary accommodation, unable to move on; and stuck while other homeless families wait for that space to be freed for them. Can the Minister not see that solving the homelessness crisis requires us first to unstick LHA levels?
