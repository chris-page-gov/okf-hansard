Turn 39 · Speaker unlabelled

Such events are unique, by their nature—often, they are once in a generation. Indeed, the reason we have the Bill is the unique nature of the events. If they happened year in, year out, we would have a very different Bill. It concerns a rugby world cup, a football Euros, an Olympics. By their nature, they do not come around all the time. It is farcical for the Government to maintain the premise that normal industrial relations would be constrained by preventing strike action during events that are so out of the ordinary. That is not in the nature of routine industrial actions, yet that appeared to be the Government’s case in the House of Lords.

Turn 40 · Speaker unlabelled

We had effective scrutiny in the House of Lords, with sensible amendments tabled, but the Government’s argument was, first, that this was not specifically in their manifesto—but we are talking about a unique set of circumstances—and, secondly, that it would somehow constrain the trade unions and normal industrial relations. But the very reason for the amendment is that strike action taking place during a global sporting event that is being held in the UK would not be business as usual. That would not be normal industrial relations; it would be a trade union trying to exploit that unique opportunity for leverage. That would not be in keeping with the spirit of the Bill.
