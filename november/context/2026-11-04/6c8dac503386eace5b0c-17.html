My Lords, I support my noble friend Lord Fuller’s Amendments 34, 35, 36 and 38, which would require non-partisan teaching and non-party political posters, which would require balanced hustings, with all the candidates invited and with proper notice. Indeed, the law requires schools to “prohibit the promotion of partisan political views” and “take steps to ensure the balanced presentation of … views”. Turn 134 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 38 addresses the conduct of election hustings in schools. Such events can provide a valuable opportunity for young people to hear directly from candidates and question them about their policies, but they must be organised fairly. Giving one party privileged access to young voters would undermine the very purpose of political education. Will the Minister confirm that schools organising election hustings should provide reasonable notice and a fair opportunity for all candidates to participate? Also, how will these principles be reflected in government guidance?

Turn 135 · Speaker unlabelled

The amendments in this group raise crucial questions about how we prepare young people to participate in our democracy. If the Government are determined to extend the franchise to 16 and 17 year-olds, they must ensure that those young people have meaningful access to accurate information, a balanced political education and the opportunity to hear competing political views. Political neutrality in schools must be protected but cannot be a barrier to open discussion. I also agree with the point made by both the noble Lord, Lord Pack, and my noble friend Lord Fuller about online age verification. Being on the electoral register could be a helpful mechanism. I hope the Minister will address the practical concerns raised in this group and explain how the Government intend to ensure that younger voters can exercise their democratic rights independently and with confidence.
