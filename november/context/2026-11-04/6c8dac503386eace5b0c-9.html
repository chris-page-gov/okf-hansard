Turn 70 · Speaker unlabelled

The case for change does not rest on that; it rests on the constitutional reality of what it is to be a nation state. A nation is not the same as the people who happen to be standing on these islands at any given moment; a nation is a political community of citizens who, in theory, have specific rights and responsibilities and have made particular commitments to stick by each other. One of those rights is to help choose the Government. Non-citizens have not made the same commitments—for one, they can always leave the country if things get bad—so they should not have the same political rights, in my humble opinion, as those who are citizens. If they want them, they should become citizens, and if that is too high a price to pay then it is entirely correct they do not get the right to choose the Government.

Turn 71 · Speaker unlabelled

It is time to tidy all this up and join the international norm. That is what we need to debate and what my amendments seek to do. Only British and Irish citizens should have the right to vote in national elections. Only those citizens should have the right to stand in elections. If those standing for election are citizens of other countries too, perhaps electors should know that, and we will come to that in later amendments. It is time to bring reason, fairness and equality into our electoral arrangements and, most importantly, to bring the rules into line with what most people in this country already think they are. The time is coming, and I beg to move.
