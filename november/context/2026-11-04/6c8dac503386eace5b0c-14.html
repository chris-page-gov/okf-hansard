Turn 108 · Speaker unlabelled

Importantly, the Electoral Commission is already required to carry out an independent evaluation of those pilots. The Government will publish the commission’s findings, including its assessment of whether the pilots achieved their stated objectives. The specific detail of the pilots will be set out in secondary legislation and scrutinised in the usual way. Furthermore, electoral registration officers will continue to apply their knowledge and judgment to assess eligibility before they send someone a notice that they will be registered to vote. I hope this provides the reassurance that appropriate safeguards, testing and independent scrutiny are already built into the approach we are taking.

Turn 109 · Lord Hayward (Con)

Could the Minister further consider her comments on the point I raised? The Minister referred quite rightly to legislation that is now in effect that is some 40 or 50 years old. In those days, the vast majority of people did not have a passport, a driving licence with a picture on it or virtually any other form of proof of identity. Therefore “appearing to be” was a judgment that one made in those days. We have introduced voter ID and all the associated requirements with it. Given that this legislation intends to modernise and bring up to date our election law, could I please ask that the Minister give that further consideration?
