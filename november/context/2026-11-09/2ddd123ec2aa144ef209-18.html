Turn 144 · Speaker unlabelled

Research has made huge steps towards the screening programme, and I am delighted that the Government are backing the science behind it. At the APPG on Prostate Cancer meeting 10 days ago, we again heard how badly Black men have been underrepresented in clinical research on prostate cancer. Recent analysis by Prostate Cancer UK has found that, within the available literature, Black men typically comprised only a small percentage of prostate cancer trial participants, despite the fact that they represent a substantially large proportion of men affected by prostate cancer. If we want evidence that works for Black men, Black men must take part in the trials that generate it. I pay tribute to TRANSFORM for committing that at least one in 10 of the men it invites will be Black.
