Turn 41 · Speaker unlabelled

Taken together, these amendments would establish a fairly straightforward framework. Before a direction is issued, there should be proper consideration of its necessity and consequences. When it is issued, there should be transparency about the reasons and responsibilities involved. Afterwards, there should be accountability on what it actually achieved. I do not think that is an unreasonable set of expectations; indeed, it is a highly desirable part of making the new relationship between Ministers and integrated care boards work effectively. I would be grateful if the Minister could use her response to explain a little more about how the Government envisage these arrangements operating in practice. I beg to move.

Turn 45 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendments 37 and 38, I assure noble Lords that placing a threshold on when the Secretary of State can use the power referred to here is not necessary. This is because, as I and other noble Lords have mentioned a number of times, the Government are committed to devolving power to ICBs and—I am happy to be requoted on this—we expect that interventions on the operation of individual ICBs will be very sparing. As noble Lords have rightly identified, and as I said, micromanagement is not the intention or the practice that we seek.

Turn 46 · Speaker unlabelled

As I have touched on previously, we expect directions to be used in different circumstances, but Ministers have a public law duty to act reasonably—it is worth reminding ourselves of that—and there is an expectation that they should consider whether a direction is necessary or proportionate.

Turn 48 · Speaker unlabelled

Directions are likely to have varying levels of detail in them. A direction relating to a service specification is likely to be highly detailed; a higher-level planning direction I would expect to be less so. It is right that we do not take one single approach in the law. The inclusion of a consultation process on this power, as suggested by the noble Lord, Lord Kamall, in his amendment, as spoken to by the noble Earl, Lord Howe, would remove the advantage of being nimble that will allow Ministers to react to changing circumstances.
