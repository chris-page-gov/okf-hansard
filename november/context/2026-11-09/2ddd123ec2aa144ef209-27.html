Turn 209 · Speaker unlabelled

Two things need to happen, I suggest, before this aim can be brought to fullest fruition. The first is that patients need to adopt a different mindset about which local healthcare professionals are most capable of meeting their day-to-day needs. That requires a co-ordinated and extended programme of information disseminated both publicly and at an individual patient level. The second thing that needs to happen is for pharmacists to access more routinely than they do at the moment patients’ summary healthcare records held at general practice level, for without such access they are not able to deploy their professional skills to the optimum extent. The barriers to this happening do not need spelling out—the most obvious is patient consent and systems to facilitate it—but it is doable, and I have seen it in action.

Turn 210 · Speaker unlabelled

That leads on to giving thought to how pharmacists can be routinely involved in the commissioning of primary care services, which, in the future, will fall as a matter of course to ICBs. I know that a lot has been going on in the department and in NHS England to take forward work in this area, and it would be helpful to hear from the Minister a little more about it. When we consider continuity of care, we must come back to the consistent finding that applies across the piece, which the noble Baroness, Lady Gerada, has experienced as a GP: namely, the better a healthcare professional knows their patient and their patient’s circumstances, the greater the chance of early intervention, the more involved a patient is likely to be in their own care and the better the likely outcome.
