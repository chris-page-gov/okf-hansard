Turn 49 · Speaker unlabelled

To respond to the point raised by the noble Lord, Lord Scriven, about publishing directions as soon as reasonably practicable, we certainly expect that in most circumstances this will be as soon as possible after the direction is issued. In response to the point that if reasons are required for a failing board, why not for every board, the reasons for a direction will be covered in meeting the responsibilities to commission the health service. I add that the Secretary of State remains responsible when and if an ICB follows the Secretary of State direction. In the instance that outcomes get worse, that would be the responsibility of the Secretary of State.

Turn 50 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 86, ICB annual reports should already provide an accurate picture of risks and financial concerns, and regional oversight teams monitor these through their review of draft and final annual report submissions, internal audit reports and other intelligence. Regional oversight teams regularly review any risks to financial sustainability and operational capacity as part of routine oversight, increasing scrutiny where risk is escalating. This means that any additional information included in the annual report, as is being asked for, would be unlikely to provide further benefit in managing risks or impacts.

Turn 53 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

There is no need for the noble Baroness to apologise; that is what we are seeking to do. The point here is that we are, by definition, confining our discussions to when things are going wrong or there are concerns. It is important to state that all the changes we are making and all the frameworks that are in place—the whole suite of measures—are there to ensure patient safety, the right outcomes and the right service to patients. In this discussion there is inevitably a greater focus on when things go wrong, but we are trying to ensure that things do not go wrong.
