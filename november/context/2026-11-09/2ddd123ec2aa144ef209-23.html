Turn 177 · Speaker unlabelled

Of course, I must remind your Lordships, as I so often do, that the bulk of social care relies heavily on the support of unpaid carers, who far outnumber paid social care staff, probably by as much as two to one. I commend the Government for the steps they are taking to recognise and support those unpaid carers. I feel that the vision and focus that we hope to have for the future of social care is the way to make our health service and social care services not only not overwhelmed but more efficient. Gaining equal status between them would be transformational for social care workers and, most of all, for those who use social care.

Turn 181 · Speaker unlabelled

I appreciate that the Minister will point to the ongoing work of the noble Baroness, Lady Casey, and the independent commission and urge noble Lords to wait a little longer. The reality is that people are already making decisions in anticipation of these changes. Families are considering how they will fund care for elderly relatives. Local authorities are planning their future services. Care providers are making decisions about investment, recruitment and long-term viability. Those working in the sector are worried about what these reforms might mean for their livelihoods. All they want is a bit more clarity.

Turn 182 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 180 is not intended to prejudge the findings of the Casey commission, nor to prescribe precisely what a national care service ought to look like. It is simply an attempt to nudge the Government to provide Parliament, the sector and the public with a clear account of what they are proposing. Hopefully, these questions might help the Government, as they develop their thinking, to think about what they have not thought about and what they need to think about as they come up with a plan.
