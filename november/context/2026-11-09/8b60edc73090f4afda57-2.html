Turn 9 · Lord Katz (Lab)

We want to ensure that people across the world, including in England, can access adequate supplies of water.

Turn 11 · Lord Katz (Lab)

I agree with the noble Baroness that it is important not just to invest in long-term infrastructure—reservoirs—but to make sure that we do the day-to-day work, whatever the conditions of drought or otherwise, to improve water infrastructure. She will be aware that the Government have set an interim target of a 30% reduction in leakage by 2032 from the 2017-18 baseline. We expect Ofwat to challenge water companies across the industry to halve leakage by 2050 compared to 2017-18 levels. That is one of the Government’s targets for Ofwat. Yes, there will be more of this in the forthcoming water Bill; I could say to the noble Baroness “when parliamentary time allows”, but I think she will be aware that this is right at the top of the Government’s priority list. Water legislation was announced in the King’s Speech, so we expect it in due course.

Turn 15 · Lord Katz (Lab)

I thank the noble Lord for his question. His point about the potential for nature-based solutions to water conservation is part of a wider picture. Yes, we have to make long-term investment in reservoirs and ensure that farmers find it easier and quicker to create their own abstraction on on-farm reservoirs, but there are nature-based solutions too. That is one of the reasons why we expect water companies to plan for one-in-500-year drought. We have to make sure that they prepare for all eventualities, and some of the smaller fixes that we find in nature-based solutions are quick wins that all water companies should promote.
