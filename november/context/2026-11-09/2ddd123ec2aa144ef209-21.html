Turn 161 · Baroness Finlay of Llandaff (CB)

My Lords, I am most grateful to the Minister for her comprehensive response to the amendments, including mine. Can she assure us that the data that she spoke about will now be supplied directly to OECD as well, so that we will be able to resume international comparisons, and that it will include quality of life data as well as simply survival data, because survival data itself may be misleading? In among the quality of life, of course, there is the impact of the family reported outcome measures as well, which can go along with that and be very informative. Unless all the data, including genomics data, is linked to demographic data, we will not get much further in untangling some of the underlying causes, which may be environmental, of the increase in malignancies that we see in the younger population. There has been an upward creep, but it is definitely there.

Turn 163 · Baroness Pidgeon (LD)

I thank noble Lords for the quality of the debate on this group. I particularly thank the noble Baroness, Lady Ritchie, for sharing such a personal story earlier. I am glad to hear that the Government are committed to improving cancer outcomes, but I still have questions. Given that it was in the 2022 Act, and part of that Bill, where is the annual mandate that was set out for NHS England going to sit now? Perhaps the Minister can write to noble Lords setting out how that will not be lost, just to assure us. It is not just noble Lords here who are concerned; the sector and charities are very concerned that it will be lost. It was in a Bill before, so let us try to understand why the Government do not think it is needed now. But given the response we have had and the debate, I beg leave to withdraw my amendment.
