Turn 10 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

Action 4 commits to testing patient power payments, and we are prioritising gynaecology services as a test. That will mean that if a woman’s feedback, which will be actively sought, is not what it should be based on their experience—and that includes pain—then payments can be withheld and invested to improve the situation. I am sure the House appreciates that often it is finance which drives change, and this is just one of our new ways of doing that.

Turn 12 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I agree with that. That is why, in the 10-year health plan and in our manifesto commitments, we are working towards the commitment to halve the gap in healthy life expectancy between the richest and the poorest regions, and setting a minimum standard of healthy life expectancy of 61 years in all areas. That is consistent with halving the gap, and means that we will give attention to all regions of the country, particularly those that need it most.

Turn 16 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I certainly agree. Looking back at the 2022 women’s health strategy, 84% of women experienced not being listened to by a healthcare professional. As I have already stated, that is totally unacceptable. A third of those women said that this had harmed their mental well-being. This is a very serious matter and that is why we are strengthening professional education across a range of conditions, testing out the patient power payments and introducing patient reported-experience measures. All those should drive improvements of care over the next five years, some more immediately than others.
