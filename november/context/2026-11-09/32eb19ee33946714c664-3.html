Turn 17 · The Earl of Effingham (Con)

My Lords, seven leading members of the WTO have challenged the Government’s steel curbs, which cut tariff-free quotas and imposed 50% tariffs. Separately, the House of Lords International Agreements Committee, chaired by the noble and learned Lord, Lord Goldsmith, from the Government Benches, wrote that the UK-US economic prosperity deal was “not consistent with WTO rules”. How can this Government credibly claim to champion rules-based trade when the evidence suggests the opposite?

Turn 19 · Baroness Bennett of Manor Castle (GP)

My Lords, the current WTO framework is seen by many as grossly preferential to the largest and richest nations. Least developed countries account for only about 1.1% of global exports, far below the 2.2% target set for 2030 under the Doha programme. Will the Government work with the global South to support its interest, which is, of course, in the interest of the security of us all?

Turn 20 · Lord Leong (Lab)

Yes, of course the Government will work with the global South, and any other countries for that matter, to resolve the current stalemate in the WTO. The dispute settlement appellate court is dysfunctional and not working, but meanwhile we have plurilateral, multilateral and bilateral arrangements with various members. There are 61 members in the MPIA, and we are working with them to resolve whatever disputes we currently have among members. We have to find a way to resolve the dispute settlement. Until and unless we find that, we have to carry on with these plurilateral arrangements.
