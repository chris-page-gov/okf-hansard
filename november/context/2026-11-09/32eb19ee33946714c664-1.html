Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Lord Leong) (Lab)

My Lords, the WTO has failed to keep pace with trading realities and must reform. The UK has communicated our vision for a WTO that is more relevant, flexible and accessible. We are actively shaping the reform debate. Our proposals include ideas to stimulate discussion on how to level the playing field in trade, such as improved transparency and discipline on harmful trading practices, and to advance more flexible arrangements among subsets of members to make the WTO more responsive.

Turn 5 · Lord Lamont of Lerwick (Con)

My Lords, I thank the Minister for that full and interesting reply. The WTO has been partially paralysed by disputes between China and the United States and by the United States withdrawing from part of its workings. Can the Minister tell the House whether the interim workaround measures, which were led by Canada and Australia and in which we participated, have helped to offset the effect of America’s withdrawal from the institution? Despite what the Minister said about transparency, will he confirm that, if we want to have growth in this country, in a world in which supply chains are politicised and there are tariff wars, it is extremely important that we do everything in our power to support a rules-based trading system?

Turn 6 · Lord Leong (Lab)

I agree with the noble Lord that we have to abide by the rules-based trading system. As he knows, Canada, Australia and the United Kingdom are among the members using the WTO’s interim appeals arrangement, which currently has 61 members. It allows participating countries to resolve disputes without an appeal disappearing into the void while the appellate body remains paralysed. We continue to press for a functioning dispute settlement system that is accessible to every WTO member country, whether big or small, advanced or developing.

Turn 8 · Lord Leong (Lab)

My Lords, the noble Lord makes a good point about trade agreements that we have signed with various countries, such as the CPTPP and the India free trade deal. Through our current dialogue, whether with the EU or the CPTPP, we are building support among members to reform the WTO. As Minister for Small Business and Enterprise, I speak to trade organisations and small businesses up and down the country and encourage them to take advantage of all the free trade agreements that we have. Governments can sign trade agreements with every other country, but it is meaningless if businesses do not take advantage of them. My ministerial colleagues and I are constantly reminding businesses to take advantage of the opportunities that we have created with these free trade agreements.
