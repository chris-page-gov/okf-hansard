Turn 75 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 51 would go further still. It would require NHS services to be provided by public bodies and exclude services contracted to private bodies. How would that apply to GP partnerships and other independent organisations that are delivering NHS care? Before contemplating such a requirement, we would need a clear account of its scope and consequences and of how patients’ access to established services would be protected. I have seen independent provision ruled out and services subsequently reduced, only for that capacity to be wanted again a few years later. By then, the service has gone and the team has dispersed. Once a service has closed and its team has dispersed, rebuilding it takes time. We cannot assume that the staff and facilities will still be available if that capacity is needed again.

Turn 76 · Speaker unlabelled

We must also remember the nurses, allied health professionals and carers who deliver this care. Their compassion and professionalism are no less because their employer is an independent provider. We should value their contribution and avoid decisions that risk losing their skills from the services that patients depend upon. For those reasons, I cannot support Amendments 33, 36 and 51. However, that does not diminish the need for rigorous scrutiny of independent provision. Providers must meet demanding standards, demonstrate value for public money and be held accountable for their performance. Decisions must also support the long-term strength and sustainability of the NHS.

Turn 77 · Speaker unlabelled

The same requirement for evidence and accountability should apply to the ministerial direct discretion proposed in Clause 10. If the Government retain this exception, will the Minister commit to publishing criteria and an assessment of the consequences for access, waiting times, quality, safety, workforce and value for money? Will affected patients, commissioners and providers be consulted? Will the reasons for a decision be published? How will a decision be reviewed if its expected benefits fail to materialise? Parliament surely must be able to examine the justification for the change and its effects.
