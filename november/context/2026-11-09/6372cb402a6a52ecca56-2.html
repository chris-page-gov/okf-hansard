Turn 15 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

Yes, I absolutely agree that my noble friend has raised a worthwhile and important debate. At the heart of this is the fact that rape myths are pervasive, widespread and widely damaging in our society. That is what we need to do something about. The Government want to ensure—I am sure my noble friend agrees—that they are not used to distort criminal trials and retraumatise victims. Victims should never feel that they are on trial. We know we need to do better on ensuring that victims of rape and sexual abuse feel safe to seek justice. I pay tribute to my noble friend Lady Levitt’s work at the Crown Prosecution Service to achieve that. But this is ongoing work, and of course the Government will continue to think about it.

Turn 16 · Lord Marks of Henley-on-Thames (LD)

My Lords, worryingly and counterintuitively, young people seem unduly susceptible to rape myths. End Violence Against Women found, for example, that only half of 18 to 24 year-olds recognise that rape is still rape even if the victim does not physically fight back, and that only 42% of that age group realise that a relationship or marriage does not mean assumed consent to sex, as against 87% of over-65s. Does this not underline the need not only for clear directions to jurors, with which I fully agree, but for more and better sex and relationship education in school?
