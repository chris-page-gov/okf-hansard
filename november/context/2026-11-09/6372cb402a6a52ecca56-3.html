Turn 17 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

Yes, it does. The noble Lord’s points about the attitudes of young people are well known and deeply concerning, and they certainly need to be addressed through relationship and sex education. Turn 21 · Lord Burnett of Maldon (CB)

I am very grateful to the noble Viscount for giving way. Will the Minister agree with me, at least in respect of the following short propositions? First, the Court of Appeal has now, for almost two decades, been giving judgments about rape myths and encouraging judges to give directions in appropriate cases. Secondly, it would be a very good idea, before this matter is debated later in Committee, were everybody to read the Crown Court Compendium dealing with this topic. It is comprehensive, detailed and instructive. Thirdly, picking up the point made by the noble and learned Lord, Lord Keen, will the Minister agree that it is unprecedented for Parliament to seek to instruct judges on how they should direct juries, as opposed to laying down the substantive law, and that to do so would be an unacceptable intrusion upon judicial independence?

Turn 24 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

I think I have made it clear, but I am happy to make it clear again, that the principle of judicial independence in these cases—in all cases, indeed—is a fundamental building block of the rule of law. We are not seeking to undermine that in any way. As I have said, independent judges already can and do give direction concerning rape myths; we do not want these myths to be weaponised, but we also do not want that to compromise the independence of judges.
