Turn 17 · Lord Katz (Lab)

The right reverend Prelate may be aware that the Minister for Water and Flooding issued a Statement on data centres last month, on 28 October, which set out a clear approach to managing water demand as the sector grows. Current evidence suggests that data centres account for quite a small proportion of water demand, estimated at less than 0.2% of the non-household sector in England, and that many facilities already use highly water-efficient cooling technology. Future growth could place extra pressure on local water resources, however, so we expect data centres to minimise water demand where they can and consider alternative sources before using additional public water supply.

Turn 18 · Baroness Andrews (Lab)

My Lords, west Sussex is one of the areas that are very badly stressed, as the Minister will know. Ardingly reservoir has 20% of its normal infill. We have not built a new reservoir since 1992—interestingly, coinciding closely with the privatisation of water—and reservoirs take 10 years to build. What realistically can be done in the next 10 years, particularly in areas where we predict persistent drought?

Turn 19 · Lord Katz (Lab)

We have to reform the sector, as the Government set out in response to the Cunliffe report. We need more streamlined, efficient regulation to ensure that water companies do the job that we have been talking about this afternoon—reducing leakage—as well as longer-term investment and promotion of better and wiser water usage by everybody, be they businesses, such as data centres, or households. That is why we are committed to setting up new, innovative responses, such as regional water councils, to ensure that water companies perform for the public and for businesses and not just for themselves.

Turn 21 · Lord Katz (Lab)

In 2024-25, the Government saw a 12% reduction in the total leakage reported by water companies. To sustain and build on this trajectory, we are investing £720 million through the current Ofwat price review period in smart technology, including meters, which will help to identify and reduce leakage. We are enabling increased rollout of smart meters via the smart water meter delivery group. We continue to hold water companies to account to ensure that they are delivering on their leakage reduction targets, rolling out more things such as smart metering and levying financial penalties if companies fail to meet these targets.
