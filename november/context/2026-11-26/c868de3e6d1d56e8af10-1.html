Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Blair McDougall)

We are committed to strengthening the UK’s critical minerals refining capability, as outlined in the UK critical minerals strategy. Our critical minerals accelerator programme offers up to £25 million to support innovative projects across extraction, processing and recycling. Separately, through the £4 billion DRIVE35 programme, BIST recently made an £18.3 million offer in principle to Tees Valley Lithium to support domestic lithium refining. Phase 1 of the refining project could represent nearly half of the UK’s 2035 domestic lithium capacity ambition.

Turn 4 · Blair McDougall

I will absolutely ensure that it is BIST driving forward the initiatives that my hon. Friend raises, not least because she and the other Cornish Labour MPs are utterly relentless in making the case that we should do so. Harnessing our growing competitive advantage in processing and recycling critical minerals and leveraging our mineral wealth are core not only to our vision for critical minerals, but to our wider industrial strategy. We have strong established refining capabilities, particularly in platinum group metals and nickel; we want to explore opportunities for future midstream capabilities, particularly in tungsten, tin and lithium, and we are working with industry and with hon. Members to bring that forward. I welcome the work that my hon. Friend has done to bring some of those investors to me personally.

Turn 6 · Peter Fortune (Bromley and Biggin Hill) (Con)

One of the main issues holding back investment in the UK chemicals sector is the lack of resolution of the issues with UK REACH—regulation on the registration, evaluation, authorisation and restriction of chemicals—registration, which significantly impacts industries downstream, including the refinement of critical minerals. Current plans from the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs would cost UK businesses some £600 million to create an overtly detailed database of chemical substances, and that unnecessary cost will lead to the loss of investment, product availability for downstream manufacturers and vital jobs across the country. What is the Business Department doing to support business in changing DEFRA’s approach in this important area?

Turn 7 · Blair McDougall

The hon. Gentleman is right to raise this issue. As a former Minister for regulation, I am aware of the challenges that both good and bad regulation can present. We are talking to chemicals and minerals industries and to our colleagues in DEFRA about this issue. In addition to the funding I described a moment ago as part of the critical minerals strategy, we have the critical chemicals resilience fund; as well as the regulatory work we are doing, we are trying to invest more widely in the sector’s resilience.
