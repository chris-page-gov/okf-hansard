Turn 7 · Alex Mayer (Dunstable and Leighton Buzzard) (Lab)

I join the Chair of the Transport Committee in thanking the brilliant officials who worked so hard to help us with the report. At its heart, this issue is about a trade-off between economic growth and the climate, but with the new concept of critical national growth infrastructure, the scales have been tipped so that the climate always loses. We have, on the one hand, the idea that the growth is so special and important that it is critical; on the other, we have Government-commissioned analysis saying that the growth is modest. Does my hon. Friend see an inherent contradiction in those two positions?

Turn 8 · Ruth Cadbury

As I have said, we are dealing with a concept that has never been discussed before. It is not as though we could say, “We have a concept. Let’s first talk about the concept and how it might be used, and then let’s use it in practice with this application.” We had never heard of the term “critical national growth infrastructure” until the draft HENPS was published, so it is very difficult to know how the process is supposed to be dealt with. There is a real fear, as many witnesses told us, as my hon. Friend knows, that all other issues might fall by the wayside in the decision making. To be fair, however, the Government have said that the proposal must comply with all four tests, so there is a contradiction there. The Government are going to have to decide what matters more: all four tests, or just growth?
