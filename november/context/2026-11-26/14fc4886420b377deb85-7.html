Turn 56 · Speaker unlabelled

It is also important to acknowledge that a lot has changed since the ringfencing regime was introduced. Our prudential regime is considerably stronger. Banks—this is topical for today—hold more capital, and their resilience is regularly tested through the Bank of England’s stress tests. We also now have a resolution regime, as the Skeoch review recognised, for managing bank failures. Major banks are required to maintain living wills for how their failure could be safely managed without recourse to public funds. That means that some of the restrictions imposed by the ringfencing regime are no longer needed, which is exactly why the Government are acting. The changes I am setting out will ensure that the regime remains proportionate and reflects developments in the wider framework, most notably the resolution regime.
