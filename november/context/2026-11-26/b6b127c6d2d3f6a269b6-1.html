Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Baroness Lloyd of Effra) (Lab)

My Lords, AI developers should keep their systems under safe control. We expect prompt, transparent reporting of unexpected behaviour. In respect of breach of systems, the Computer Misuse Act applies to AI developers, and they must not breach systems. If breaches happen, victims should be informed immediately. If personal data, such as NHS records, is accessed, the Information Commission should be informed within 72 hours. The cyber Bill strengthens protections for essential services such as NHS trusts. Regulated AI services must report significant loss of control incidents within 24 hours.

Turn 5 · Baroness Benjamin (LD)

My Lords, I thank the Minister for her Answer. The Government have said that AI developers must report a breach within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, but in Australia OpenAI did not become aware that its agent had broken into the Medicare system until two months later. OpenAI says that its agent broke in without being asked to do so. Would a similar intrusion into the NHS have to be reported? If an AI agent hacks the NHS system on its own, who would the Government hold criminally responsible? Also, the reporting duty covers only personal data that risks harming individuals. The cyber security and resilience Bill’s 24-hour rule applies to regulated organisations, not AI developers. Will the Government amend the Bill so that AI companies and their AI agents must report, too?

Turn 6 · Baroness Lloyd of Effra (Lab)

My Lords, as the noble Baroness says, the Bill sets out duties for essential services to manage their risks to their systems. Where an AI digital service, such as a cloud computing service, falls within scope, it will have to follow those reporting duties. This includes notifying regulators of significant loss of control incidents within 24 hours. While the Bill is not the right legislative framework to place duties on developers, where stronger protections are needed, we will act. Through recent amendments, the Bill will allow the Government to have a better understanding of significant loss of control incidents and how they are affecting essential services such as the NHS.
