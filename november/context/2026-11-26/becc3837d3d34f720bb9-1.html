Turn 2 · The Minister for Science, Innovation and Investment (Chris McDonald)

Good morning Mr Speaker. We are backing strategically important industries through our industrial strategy, with growth-driving sectors and frontier technologies underpinned by the strength of our foundational industries to deliver growth in every postcode. Through the Office for Investment we work directly with domestic and international investors to enable major projects to crowd in private capital. We are also tackling barriers to investment, from planning and energy costs to access to finance. Since the industrial strategy was launched, more than £390 billion of private investment commitments have been secured across those key sectors.

Turn 4 · Chris McDonald

The Government place a high priority on our critical industries through procurement, investment through access to capital with the British Business Bank and, as the hon. Lady mentioned, schemes such as the critical chemicals resilience fund and our £2.5 billion support for the steel industry. I am familiar with the situation with the Inovyn saltworks, which she raised with me last year when I was the Minister for industry. We had a productive meeting then, and I confirm that the Under-Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Blair McDougall) agrees with the opportunity for an additional meeting, and would be happy to meet the hon. Lady and INEOS.

Turn 8 · Julia Lopez (Hornchurch and Upminster) (Con)

I know that the sympathies of the House will be with the nearly 11,000 employees of Poundland who, as Christmas approaches, face an uncertain future. We do not think the solution to Poundland’s collapse is public sector investment into a business that, for all its importance to our high street, would not meet the threshold as strategically important. Nationalisation in this instance is a political sideshow and a trade in false hope, and I believe that the Government agree. Poundland employees need practical help, and industry—strategic or otherwise—needs a climate in which it can survive. What will the Government be doing to support the protection of employee pensions? When it comes to the future of town centres, and any industry, will the Government address the economic policies that are making it hard to survive and operate a viable business?
