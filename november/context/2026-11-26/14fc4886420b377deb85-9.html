Turn 65 · Speaker unlabelled

Ringfencing falls on five banks: Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest and Santander UK. Between them, they serve most households and most small businesses in the country. It creates real distortions. As my hon. Friend explained on Second Reading, a growing business can reach a size where it is pushed from the ringfenced bank to the non-ringfenced bank, simply because of where a regulatory line was drawn, at exactly the moment it needs a full range of banking services to expand.

Turn 66 · Speaker unlabelled

We are alone as a country. My hon. Friend the Member for Grantham and Bourne (Gareth Davies) said on Second Reading that New York does not impose ringfencing, nor does Amsterdam, Frankfurt or Paris. The European Union considered structural separation of its own, and never enacted it—something the European Union perhaps got right. I am not one for praising the European Union, oddly. I note that just now there is a motion in the House of Lords on closer UK-EU relations. I know the Government are fixated on dynamic alignment, so perhaps they will practise what they preach and follow the European Union’s example by removing ringfencing so that we can get our banking system on a competitive footing. Let us see. I just posit that, having noticed that debate is happening in the House of Lords. Perhaps the Minister will comment on whether there is value in that.
