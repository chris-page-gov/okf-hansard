Turn 25 · Baroness Bennett of Manor Castle (GP)

My Lords, I join the Front-Bench speakers in resoundingly welcoming this Statement, particularly its commendation of the campaigners who had to work so hard and so long to get us to this point. As the noble Baroness the Leader said, it should not have taken nearly this long. It is not very clear to me whether this plan covers only British servicemen and their families or others who served Britain. I note that, from 1957 to 1958, Fijian soldiers participated in nine British nuclear test explosions at Malden and Kiritimati—Christmas Island—in the Pacific, which is now part of the Republic of Kiribati. The Fijian Government have spoken of the tragic health consequences for those who participated in these tests and emphasised the continuing need for proper compensation from the British Government. Turn 27 · Lord John of Southwark (Lab)

My Lords, as I mentioned to my noble friend earlier today, I had an uncle who served in the RAF on Christmas Island, Brian Bush. If he were alive today, he would be 95, but sadly he died in 1993 aged 61 from bowel cancer. I have no idea whether that bowel cancer was caused by or contributed to by his service on Christmas Island, but can my noble friend say anything about how cases such as his could be brought into this inquiry so that there are learnings about the health of those who died 30, 40, or 50 years ago from unexpected cancers, and how his medical record will be considered in a meaningful way by the panel?

Turn 28 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

My Lords, we are currently working with Bishop James Jones on the terms of reference. There are a lot of issues here, but health is a crucial one, because we need to try to understand how this has filtered through several generations. My noble friend talks about his uncle who died a tragically young death, but there may be other members of the family who were affected—I do not know whether he had children. It is about the impact on families, as well as on individuals. I expect to have the terms of reference in January. That will be the time to examine that further. I am sure that campaigners will be raising all these issues, to ensure that the terms of reference cover the issues that affect them most.

Turn 29 · Baroness Bennett of Manor Castle (GP)

The Prime Minister said that when he was Health Secretary in 2009, he was misled by civil servants to say that blood test records were not available. Indeed, Tobias Ellwood, a Conservative MoD Minister, was told the same thing. What are the Government going to do to ensure that Ministers are not misled in a similar way in future? We have had so many crises like this where the information has had to be dragged out by campaigners over decades. How can we address the broader issue of the real situation not emerging from the Civil Service?
