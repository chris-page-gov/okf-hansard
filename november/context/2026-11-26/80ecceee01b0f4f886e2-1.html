Turn 4 · Catherine Atkinson (Derby North) (Lab)

I thank the Backbench Business Committee for making time for this statement. When I was training to be a barrister, I once brought a shopping bag full of medication bottles into a tribunal and lined them up, one by one. My client had been refused benefits after being told that he was not sick enough. I had asked him to bring his medication to our meeting at the Free Representation Unit, a charity that provides free legal representation. The bottles helped us to demonstrate the reality of his daily life, and we won his case. He already had the right to appeal. What made the difference was having someone who understood the rules and could help him make his case. Without that help, too many are left unable to challenge a wrong, and they go without support to which they are entitled. Turn 6 · Catherine Atkinson

The hon. Lady makes a powerful point. Often, AI options, as long as the information that they give is accurate, give people what they need to avoid complicated and expensive legal processes. For many, however, including those who are digitally excluded, that cannot be the only answer. The concern is that people do not have authoritative information online and cannot find the advice that they need in person, either. We should be working to ensure that they can do both.

Turn 7 · Helena Dollimore (Hastings and Rye) (Lab/Co-op)

May I thank the Chair of the Select Committee for that important statement about the importance of free legal advice for those who need it most, especially at a time when this Labour Government are working to expand many rights in law? The Renters Rights Act 2025 gives renters more rights; Awaab’s law gives tenants the right to action within 48 hours if they have black mould in their home; and there are new powers of community ownership. There are many brilliant local charities in my constituency, such as the Hastings Advice and Representation Centre and Citizens Advice 1066, working to help people with that advice. Luckily, they have benefited from Government funding. Will my hon. Friend join me in thanking the volunteers involved for that work, and all the solicitors working pro bono across the country, and in Hastings and Rye, who support people in this way?
