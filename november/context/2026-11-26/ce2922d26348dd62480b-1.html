Turn 2 · The Minister for the Future of Work (Kate Dearden)

The Government are committed to a sustainable post office network that serves communities all across the country. The Post Office determines whom it partners with to deliver that network, and our priority is ensuring that communities continue to have reasonable access as the network changes. That is why we maintain a minimum network of 11,500 branches, alongside six access criteria that ensure that 99% of the population remain within 3 miles of their nearest post office.

Turn 3 · Wera Hobhouse

TG Jones used to host Bath’s main post office under the retail partnership model favoured by the Post Office, but when the store closed, Bath residents and businesses—everybody—lost major services delivered by the post office. While a new location is being sought, in the run-up to Christmas Bath is still lacking major post office services. I have just heard about another possible closure in my constituency at a site operating under the same partnership model. Does the Minister recognise that the franchise model has been a failure, and will the Government reform the post office system to guarantee that stand-alone post offices are not beholden to the fortunes of a host retailer?

Turn 4 · Kate Dearden

I recognise the hon. Turn 5 · Dame Nia Griffith (Llanelli) (Lab)

The Post Office has left Kidwelly in my constituency without a post office since March, despite an experienced postmaster having been ready to take over for some months. It has refused even to provide a temporary mobile service, defining this fine medieval town with its splendid castle as a “rural area”. What can the Minister do to put pressure on the Post Office to speed up the so-called onboarding process, get Kidwelly post office opened as soon as possible and, in the meantime, provide a mobile service so that residents have the service they need and deserve in the run-up to Christmas?
