Mr Speaker, I would have asked to make an intervention, if that question had gone on any longer. Turn 15 · Jonathan Reynolds

There was a strange exchange with the Leader of the Opposition, in which she seemed to say that 0.5% was not very significant, but she had nothing to say about the 4% reduction in GDP in the long run caused by the original Brexit deal that the Conservatives achieved. This is not reliving those Brexit arguments, and I respect the Liberal Democrats’ position. The package is within our red lines. It is the single biggest growth measure that we will be asked to vote on in this Parliament. If the Conservative party or Reform opposes it, they will be opposing that growth, and instead backing the bureaucracy. This is a substantial intervention, and in the summit, and on “Made in Europe”, there has been significant progress for British manufacturers.
