Turn 11 · Jonathan Reynolds

The hon. Lady has probably heard me talk many times about my personal obsession with the record high industrial energy prices we inherited from the Conservative party, and the need to take action in that regard. A difference between businesses and households is that businesses tend to be on fixed-rate arrangements, but as she says they will be concerned about going into next year, when those arrangements may expire. For manufacturers, who are the most exposed to those pressures, the British industrial competitiveness scheme is open and will reduce energy bills for eligible businesses by a quarter. The deadline for that scheme is on Monday, so hon. Members should ensure businesses in their constituencies get their applications in. All of this is part of the need for a much more resilient energy system that is less exposed to fluctuations in the price of gas.

Turn 16 · Jonathan Reynolds

I can give the hon. Gentleman that assurance. The administration process will be run in the proper fashion, according to established rules. At the same time, in case they are unfamiliar with this process, I reassure the people who work for Poundland that they will get paid. While the stores continue to trade, payroll will be met. There are many different and contesting parts to this story, and I think he is hinting at part of that. He is right; the administrators will produce a report on the circumstances that have led to this set of events, including the directors and the process that has got us to this stage, but at the minute we should all try to reassure as many of our constituents as possible. There is still hope that we get a significant part of the business being purchased by a new owner out of this process.
