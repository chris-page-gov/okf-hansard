Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Blair McDougall)

The British industrial competitiveness scheme is reducing energy costs for thousands of manufacturers, including those in the advanced manufacturing sector that Rossendale and Darwen is renowned for. Alongside that, the Government have announced that we will introduce a comprehensive cost-control framework at spending review 2027, which will set budgets for components of energy costs, including policy costs, to drive down bills over the next 10 years. We have also given a commitment that no new levies will be introduced on energy bills before the spending review, unless they help to cut bills or are essential for security of supply.

Turn 3 · Andy MacNae

I thank the Minister for his response, and for all the work he is doing in this area. I think there is general consensus that high energy costs are the biggest barrier to reindustrialisation, and are driving deindustrialisation. BICS is welcome, but it applies to only 10,000 businesses; extending it to all manufacturing, as called for by industry, unions and many of us in this place, would give immediate and very welcome respite. Does the Minister agree that we need a more permanent approach to delivering competitive energy pricing—one that gives long-term confidence to invest and grow businesses in the UK—and that to deliver that, we must have a bold approach to removing transition and infrastructure costs from bills, and must redesign our energy market to eliminate excessive profiteering and rent-seeking?

Turn 4 · Blair McDougall

I thank my hon. Friend not just for his question, but for being such an advocate for reindustrialisation, on which he is one of the leading voices in this House. He is absolutely right that uncompetitive energy prices are a huge barrier to achieving our reindustrialisation ambitions. As I just said, we have an ambition to reduce our energy prices to European levels over 10 years. The electricity generator levy was increased to 55% in response to the high prices that generators are benefiting from because of the crisis in the middle east. He is absolutely right: this is a long-term problem that requires a long-term strategic approach.
