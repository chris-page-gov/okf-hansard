Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Ministry of Justice (Lord Lemos) (Lab)

My Lords, there is no legal concept of a defence of third-party consent. One person cannot consent to sexual activity on another person’s behalf. In law, a person consents only if they agree “by choice” and have “the freedom and capacity to make that choice”. While arguments concerning consent and reasonable belief in consent may be raised in individual cases, it is for the courts to consider them in accordance with the law. Yesterday the Prime Minister announced that the Government will launch a national public conversation next year on consent and abuses of power, seeking views on whether existing laws and protections go far enough.

Turn 6 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

My noble friend is right. The abuse suffered by victims and survivors of these appalling crimes is deeply distressing. Our thoughts are with all those affected. We pay tribute to the victims of these kinds of crime who have brought their cases to justice. Those who commit such offences cause profound harm. The law is clear: there is no legal concept of third-party consent and one person cannot consent to sexual activity on another person’s behalf. Where a victim is asleep or has been drugged without their consent, the law provides for strong protections, including presumptions against consent and reasonable belief in consent in certain circumstances. That is why the Government have announced a national conversation on consent and abuses of power.

Turn 7 · Lord Keen of Elie (Con)

My Lords, as the Minister implied, the law in this area is clear—albeit it is not always possible to stop the sort of evil conduct that the noble Lord, Lord Hain, has alluded to. Section 74 of the Sexual Offences Act 2003 makes it absolutely clear that consent can be given only by a person themselves, as long as they have the freedom and capacity to make the choice with regard to that consent. Does the Minister consider that the present law, albeit with the guidance alluded to, should be sufficient to make clear to those who are involved or tempted to be involved in such behaviour that they can never establish consent in a court of law?
