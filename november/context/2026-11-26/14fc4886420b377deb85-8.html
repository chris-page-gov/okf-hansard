Turn 61 · Speaker unlabelled

Since ringfencing was designed, we have built a completely different set of protections. We have a resolution regime that allows the Bank of England to take control of a failing bank and keep its critical services running over a weekend. We have bail-in, which means that when a bank fails, its shareholders and bondholders, not taxpayers, take the losses. We have a minimum requirement for own funds and eligible liabilities—a layer of debt that exists specifically to absorb losses and capitalise a failing bank. Banks hold far more and better-quality capital and liquidity than they did before 2008. Only last year, Parliament passed the Bank Resolution (Recapitalisation) Act 2025 to strengthen those arrangements further. We have moved on measurably from 2011, so we should now be thinking about the risks in 2026, and about the risks of doing nothing or not going far enough on ringfencing.
