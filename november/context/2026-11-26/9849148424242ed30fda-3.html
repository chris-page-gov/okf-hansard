Rape Trials: Third-party Consent

Lords · 2026-11-26 · Turns 17–20 of 20 in this section.

Hansard speaking order. A reviewed question or reply has its own link. Open Hansard for the full wording of any excerpt.

Turn 17 · Lord Garnier (Con)

My Lords, I am sure the whole House is entirely certain that there is no such thing as a third-party consent defence to rape, which is ludicrous. I am sure that having a national conversation is a wonderful idea, but surely the more direct answer is to get the MoJ to have a discussion with the Sentencing Council to make sure that those who pimp the women who are the victims of these terrible offences are given a sentence at the top end of the range—and, if the range is not long enough or high enough, to invite the Sentencing Council to recommend higher sentences.

Turn 20 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

I entirely agree with that. As the noble Lord knows, we put out new guidelines from the Department for Education on sex and relationship education in 2025, and these have been rolled out from September 2026. We want to see the emphasis on relationships, and we certainly want to see the emphasis on boys as well as girls. I take the noble Lord to another point, implied by his question, on the online space, about which we should be extremely concerned, for all sorts of reasons but particularly for its impact on the lives of young people. I want to make it clear from this Dispatch Box again that the Online Safety Act places legal duties on regulated service providers to protect users from illegal content and criminal activity.
