Turn 25 · Dame Diana Johnson

As I said, I have made a very careful note of the six points that my hon. Friend asked to be addressed. I will raise them directly with the cancer Minister to see what more we can do on each and every one of them, including his persuasive point about the panel. Throughout the cancer journey, we are determined to end inequalities in care. I again thank my hon. Friend the Member for Stockport for bringing this issue before the House. Our ambition is simple: to make equal access to high-quality cancer care the standard, not the exception. That is what the national cancer plan is about and that is what this Government are working to deliver now.

I am grateful to everyone who has contributed to the debate. The central message has been clear throughout: cancer does not discriminate, but cancer care too often does. Someone’s chances of survival, the speed of diagnosis and the support they receive should never depend on their postcode, income or other factors that they cannot control. We have heard about the stark inequalities that still persist. We owe it to every person living with cancer, every family who has lost someone and every dedicated NHS worker and volunteer to do better. I am very grateful to the workforce of my local hospital, Stepping Hill.
