Turn 2 · The Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Jonathan Reynolds)

Economic growth is a top priority for this Government. Despite the international pressures that I mentioned in my last answer, the performance and resilience of the UK economy has been strong. Whether it is our industrial strategy, unlocking key markets with our trade deals, our reform of the planning system, our record commitment to science and innovation or the big increase in public investment that comes from the new fiscal rules, the statistics are unequivocal that the economy is stronger today than when this Government came to power.

Turn 4 · Jonathan Reynolds

I agree wholeheartedly with what my hon. Friend puts forward. I believe that all economic growth is felt by our constituents, but at times in our history we have had differential performance in different parts of the UK, and people have not always felt that they have had the benefits of a high-performing economy. Her question hints at something that I believe is fundamental to the Government’s agenda: the devolution agenda, particularly fiscal devolution. For the first time, many parts of the country will have economic incentives to back growth, development and businesses in their area, and they will retain a share of the proceeds of that success. We have had some embryonic steps towards that in the last few years, but what this Government are doing is a major change in that regard.
