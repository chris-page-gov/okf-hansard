Turn 4 · Baroness in Waiting/Government Whip (Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath) (Lab)

Our approach to educational technology is underpinned by sector engagement and our research portfolio, which is published on GOV.UK. When used effectively alongside high-quality teaching, technology can improve outcomes, reduce workload and support inclusion. However, evidence about what works, for whom and in what circumstances remains limited. That is why we are strengthening the evidence base through the Technology in Schools survey, the £24 million EdTech Testbed programme and the AI Tutoring Tools Pioneers programme, alongside wider research partnerships.

Turn 5 · Baroness Laing of Elderslie (Con)

I thank the Minister for her sincere Answer. Would not £24 million be better spent on buying books for schools? Why, then, are the Government yet again consulting £24 million-worth of expensive management consultants and ticking more boxes on irrelevant forms instead of taking action? It is not the Minister’s fault and I do not blame her. Turn 6 · Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath (Lab)

In January 2026, the DfE published product safety standards for generative AI in education which outline the capabilities and features that products and systems should need to meet to be considered safe for users in educational settings. The statutory guidance Keeping Children Safe in Education makes it clear that schools and colleges should do all they reasonably can to limit children’s exposure to harmful content, including having appropriate filtering and monitoring systems in place. We are clear that the unsafe use of technology in schools is not acceptable. Schools and colleges have a statutory responsibility to keep children and young people safe, online as well as offline. Turn 8 · Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath (Lab)

As my noble friend said, technology used purposefully as part of high-quality teaching is important for a number of subjects and qualifications, including computing, and can support the practical applied learning we want every child to experience, including the new vocational GCSEs. Good implementation matters. We will fund the National Centre for Computing Education, providing world-class CPD for teachers, and will work with schools and the sector to provide appropriate resources, guidance, professional development and access to specialist expertise and facilities.
