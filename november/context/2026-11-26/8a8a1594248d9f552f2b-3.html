Turn 17 · Ms Billington

I agree with the hon. Lady that there are excess profits in the system. As it happens, the energy price cap has contributed considerably to limiting the profits made by suppliers, for example, and those profits are made in other parts of the system. That is one reason why we introduced a windfall tax, so that oil and gas companies pay 38% extra tax on top of everything else. That contributes to the Exchequer, meaning that we can invest in public services that the hon. Lady’s constituents will benefit from. I agree that decent access to affordable energy is a health measure and we should see it like that, as well as our warm homes plan being a job creator for growth in every postcode. I hope to see her support such measures in the future.

Turn 19 · Ms Billington

I share my hon. Friend’s impatience and that of her constituents in Llanelli. The Energy Independence Bill, which will have its Second Reading next week, includes measures to break the link between electricity prices and the price of gas. It is worth putting on the record the fact that as recently as the 2000s the gas price dictated the electricity price more than 90% of the time, but that is now down to 60%. That is because of the commitment to renewable energy made across this House over time—it is a pity the Opposition do not stick to that consensus—and we must continue to push further once we have that break, and ensure that we reap the benefits of that renewable energy.

Turn 21 · Ms Billington

I agree with the right hon. Gentleman and I am grateful to him for raising that issue. The situation for people off grid who are reliant on heating oil has been very bad for a long time. I am pleased that we have finally agreed to regulate the heating oil market, on the recommendation of the Competition and Markets Authority, and we will work at pace to get that right. In the interim, we have allocated £53 million to local authorities and the devolved Administrations, to ensure that people can access some kind of support. I have said this before at the Dispatch Box and I will say it again: anyone in a local authority who is administering that crisis and resilience fund should be aware that I am keen for that money to get out the door. We have already started to have freezing temperatures this winter, and people on heating oil in particular must make sure that they get that help.

Turn 23 · Ms Billington

I am grateful to my hon. Friend for raising that issue, and I am aware of the ongoing issues with the Pimlico heating network. On the household energy payment, the £100 million discretionary fund that we announced is supposed to target people who do not fall into certain criteria, and could potentially include help for people on heat networks. Those on heat networks or who use heating oil, and people in temporary accommodation or houses in multiple occupation might find it much more difficult to access help, and that is what the discretionary fund is for. Heat is now regulated by Ofgem, and if residents of the Pimlico heating network are finding issues to do with back billing and so on, they have recourse to go to Ofgem and ensure that they are not being exploited or having to pay things they should not pay. They can also go to the Energy Ombudsman, of course, if Ofgem fails them.
