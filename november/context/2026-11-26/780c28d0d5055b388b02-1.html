Turn 3 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Blair McDougall)

The British industry supercharger cuts electricity costs for around 600 of the most electricity-intensive businesses, including Wright’s Flour in Harlow—[Interruption.] I am sorry to get Harlow into Hansard before my hon. Friend the Member for Harlow (Chris Vince). Last April, the Government uplifted relief from network charges for supercharger recipients from 60% to 90%. Around 170 of the most electricity-intensive and trade-exposed businesses also benefit from the energy-intensive industries compensation scheme, and the British industrial competitiveness scheme is currently open for applications.

Turn 6 · Blair McDougall

I thank my hon. Friend, and I thank the Lea Valley Growers Association for its contribution to our food resilience. We have met officials from DEFRA, and we continue to work together to assess the impact of high energy prices on the food chain, including the profitability of growers and controlled environment horticulture businesses, and to explore the potential options for further support. DEFRA is currently facilitating the development of the industry-led horticulture sector growth plan, with the aim of increasing primary producers’ profitability, improving productivity and strengthening the contribution of food to our national security.

Turn 8 · Mark Garnier (Wyre Forest) (Con)

My constituents in Wyre Forest—like all our constituents—are bitterly disappointed that their household energy bills are due to increase by 26% in January, but energy-intensive industries are equally worried that the cost of their energy is the highest in the western world. There is a way, however, that the Government could support energy-intensive industries: by approving drilling in Rosebank and using the extra tax receipts from the producers to reduce bills for energy-intensive businesses. Why are the Government taking such a long time to make a decision on Rosebank, and what conversations are the Secretary of State and the Business Department having with the Energy Secretary about the urgency of a decision on this key energy problem?
