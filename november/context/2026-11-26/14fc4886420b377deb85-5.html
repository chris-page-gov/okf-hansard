Turn 33 · Lucy Rigby

Clause 34 introduces schedule 3, which amends FSMA 2000 to provide for a notification process for the appointment of certain senior managers. The senior managers regime plays an important role in ensuring that individuals who lead financial services firms are fit and proper, and that they can be held accountable for the decisions they take. Regulatory pre-approval for every appointment creates administrative costs and delays where the risks do not warrant that level of scrutiny, which can make it harder for firms to fill senior vacancies and to plan succession. Turn 34 · Mark Garnier

I thank the Minister for her remarks. Clause 34 and schedule 3 make changes to the SMCR and replace the regulatory approval requirements with a notification-based approach. As we have heard, under the existing regime, firms need regulatory approval before an individual can perform a senior management function. The Bill changes that so firms can appoint individuals and then notify the regulator. Put simply, firms do not need prior approval. The intention is to speed up appointments, which is a good thing, to reduce administrative costs for firms, which is an even better thing, and to allow regulators to focus their resources on high-risk individuals. Turn 39 · Lauren Edwards (Rochester and Strood) (Lab)

It is a pleasure to serve under your chairmanship, Sir Edward. I want to make a few comments in opposition to new clause 34, primarily because an individual could be changing to a financial firm with a very different business. For some SMCRs, that may not matter—for example, in IT controls—but for others, an individual could be moving to an insurer with a very different line of business. For example, they could be moving from a general insurer to a life insurer, and it is important that regulators have the opportunity to check that individual’s knowledge and understanding of the risks in that business before giving their approval. The new clause is well-intentioned, and there is an argument for streamlining, but not for its current drafting.
