Turn 50 · Edward Argar (Melton and Syston) (Con)

I can see the logic of what the Minister is saying. I was a Minister for six years, including briefly a Treasury Minister, and I can see her point about the use by Governments, through this House, of statutory instruments and delegated legislation. But what is being asked for in the clause is very measured and proportionate: simply the publication and laying before Parliament of a report. That does not constrain the Government, nor will it prevent the Government from being able to do something; it merely puts the information transparently in the public domain, for Parliament and for others to see. Even if the Minister cannot accept the inclusion of the clause today, I gently encourage her to reflect further on the fact that the clause does not really trammel her powers; it just adds to the transparency and trust in the process that will be built through that transparency.

Turn 51 · Lucy Rigby

I appreciate the right hon. Gentleman’s comments about tramelling powers—it is not my personal power, of course, but the Government’s—but I believe a further statutory reporting requirement is not necessary to achieve the ends to which he refers. We have already set out the purpose and justification for the Bill’s delegated powers in the delegated powers memorandum, an updated version of which was published when the Bill came to this House. Parliament will scrutinise the exercise of those powers if relevant legislation is brought forward. But as I said, the clause is about an additional reporting requirement, without changing the substantive safeguards that apply to the exercise of those powers, so I do not think it is necessary. Question put, That the clause stand part of the Bill.
