Turn 35 · Baroness Antrobus (Lab)

I will have to write to the noble Lord, Lord Frost, on that. I had understood that he meant it was limited to three countries; we have slightly different interpretations of his question, but I will ensure that that is sorted for him. I thank the noble Baroness, Lady McIntosh of Pickering, for warmly welcoming the youth experience scheme. She mentioned reciprocity, which is really important. Several comments have been made in the debate implying—I do not want to use terms that are inflammatory—that there will be 100,000 people coming in this direction, without realising that it is reciprocal and 100,000 of our young people will have the opportunity to go there. Many of them have been denied this, including those of my stepchildren’s age, for the entirety of their late teens and 20s. It is a reciprocal agreement, not a one-way flow.

Turn 36 · Lord Moylan (Con)

My Lords, the point I made was that, while it is reciprocal on paper, the Treasury’s assumption in calculating the growth impact is that those young people from Britain will not go in full numbers, because it is assuming that this will create immigration. In other words, there will be more people coming in than leaving; that is what net immigration consists of.
