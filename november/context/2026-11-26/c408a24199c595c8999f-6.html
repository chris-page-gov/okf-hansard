The clause will require the Treasury to lay before Parliament a report setting out how the powers conferred in several parts of the Bill will be used. It was added in the other place, but the additional reporting requirements are not necessary. Delegated powers are a long-standing and accepted part of the legislative and regulatory framework for financial services. The pace of innovation, which we were just discussing, means that detailed primary legislation can become out of date and ineffective. Delegated powers allow the framework to keep pace with changes. They are not a shortcut or a substitute for good policymaking, and they are part of how the system is intended to work. Turn 48 · Bobby Dean

We also do not support the removal of clause 52 and are against new clause 20, which states that the Treasury may by statutory instrument make regulations that “amend, repeal or revoke primary legislation” where it considers it consequential, which I do not think has been properly defined here or elsewhere. That applies to any provision in the Bill, so Ministers will effectively be given a permanent power to alter primary legislation, and the Executive will be allowed to bypass parliamentary scrutiny. We are not sure why the Government are trying to reintroduce a provision that was so decisively chucked out on a cross-party basis in the Lords, so we will vote against it.
