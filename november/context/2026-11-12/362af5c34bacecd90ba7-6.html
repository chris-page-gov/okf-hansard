Turn 44 · Gareth Bacon

Q You touched on the topic of my second question, which is about the impact that having to remediate cladding has had on the ability of housing associations to deliver new housing stock. Has it been a marginal or massive impact? If you have facts and figures on that, they would be welcome. Kate Henderson: Not having funding for remediation has undoubtedly taken huge capacity out of the social housing sector. Since the fire, we have been working very hard to remediate buildings, but the pace of that, without Government funding, was much slower than we would have liked. Last year’s funding has changed this, but it is not just about money; it is also about the system around it. It is about the capacity of the Building Safety Regulator, and not just external walls but internal defects, and the system of assurance and having enough technical competency to support us in that work.

Turn 46 · The Chair

Q Did you want to briefly add anything on those questions, Ms Mangaroo? Jaclyn Mangaroo: I agree with a lot of what you said, Kate, particularly around the upskilling and resource elements. What we and our members see is that some of the delays start much earlier in the gateway 2 process. The real problem, which this Bill, with some amendments, will hopefully tackle, is where buildings are completely stalled from the beginning—where scope of works cannot be agreed and, like Kate said, where FRAEWs differ.
