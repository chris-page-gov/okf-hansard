Turn 49 · Florence Eshalomi

Q What practical support do you think volunteer resident directors would need to fulfil their duties in this Bill? Jaclyn Mangaroo: We welcome the remediation backstop, but that it would be great to see that come further forward so that you do not have to go through enforcement or sanctions to get to that remediation backstop. Particularly where we have evidence that buildings are stalled in the very early stages of project scoping, I think that that would be a reasonable opportunity for the regulators to step in and bring in a remediation manager or bring forward a remediation order or remediation contribution order. I do not think that some of those resources are necessarily being used proactively. I have not seen any transparent data on their use by Homes England and other regulators, and I think that could really help.

Turn 56 · Andrew Cooper (Mid Cheshire) (Lab)

Q Jaclyn, I am interested in what happens when a building owner is liquidated. You are probably aware that the Government have commissioned some work with the Law Commission to reform bona vacantia. That will take a few years, but in the meantime this Bill sets out a possible workable route in terms of appointing a manager. Do you think that goes far enough? Is that a workable way forward? Jaclyn Mangaroo: Hopefully, issues in those cases will be few and far between, but the backstop and the appointment of a remediation manager is the only way to go. It is important that residents have some certainty and a point of contact in somebody who will be responsible for that. At the moment, yes, I think the Bill does go far enough in those cases.
