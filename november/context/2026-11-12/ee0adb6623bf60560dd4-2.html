Turn 10 · Baroness Winterton of Doncaster (Lab)

I have deep respect for the noble Lord’s knowledge in this area. Again, I hope I can reassure him that, although human resources, the budget and the relationships between staff in the UK and overseas are obviously kept under review, we are quite certain that the number of staff we have who are knowledgeable about China and are training is adequate. For example, more than 1,700 people trained in the last financial year, there are more than 800 members of the cross-government China cadre and £1.6 million has been spent on the China capability programme. We are putting in the resources necessary to support the programmes that we have with China and our strategic objectives with regard to China.

Turn 12 · Baroness Winterton of Doncaster (Lab)

With regard to the last part of the noble Baroness’s question, I am afraid that I do not know the figures for Arabic personnel. She is quite right about institutional knowledge, but it is not static in any organisation. There will be staff movements; that is an important part of any healthy business model. Frankly, when senior staff move on, we look to make sure that other talented colleagues can come in to take their place. We have many officials specialising in China policy in key areas of focus, even beyond foreign policy. As the noble Baroness says, diplomacy is incredibly important, but we also look at areas such as trade, science and innovation, and defence and security. As I say, we take a cross-government view with regard to China expertise.
