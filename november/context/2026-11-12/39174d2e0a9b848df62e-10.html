Turn 73 · Speaker unlabelled

Within the probing nature of these amendments, I ask the Minister whether a commercial operator should be able to enter and examine or seize items from any private vehicle, be it a private car, lorry, heavy goods vehicle or haulage vehicle. What independent safeguards would apply to that? Picking out the theme from the previous grouping, I suspect we have unanimity on this point: there is no grand criminality in avoiding these toll charges. There is no mass, deliberate effort to evade a fiver, tenner or whatever it might be in order to use any particular road. It must, then, be a pretty extreme position that gives a private operator—a licensee—such excessive and intrusive powers to enter vehicles and seize items from vehicles. That rubs up with the principles of fairness in private property that I, for one, thought we enjoyed in this country.

Amendment 88 would strengthen the safeguards around vehicle searches in the Bill by restricting private enforcement powers under clause 29 and requiring a judicial warrant issued by a magistrate, or a police presence, before entering or searching a vehicle. Under standard civil enforcement rules in schedule 12 to the Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007, court bailiffs recovering civil debts cannot enter or search vehicles without a judicial warrant of control.
