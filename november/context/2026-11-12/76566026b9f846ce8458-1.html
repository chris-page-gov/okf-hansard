Turn 6 · Perran Moon (Camborne and Redruth) (Ind)

Meur ras, Mr Efford. It is an honour to serve under your chairship. I thank the Committee and its chair for this report, which makes for illuminating—if concerning—reading alongside last week’s critical minerals report. As a Cornish MP and chair of the electric vehicle all-party parliamentary group, I have two separate but related questions. First, the report recommends “targeted interventions” to secure sovereign capabilities and hedge against growing dependence on China. Are Cornwall’s tin, lithium and tungsten projects the sort of sovereign capability the Committee has in mind? Secondly, given the warnings in the report about China shock 2.0, does the Chair of the Committee agree that heavily subsidised Chinese electric vehicles are a far greater threat to UK automotive manufacturers than the UK’s zero emission vehicle mandate?

Turn 7 · Liam Byrne

Let me take the hon. Member’s questions in order. We believe that the right approach to derisking trade with China is not a wholesale decoupling. Instead, precision derisking is needed, where we take aim at specific things where we need to diversify supplies and ensure that we are not reliant on supplies from China. The best way to do that, given that we must make choices, is to look at which capabilities we as a country want to be sovereign and which we want to develop with close allies such as the Europeans, Canadians, Japanese, Australians or Koreans. This debate has rumbled on over the last couple of years, and the Government have set out different lists in different strategies.
