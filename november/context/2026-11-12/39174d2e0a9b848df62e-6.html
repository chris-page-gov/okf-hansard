Turn 42 · Justin Madders

I will address clause 26, which requires the ORR to publish a code on how tolls should be charged. The code will set out the principles and standards that tolling arrangements should meet. Its purpose is to ensure that tolls are charged fairly and transparently for road users. The ORR may update or replace the code at any time, and any updated or replacement code must also be published. The ORR may leave out information if publishing it would be likely to harm a person’s commercial interests. Clause 26 therefore provides clear and independent oversight of how licence holders charge tolls, while protecting genuinely sensitive commercial information, and I commend it to the Committee. I will respond to the Opposition amendment once I have heard what the shadow Minister has to say.

Turn 48 · Justin Madders

I am happy to give that assurance. The ORR will consult on the tolling code in advance of the first tolling scheme. I cannot give the shadow Minister a precise timing, because this is obviously all dependent on when the financial transactions are completed. That is, in essence, why we cannot support the amendment. To make the commencement of the Bill dependent on the publication of the code would create a Catch-22 scenario: Government guidance and the licence could not be finalised until the commencement of the Act, but without Government guidance and the licence, the ORR could not meaningfully finalise and consult on the content of the code. That is why, unfortunately, we cannot accept the shadow Minister’s amendment.
