Turn 58 · Olly Glover

I have a question for the Conservatives on their amendment 52. They seem to have been on a rather rapid journey, having had a significant change of mind on the principle of caps since the Civil Aviation (Consumer Protection and Regulatory Reform) Bill Committee. Then, the hon. Member for Bognor Regis and Littlehampton delivered a withering and scathing speech on one of our amendments, which proposed a cap on airport drop-off fees. She argued that it would create a moral hazard and encourage everyone to charge the maximum, yet their amendment 52 tries to set a cap at £100 for failure to pay a toll. Perhaps Conservative Members could address why they feel that is not a moral hazard.

Turn 59 · Greg Smith

There is a fundamental difference between a fee to do something and a penalty for having forgotten to do something. I therefore think the two positions are entirely compatible. We have a very clear view on airport drop-off charges, but it is nothing to do with this Bill. They are a hindrance to airport users, but there is a fundamental difference between a charge to use a toll road or to drop someone off at an airport and a penalty for failing to do so.

Turn 63 · Justin Madders

I quite enjoyed the little sideshow there. It was entertaining. I will deal first with the shadow Minister’s questions about Government amendment 13. Of course, we do not envisage that this is going to be a regular situation. It could cover, for example, a situation where a vehicle has been abandoned on a highway, but it is also for situations where there may be a persistent offender and further action is needed to enforce against them. I turn to the shadow Minister’s amendment 52, which would introduce a £100 cap on penalty charges for non-payment of a toll. That is actually £20 lower than the current cap for a penalty charge notice issued at Dartford and on quite a lot of other road charging schemes, so it is very generous of him to give a discount.
