Turn 53 · Justin Madders

I will begin by addressing the oversight and enforcement provisions in the group. Clauses 42 to 49 set out how the Office of Rail and Road will enforce licence conditions. These provisions are similar to those found in other similar pieces of legislation, such as the Transport Act 2000, that include enforcement regimes of this type. Clause 42 enables the ORR to issue a contravention notice where it considers that a licence condition is being or has been breached. The notice must identify the condition and contravention concerned, the period over which the contravention occurred or is occurring, the action that the ORR may take and a reasonable period during which representations can be made. Turn 54 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, clause 49 ensures that enforcement orders issued by the ORR can be effectively enforced. A person who receives an enforcement order or an urgent enforcement order must comply with it. An urgent order must be complied with even before it has been confirmed. The ORR can go to court to enforce those requirements if necessary. That can be through civil proceedings—for example, through an injunction or any other appropriate remedy.

Turn 55 · Speaker unlabelled

The clause can also protect people who suffer loss or damage because an enforcement order or an urgent enforcement order has not been complied with; indeed, they may be able to bring their own civil claim. There is a safeguard for the person subject to the order because it is a defence to show that they “took all reasonable steps” and “exercised all due diligence” to avoid breaching the order. Clause 49 therefore ensures the ORR’s enforcement orders have real effect, and provides a route for those affected by non-compliance to seek redress.
