Turn 5 · Baroness in Waiting/Government Whip (Baroness Hyde of Bemerton) (Lab)

Provisional data released by UKHSA in May shows that steady progress has been made since the publication of the HIV action plan last year, with new HIV diagnoses continuing to fall. Compared with 2024, the provisional number of new HIV diagnoses among gay and bisexual men fell in 2025 by 7%. Over the same period, there was a 15% decline among heterosexual men and a 22% decline among heterosexual women.

Turn 6 · Lord Black of Brentwood (Con)

My Lords, I applaud the Government’s commitment, which is strongly supported on these Benches, to end new HIV transmissions by 2030, which would make us the first country in the world to do so. I welcome significant new investment in HIV testing and care. However, to meet the ambitious 2030 target, do we not need to do more on prevention, which means widening access to PrEP? Turn 7 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

I congratulate the noble Lord on his appointment as the incoming chair of the Terrence Higgins Trust and on all his excellent work in this area to date. The ring-fenced public health grant funds local authorities to provide open-access sexual health services, including PrEP. The grant rose to over £3.8 billion in 2025-26, the biggest real-terms increase for a decade. The HIV action plan is backed by over £170 million and includes action on innovative PrEP provision. Local authorities are responsible for commissioning services and are best placed to do so, and can use the NHS shared business services framework to procure both online and postal PrEP.

Turn 8 · Lord Cashman (Non-Afl)

My Lords, I refer to my register of interests, particularly as a patron of the Terrence Higgins Trust. I congratulate the Government on their HIV Action Plan for England, 2025 to 2030—and previous Governments. This is an example of what we can achieve when we work together across the political spectrum. However, can the Minister say how we will connect with the 5,000 people who are currently living with diagnosed HIV but not accessing treatment? These people are at risk of becoming seriously ill—indeed, some already have. I look forward to the Minister’s reply on this urgent issue.
