Turn 129 · The Chair

We will now hear oral evidence from Dame Judith Hackitt. We have until 4.40 pm for this panel. Dame Judith, could you briefly introduce yourself for the record? Dame Judith Hackitt: Yes. I am Dame Judith Hackitt. I authored the independent review of building safety and fire regulations immediately after the Grenfell Tower fire, which led to the series of recommendations that formed the Building Safety Act. Since then, I have been chairing the industry safety steering group, working on driving culture change within the built environment sector.

Turn 131 · Gareth Bacon

Q Would you like to elaborate on that? Dame Judith Hackitt: With pleasure. The most important thing that we need to do within this remediation Bill is to give certainty to residents, in particular, who have been waiting a very long time to know whether their buildings need remediation and whether they are safe. Although some of the recommendations move us in the right direction, they do not take away the uncertainty and the room that exists for people to debate in court or through lawyers what needs to be done and by whom. Unless we address that and give residents certainty, we are missing an important opportunity in this Bill.

Turn 133 · Gareth Bacon

Turn 136 · Florence Eshalomi

Q Thank you, Dame Judith, for joining us. Following on from the shadow Minister’s questioning, you will be aware that the Bill seeks to legislate for a single consistent approach on external walls to help give more clarity to residents, developers and manufacturers. Do you feel that having Homes England audit all assessments that need remediation is a good way forward in ensuring transparency and trust? Dame Judith Hackitt: I personally do not have a view one way or the other on whether Homes England is the right place to put that. What I do have a view on is that it needs to be an agreed risk framework that everyone has bought into. It needs to be a body that has the technical competence to oversee that and, in cases where there is a dispute, to overrule that and agree the standard to which the buildings are going to be remediated.
