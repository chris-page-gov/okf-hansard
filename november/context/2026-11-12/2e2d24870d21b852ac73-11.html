Turn 84 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 66 sets out the court’s powers when it considers an application for a highways administration order. The court may make the order, dismiss the application, adjourn the hearing, make an interim order or make any other court order it considers appropriate. It may also treat the application as a winding-up petition and exercise the powers available on such a petition. A highways administration order is therefore not automatic. The court may make one only if it is satisfied that the licence holder is unable to pay its debts, is likely to become unable to pay its debts or meets the specified public interest winding-up test.

Turn 85 · Speaker unlabelled

The public interest ground is subject to an additional safeguard. The court may not make an order on that ground unless the Secretary of State brings a petition for winding up on grounds of public interest under section 124A of the Insolvency Act 1986 and has certified that, disregarding the objective of the highways administration, it would be just and equitable to petition for the company to be wound up in the public interest. The clause also prevents a highways administration order from being made where the company is already in administration or has gone into liquidation. That avoids overlapping insolvency procedures.

The court may make an interim order while the application is being determined. An interim order may restrict the exercise of powers by the company or its directors or confer a discretion on a person qualified to act as an insolvency practitioner in relation to the company. A highways administration order takes effect at the time appointed by the court, or, if no time is appointed by the court, when the order is made. When an application is made in relation to a company incorporated outside Great Britain, the relevant powers to restrict the exercise of a power of the company or its directors via an interim court order are limited to the exercise of such a power within Great Britain or in relation to the company’s affairs or businesses carried on in Great Britain and its property in Great Britain.
