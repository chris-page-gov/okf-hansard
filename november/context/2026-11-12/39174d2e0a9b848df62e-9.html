Turn 66 · Jim Dickson

While we are dealing with the administration of tolling and charges that result from non-payment, I want to mention that the operator that administers the Dart charge in Dartford changed in 2023, and during the transition there were numerous problems: people being charged for cars that were said to have used the crossing when they had not; people whose resident discounts disappeared from their accounts and who were then charged the full amount; and other problems with the way in which the administration was done. Could the Minister give some reassurance that such administrative errors around both charging and penalties, if they occur in the future, either on the lower Thames crossing or with the Dart charge, will be subject to the regulator’s intervention to ensure that the licence holder performed their responsibilities properly?

Turn 70 · Greg Smith

There are quite a few amendments in the group, but they largely speak to very similar themes. Amendment 55 would ensure that the powers for examining a road vehicle rest with the police, rather than any licence holder, and amendments 56 and 57 would do the same. Amendment 60 is slightly different but also comes back to a debate that we had in the previous group. It would ensure that regulations on examining a vehicle were subject to the affirmative procedure, so I will not repeat those arguments, and the same applies to amendments 61 and 62. Similar again to the previous group, amendment 74 would prevent commencement of the Act, if the Bill receives Royal Assent, until the Secretary of State has published guidance about the exercise of the powers in clauses 29 and 30.
