Turn 97 · Gareth Bacon

Q Good afternoon, Mr Cook. What progress are developers making in remediating their buildings? Alexander Cook: First, on behalf of Vistry, we have been at the forefront since the tragic events of Grenfell in 2017 to make sure we are proactively managing and liaising with building owners. We signed the pledge in 2022 and the developer remediation contract in 2023. To date, 51% of our developer buildings started are completed. It is worth noting that, if 27 buildings had not had delays from blockers this year, we would have been at 70%. I can give some real world examples from our experience of where that has happened.

Turn 99 · Gareth Bacon

Q Nobody would dispute that remediating buildings and making them safe have to be a high priority, but when you do that there is an opportunity cost. Has it had an impact on the ability to deliver new housing? Alexander Cook: Vistry has a dedicated special projects team with funding allocated to remediation, so it is a priority for us that does not take away from the day job. We have, however, had challenges on the new-homes side with Building Safety Regulator applications, which have delayed delivering those homes for up to nine months. We obviously acknowledge that residents need safe buildings, and that is the priority, but some of those statutory approvals are where the blockers have been.

Turn 100 · Gareth Bacon

Q We had Building Safety Regulator representatives in this morning. They acknowledged that it was initially slow but felt they had very much improved the situation more recently. The nine-month thing you mentioned has come up before. Would you agree that it has got better? Alexander Cook: We have seen improvements. Of our applications? Not yet. We have had seven remediations applications submitted, all of which had time extensions and three of which are now approved, so we can see the backlog starting to come down. We have not had a fast application or a statutory timeframe application as yet, but we know industry colleagues that have, so we can see the improvements that have been made. Hopefully, going into next year, their recruitment will improve and their capacity and consistency of decisions will help all of us move forward.

Turn 102 · Florence Eshalomi

Q To date, developers and taxpayers have contributed millions of pounds in remediation costs and construction product manufacturers have not paid anything. Do you feel that getting co-operation from them will help when it comes to the challenges and finances that Vistry and other developers face? Alexander Cook: We welcome the changes on manufacturer redress and section 149. That will help make sure that all responsible parties are held to account and share the costs. It will not make any difference in terms of us accelerating our works and delivering what we need to.
