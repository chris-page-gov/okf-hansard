Turn 9 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

I pay tribute to the noble Lord, Lord Cashman, for all his work in this area. The HIV action plan committed £9 million over three years to establish that first retention and re-engagement initiative. It is a core part of the plan and is intended to support people diagnosed with HIV who, as the noble Lord said, are not currently retained in specialist care, and to help people re-engage where perhaps they have fallen out, and to restart effective treatment and achieve viral suppression. It will amplify existing work within local services and partner with the voluntary and community sector and industry to increase the number of patients who are re-engaged in their care. Detailed plans for that national retention and re-engagement initiative will be set out in due course.

Turn 13 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

Indeed, the Government are not complacent about those who are not captured within the very welcome statistics on new rates of transition and the overall incidence falling. The HIV action plan focuses on five key population groups to ensure that no one is left behind, and this includes ethnic minority and white GBMSM, Black African heterosexual men and women, and ethnic minority heterosexual adults. That is what the £4.8 million in the HIV Prevention England programme is for: to reduce the size of that undiagnosed population and to address some of the inequalities the noble Baroness raises.
