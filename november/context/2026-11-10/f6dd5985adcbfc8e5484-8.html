Turn 60 · Speaker unlabelled

6: After Clause 2, insert the following new Clause— “Legal aidUnder powers contained in the Legal Aid, Sentencing, and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012, regulations made by the Lord Chancellor must provide, no later than the day on which this Act is passed, that—(a) a defendant’s eligibility for legal aid, and(b) the remuneration paid to providers of legal aid services,is calculated on the basis applicable in the Crown Court, irrespective of whether the matter is heard in the magistrates’ court or the Crown Court.”Member’s explanatory statement This amendment is intended to harmonise legal aid eligibility, and the remuneration of the providers of legal services, irrespective of whether an either way offence is retained in the magistrates’ court or sent to the Crown Court, thus reducing the risk of unrepresented defendants in the magistrates’ court.

Turn 63 · Speaker unlabelled

The purpose of this amendment is that, whatever move is made, there should be no detriment to the legal profession. This is not a special plea for lawyers—although, coming from a lawyer, noble Lords all no doubt think it is. But it is a matter of practical reality. If we do not get lawyers into the magistrates’ courts who are prepared to stay there and do the work, the work will be done much less efficiently. That is the public interest. Secondly, the part of the legal profession that deals with magistrates’ courts is ageing, like many of us, and it has proved very difficult to attract new people to the profession.

Thirdly, there is an element of cross-subsidy. The rates in the magistrates’ courts are significantly lower, the eligibility criteria are different, and it is only by the cross-subsidy that occurs from work done in the Crown Court that those who practise in this area are able to do so.
