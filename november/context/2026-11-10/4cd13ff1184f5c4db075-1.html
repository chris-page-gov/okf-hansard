Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (Lord Wood of Anfield) (Lab)

My Lords, this order is required before the UK can ratify the convention establishing an International Claims Commission for Ukraine. Noble Lords will remember that the UK signed the convention on 16 December 2025. The international claims commission builds on the register of damage for Ukraine established two years earlier, in 2023, which collects and records claims from individuals, organisations and public bodies in Ukraine for damage, loss and injury inflicted by Russia. Once the commission has been established, the register and its work will be transferred to the commission. The commission will review, assess and decide on claims recorded in the register, as well as subsequent claims, and determine the amount of compensation due in each case. Turn 5 · Speaker unlabelled

This order grants the International Claims Commission for Ukraine the privileges and immunities it functionally requires so that it may operate effectively. Doing so is a pre-requisite so that the UK can ratify the commission’s treaty. However, we do not expect many of these privileges and immunities to be used in the UK. The commission will not be based in the UK, visits of its staff to the UK are envisaged to be limited and no visits are being planned right now. The privileges and immunities in this order are limited to what the commission, and the persons connected with its work, functionally require to perform their roles independently and effectively.

Turn 7 · Speaker unlabelled

The order also confers specific privileges and immunities on representatives of other member states of the commission, the commissioners, the executive director and members of the secretariat, and experts engaged by the commission. These include immunity in respect of official words and acts. No individuals will receive a full set of so-called personal or diplomatic immunities.

Turn 8 · Speaker unlabelled

These privileges and immunities are granted to ensure the independent and effective functioning of the commission; they are not for personal benefit. The convention and this order contain a clear waiver, so that a privilege or immunity may be waived by the appropriate authority. For example, the Secretary-General of the Council of Europe may waive immunities for the executive director of the commission, if requested by a member state.
