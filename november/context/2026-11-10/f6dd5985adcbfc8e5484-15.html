Turn 113 · Lord Marks of Henley-on-Thames (LD)

My Lords, I am very grateful to those who have spoken in this short debate. I am bound to say that, at the moment, I am disappointed by the position taken by the Minister. I can see his reasoning, but I am not sure that there is a great deal of understanding of the position of defendants or the arguments put particularly by the noble and learned Lord, Lord Phillips of Worth Matravers, and in relation to Crown Court trials generally by the noble Lord, Lord Carlile. I appreciate that goes much further than my amendments do. Nevertheless, that may be something we can continue to discuss. I would envisage further thought on whether jury waiver by defendants would not be appropriate in the Clause 3 cases. Meanwhile, I beg leave to withdraw my amendment.

Turn 119 · Lord Marks of Henley-on-Thames (LD)

My Lords, in this group I have Amendments 13A, 13B and 15A. I have spoken on the principles associated with complexity and length in previous groups, and indeed my amendments echo my Amendment 17 in group 4, on which I have already spoken. The amendments would require, before a “judge alone” trial were ordered, that the case would genuinely be of sufficient complexity to warrant a trial by judge alone and would have a potential length of at least five months. A great deal has been said about complexity, but I would just like to make some points about length. I do not accept that the likely length of a trial is itself any reason for abandoning a jury trial.
