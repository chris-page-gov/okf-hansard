Turn 9 · Baroness Chakrabarti (Lab)

I am grateful to my noble friend for that rhetorical question, to which I feel the Committee knows the answer. I understand all the points about the backlog, the costs and our concern to speed up justice, which victims are crying out for, but, at the same time, a vindication for someone wrongfully accused or for victims who have been denied that vindication of their rights means that juries really matter to ordinary people in this country. I know that noble Lords in this Committee understand that, because a brief conversation with any number of their friends, neighbours and family members who have nothing to do with the legal profession, or perhaps any other profession, will indicate as much.

Turn 13 · Speaker unlabelled

It is impossible to say what guidelines Parliament should lay down when we have more details on the assumptions that underlie the decision, expressed in the Bill, to transfer a large bulk of cases from the Crown Court to the magistrates’ court, but it is very clear that we must address this question. If not, the matter will be left in the framework in which it currently stands, and a major change will have occurred. It may be the right thing to do, but—certainly for my part—one cannot tell at present.
