Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

The noble Lord, Lord Rennard, asked about the practical impact on retailers who operate the Challenge 25 scheme. The noble Lord, Lord Evans, also asked about advice to retailers. I confirm that we have published guidance for retailers on the changes coming into force, which will be updated to reflect the age verification defence in these regulations, subject of course to parliamentary approval. The broader point is that we will always continue to engage with retailers to help ensure that they are aware of the changes and what they need to do. They are very much part of the solution, and we have welcomed their commitment to working with us. The age of sale for vaping and nicotine products will be 18 and over, so Challenge 25 will continue to be a very useful tool in the box, and we will continue to encourage its use.

Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

The noble Lord, Lord Evans, asked about costs and expressed concerns about cumulative burden. The Act’s impact assessment considered the cost of age verification under the new age of sale regime. The Explanatory Memorandum for this instrument covers our assessment of additional indirect costs to businesses of digital elements. I emphasise that these are indirect costs, because, as I mentioned in my opening comments, the use of digital verification services is entirely voluntary.
