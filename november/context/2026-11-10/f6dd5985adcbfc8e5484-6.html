Turn 47 · Speaker unlabelled

Certainly, this could provide for a transfer of a greater block which would remain in the magistrates’ court. That would provide greater certainty, because concern has been expressed by some that, if a particular part of the country was under pressure in the Crown Court, the magistrates might be inclined to keep more of the cases themselves than they would in other parts of the country where there is less pressure on the Crown Court. The pressure is different between Crown Courts, so one could end up with inconsistencies, which would be highly undesirable. The other way in which it could be used is as a sort of bright-line list of offences, where they alone are to be transferred, or where they are used to provide greater certainty, or where they are used as a list of presumptions.

Turn 48 · Speaker unlabelled

We urge that reclassification be considered as one of the options in trying to find a way forward that achieves a consensus on how we transfer more cases to the magistrates’ courts and relieve the pressure on the Crown Court, because that should certainly be done. The greatest certainty that can be provided is in ensuring, I hope, that we do not have rights of appeal on the question of election, because that would add at least two or three weeks—maybe much more—and generate many more cases. We need certainty, not appellate rights. That is why we would urge one of these solutions. I cannot tell which is the right one until we have the figures to work out the assumptions, but one of these is surely a better solution. I beg to move.
