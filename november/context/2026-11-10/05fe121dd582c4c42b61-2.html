Turn 10 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

My Lords, joined-up care requires changes to funding flows in order that we can shift our resources. That is where the focus will be. I am glad that from next year we will be trialling for neighbourhoods and places payment mechanisms which mean that savings from reduced unplanned hospital care can go into increased investment in community services. That will not only provide extra funding in terms of neighbourhood health but drive change, which is what we are seeking. In addition, we announced in the Budget 250 neighbourhood health centres, with 120 delivered by 2030, and the first wave of 27 sites comes with a commitment of £50 million.

Turn 12 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

By expanding urgent community care, we will reduce avoidable emergency department attendances, hospital admissions and ambulance conveyances. That is why we are engaging not just an urgent community response but virtual ward services. I also point to the fact that we are supporting patients to book the most appropriate urgent care service through NHS 111 or the NHS app before attending emergency departments. There are a number of very good examples around the country of how people are doing that. Winter is going to be hugely challenging, and that is why, for example, we are putting in £190 million to help the NHS manage winter pressures and £140 million for new urgent care schemes.

Turn 14 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

We are supporting ICBs, providers and everyone across the NHS to work together differently. That is the main point I want to get across. In March this year, we published Fit for the Future: Towards Population Health Delivery Models, which set out three new population-based contract types. They aim to bring various aspects of services into a single contract, and they will deliver a shift from what is currently fragmented, service-by-service commissioning to end-to-end pathways around the patient. That is where we need to get to. GPs are very much part of that, and we have involved them throughout.
