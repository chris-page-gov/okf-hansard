Turn 1 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Education (Josh MacAlister)

With permission, Madam Deputy Speaker, I would like to make a statement on education technology and mobile phones in schools. Today’s children are growing up in a world shaped by smartphones, social media and artificial intelligence. Technology can bring enormous opportunities, but schools remain—and must remain—a place where children can focus, learn and connect with one another. Technology’s role in education should be to support learning, not to get in its way. Earlier this year, with support from across the House, the Government strengthened guidance on mobile phones in schools to back teachers and parents in making schools mobile phone-free. Turn 6 · Josh MacAlister

Well, we put it on a statutory footing because we had a piece of legislation. The question that is worth a moment of response was about the educational outcomes being within the scope of the certification process. AI tutors are a real concern, but there is an opportunity there. The Government are funding some of the leading companies around the world to develop products. They will then go into a separate programme for which we have funding, led by the Education Endowment Foundation, to establish the impact on educational outcomes and attainment. It is entirely right to suggest that there should be no widespread deployment of this technology before we know its full effects on cognitive development. Of course, there are also safety issues.
