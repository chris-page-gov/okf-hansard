Turn 123 · Lord Sentamu (CB)

My Lords, like the noble and learned Lord, Lord Leveson, I had not intended to speak. Commonwealth countries have been referred to. I originally come from Uganda, and I did a bit of law there. Although Uganda follows the British system to the letter, it chose not to have jury trials. All the trials are done by judges, and in more serious cases the judge sits with two assessors. I have not seen any failure to deliver justice properly. The amendment from the noble Lord, Lord Carlile, is a halfway house, in which trial by jury is still affirmed but, in some cases, “judge alone” would be the order of the day. The same would be true for some senior magistrates’ cases.

Turn 125 · Speaker unlabelled

By the way, I am not attracted by the suggestion that trial by jury gives a democratic reality to our system. Democracy, at the heart of it, is to do with a government of the people, by the people and for the people. The moment you extend it beyond that definition; you invite in quite a lot of uncertain things. I was also not quite persuaded earlier by the whole question of people’s behaviour determining the nature of how the trial will go. If someone commits the same kind of crime as another person, both are entitled to a just system that does not take in the antecedents before hearing reports about those people.

Turn 127 · Speaker unlabelled

Will the Government please listen? The reasoning of those wanting to defend trial by jury is grounded and founded. I say to those advocating “judge alone” cases that I have experienced them and been in places where they happen. After all, we still have the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court. Let us therefore not use the worry that we all have—that it takes such a long time for cases to be heard—as the reason for not having all cases heard by a jury, where logic, for the sake of justice, would invite a single judge to hear cases. From my experience in this country since 1974, I have nothing but the highest admiration and desire for our court system. However, like all of us, it sometimes needs tweaking and changing. But let us change it for the better delivery of justice rather than simply because of the numbers waiting for their cases to be heard.
