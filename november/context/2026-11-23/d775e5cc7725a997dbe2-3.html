The Government have spent the past two years making it more expensive for employers to employ young people through their jobs tax, through the burdens of the unemployment rights Act, and through above-inflation rises in the minimum wage. Turn 18 · Pat McFadden

The hon. Member refers to the minimum wage. The precise rate, of course, is set by the Low Pay Commission—an internationally admired model. The Low Pay Commission considered the case he was making and found that there was no evidence to suggest that this had a damaging effect on youth employment. It is of course an important part of its remit to consider the employment effects of any rate that it sets, and we expect it to do that when it sets the rates.

Turn 20 · Pat McFadden

They are jobs, but I also say to the hon. Member that I welcome supermarkets offering work experience as well, because that can be a critical route to a job. When I ask employers, “How much more likely are you to hire somebody if they have gone through work experience?”, they often say “Significantly higher”, so I do not think we should posit these things as against one another; one can be a route to another.

Turn 22 · Pat McFadden
