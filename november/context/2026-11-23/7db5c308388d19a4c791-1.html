Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (Baroness Taylor of Stevenage) (Lab)

My Lords, my noble friend is right to be concerned about women experiencing rough sleeping, as I am. Women experiencing rough sleeping face the particular risks of violence, abuse and exploitation. That is why my department has a specific action plan to tackle women’s rough sleeping. The Prime Minister launched an urgent national drive to end rough sleeping, and we are providing over £4.4 billion over three years for homelessness and rough sleeping services. We encourage local areas to work proactively with specialist women’s services, including domestic abuse services, so that women experiencing rough sleeping get the right support and that assessment of numbers of women rough sleeping is based on need rather than a physical count undertaken by outreach workers.

Turn 5 · Baroness Nye (Lab)

I thank my noble friend the Minister for that reply, and for the Prime Minister’s recognition of the problem. Given the strong link between domestic abuse and homelessness, and with White Ribbon Day reminding us of the urgent need to tackle violence against women and girls, it is essential that women escaping abuse who are sleeping rough or in hidden, unsafe locations are counted and identified so that adequate provision can be made. Will the Government therefore commit to funding a national rollout of the women’s rough sleeping census to ensure that such women are no longer invisible?

Turn 6 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

We recognise that women sleeping rough can be less visible through traditional outreach practices; some women may be hidden from view or may not be seen bedding down at night. It is therefore important that we draw on a wide range of evidence to understand the scale of the problem. Since June 2025, the monthly rough sleeping survey has included a question on the number of women sleeping rough and we also use the annual rough sleeping snapshot, which provides an estimate of the number of women sleeping rough on a single night in England. We encourage all local authorities to undertake a women’s census in their areas, because it helps councils understand where women sleep rough and what their needs are.

Turn 8 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

The Prime Minister has pledged a significant sum of money to make sure that we offer everyone who is rough sleeping a place by Christmas this year, and we are working very hard to deliver against that pledge. Not everyone will accept those places, and we understand that. It is important not only that we offer places to sleep but that we have the support sitting alongside it. We are working with our colleagues in local government and around the country to deliver that offer, as the Prime Minister has promised.
