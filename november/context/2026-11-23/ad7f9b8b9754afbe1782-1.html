Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Work and Pensions (Lilian Greenwood)

We regularly check disability benefit decisions to make sure they are correct. Where we find mistakes, we look at what happened and use those lessons to improve future decision making. We share that learning across teams and focus support where it is most needed. That helps us to make more consistent decisions, ensures that people receive the right entitlement and reduces the risk of incorrect payments.

Turn 3 · Liz Jarvis

My constituent’s 12-year-old daughter is blind in one eye and sight-impaired in the other, and she was awarded the higher-rate disabled child element of universal credit in 2018. In awarding that benefit, the Department for Work and Pensions failed to request a certificate of visual impairment. Upon reviewing the case, seven years later, it decided that she did not qualify. My constituent must now repay nearly £27,000 for a mistake she did not make, even though the DWP has admitted in writing that it shares responsibility for this overpayment. The DWP has given her just £400 in compensation for its mistake. Will the Minister review whether it is right for the DWP to recover sums in full when her Department accepts fault?

I thank the hon. Member for raising this particularly difficult-sounding case.
