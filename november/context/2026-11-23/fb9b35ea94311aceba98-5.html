Turn 34 · The Prime Minister

I have made no such commitment about the currency and will not be making any such commitment about the currency. The right hon. Gentleman talks in terms that I think show the problem with the Conservatives: hard line and doctrinaire, for years they have gone to Brussels and insulted people; they have gone there and damaged goodwill, and that has been against the British national interest. It is in our interest to work with people when they are our nearest neighbours. It became very plain to me, as part of this negotiation, the depth of the damage they did to relations with people with whom we have shared so much in the past. That is the damage that this Labour Government are correcting.

Turn 37 · The Prime Minister

We will make sure that we can extract all the value we possibly can from this agreement. The electricity linking opportunity could take a significant number of pounds off bills in the UK, particularly in Northern Ireland, as I have said. [Interruption.] I am surprised by the noises from the Conservatives—it is like the cost of living does not matter. Well, it does not matter to them and many of the people they know, but it matters in Makerfield and many other constituencies across the country. We will take pounds off bills wherever we can, and we will keep the House updated on that point. It comes over, does it not? The CBI—the Confederation of British Industry—says that this is progress, but the Conservatives say that it is not. What a position they have got themselves into.
