Turn 4 · Peter Swallow (Bracknell) (Lab)

The hon. Member raises a crucial point. While I do not think anyone would accept that the current system, which is based on rates from the ’90s, is in any way sensible or fair, individuals living in properties in more expensive parts of the country are not necessarily flush with cash either. Private rents in Bracknell Forest have risen by nearly 30% in the last five years, and the ratio between lower-quartile house prices and lower-quartile earnings is higher in Bracknell Forest than in the rest of England. Does that not show that we have to take into account the affordability of homes when considering this issue, as well as just the cost of them?

Turn 7 · Jamie Stone

I almost feel that the hon. Gentleman has had a sneak preview of my speech; I can only commend clairvoyance when I see it. The Institute for Fiscal Studies suggested two years ago that at least half of properties may now be in the wrong council tax band relative to their current value. The geographical inequalities have been identified, as others have already said, with properties in parts of the north and the midlands in England more likely to be in relatively high bands, and those in London and the south-east more likely to be in relatively low bands. I am repeating what has already been said, so I will not return to that issue. In a 2025 report, the Housing, Communities and Local Government Committee also described the current system as regressive and unfair, recommending that “the Government should begin the process of overhauling or replacing” it.
