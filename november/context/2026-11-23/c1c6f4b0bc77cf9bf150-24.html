Turn 185 · Speaker unlabelled

In all these things, this is not going to be a situation where there is an absolute, right answer. It is about finding a balance and a landing zone in the appropriate way that we deal with this issue. I suggest that there are three particular components to that. First, if explicit consent is to be enshrined very heavily within the legislation—and I think it is right that it is dealt with in primary legislation—guidance should be given and medics should explain, when seeking that explicit consent, the value in data and of getting information where possible, while always leaving it up to the individual patient as to whether they give that information.
