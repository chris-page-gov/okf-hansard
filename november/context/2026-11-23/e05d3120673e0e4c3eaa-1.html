Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (Lord Leong) (Lab)

My Lords, first, this week is Family Business Week, so I take this opportunity to thank all family businesses across the country for all they do, employing locally, looking after staff and supporting and contributing to their local economy. We are better and richer for it. Access to the right finance at the right time is vital to ensuring that small businesses can start, grow and thrive. The British Business Bank is central to this Government’s small business support. That is why we are more than doubling the British Business Bank’s growth guarantee scheme lending capacity to £3.35 billion a year and, by 2028-29, increasing the BBB’s financial capacity to £30.6 billion. In 2025-26, the bank provided £9.4 billion of finance to 38,000 businesses, 87% of which were outside London.

Turn 5 · Lord McTague (Lab)

My Lords, with government backing, the British Business Bank has helped many firms get started. Yet, when young firms seek to scale, borrowing or raising equity can be very costly and extremely risky. For founders without family wealth, that can mean putting their homes on the line. What more can my noble friend the Minister do to encourage UK banks and pension funds to back these businesses and stop the UK becoming an incubator for foreign investors?

Turn 6 · Lord Leong (Lab)

My Lords, my noble friend raises an important challenge. Britain must not simply start great businesses, which we do, only to see them scale and succeed overseas. We are expanding the British Business Bank’s capacity to £30.6 billion, strengthening growth capital and working to unlock greater pension fund investment through the Mansion House reforms. We are introducing a mandatory code of conduct for growth guarantee scheme lenders so that personal guarantees are used fairly. Our ambition is to help British businesses start, scale and stay here.
