Turn 129 · The Prime Minister

Is it not good enough to have M&S Food, Morrisons, Tesco, Aldi and Lidl all coming out and saying that this deal will lift the pressure off the weekly shop? I could show the hon. Member a photo of what Morrisons showed me follows every shipment; from that, it is obvious that this deal will reduce cost pressures, and that will be passed on to the public. Conservative Members should be asking not how much these measures will remove from bills, but by how much their resistance in the House to backing what we are doing will put food bills up.

Turn 130 · Alistair Strathern (Hitchin) (Lab)

In a dangerous world, relations with our largest trading partner must be governed by our national interests, not nostalgia or narrow ideology. Unfortunately, the deal we inherited from the last Government failed on every single one of those terms. The Conservatives were too afraid of the siren voices from Reform to do right by the British people then; regrettably—and I do mean that—it sounds like they remain afraid of those voices. I am particularly excited by the opportunities on the table for young people, but the Prime Minister will know that in the past not every young person—that includes our care leavers—was able to fully take advantage of them. Can we kick-start a conversation about how we ensure that every young person in Britain can benefit from the opportunities he has announced today?
