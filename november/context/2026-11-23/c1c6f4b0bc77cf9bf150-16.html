Turn 126 · Speaker unlabelled

This amendment seeks to ensure that a single patient record should embrace relevant social care data and that social care providers and people drawing on care are represented in its design. The challenge here, partly touched on by the noble Lord just now, is that a single patient record will need to have all the medicines, treatments and everything else, but social care is not the same. There is a disconnect between the things that people say define a good life—safety, continuity, control, relationships and belonging—and the indicators that are used to track performance, which focus more on episodes of care, process compliance and crude activity counts. The challenge therefore is how we have a single patient record that can recognise those issues.

Turn 128 · Speaker unlabelled

So there are two challenges that I would like my noble friend to reflect on. The first is to ensure that the relevant social care information forms part of the single patient record, which supports a more complete picture of people’s needs, care and support, particularly at discharge, reassessment and other transitions between services. We all know how important transitions are. The second is to embed co-production in the design of a single patient record. People who draw on care and support alongside adult social care providers should help to determine what information is captured, how it is used and how it supports people’s outcomes, choices and experience.
