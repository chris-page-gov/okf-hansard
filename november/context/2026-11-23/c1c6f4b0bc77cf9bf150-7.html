Turn 49 · Speaker unlabelled

I said that I would mention numbers. For 2024-25, it appears that 9.1 million individual patients received antidepressants. I was challenged by a noble friend during pleasure—the short break that we had earlier—so I went back to an authoritative source, who reassured me that it was 9.1 million individual patients. I find that very surprising and feel inclined to check it again; maybe the Minister will be able to be clearer on that. That was for over-19s but, in addition, there were 187,000 people under the age of 19. These are very large numbers and lots of studies show that antidepressants do not benefit some or many of the patients for whom they are prescribed.

Turn 50 · Speaker unlabelled

Turn 51 · Speaker unlabelled

We are at a point where we really need to get a grip on this, because the numbers are going up significantly year by year. It is important to point out that these are, to put it another way, dangerous, dependency-creating drugs. For those of us lay people—I needed to have this described to me—it is not that people are addicted to these drugs, but rather that they are dependent on them because the drugs affect the chemistry of the brain and the way that it works. These are very important, and there is an obvious parallel to be drawn between this and the consent that is required for any operation, even the simplest.

Turn 52 · Speaker unlabelled

What I am proposing in this amendment is pretty drastic: requiring all clinicians to do this and to document the fact that they are doing it. I suspect that most clinicians do it to a significant extent anyway, so it is a fairly simple amendment. To sum up, these drugs are very useful when prescribed to the right patients in the right way. There are potentially serious side-effects. These are dependency-forming drugs; there are significant problems coming off them. There are alternatives for many of these patients. There are direct costs here, which are pretty huge, but there are also hidden costs in, for example, productivity or people’s ability to work.

Turn 53 · Speaker unlabelled

More importantly, perhaps, there are enormous hidden costs for patients who are on these drugs. Given where we are in the debate, I will not bring out patient stories, but I have a number of examples of people who talk about the tragedy of losing years of their lives while they are on these drugs and trying to come off them. It is certainly costing those individuals money as well. This amendment is not about stopping prescribing these drugs but about appropriate levels of prescribing.
