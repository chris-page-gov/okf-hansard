Turn 9 · Mr Brash

According to a recent report by Impetus, young people in Hartlepool are at the fourth highest risk in the country of becoming NEET. Does the Secretary of State therefore agree that we must implement the Milburn recommendations in full, including reduced employer national insurance contributions for young people aged 21 to 24, and will he urge the Chancellor and other Cabinet colleagues to act as quickly as possible to ensure that we do so?

Turn 10 · Pat McFadden

With regard to national insurance, I repeat what I said to the right hon. Member for Chingford and Woodford Green (Sir Iain Duncan Smith) a few moments ago: there is an existing tax break in the system for employees under the age of 21, and Alan Milburn was clear that the problem of youth inactivity long predates any recent changes to national insurance.

Turn 14 · Pat McFadden

I thank my hon. Friend for his question —there was a lot in it. He is right to draw attention to the work that he and his local college have been doing, and I am grateful to him for briefing me on that. The issue of 16 and 17-year-olds is important because they should be participating in education—we should not have 16 and 17-year-olds in that position. On future jobs, I made reference to the agreements we have recently reached with several other sectors. On apprenticeships, we are trying to tilt the funding more towards young people, for the obvious reason that they have their whole lives in front of them, and I think the public expect that apprenticeship funding should go more towards young people trying to get their first opportunity in life.
