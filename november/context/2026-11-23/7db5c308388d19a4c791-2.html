Turn 9 · Baroness Thornhill (LD)

My Lords, many women who become homeless do so because they are fleeing domestic violence, yet we know that such women are then placed in mixed accommodation, where they can face further harassment, intimidation and even be assaulted. What exactly is holding the Government back from expanding nationally the Housing First initiative, which was very successful in the pilot, so that those vulnerable women can access the safety and dignity of a secure home of their own?

Turn 10 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

The noble Baroness will be aware that I am a great advocate of Housing First—I delivered a Housing First scheme myself when I was leader of the council in Stevenage. Stable accommodation, alongside that personal support that I was speaking of just now, can provide an important route away from entrenched rough sleeping for people with multiple and complex needs, and housing-led approaches can help people establish that stable home while receiving support tailored to their circumstances. As the noble Baroness indicated, for women, that support should reflect risks, including domestic abuse, violence, exploitation and previous trauma, and local areas are best placed to determine how it will work in their area.

Turn 12 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

We recognise that women leaving prison can face distinct and interconnected barriers to securing accommodation. That is why we are combining wider action to improve prison leaver accommodation with targeted support for women. A minimum of 10% of beds across the community accommodation service are allocated to women in women-only properties, and we are strengthening prevention and planning before release. Ten of the 52 prison-based strategic housing specialists work across the women’s estate to identify needs and barriers earlier and to improve accommodation planning ahead of release.

Turn 14 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

I recognise the noble Lord’s great expertise in this area. He is right: domestic abuse affects the whole family. The work I have done on domestic abuse indicates that, even pre-birth, foetuses can be affected by the chemicals that result from violence in the home, so it is very important that we tackle this. We are developing a domestic abuse and homelessness prevention toolkit to improve the consistency of support from councils for victims and survivors of abuse, drawing in the evidence of best practice—and there is a great deal of it—in keeping survivors safe in their homes. We need to ensure that victims of domestic abuse are automatically in priority need. That means councils must offer emergency accommodation while they assess their homelessness applications.

Turn 16 · Baroness Taylor of Stevenage (Lab)

That is a really important point. The local rough sleeping count has just been undertaken. The experienced outreach workers who are used work with homelessness-experienced women to make sure that they understand that this is not a straightforward issue of going around the normal places that may be used by rough sleepers and counting them; they need to take account of the actual need that is occurring and the huge range of places of safety that women will use. The rough sleeping guidance is clear that we should ensure that women experiencing rough sleeping can access safe, appropriate support. That includes trauma-informed approaches and access to safe accommodation.
