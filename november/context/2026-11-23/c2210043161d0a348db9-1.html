Turn 1 · The Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs (Ed Miliband)

With permission, I will make a statement on Myanmar. On 19 November, the International Court of Justice found that the Myanmar regime had committed genocide against the Rohingya people. The judgment represents the first finding of state responsibility for genocide since the 1948 convention on the prevention and punishment of the crime of genocide, and it lays bare what the actions of the Myanmar regime meant for the Rohingya: indiscriminate and targeted killing of civilians including those fleeing their villages, summary executions, widespread sexual violence, and the burning of people and homes. Turn 6 · Emily Thornberry (Islington South and Finsbury) (Lab)

On days like this, it is tempting to see the very worst of humanity, but my reaction to this judgment was to be moved by The Gambia. It is a country whose people are unlikely to have frequently visited Myanmar or even to have met anyone from there, but who stood up in the highest court in the world, argued in defence of a persecuted people and demanded that there should be justice. It was an act of international solidarity that, frankly, we need to see more of. Does the Foreign Secretary agree that the ICJ’s vital work in coming to this determination serves as a reminder that, despite the continued attack on international law—including today, very disappointingly, by His Majesty’s Opposition, which is a new low—justice and truth will find a way? Frankly, the brave men and women who worked to achieve the determination deserve our full support.
