Turn 241 · Carla Lockhart (Upper Bann) (Ind)

Last week, the Secretary of State for Northern Ireland refused to pull the Stormont brake, despite all Unionist Members of the Legislative Assembly backing its use. Northern Ireland businesses, farmers and consumers remain under the jackboot of EU law, and the Irish sea border posts are still standing. When the Prime Minister struck his deal with Brussels, did he secure a date for dismantling those border posts, or did he simply agree to put the rest of the United Kingdom under the same EU rules that Northern Ireland has been left under? When are the border posts coming down?

Turn 242 · The Prime Minister

We will look carefully at the implementation of this deal and how it affects Northern Ireland. I am working with the Northern Ireland Secretary to address ongoing issues relating to the budget. We all have to take care to work with each other, at this moment, to deal with the issues that matter to the hon. Lady’s constituents and to people across Northern Ireland—and we will, as I have said in the House this afternoon. May I conclude by saying that we have had a good exchange this afternoon, but I think it is important that we move forward in a slightly better way? I ask this of everyone, on all sides. This is definitely one for problem solving, not point scoring. Let us take a practical mindset to this deal. Let us get the best deal we can for Britain, and then we will all be able to enjoy the benefits in our constituencies.
