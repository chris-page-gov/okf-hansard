Turn 49 · The Prime Minister

I want to praise my right hon. Friend for the groundwork that he, too, laid for this deal—he possibly did more on the detail than anybody, and we really appreciate what he has done. He is right that we need to act swiftly to show our intent, which is why I have asked the Chief Whip to put the European partnership Bill before this House before the end of this week, and we will be looking to have Second Reading this side of the Christmas break. We want to get on with this—we want to get it in place to make sure we meet the deadlines of the deal.

Turn 50 · Esther McVey (Tatton) (Con)

With regard to the Prime Minister’s UK-EU deal on freedom of movement for young people, the UK wanted the number to be capped at under 50,000 a year. The EU wanted that number to be 100,000 a year. Talking of freedom of movement—if that is how what happened in No. 10 today can be described—perhaps the Prime Minister’s time would be better spent polishing up his negotiating skills, rather than concentrating on the choreography of a dance routine of a TikTok influencer.

Turn 51 · The Prime Minister

My goodness, that must have looked good when the right hon. Lady did it in front of the mirror in her office, but I am not sure about this Chamber. I find it hard to take lessons from the party of the Boris wave—she was right in the middle of all of that. The Conservatives let net migration get out of control, and the point they miss completely is that when young people come from Europe to study here, they develop an attachment to this country; they have a lifelong affiliation to it that continues in Britain’s interests, the same as when young Brits go abroad. I thank the right hon. Lady for that performance, but I think she should spend a bit more time in front of the mirror next time.

Turn 53 · The Prime Minister

When I look at what has happened in the last decade—not just Brexit, but covid, the invasion of Ukraine and all the other challenges—we have to ask: have we been put in a stronger position? Were some of our enemies not both developing plans to invade other countries and seeking to weaken the democratic resilience of other European countries? That is exactly what we have been living through in the last decade. As we go into the next, we should have our eyes wide open to all those threats. Those of like mind—the democracy that we are and the club of democracies that we were once part of—should work together, because it is in our common interest to do so, whether economically or for defence and security. My right hon. Friend is absolutely right, as is customary, to put it in the way that he does.
