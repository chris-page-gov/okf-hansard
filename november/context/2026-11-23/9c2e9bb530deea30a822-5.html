Hansard speaking order. A reviewed question or reply has its own link. Open Hansard for the full wording of any excerpt.

Turn 33 · Dr Mullan

We had this debate in the Public Bill Committee. It is not about force, but about whether someone’s mouth can be taped to prevent them from shouting out. The Supreme Court in America has repeatedly confirmed that that is a reasonable use of force, but we have never introduced it, and it is not in any of the approved techniques. I presume that that is because in most other circumstances, it is not appropriate, but what is the harm in taping somebody’s mouth so that they cannot be disruptive during a court proceeding?

Turn 34 · Catherine McKinnell

I appreciate the hon. Gentleman’s passion on this issue, and the importance he places on these measures to ensure that justice is delivered for victims. However, the debate on these regulations is not the appropriate one in which to discuss further measures that may or may not be considered in policy on the use of force. His comments are on the record and will be noted. We have to ensure that any decision to use force is necessary, reasonable and proportionate. Those rules are clear, and training has to be delivered to make sure that the law of this country is operated within. I hope I have answered the hon. Gentleman’s questions, and that he can support these important regulations. Question put and agreed to.
