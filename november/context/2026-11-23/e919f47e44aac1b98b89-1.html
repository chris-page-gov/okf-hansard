Turn 1 · Paul Holmes (Hamble Valley) (Con)

On a point of order, Madam Deputy Speaker. This point of order relates to the timeliness of ministerial correspondence. On 16 August, I sent an important and time-sensitive case to the Ministry of Justice, which transferred it to the Department of Health and Social Care on 2 September. My team has consistently chased up the Department for a response, because my constituent is now quite rightly frustrated and questioning the efficiency of my team, rather than the Department at hand. I am sorry to bother you and the House with a point of order regarding this, and I know that you are not responsible for ministerial correspondence, but could you advise me how I can chase the Department through other means in order to get a much-needed response to my constituent?

Turn 3 · Dr Al Pinkerton (Surrey Heath) (LD)

On a point of order, Madam Deputy Speaker. Earlier, during the Prime Minister’s statement on the UK-EU summit, the Leader of the Opposition claimed that the UK already has “the deepest trade agreement any country has with the EU”. As you will know, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein are all members of the European economic area, which gives complete and untrammelled access to the EU single market. Switzerland’s European Free Trade Association arrangements follow close behind, and Turkey has a customs union with the European Union. Madam Deputy Speaker, I am sure that you and the House would agree that that long list of exceptions is perhaps rather too long for the Leader of the Opposition’s point to stand. As a former Trade Secretary, and someone who has congratulated herself on never making gaffes, I am sure she would welcome the opportunity to clear up this wholly uncharacteristic error …
