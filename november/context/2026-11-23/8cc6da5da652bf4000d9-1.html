Turn 2 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Work and Pensions (Lilian Greenwood)

Universal credit is primarily reserved for people settled in the UK. Overall, the proportion of universal credit claimants who are foreign nationals has fallen since August 2025, from 16% to 15.4% in August 2026. That drop comes at a time when the Government are tightening the routes to settlement and rewarding those who make the strongest economic or public service contributions.

Turn 3 · Jerome Mayhew

Once indefinite leave to remain is granted to a foreign national, they can claim universal credit on the same terms as a British citizen. Last year, foreign national households received almost £12 billion in universal credit, which is a 25% increase in a single year. The Home Secretary tried to toughen the rules, but she was too weak to win her battle with the Labour Back Benchers. Her failure opens the door to hundreds of thousands of new applications for indefinite leave to remain and, thereafter, universal credit. The cheques are written by Labour, but they are paid for by taxpayers. How much will the Home Secretary’s U-turn cost our taxpayers?

Turn 4 · Lilian Greenwood

The Boriswave would have allowed 1.9 million more people to become settled in the UK, which would have significantly increased welfare spending. This Government have taken action to reduce that number through the earned settlement changes, as he will know, and that will save billions of pounds.
