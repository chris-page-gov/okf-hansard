Turn 13 · The Prime Minister

I would describe it as good progress. I would also say to the Chair of the Foreign Affairs Committee that this is a future-facing deal. The Leader of the Opposition was at the CBI just now, saying that Europe is “not the market of the future”. She repeated something similar at the Dispatch Box. Again, I say to the Opposition that this is our largest market—40% of our exports, 50% of our imports. On what basis do we turn away from it, and say that it is not a priority? She is finding any reason she can to dismiss it, but the Opposition would start to get the ear of the country again if they were to work with us, and get this Bill through the House.

Turn 16 · The Prime Minister

This deal is not a prelude to anything. It is a pragmatic approach, and it will continue to be a pragmatic approach. If it is in Britain’s national interest, I will recommend it to this House. If it is not, then I will not. There is no fixed opinion here; there is no fixed destination. As I said to the House a moment ago, Europe could change again in the next year or 18 months, and obviously we will have to watch how life changes, but where Britain can make progress, surely we should make progress. Can anyone in the Opposition parties, or indeed the right hon. Gentleman, say that the last decade has been good enough for Britain? Well, I cannot. It has not been good enough—for our young people, or for anybody. Where we can make that better, we should.
