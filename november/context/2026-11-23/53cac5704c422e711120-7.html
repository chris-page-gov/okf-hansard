I am glad that the hon. Lady asked the question, because we have set that alternative out repeatedly over the last six to eight months. We have called for an essential energy guarantee. It would use existing Department for Work and Pensions data, which would be shared with energy companies, as well as the child benefit data used by His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs. By combining that data, the Government could reduce the cost of essential energy, using an analysis provided by Ofgem every three months of what counts as average energy use. Turn 51 · Daisy Cooper

It is my understanding that work has been under way on that in Government for several months, and that the collation of the data was supposed to be complete by this autumn. I have not seen a Government update on that, but those data sets already exist. The Government could have used that data to provide a more targeted scheme that provided an average of £100, while high energy users could have received up to £180. That is another way that this could have been done. It would have been a social tariff—something that I believe a number of Labour Members have supported. This is a mechanism the Government could have used. It is unfortunate that some people will lose out, and that the measure is, as a number of think-tanks have said, unnecessarily untargeted. It could have been better targeted if the Government had used the data they already have.
