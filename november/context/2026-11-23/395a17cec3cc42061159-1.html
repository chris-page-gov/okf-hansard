Turn 4 · Baroness in Waiting/Government Whip (Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath) (Lab)

My Lords, the Government reviewed sectors across the economy to identify industrial strategy sectors and their underlying frontier industries. This review included food and drink manufacturing. Sectors were assessed on growth and their contribution to the strategic objectives in respect of regional inequality, net zero and economic resilience. They were assessed not only on their economic potential but on the ability for government policy to tackle their barriers in a cost-effective way.

Turn 5 · Lord Smith of Ranmoor (Lab)

I thank my noble friend the Minister for that Answer. I declare my interest as chairman of Cranswick plc and a non-executive director of Pret. The farming, food and drink sector is not shy about describing its current scale. It has end-to-end sales of £329 billion and is proud of its 5.2 million total food chain employees. It identifies the opportunity and ambition to grow annually at around 3% and to shift UK self-sufficiency from around 60% to a solid 70% by 2040. That growth is to be spread widely across the nation—so, growth, resilience and devolution. In attracting investors, they are keen to see capital deployed to where they perceive the Government have highlighted established priorities. Does my noble friend agree that being a part of the 10-year plan would assist in achieving that objective?

Turn 6 · Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath (Lab)

I recognise that long-term certainty from government helps businesses to invest with confidence. Food and drink is our largest manufacturing sector, rooted in communities across the UK, and has an important role in delivering growth, resilience and good jobs. Our focus is on creating the conditions for investment through long-term stability, regulatory certainty, innovation and skills, while continuing to work with industry on how its ambitions support our wider long-term growth plans.

Turn 7 · Lord Harper (Con)
