Turn 9 · Lord Leigh of Hurley (Con)

My Lords, one of the most important sources of finance for small businesses is the enterprise investment scheme, but that scheme was restricted by EU state aid rules. Now that we are out of the EU, will the Minister have a look at the restrictions, which include—since he mentioned it—restrictions on family investment, to widen the scope of investment into the EIS? Will he assure us that, as the Government seek to get closer to EU rules, this will not impede on our enterprise investment schemes?

Turn 10 · Lord Leong (Lab)

First, let me say that tax-advantaged investment schemes are vital to unlocking private capital. In 2024-25, EIS raised something like £1.575 billion, SEIS, which is really important for start-ups, raised some £276 million, and VCTs raised £881 million, collectively mobilising something close to £2.7 billion. That is vital for businesses and start-ups. These schemes demonstrate how patient capital can transform promising start-ups into successful businesses, driving innovation, jobs and economic growth across the country. This Government will be keeping those schemes.

Turn 12 · Lord Leong (Lab)

The noble Lord raises a couple of points. I recognise the frustration of many small businesses struggling to secure affordable finance. The start-up loan is one of the success stories: one in five small businesses gets a loan to start their business. As for pension funds, the Government have a clear ambition to connect more of the UK’s long-term savings into productive investment. The first Mansion House reforms update, which was published last month, says that accord signatories have invested £22.1 billion in private markets globally, including £11 billion in UK private markets. We are ensuring that private UK pension funds are also investing in UK businesses.

Turn 14 · Lord Leong (Lab)

My Lords, ethnic minority entrepreneurs make an enormous contribution to our economy yet face persistent barriers to finance. We are strengthening community lending through CDFIs and our £150 million Community ENABLE scheme, with another £165 million announced by the Chancellor in the Budget. Over one in five start-up loans supported ethnic minority founders last year. Our ambition is to have 85,000 loans over five years. Aston University estimates that removing those barriers would unlock £75 billion annually. This is an opportunity that Britain cannot ignore. I have said many times that talent is everywhere but opportunity is not.

Turn 15 · Lord Sharpe of Epsom (Con)

My Lords, in September 12 challenger and specialist banks, including Revolut, Monzo, Shawbrook and Paragon, warned the Chancellor that the bank corporation tax surcharge disproportionately affected mid-sized and specialist lenders, calling for the tax-free allowance to be increased from £100 million to £500 million. They highlighted that challenger and specialist banks now account for 60% of gross SME bank lending, yet the Chancellor not only rejected their request but announced that the surcharge would double, from 3% to 6%. Can the Minister explain why the Government have chosen to increase taxes on the very lenders financing small businesses, when doing so risks restricting access to the finance that those businesses need to grow?

Turn 16 · Lord Leong (Lab)

My Lords, the challenger and specialist banks are transforming SME lending, as the noble Lord rightly said. Some 60% of all lending to SMEs is coming from challenger banks. As to the point about the surcharge, I need to come back to the noble Lord once I get further information from my officials. Such banks have provided some £40 billion in new lending, exceeding the £27 billion provided by the five largest banks. This is a good story, and we must support the challenger banks in supporting SMEs.
