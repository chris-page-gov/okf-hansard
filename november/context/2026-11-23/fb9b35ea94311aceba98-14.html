Turn 106 · The Prime Minister

The details are to be worked up, but my hon. Friend is absolutely right that the deal restores to young people freedoms that were lost at the time of Brexit. As I said, they have in many ways been the biggest losers. Many people in this House took those freedoms for granted when they were young, but have gladly removed them from the next generation. I think young people can have some hope with this deal that things that might give them the chance to broaden their horizons will once again be possible.

Turn 108 · The Prime Minister

I am aware of the wonders of Glastonbury cider, and I want it to have the widest audience possible. I would say to the hon. Lady that it is one step at a time. We have to be careful at every stage to ensure that we are in tune with public opinion, which, in my view, is telling us that the public want a deal of this kind. In a recent poll, 73% said that they want closer ties with Europe. That is what the people are saying. They have not been heard by the Opposition, but we will not ignore them. We will continue to take that pragmatic approach going forward.

Turn 111 · The Prime Minister

I do think the deal will bring growth in every one of those postcodes—good growth. Scotland stands to gain greatly from this deal, as it can face the longest export journeys; when we consider perishable products that could be spoiled by delays at the port, this deal is clearly in my hon. Friend’s constituents’ interests. I remember that she stood up in this House and spoke of constituency examples of businesses with containers stuck at the port, which cannot be in their interests. I believe that this deal will remove those delays.
