Turn 47 · Speaker unlabelled

Antidepressants carry the risks of suicidal impulses, suicide and sexual dysfunction, among many others. The problems with antidepressants also include how you get off them, how you withdraw from them and the impacts of that. The evidence suggests that somewhere between 30% and 50%—I appreciate that that is a big range—of people have difficulty in coming off antidepressants at the end of a course of treatment or when they choose to do so. For people who have been on antidepressants for over a year, 15% have serious problems with coming off them. This is a big issue.

Turn 48 · Speaker unlabelled

There is already guidance from NICE, as I am sure the Minister will remind us, which recommends that healthcare professionals discuss the benefits and harms with patients. However, the existence of guidance is not enough, and it is clearly not working. I and others who have been contacted by patients have received many examples from patients who felt that they were never warned about the problems that they might encounter, either with the drugs or indeed coming off them. The recent MHRA review recommended strengthening the warnings on the package in which your prescription comes, but I believe that this still does not go far enough. It should be required that patients are given the full picture about both the potential harms and the benefits of the antidepressants before they make a decision.
