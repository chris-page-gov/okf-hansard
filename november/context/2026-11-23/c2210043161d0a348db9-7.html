Turn 49 · Mr Adnan Hussain (Blackburn) (Ind)

I, too, welcome the Foreign Secretary’s acknowledgment of the ICJ ruling and hope that Britain will now help to secure justice for the Rohingya. Given the seriousness of the situation in Gaza and the risk of genocide, will the Foreign Secretary expressly confirm whether this Government recognise that there is a serious risk of genocide? As a signatory to the genocide convention, Britain has a legal obligation to take all reasonable measures within its power to prevent genocide once that risk is known. Does the Foreign Secretary genuinely believe that sanctions against individuals and entities involved in violence and settlement activity on the west bank, alongside statements calling for humanitarian aid to enter Gaza, are sufficient to discharge that obligation? If not, what further action will the Government commit to?

Turn 50 · Ed Miliband

As the hon. Member will know, we announced a comprehensive set of measures on 8 September, including a new sanctions regime relating to the illegal settlements. We announced further action on Gaza after the extension of the global human rights regime, and have rallied the international community to shine a spotlight on what is happening in Gaza, and to seek to drive aid in. I undertake to him that we will do absolutely everything we can to relieve the suffering in Gaza. As I said in my statement, genocide is a finding that comes from a competent court, and there is a case that is being considered by the ICJ.

Turn 51 · Luke Akehurst (North Durham) (Lab)

I welcome the Foreign Secretary’s statement and welcome the ICJ’s ruling that the military junta controlling Myanmar has been committing genocide against the Rohingya. Sadly, although that is the most revolting of its crimes, it is just the tip of the iceberg of the ways that it is repressing other ethnic and religious minorities, and committing human rights abuses against its political opponents in Myanmar’s diverse society. It is disappointing that until today, the UK had not introduced new sanctions on the military regime for two years. I welcome the announcement of the sanctions on jet fuel for use by the Burmese military, but will the Foreign Secretary look at further sanctions against the Burmese state banks, the oil and gas extraction industry that funds its war machine, and the communications network that the Government use?
