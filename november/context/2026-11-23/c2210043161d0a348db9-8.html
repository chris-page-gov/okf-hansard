Turn 57 · Pam Cox (Colchester) (Lab)

I thank the Foreign Secretary for his sobering statement on this landmark judgment. I visited Myanmar in 2013; even at that early point, I saw violence rising against the Rohingya people, which has escalated so terribly since. Since becoming an MP, as part of the British Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union, along with others in the Chamber, I have had the privilege of meeting some of Myanmar’s exiled politicians, who continue to work for justice at great personal risk. Would the Foreign Secretary enable a dialogue with those brave exiled politicians as part of his work on the ICJ judgment?

Turn 58 · Ed Miliband

I thank my hon. Friend for that question; yes, I would very much like to do that. When I met the representative of the Rohingya this morning in my office, he asked me whether I would meet some of the community in this country, and I said that I absolutely would. My hon. Friend is right to draw attention to her experience in 2013; reading the report, it is clear that, unfortunately, this genocide was a long time coming. Well before 2017, there was a systematic pattern of oppression of the Rohingya people, which led up to the terrible events of that year.

Turn 60 · Ed Miliband

I thank my hon. Friend for his question; he is so right about early warning systems across the international community on atrocity prevention and the actions we need to take. The judgment that the Court has found should drive us all to think about how we can do better at atrocity prevention, working with international partners, rather than having to deal with the appalling consequences of a genocide nine or 10 years after the event.

Turn 61 · Tony Vaughan (Folkestone and Hythe) (Lab)

I strongly welcome the Foreign Secretary’s statement and the leadership he is showing on the international rule of law. The need for accountability of the perpetrators of these atrocities is fundamental for victims. That includes Myanmar’s military leader, for whom I believe the ICC has a pending arrest warrant. However, that Court is under threat. I recently met the vice-president of the International Criminal Court, who explained to me the personal impact on judges and officials, which holds them back from doing their duties. What steps can the Foreign Secretary take, working with our European allies, to protect the international rule of law, and particularly to protect the International Criminal Court from attack so it can get on with doing its job?

Turn 62 · Ed Miliband

I am really glad that my hon. and learned Friend raised that point. The threat to the ICC is real. I am working with my Dutch counterpart and others to seek to protect the Court. We have expressed our strong disagreement with our US allies on this question. The Under-Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs, my hon. Friend the Member for Bridgend (Chris Elmore) will be at the Court next week, and I undertake to do everything I can to protect the work of the Court, because it is so important. Lastly, I say to all Members in this House who take an interest in these issues that I undertake with the team of Ministers to keep working on them. This cannot just be a one-off moment when we react to the judgment. I recognise our responsibilities to the people of Myanmar and the Rohingya.
