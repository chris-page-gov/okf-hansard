State Pension: Impact of Ending the Triple Lock

Turn 9 · Baroness Bowles of Berkhamsted (LD)

My Lords, with inflation rising again and real wages stagnating, we have the ingredients for the ratchet effect on the triple lock before 2030 to be particularly pronounced. There is nothing that the Minister can do about that, but it will be newer pensioners who capture that ratchet while the oldest and poorest on pre-2016 CPI-linked SERPS do not get that increase. They will continue to fall further behind. Is it not time to switch catch-up to those oldest, poorest pensioners, starting by using data matching to automatically award unclaimed pension credit?

Turn 12 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My Lords, when we talk about the state pension, we are looking forward not to the next five years but to the next 20 or 30 years; the savings are projected across that period. It has been important to make sure that the state pension reached an appropriate level. As I say, it will continue to rise in line either with CPI or by 2.5%, but we will then look at the record level that it is expected to be at by 2029-30 and make sure it carries on being at that share of average earnings for the future. It will then continue to keep pace with the rates of increase of those paying for it.

Turn 14 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My noble friend is right to point out that my position and that of the Prime Minister are the same and always will be, just for the record—whatever the question, to be really clear. On my noble friend’s point: the state pension, being a universal benefit, will go right across the income scale, but he is absolutely right that we need to make sure that it is sustainable. The reason why we are adjusting the triple lock is to make sure that it is sustainable, because we want it to be there in the future. Future generations will need it. It is important. This way, today’s pensioners get to benefit and tomorrow’s pensioners will know that the pension will be there waiting for them when they retire.

Turn 16 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My Lords, when we published the analysis, we also published information with projections looking at what would happen to pensioner poverty. The projections see it coming down from its current 14% to 8%. However, the key to tackling pensioner poverty cannot lie just in the state pension. That is why we introduced pension credit. It is why a Labour Government looked at the terrible rates of pensioner poverty in the early 2000s and said that we have to do something about it. Our job is to make sure that all those who may be eligible make a claim and get the money; we are doing everything we can to make that possible. I say to all noble Lords and anyone else who is listening: if you know a family member who may benefit from this, please encourage them to apply.
