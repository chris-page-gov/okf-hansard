Turn 18 · Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath (Lab)

My noble friend may be aware that regional growth is core to both reindustrialisation and the industrial strategy, which was developed in partnership with mayors, devolved governments and local leaders across the UK. This is because higher national growth and the success of the industrial strategy 8 will come only from unleashing the potential of places across the country. The industrial strategy aligns with mayors’ 10-year local growth plans to unblock delivery, increase investment and land major projects across the UK. Local leaders have a vital role in helping every corner of the country to showcase strength, and in delivering national priorities.

Turn 19 · Lord Lansley (Con)

My Lords, the industrial strategy 8 does not include food and drink manufacturing. As a consequence, food and drink manufacturing is not eligible for the British industrial competitiveness scheme, which would reduce intensive energy costs. That could represent a loss of £300 million or more in the potential reduction of energy bills for the food and drink sector. The sector is energy intensive, like other sectors, so why is it left out?

Turn 20 · Baroness Ramsey of Wall Heath (Lab)

On energy costs, the British industrial competitiveness scheme applications are open. The scheme will reduce eligible manufacturers’ electricity costs by up to 25% from April 2027, with an additional payment in the first year. I repeat: advanced manufacturing is one of the eight industrial strategy priority sectors, and this benefits much of the food and drink industry.
