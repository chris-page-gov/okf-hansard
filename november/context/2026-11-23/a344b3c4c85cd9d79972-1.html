Turn 4 · The Minister of State, Department for Work and Pensions (Baroness Sherlock) (Lab)

My Lords, the Government have published analysis of the adjusted triple lock’s long-term impact, including on pensioner poverty and expenditure savings. Individual annual impacts depend on future inflation and earnings. We are adjusting, not removing, the triple lock. From April 2030, the state pension will increase by at least the highest of 2.5%, CPI inflation or more, if needed, to retain its forecast record high value relative to average earnings in April 2029.

Turn 6 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My Lords, I am not sure whether the noble Lord is referring to spending on pensions or on working-age social security. With pensions, the Government have made their intentions completely clear and will legislate to adjust the triple lock in the way I just described before the end of this Parliament. On working-age welfare spending, as the noble Lord knows, we have commissioned the Milburn review, which had reported, and the Timms review, which will report very shortly—I think that is the technical term in government-speak. Once we have both of those, we will respond. If there is any legislative change, there will be ample opportunity for the House to comment on it.

Turn 8 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My Lords, my noble friend is right in that, obviously, the amount any pensioner will get will depend on a whole range of factors. It will depend on inflation, wages and their individual circumstances, including their national insurance record. That is the case. We have, however, made it clear that, under this adjusted triple lock, the state pension will continue to rise every year. What it will not do is continue to constantly run ahead of wages, particularly the wages of the working people who are paying for it.
