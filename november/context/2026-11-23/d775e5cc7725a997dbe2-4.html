Turn 28 · Helen Whately (Faversham and Mid Kent) (Con)

For months, and yet again today, we have watched the Secretary of State extol the virtues of his youth guarantee. Well, the results from his trailblazers are in, and they are damning. In London, less than half of young people got into work, education or training. In the west of England, they could not even get young people to turn up. With the evidence staring him in the face, will the Secretary of State accept that his pet project has failed and, rather than pouring billions of pounds more of taxpayer money down the drain on subsidised work schemes, will he lighten the load on businesses, the real job creators of this country, so that they can give Labour’s lost generation a chance?

Turn 29 · Pat McFadden

I think that is a very revealing question. I watched Prime Minister’s questions last week, when the Leader of the Opposition attacked the youth guarantee, and now the shadow Secretary of State has nailed her colours to the mast by asking us to abandon it. Let me give her a very direct answer: we will not abandon it, because we believe in young people and want to support them. If the dividing line between us is that we support our youth guarantee to expand opportunity for young people and they would abandon it, then let us have the debate on those terms.
