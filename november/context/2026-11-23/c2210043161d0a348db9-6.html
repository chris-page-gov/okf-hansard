Turn 41 · Shockat Adam (Leicester South) (Ind)

As many Members have said, nothing quite prepares one for the kaleidoscope of emotions one feels when visiting Cox’s Bazar and meeting the Rohingya. I had the opportunity to do so with the all-party parliamentary group on Bangladesh, and one of the things that stood out for me was the young people asking for an education to better themselves, their community and their homeland when they eventually go back. We showed the best of who we were in 2022, when we opened our doors to Ukrainian students. Now that a genocide has been committed against the Rohingya, will we facilitate safe routes to education for young Rohingya survivors of genocide, or a way to invest in their camps, so that they can get an education there?

Turn 43 · Fleur Anderson (Putney) (Lab)

I too welcome the statement on the ICJ ruling of genocide by the Myanmar military regime, but the international community has to reflect on why these rulings take so long, and do not stop atrocities as they are happening. I welcome the Secretary of State saying that further action will be taken, and I have a few questions. First, why is only one diplomat from Myanmar being expelled, when all of the diplomats represent the military regime? Secondly, as other hon. Members have said, the aim of a genocide is to wipe out a people; that is why the education of young people in Cox’s Bazar is so important. It will train them, and enable them to return to Myanmar and have the best opportunities. It will also enable the Rohingya to stand up, as a people whom Myanmar’s military regime was trying to wipe out.
