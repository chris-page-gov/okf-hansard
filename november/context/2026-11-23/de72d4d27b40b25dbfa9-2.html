Levels of Welfare Spending

Commons · 2026-11-23 · Turns 9–10 of 10 in this section.

Hansard speaking order. A reviewed question or reply has its own link. Open Hansard for the full wording of any excerpt.

Turn 9 · Mr Peter Bedford (Mid Leicestershire) (Con)

It is disappointing that the Minister could not give my hon. Friend the Member for Berwickshire, Roxburgh and Selkirk (John Lamont) a straight answer to a legitimate question. Alan Milburn’s own figures show new spending of £5.8 billion a year, and after a decade, taxpayers might —might—save £200 million a year, with the Institute for Fiscal Studies calling this “highly uncertain”. Conservatives believe that welfare reform should save taxpayers money, not cost them more. The Government’s plans will mean higher taxes, more borrowing, or most likely both. Given that these reforms will not pay for themselves for at least a decade, who does the Minister expect to pick up the bill in the meantime?

Turn 10 · Andrew Western

The shadow Minister may be aware that the Prime Minister has already asked Departments to look to reprioritise spending, but I would tread carefully in relation to what the IFS has said about proposals. It was not the Government’s proposals that were described as having “significant uncertainties” but those of the Conservatives. Just for clarity, that is think-tank speak for “the Conservatives stuck their finger in the air long after losing the fag packet.”
