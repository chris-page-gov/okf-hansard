Turn 89 · Lord Babudu (Lab)

My Lords, I will speak briefly to my Amendment 279QJ. Like many amendments in this group and on earlier days, it aims to make the Health Bill better suited to enabling the 10-year health plan’s intended shifts—particularly the shifts from treatment to prevention and from hospital to community. My amendment is about prevention. It is modelled on the mental health reporting requirement the then Government added in 2022, after pressure from this House, which pushed for a statutory spending standard for mental health. Turn 91 · Lord Davies of Brixton (Lab)

I will speak to Amendment 113 in the name of the noble Baroness, Lady Tyler of Enfield, and the other amendments in this group that seek to strengthen and protect investment in mental health services across the NHS. It is important to understand that “investment” is not used here as a synonym for spending; it carries with it the implication of improvement as well as spending. I support the proposal to place the mental health investment standard on a statutory footing. The principle is straightforward. While mental health has been recognised as being of equal importance to physical health, funding has too often failed to reflect that aspiration. If we are serious about parity of esteem, we must ensure that mental health services are not left behind whenever NHS finances come under pressure.
