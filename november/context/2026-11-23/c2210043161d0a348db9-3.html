Turn 19 · Dr Rosena Allin-Khan (Tooting) (Lab)

Nothing prepared me for working on multiple occasions with the Rohingya as they fled over the border: dead bodies; babies burned on fires, with parents having to choose whether to jump in the fire to pull them out or run with the children still left alive; and mutilations on a scale I could never have imagined experiencing. I applaud the judges who bravely, in the face of a lot of pressure, did the right thing last week and called out what we all knew to be true: a genocide has been committed. Given that the perpetrators will stop at nothing until they finish what they have set out to do, may I ask the Foreign Secretary what our Government are doing to act at great haste to save as many lives as possible now that there will, I believe, be renewed and redoubled efforts to kill as many people as possible?

Turn 20 · Ed Miliband

My hon. Friend speaks very movingly about her experiences and I thank her for her work. My answer is that, in this moment, following the finding of genocide, we have to rally the international community and create the maximum possible pressure, on all countries, to say to the regime in Myanmar, “Enough is enough.” That is true of all countries, including all P5 members. That is my responsibility as the Foreign Secretary, and it is the responsibility of the UK Government and other Governments. I have been pleased at the extent to which Governments across the world are starting to rally on this issue—it is why we made a very early statement last Thursday—but I totally agree with her that there is much more to do. Frankly, in this case, the best thing we have at our disposal is shining the spotlight of the judgment harshly on what is happening to try to get change.

Turn 24 · Ed Miliband
