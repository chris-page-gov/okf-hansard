Turn 18 · Helen Hayes (Dulwich and West Norwood) (Lab)

I warmly welcome the Prime Minister’s willingness to reckon with the disastrous impacts that Brexit has had on our country’s economy and communities, and the work that he is doing to bring us closer once again to our friends and neighbours in the EU. The subject of university tuition fees is central to the deal that he is delivering. The Education Committee published a report earlier this year that documents the extreme financial precarity of many of our universities, which have the potential to be powerhouses of economic growth and opportunity. As the Prime Minister continues to progress negotiations with the EU, will he guarantee to do so in dialogue with our universities, in a way that bolsters their financial sustainability, rather than adding further pressures to it?

Turn 21 · The Prime Minister

The UK is not joining the EU digital identity wallet. This agreement does not commit us to doing so, or to adopting the EU’s digital identity system. It does propose a digital partnership, but that is to deepen co-operation across our digital and technology sectors. That is the reason why it was warmly welcomed by the European chief executive of Vodafone on Friday.

Turn 23 · Liam Byrne (Birmingham Hodge Hill and Solihull North) (Lab)

The bottom line is that the deal the Prime Minister has brought home is in our national interest. The Conservative party’s collective amnesia is something to behold. This deal is good for business; it is welcomed by business, and to oppose it is to oppose what is in business’s interest. I want to ask the Prime Minister about “Made in Europe”.
