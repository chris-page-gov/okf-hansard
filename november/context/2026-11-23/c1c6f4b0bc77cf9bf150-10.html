Turn 76 · Baroness Hollins (CB)

My Lords, I support the amendment from the noble Baroness, Lady Tyler, and her intention to put the mental health investment standard on a statutory footing. All the leading mental health charities—the Royal College of Psychiatrists, Mind, Rethink, the Centre for Mental Health and the Mental Health Foundation—have warned that the Government have weakened protections for mental health funding. It is a worry to me that the Bill contains so little about mental health. For years, the investment standard has safeguarded mental health funding, corrected historic underinvestment and kept mental health on the NHS agenda, despite competing pressures. I add my voice to ask the Minister when a satisfactory investment plan will be brought forward.

Turn 79 · Speaker unlabelled

I have spoken in this House before about the serious risk of reducing my profession to a call centre. GPs are far more than that; we are experts at managing undifferentiated problems. We do this 1 million times a day, in dealing with the biopsychosocial aspects of our patients. Access to general practice is often criticised and I will not pretend that access is perfect. Many patients still find it difficult to see us, and that matters, but we should recognise the extraordinary volume of care that GPs and their teams deliver: in the 12 months to February 2026, a record 388 million appointments. Analysis by the King’s Fund, reported this morning in the Daily Mail, found that the number of qualified GPs in England has increased by fewer than 200 since 2015, yet we have seen an enormous rise in the population. We cannot expect access to improve without investing in capacity.
