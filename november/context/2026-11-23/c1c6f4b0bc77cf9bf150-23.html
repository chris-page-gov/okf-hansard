Turn 179 · Speaker unlabelled

Thirdly, one of the rationales that is provided for the SPR is to provide a level of efficiency for clinicians. By having that one source of information, there may well be a range of questions which they can pick up immediately by way of answers and do not need to ask again. But if there is information that is excluded, it can lead to one of two situations: either a clinician will feel that this must have been asked at an earlier stage and that the information is not there, and therefore may not ask a question that may be of great relevance to how that patient is treated, or, alternatively, it slightly negates the purpose of short-circuiting the need for clinicians to ask again, and the same questions are simply asked a second time. We need to ensure how we tease that out.

Turn 180 · Speaker unlabelled

Fourthly, there is the issue around patient safety. We know that sensitive medical information can at times be just as valuable as other medical information. Therefore, potentially excluding it can lead to circumstances where patient safety is put at risk. We also know that there can be circumstances where sensitive information can act as a red flag to highlight where there are other problems. For example, some of the studies on domestic abuse have shown that it is some of that sensitive information that is available that can start to highlight to other agencies, and particularly to medics, where there are issues and flag up a particular problem. If that is something that is outside the remit of the single patient record, there can be problems with that.
