Turn 81 · Speaker unlabelled

The funding imbalance is impossible to ignore. In 2016, to much fanfare, the Government committed in the General Practice Forward View to funding for general practice growing at least twice as fast as for the rest of the NHS. Nearly 10 years on, the figures tell a very different story. Data from the Lords Library shows that funding for acute hospitals has risen by 46%, while for primary medical care it has risen by 24%. The share of funding going specifically to general practice is now at its lowest level for a decade. That is a failure of successive Governments; we have moved the patients without moving the resources.

Turn 82 · Speaker unlabelled

Earlier this month at the Select Committee, the Health Secretary acknowledged that the Government have not done enough to shift funding into primary care. That acknowledgement matters—it really does—but acknowledging the problem is not the same as putting a mechanism in place to address it. This amendment would move beyond another government commitment by establishing a measurable standard against which ICB investment decisions can be judged. This standard has broad support, including from the All-Party Parliamentary Health Group, the Royal College of GPs, the BMA and the Health Foundation.

Turn 86 · Speaker unlabelled

I anticipate that the Minister may argue that ICBs need flexibility to make decisions based on local needs, and I agree that flexibility over how money is spent is not the same as flexibility over whether primary care receives its fair share. This amendment would not dictate how ICBs distribute investment between primary care services; it would simply establish a minimum expectation that primary care shares in the growth of NHS funding. Without this mechanism, the Government’s ambition to shift care into the community risks remaining just that—an ambition.

Turn 88 · Speaker unlabelled

The founders of general practice believed that it should be an equal partner in our health service. Nearly 75 years later, that equality is not reflected in the way the NHS distributes its resources. This amendment offers a practical way of doing that. It would turn an aspiration into an obligation and a promise into something that is measurable. I hope the Government will support it.
