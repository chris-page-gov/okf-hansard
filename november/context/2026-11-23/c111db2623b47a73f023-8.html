Turn 58 · Speaker unlabelled

This is an important and proportionate amendment. It does not prescribe a particular model of service delivery, impose unnecessary bureaucracy or prejudge the outcome of an assessment. Instead, it asks the Government to establish whether the current arrangements are delivering what they were intended to deliver, and to act if they are not. We rightly ask about prevention, early intervention and improving the quality of healthcare, but we should not overlook the importance of learning from deaths. A health service that cannot reliably understand when, where and why people are dying will struggle to identify where things are going wrong and where improvements are needed.
