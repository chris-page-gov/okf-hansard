Turn 93 · Pam Cox

My hon. Friend makes the important point that the British courts have become the SLAPPs capital. That is all the more egregious because SLAPPs may seem like a modern issue for modern times, but they are linked to the historical and long-standing question of governance around the right to petition. SLAPPs can be used to bring legal action against those who seek to bring public interest matters to the Government of the day. Raising such matters with the Government is an important historical right, and one of the ways that was done in the past was by bringing a petition. In fact, the right to petition was introduced in the 10th century, before Magna Carta, which could mean that the right to speak truth to power preceded Magna Carta. Given that context and tradition, does he agree that the Bill protects a power that is central to our long-standing democracy?
