Turn 69 · Adam Thompson

My hon. Friend makes an incredibly important point, and I thank him for putting it on the record. Walliams and his team of lawyers have argued that to publish allegations made against them, to let the public know that he hurt women, would go against articles 2 and 3 of the European convention on human rights—that is, to name him as the person with allegations against them would be against his right to life and would not protect him from inhuman or degrading treatment. The aim was to use the law to stop victims speaking out, exactly as strategic litigation against public participation does. That is the kind of thing that we should focus on as we debate this legislation today.
