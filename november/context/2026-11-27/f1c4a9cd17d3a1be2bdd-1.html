Turn 2 · Helen Hayes (Dulwich and West Norwood) (Lab)

I am grateful for this opportunity to raise the issues affecting small businesses across the country that are based in railway arches and tenants of the Arch Company. Railway arches, created within the viaducts of the Victorian railway infrastructure that criss-crosses our towns and cities, have long provided convenient, affordable workspace for a wide range of different small and medium-sized businesses. The first Marks & Spencer shop in London was in a railway arch in Brixton. Arches are sometimes in town centres and unsuitable for retail and other high street businesses, but they are often located adjacent to town and city centre areas and are therefore suitable for essential businesses such as car mechanics and light manufacturing, which do not need a shopfront but are really relied on by those who live and work in the local neighbourhood. Turn 4 · Helen Hayes

I fear the Minister is taking a briefing from the Arch Company on the situation, and I must speak for Roger Hartley, with whom I am in regular contact. The redevelopment proposal to which he refers is an idea of the Arch Company. There is no independent, random third-party bearing down on the situation. The company has decided that it wants to do something more profitable with the space. The spaces in question are a unique network of arches. It is not easy to reaccommodate the organisation anywhere else, and what it has been offered so far simply will not work for it. The organisation is at risk as a result.
