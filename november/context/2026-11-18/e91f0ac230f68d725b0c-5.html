Turn 35 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

From the sound of it, the noble Lord has done as good a job of lobbying Alan Milburn as he does of lobbying me on UTC sleeves. The key point, which he has been arguing for many years, is the ability of young people to have that exposure from the age of 14 to a technical education that can show them the routes into work. That is precisely why the Prime Minister has already started us down a route of developing those technical routes from 14 to 16. It is why we are already reforming qualifications post 16 in order to ensure that those routes are available. The UTC sleeve is an important way in which that can happen, and we will certainly consider that as part of our response to developing those better routes to technical education for young people.

Turn 38 · Baroness Lister of Burtersett (Lab)

My Lords, Alan Milburn’s very welcome report emphasises: “Poverty and inequality are at the core of the NEET crisis”. In the long term, the hope is that his new settlement will reduce poverty. It is worth noting the big reduction in child poverty that has just been announced because of the abolition of the two-child limit. What assessment have the Government made of the impact on poverty in the shorter term? There are fears among disability groups not just that cuts to benefits for some disabled young people will cause more hardship but that that increased hardship will itself make it harder for them to take up the work or educational opportunities that are being opened up for them.
