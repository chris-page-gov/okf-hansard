Turn 108 · Lord McLoughlin (Con)

My Lords, the case has been made eloquently by my noble friends Lady Harding and Lord Young. It is incumbent on the Government to show why they find Amendment 16 unacceptable, when it says that GBR should be “showing separately the costs of, and revenues from, its online ticket retailing activities”. There is a fear that that money could be hidden in other departments, as opposed to what GBR is doing. If the Minister is trying to reassure us that he wants to see growth—and we have seen fantastic growth in the way that these companies employ people and offer a greater range of tickets across the spectrum—then he needs to give reassurance on this. At the moment, I fear the Bill does not give that reassurance which he seeks to give; this amendment would do it.

Turn 110 · Speaker unlabelled

While the Government’s amendment takes a welcome step towards ensuring that the systems underpinning GBR’s ticket retailing operate on a fair commercial basis, the amendments in the name of the noble Baroness, Lady Harding, and my noble friend Lord Dixon would go further by bringing full transparency to what GBR’s own retail operations cost and how this is paid for. Transparency is so important. Turn 111 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 10 would ensure that GBR’s online retailing is carried out on terms “no more favourable” than those of independent retailers and is not cross-subsidised by its other activities or by public funding. Proper cost and revenue records would back this up, along with an annual assessment published by the ORR. Amendments 16, 18 and 21 would carry that transparency through to the business plan and to the ORR’s advice on it and would make that advice public. Amendment 9 sets the principle but leaves the detail to an approved document that the ORR will settle. The noble Baroness’s amendments would put the core safeguards in the Bill itself, with a regular published check on cross-subsidy. That would give independent retailers and those who invest in them the assurance they need that they are competing on fair terms, not just at the outset but throughout the life of Great British Railways …
