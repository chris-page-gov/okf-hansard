Turn 33 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendments 65 and 72 would strengthen the duties of the passenger watchdog and the London Transport Users’ Committee respectively. I turn to the duties placed on those organisations. There is an obvious gap in the Bill: neither body is subject to the general duties set out in Clause 18, or even to the full passenger and disability duty that applies to Ministers, GBR and the ORR. If we look at Clauses 36 and 53, we see that the duties placed on those two bodies are extremely limited. There is an overarching requirement to act cost-effectively alongside a duty relating to disabled people, but there is no explicit duty to protect or promote the rights and interests of passengers, and I find that difficult to understand. Turn 34 · Speaker unlabelled

We are seeing two changes happening together. First, the Bill removes the requirement for railway licensees to have regard to general duties, and, secondly, it transfers responsibility for passenger-related standard setting from the ORR to a new watchdog that is not subject to those same duties. The result is that the connection between the general duties and the standards governing passengers’ rights and accessibility is weakened at both ends. This is not a sensible way to design a new system of passenger representation.

Turn 35 · Speaker unlabelled

My amendment would bring the duties into line with the changes I have proposed to Clause 18. It would create two separate and equivalent duties to protect and promote the rights and interests of passengers, and to protect and promote the rights and interests of disabled people. It would give everyone a consistent framework to work within. Can the Minister explain why the Bill has not provided for that, and whether the Government will consider bringing these duties into line? I appear to have run out of time; is there a time limit?
