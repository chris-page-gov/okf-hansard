Turn 51 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendments 40, 41 and 45, as I said in Committee, we are not making any changes to the safety regime in the Bill. Clause 19 replicates the existing safety duty in the Railways Act 1993 and does not extend to GBR, because GBR will already be under the existing comprehensive safety regime that governs our railways. As we discussed in Committee, the reason the ORR’s role as competition regulator is exempted from the safety duty is because it is a role that it carries out concurrently with the Competition and Markets Authority. These two regulators must be working under the same rules. This is a continuation of the current arrangements, which have worked effectively for decades. Importantly, this does not prevent the ORR considering safety when exercising its competition functions.

Turn 52 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, on the amendment from the noble Baroness, Lady Jones, on prioritising the safety functions of the ORR, I agree that safety must remain central to Britain’s railways, but I can assure her that her amendment is unnecessary. The ORR is already the independent safety regulator for the railways and acts in accordance with obligations set out in an extensive and powerful statutory framework. Nothing in the Bill weakens or diminishes those responsibilities. The ORR already discharges its responsibilities while maintaining high standards of safety, and there is no reason to think that will not continue.

Turn 56 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 131, the Government have already committed to applying the specific public sector equality duties under Schedule 2 to the Equality Act 2010 (Specific Duties and Public Authorities) Regulations 2017. However, we intend to do so not in primary but in secondary legislation after Royal Assent, using the powers available to the Secretary of State under Section 153 of the Equality Act 2010. This is to ensure that the equality duty applies correctly to GBR and its railway subsidiaries by waiting until GBR’s corporate structure is confirmed before making the regulations. I encourage the noble Baroness, Lady Jones, not to press her amendments in this group.
