Turn 9 · The Attorney General

I thank the right hon. Lady for that question. It is an issue close to my heart, because I was an employment rights lawyer for over a decade before entering this place, particularly representing women facing discrimination in the workplace. The Employment Rights Act 2025 adds new protections, and we have outlawed non-disclosure agreements in the workplace, but we will always look to see where we can go further to protect women and girls.

Turn 10 · Vikki Slade (Mid Dorset and North Poole) (LD)

Abuse rarely ends with the separation of a couple, with non-molestation orders ignored all the time. One of my constituents secured a restraining order against her ex-partner, but it took her 12-and-a-half years to fully extract herself. One of the biggest issues she faced was being economically attached to him and unable to leave their mortgage. Surviving Economic Abuse, which I have been working with on this, has made it clear that without a legal definition, prosecution of these behaviours is only achievable as a sub-category of coercive behaviour, so judges lack precedent and know-how to deal with it. Will the Attorney General take steps with Cabinet colleagues to produce a legal definition and training on how to prosecute this type of crime?

Turn 11 · The Attorney General

The hon. Lady makes a really important point about economic abuse being used as a form of violence against women and girls. I will be happy to speak to my colleagues in the Ministry of Justice about what more we might be able to do. We have introduced domestic abuse protection orders and stalking protection orders to make sure that we have the right tools to go after perpetrators, but I will discuss the points she raises with colleagues.

Turn 14 · The Attorney General

I agree with the Lord Chancellor that rape myths have no place in our courts or our society, and advocacy must not perpetrate myths that have been repeatedly shown to distort understanding of sexual violence. Judges can already give directions to counter rape myths. We are going further in the Courts and Tribunals Bill to stop irrelevant sexual history and previous allegations from being weaponised against victims.

Turn 16 · The Attorney General

The hon. Gentleman will be well aware of the Law Officers’ convention in relation to disclosing what advice may or may not have been given. But I will say this: the Government are determined to tackle violence against women and girls. When it comes to rape, we have announced specialist rape courtrooms in every court in the country, making sure that victims who bravely come forward get the support they need from the criminal justice system.
