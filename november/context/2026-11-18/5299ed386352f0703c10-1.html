Turn 2 · The Solicitor General (Andy Slaughter)

The Serious Fraud Office is delivering results. Over the past five years, the SFO has secured over half a billion pounds for the taxpayer, investigations announced in the last year included suspected criminality worth over £350 million, and two recently charged cases involved potentially 50,000 victims. I know that the right hon. Gentleman takes a legitimate and forensic interest in the important work of the SFO, and I am very happy to talk to him about this any time.

Turn 3 · David Davis

Last week, the Court of Appeal recommended that six victims of the miscarriage of justice in the Libor scandal should have money that was wrongfully taken from them returned with compound interest, yet the Serious Fraud Office appears unwilling to do that. The SFO is not just being ineffective in this; it is being immoral, piling injustice upon injustice. With a strict court deadline this Friday, will the Minister now direct the SFO to follow the express wish of the Court of Appeal, rather than wasting further public money in the courts?

Turn 4 · The Solicitor General

I do not think it is for me to direct the SFO in this matter. As the right hon. Member says, the Court of Appeal handed down its judgment last week—last Friday, I think. It is therefore reasonable, notwithstanding any timetable, that the SFO is still considering the matter. The court declined, as he says, to make a ruling on interest, but there are a number of elements to the case.

Turn 6 · Catherine Atkinson (Derby North) (Lab)

Fraud makes up nearly 50% of overall crime, and the Serious Fraud Office is the UK’s agency dedicated to tackling serious economic crime. But it needs powers comparable to those of the National Crime Agency to conduct surveillance and accompany police on searches to keep pace with increasingly complex economic crime and corruption at home and abroad. Its work protects people’s savings and the honest businesses on which jobs and prosperity depend. Will the Attorney General bring forward legislation to give the SFO the powers it needs to investigate these crimes more quickly and efficiently?

Turn 7 · The Solicitor General

This is the first opportunity I have had to answer a question from the new Chair of the Justice Committee, so may I begin by congratulating her on her new position—and on the fact that she is doing a much better job than her predecessor? [Laughter.] As evidenced by this question, she is absolutely right that the SFO needs more effective powers to investigate serious economic crime swiftly and effectively. We do keep those powers under review, and we will seek a suitable legislative vehicle to strengthen them for complex investigations in the future.
