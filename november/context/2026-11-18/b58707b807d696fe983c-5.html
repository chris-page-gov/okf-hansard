Turn 35 · Neil Coyle

The shadow Minister is very kind. A lot of the debate has focused on food so far. My constituency is home to Borough market, Bermondsey Street and the Roses greasy spoon, next to my constituency office, where they do not sell foie gras. My constituency does more for food in this country than many others, I will wager. The shadow Minister’s deal left Neal’s Yard at Bermondsey market paying £320 a week for produce certification that was not there before. That is damaging British jobs and British produce on a weekly basis. The shadow Minister is clearly not going to apologise today. Is his message to Neal’s Yard and other British producers to suck it up further, or will he congratulate this Government on trying to cut those costs and improve our economy?

Turn 36 · Mike Wood

I was clear earlier: we should be working with European Union, where there is a mutual benefit, for mutual recognition to reduce those unnecessary barriers. The way to do that is not to impose those same regulations on the 92% of food producers who do not export or import from the European Union, adding their costs to the same level as the exporters; it is to reduce the costs across the board. This is not the right way to do it.
