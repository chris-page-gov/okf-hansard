Turn 53 · Mr Falconer

The right hon. Gentleman goes back into a debate that roiled both the Conservative party and the Labour party in the 1970s. He talks of betrayal, and I would just caution him on that. I recognise that this is a very emotive subject but, because our constituents often take a lead from us, let us in this House try to avoid talk of betrayal and traitors, and the sort of antagonism that was such a feature of previous debates. We can legitimately disagree on these questions, and we can do so respectfully, and it is in that tone that I will try to conduct my interventions.
