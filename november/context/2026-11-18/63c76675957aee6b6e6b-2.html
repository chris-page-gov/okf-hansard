Turn 14 · Speaker unlabelled

I also stress that these provisions are intended primarily as a deterrent, with prosecution expected only as a last resort. Indeed, we are not aware of any prosecutions having been brought for these offences under previous legislation. To be clear, enforcement authorities can recover only the costs that they have incurred in connection with enforcing the advertising and trading offences under this Bill. Those costs cannot exceed those incurred by the authority. I assure your Lordships’ House that the Government will work closely with the sector, and is already doing so, to develop guidance on how these provisions will operate in practice. For the avoidance of doubt, this guidance will make it clear that cost recovery should not include prosecution costs and can be used only to recover the costs incurred through enforcing the Bill’s advertising and trading offences.

Turn 16 · Speaker unlabelled

The noble Lord, Lord Addington, raised the question of how and when businesses will find out how the provisions will affect them. I assure your Lordships’ House that businesses, and sector business organisations, will be supported to understand the effect of these provisions. Regulations must provide for guidance to be made available on the provisions. We expect local authorities to be under a duty to share this with licensed traders. We expect that guidance will be made available at least six months before an event takes place, with local businesses and communities engaged as regulations are developed.
