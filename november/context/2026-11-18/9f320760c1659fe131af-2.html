Turn 12 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

I am unaware of the specific details around the case mentioned in HMP Highpoint, but I take the point that CSCS cards are an incredibly important progression route for many people who are currently incarcerated. As I am sure the noble Baroness is aware, governors have localised commissioning powers so that they can commission specialist provision in terms of local education assessed against labour market needs. The Government are expanding provision to vocational training, prison industries and work-based learning so that people can secure employment on release. One example is through the future skills programme, which aligns training with employer demand and includes a guaranteed interview before release. I will happily write to the noble Baroness about that specific prison.

Turn 16 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

We recognise the issue of recall. It serves nobody if people are going into prison on very short sentences and then coming out. That is why we progressed the Sentencing Act through the other House and this House; it has gone on to statute and become law. There may be some learning-curve amendments from that, but fundamentally we are having to address the overcrowding crisis through the provisions of the Sentencing Act and using this progressive route to enable people to resettle into crime-free lives, of which education and employment—as we have already alluded to—are a key part.
