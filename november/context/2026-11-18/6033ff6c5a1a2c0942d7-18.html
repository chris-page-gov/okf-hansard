Turn 139 · Lord Young of Cookham (Con)

My Lords, I add a very short footnote to what my noble friend Lord Harper said about the prospects for the Department for Transport’s budget. The OBR publication published at the same time as the Budget projects that unprotected government departments, which is the Department for Transport, face real-terms day-to-day spending cuts of 2.6% per year during the final years of the forecast period. It is even worse than what my noble friend said, when he implied that it might be able to hang on to its existing budget. If the Government are to stay within their fiscal rules, there will be a real-terms spending cut of 2.6% each year.

Turn 141 · Lord Hendy of Richmond Hill (Lab)

My Lords, on Amendments 17, 20 and 38, while I agree with the noble Lord, Lord Moylan, that railways need to become more efficient with taxpayers’ money, I do not agree that these amendments would create the right incentive or generate the right behaviours in Great British Railways to achieve that. In my view, there is a tangible difference between minimising taxpayer subsidy and getting the best value for money out of the subsidy that the Government choose to put in. Minimising costs pushes the balance towards GBR cutting costs and raising fares, rather than seeking true efficiency and getting the most value out of every pound in the railway. The balance here is so important because of the knock-on impact that the imbalance can have on the longer-term prosperity of the railways and the wider social and economic benefits that the railway can provide.
