Turn 9 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

On Monday, I answered another question on tenancies and explained that we are working with, for example, the Duchy of Cornwall, and others, to ensure longer-term tenancies. That is a priority for the Government. That is the first point. Planning case law is also clear that personal circumstances and the hardship of people affected by development can be a material consideration in planning decisions. On that basis, the Government recognise that tenants should receive fair compensation if they lose land to non-agricultural development. The forthcoming Law Commission work is an important part of that to ensure that tenants are properly supported and able to be represented. It will explore statutory compensation provisions as well. We hope to see it as soon as we can.

Turn 10 · Lord Cameron of Dillington (CB)

My Lords, the land use framework was envisaged as a device to optimise our use of land, on this very small, overcrowded island, to ensure that we secure the best outcomes for all of us in terms of food, energy, forestry, health, habitats and, indeed, housing. Can the Minister tell me when the Government might consider publishing a report on what is actually happening on the ground versus the ideal of what a sensibly resilient nation might insist on or at least aspire to? If no such report is on the horizon, perhaps she could explain to us all how we are going to best use the land use framework to optimise our use of land.

Turn 11 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

The purpose of the land use framework is to optimise our use of land. There are so many different pressures—energy, food production, housing, recreation—that it is important that we have a proper overview and cross-departmental oversight. That is what the land use framework is designed to do. Regarding a report, as the noble Lord says, any policy that is coming into place needs to be properly observed and monitored as it moves forward to ensure that it is doing what it is designed to do. Nice words do not end in the results that we want to see. I suggest that the appropriate time to look at doing such a report would be once the land use framework has had time to bed in and start making changes.

Turn 13 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

Part of the design of the land use framework is to ensure that we spend our money wisely and work with other departments to make sure that development happens in the best place. Alongside that are other policies, including the implementation of many of the recommendations in the report by the noble Baroness, Lady Batters, on food productivity. If we can increase productivity, that is what helps our food security. We can have an enormous amount of land that is unproductive or not as productive as it should be. It is about being most productive and using our land to its best ability.
