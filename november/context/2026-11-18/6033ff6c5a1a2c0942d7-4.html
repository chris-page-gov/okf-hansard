Turn 27 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 131, I want to raise a concern about the public sector equality duty and the way it is being carried over into the new railway arrangements. At the moment, Network Rail is included in the Equality Act’s specific duties regulations, which require public authorities to publish equality objectives and information. But when Network Rail is replaced by GBR, the Bill does not make the necessary change to include GBR. The Equality and Human Rights Commission has highlighted this problem and recommended that GBR should be added to the regulations.

Turn 28 · Speaker unlabelled

It is important to be clear about what is at stake here. The public sector equality duty does not affect a small group of people. It applies across all the protected characteristics in the Equality Act: age, disability, race, religion et cetera. In other words, it matters to passengers and staff across the railway, which means the vast majority of people who use or work on our railways.

Turn 30 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to Amendments 117 and 127. GBR’s licence conditions and all other railway licensing conditions will be deregulated by removing the current requirement that they have regard to general duties. Amendment 117 would restore this requirement to have regard to the duties by amending Section 9 of the Railways Act 1993. Amendment 127 does the same for the statements of national regulatory provision which are applicable to non-GBR companies operating on the network. The amendments are a simple restoration of the principle already in place: preventing deregulation. They would also provide regulatory coherence with the licence and ensure that GBR had a clear compass about what it was doing.

Turn 31 · Speaker unlabelled

It is important to note, as with my other amendments, that a real deregulation of passenger rights is associated with this. Not only is the initial link with the general duties broken but all passenger and accessibility-related standards go through a double deregulation, because the role moves from the ORR to the passenger watchdog, which is not itself subject to the general duties. With regard to passengers, therefore, the link between the duties and licence is entirely broken. We are trying to create a new public railway body, so this is an opportunity to establish a coherent regulatory framework with clear responsibilities and expectations. Instead, the Bill risks removing important links between the railway’s general duties and the rules that govern how it operates. I hope the Minister can agree that we do not need to remove these protections.
