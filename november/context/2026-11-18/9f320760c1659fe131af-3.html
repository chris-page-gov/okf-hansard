Turn 18 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

I thank the noble Lord for all his excellent work in this arena and with the Prison Reform Trust. I know he is aware that, at present, prisons work hard to ensure that young people can access a broad range of educational opportunities including functional skills, vocational training, distance learning and work experience. Every prisoner receives an education assessment and careers guidance, which informs digital work, and a learning plan that supports progression throughout their time in custody. I welcome Alan Milburn’s ambition to increase apprenticeship opportunities for young people. HMPPS is already working with employers to expand apprenticeship provision in custody, including in sectors such as construction and hospitality, helping prisoners develop the skills they need for successful employment on release.

Turn 20 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

The noble Baroness will be unsurprised that I will need to confirm that and write to her as I do not have it in my pack. I take the opportunity to thank NA and AA for their work. Having worked in prisons for over a decade, I recognise their essential work. This Government have invested over £300 million in trying to keep prisons safe and secure. A core part of that—£75 million—has been invested in improving physical security so that we can tackle drugs and other contraband coming in, as well as increasing access to mutual aid across the estate. We are working closely with the fellowship organisations that the noble Baroness mentioned.

Turn 22 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

In my time working in prisons, I remember being shocked the first time I heard the statistic that the average reading age for a prisoner is 11 years old. I pay tribute to the work of organisations such as the Shannon Trust, which are vital in ensuring that, beyond the higher education opportunities, basic numeracy and literacy are widely accessible within all our prisons.

Turn 24 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

The noble Baroness is right to raise the overrepresentation of care leavers, particularly in the women’s estate and the youth estate. We are committed to tackling that problem. We are committed to ending that pipeline.
