Turn 194 · Sir John Hayes (South Holland and The Deepings) (Con)

I want to start with a revelation and an admission. The revelation is, in a sense, more of a declaration. If you were to come to my home in Lincolnshire, Madam Deputy Speaker—I hope that one day you might—and we were to enjoy a pot of tea in my study, as you gazed at the walls, alongside the portraits of Benjamin Disraeli and the prints of Chesterton, T.S. Eliot and so on, you would see a small, framed poster from the University of Nottingham in the late 1970s. That poster records the fact that even then I called for us to leave what was then the European Economic Community—the Common Market. From the very beginning of my consideration of these matters, I recognised that the Common Market, which metamorphosised from the European Economic Community into the European Community and ultimately the EU, was incompatible with both our national interest and the common good.
