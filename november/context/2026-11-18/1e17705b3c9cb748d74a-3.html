Turn 17 · Baroness Uddin (Non-Afl)

My Lords, I declare my interest as a former safeguarding and child protection officer. I thank my noble friend Lady Hazarika and my noble and learned friend Lady Harman for suggesting that we need collective action to address these massive issues. My noble friend talked about wider society and the difficulties of consent, but what procedures and support are in place for vulnerable young people who come into this House for volunteering or work placement regarding whistleblowing? What processes do we have in place to ensure that their safeguarding is as paramount for us here in this House as it is outside?

Turn 20 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

The noble Lord is right. One issue that we are concerned with, and which the noble Baroness, Lady Casey, was concerned we should be clear about, is that we should not inadvertently criminalise consensual relationships between young people—I am not quite sure if that is exactly what the noble Lord had in mind. As I have said, we will look again at the wider question of consent of 16 year-olds for sexual relations with older people. If there is a need to do something, we will, but the age of consent has been set at 16 for a very long time—although only much more recently for young gay men—and it would be a big and far-reaching change. Still, we will do what we can on sexual exploitation.
