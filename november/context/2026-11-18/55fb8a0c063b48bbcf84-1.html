Turn 2 · The Attorney General (Ellie Reeves)

The Good Friday agreement is one of the proudest achievements of any Labour Government, but after many years of bereaved families being let down, this may be our last chance to get this right. In line with the recommendations of the Joint Committee on Human Rights, the proposed amendment is intended to strengthen confidence in decisions to prosecute serious troubles-related offences. Any decisions would be quasi-judicial and made independent of Government, applying established evidential and public interest tests.

Turn 3 · Andy McDonald

The Justice (Northern Ireland) Act 2002 implemented reforms arising from the criminal justice review of 2000, following the Good Friday agreement, and created an independent Public Prosecution Service for Northern Ireland. The Act states: “The functions of the Director shall be exercised…independently of any other person.” Those reforms were key safeguards, ensuring the independence of prosecution decisions free from political interference. Does the Attorney General recognise that requiring her consent for serious troubles-related prosecutions risks rolling back that safeguard, and will she reconsider the provision to protect the independence of Northern Ireland’s justice system and public confidence in it?

Turn 4 · The Attorney General

I give my hon. Friend the assurance that there will be no political interference in decision making. As I said in my first answer, all decisions would be made quasi-judicially and independent of Government. There is precedent for that approach, with a number of offences for which Attorney General consent is already required. As the Joint Committee on Human Rights said, that approach could add an extra layer of assurance that there is accountability for prosecution decisions in this sensitive area.

Turn 6 · The Attorney General

Any decision that I would take would be made quasi-judicially, free from political interference and independent of Government. It does not change the legal tests—the long-established evidential test and the public interest test—and I will apply those without fear or favour and free from political interference.
