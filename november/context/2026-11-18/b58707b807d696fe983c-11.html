Turn 82 · Dr Pinkerton

I could not agree with my hon. Friend more. Some £7.7 billion has been spent just on the transaction costs of the paperwork itself. I am sure that businesses like the ones she mentions have also had to pay for additional staff in order to administer that paperwork. Earlier this week, I visited Hedley’s, a fantastic logistics firm in west London that now has three members of staff dedicated purely to the administration of customs forms. Those costs represent money that is not being invested in machinery, plant, apprenticeships, innovation or increased wages. That is money being consumed by navigating barriers that the Conservatives themselves created.

Turn 86 · Dr Pinkerton

Sovereignty is a bugger—if that is a parliamentary term, Madam Deputy Speaker—and the EU was clearly exercising its own in that case; it was under no obligation to offer us anything. Frankly, given the level of distrust that the Conservatives had engendered in the entire process, I am not surprised that we got such a poor deal. Research published by the National Bureau of Economic Research estimates that Brexit has left Britain’s economy 6% to 8% smaller than it would otherwise have been, with substantial damage to business investment and to productivity. The resulting economic damage is today costing the Treasury up to £90 billion annually in lost tax revenue. That is almost £250 million every single day.
