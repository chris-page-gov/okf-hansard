Turn 9 · Perran Moon (Camborne and Redruth) (Ind)

In Cornwall, we are 638th out of 650 constituencies for mobile coverage. On average nationally, 38% of premises choose between all four networks for a high-quality 5G non-stand-alone signal. In Camborne, Redruth and Hale that figure is zero. If consumers have nowhere to switch to, operators have next to no competitive impetus to improve quality. Does my right hon. Friend agree that one of the remedies for this absence of competition—and one way to incentivise proper investment in Cornwall—would be for Ofcom to make full spectrum licences conditional on robust performance standards?

Turn 11 · Henry Tufnell

I thank my hon. Friend for promoting me—I am not a right hon. Member—but I agree that we are too often in the situation of comparing how bad our constituencies are, and that we need organisations such as Ofcom to really grasp the nettle to make sure that this is not a race to the bottom in that sense. On that point, I urge the Minister to make sure that communities such as ours in Cornwall and west Wales are placed at the forefront of the Government’s efforts to improve telecommunications connectivity. Turn 15 · Henry Tufnell

The right hon. Member’s point is well made. Prolonged outages leave both his constituents and mine completely cut off and unable to contact family members or emergency services—something that is particularly important when we are dealing with severe weather events. That lack of connectivity is compounded by digital exclusion. More than a quarter of my constituents are aged 65 or older and nearly half are over the age of 50. AbilityNet is a fantastic organisation offering free advice and support for older people and those living with disability to help them access and use technology. As more and more of our essential services move online, such organisations are crucial to ensuring that no one is left behind.
