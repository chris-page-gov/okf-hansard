Turn 17 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

It is important to point out that the land use framework is not a material consideration in planning. Having said that, the principles, data and evidence that the framework provides will help to drive faster, clearer and more co-ordinated land use decisions. In August, we published the revised National Planning Policy Framework, which sets out those clear policies. That is an area in which Defra and other departments, particularly MHCLG, work together to try to deliver exactly what the right reverend Prelate is talking about.

Turn 20 · Baroness Shephard of Northwold (Con)

My Lords, farmers produce 60% of our food. The agriculture sector is therefore a vital part of our national security. To fulfil that role, it needs to be able to plan, adapt and invest. For that, it needs an efficient and responsive planning system. The current planning system is fraught with delay and indecision but, worse, its future, structure, functions and accountability are entirely uncertain because of the Government’s local government reforms. Will the Minister—she will know that I am normally on her side in these things—undertake to explain this to her colleagues in the relevant departments?

Turn 21 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

I can assure the noble Baroness that I talk regularly to my colleagues in the relevant departments. She mentions a critical point: much of what we want to do moving forward within the agriculture sector relies on the right planning applications and responses. For example, if we want to have fewer chickens in a shed—if we want to have less stocking density—we need bigger sheds, so we need planning applications that look to the reasons behind that. There is much that we need to do in Defra to continue working with MHCLG. We have been doing that constructively, and we will continue to do so.
