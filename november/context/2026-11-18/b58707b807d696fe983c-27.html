Turn 209 · Sir Iain Duncan Smith

Another honour, maybe. However, I do want to ask my right hon. Friend a question. Towards the latter stages of covid, I was asked by the then Prime Minister to look at how we could change some of the regulations that were imposed on us. I spoke to the scientists from Oxford who developed the first vaccine. When they sat in front of us, they confessed that although they had all voted to remain, when covid happened and they were hunting for a vaccine, they realised just how constrained they had been in developing new medicines when they were in the EU, because of the nature of the controls. We had lifted all of those controls, and they got the first vaccine, and millions of lives were saved as a result.

Turn 211 · Daisy Cooper

I invite the right hon. Member for Chingford and Woodford Green (Sir Iain Duncan Smith) to consider correcting the record, because that claim was fact-checked at the time and found to be untrue. Under European law, the UK was permitted to act independently to approve a vaccine in an emergency, and it was made clear—I was a Member of the House at the time and I remember fact-checking Boris Johnson and Matt Hancock when they made that claim—that it was not the case that we were able to accelerate the vaccine because we were outside the EU. We would have been able to do so anyway, because of the exemption for trialling novel vaccines under a specific medical exemption. I refer the right hon. Gentleman to the Full Fact article on that point.
