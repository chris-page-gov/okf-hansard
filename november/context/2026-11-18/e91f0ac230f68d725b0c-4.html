Turn 26 · Baroness Coffey (Con)

My Lords, I was Secretary of State when we had the lowest ever NEETs level and youth unemployment on record, whether measured in percentages, actual numbers, seasonally adjusted totals or real people. The point is that this is about real people. The Milburn review is a mixture of gold and pyrite. I wonder whether the Labour Government and the Prime Minister will understand that chapter 5, on the NHS, is the gold; the risk is that the rest is the pyrite. I desperately want to see more young people in jobs, but Alan Milburn has asked for £500 million. What is going to be cut? As one good example, next year £570 million will be spent on quite a small number of young people on Erasmus. Would it not be better to put that money into making sure that young people get going and have the youth opportunities that they deserve and merit, rather than choosing something to do with Erasmus?

Turn 29 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

The noble Lord makes an important point. We believe that the sorts of services that exist at local level, such as the youth opportunity service, can be co-ordinated at that level, commissioned by mayoral authorities but, in many cases, as I said to my noble friend, delivered by the voluntary sector, local authorities and the local health service. Those services are best organised around a young person at that level. The test will be the change that it makes for young people and the ability to deliver the opportunity and support guarantee that we have also committed to as part of our response to this report.

Turn 30 · Lord Cameron of Dillington (CB)

My Lords, I am hugely supportive of this new deal for our young. It is a vision that simply must be made to work for all our young. That is what I wish to highlight, because nowhere does this report address the specific problems of the rural young. How do they get from home to the local technology college, which is probably 12, 13 or 14 miles away—every day there and back? How do they get to their first job? It is possibly only seven or eight miles away, but even so, how do they get there and back every day without any transport? I would love to talk to the Minister and her team about the rural solutions to all the rural NEET problems. Believe me, NEET problems in rural areas are considerable, certainly where I come from in Cornwall. They really need considering in depth on their own, so I hope there is going to be serious consideration of the problems of a rural rollout of this review.
