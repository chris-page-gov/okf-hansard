Turn 10 · Speaker unlabelled

This new settlement must give people the best chance of higher living standards for the whole of their lives. It must change the equation that sees us spend £25 on the consequences of inactivity for every pound that we spend on opportunity. That work has already begun, with our £2.5 billion youth guarantee, fully funded apprenticeships for the under-25s, more work experience places, hiring incentives for employers, youth hubs in every community and, for the long-term unemployed, a guaranteed fully funded six-month job.

Turn 11 · Speaker unlabelled

But we must do more. Alan Milburn is right to argue for a new system that is more personalised, more devolved and focused on participation first. Instead of asking a young person to prove what they cannot do, the system should begin by asking what they need to build a future. His first key recommendation is for a new opportunity and support guarantee, backed by legislation, so that every young person who is not earning or learning has a credible route into education, training, an apprenticeship, high-quality work experience or a job. We support that recommendation.

Turn 12 · Speaker unlabelled

Alan Milburn’s second recommendation is for a more personalised system that treats people as human beings, not as departmental clients, with employment, education, skills, health and wider support brought together around the person; and, crucially, he recommends that this be delivered locally. The Government also accept that second recommendation, which will mean more local delivery, with proper standards and accountability for outcomes. This is common in other countries with significantly lower rates of youth inactivity, because local leaders have the knowledge, the relationships and the networks to connect young people with the support and opportunities that make sense for them.

Turn 14 · Speaker unlabelled

The report recommends a youth allowance, which people will receive while they receive help from the youth opportunity service, with extra support for care leavers and those outside the family home. It says that young people whose disabilities or health conditions create significant, enduring additional costs—including people with conditions that fluctuate—should continue to receive help with those costs, and that support should not be put at risk if they start work. Because people should not always have to wait for a national assessment for short-term help, it proposes a dedicated locally held fund to help with one-off costs, or temporary cash support.

Turn 15 · Speaker unlabelled

For those with the most severe and lifelong conditions, for whom work is not a realistic prospect, Alan Milburn recommends a stronger safety net, providing dignity and security, and an end to unnecessary reassessments. The report points out that in some cases extra cash may not always be the right way to support a young person, because when participation is realistic, the right priority should be earlier and more intensive support with their health, education and employment, to help them build a better future; and to help change the current situation in which a 20 year-old who signs on for long-term sickness benefits is more likely to mark their 30th birthday and still be on those benefits than to have had a steady job in any of the intervening 10 years.
