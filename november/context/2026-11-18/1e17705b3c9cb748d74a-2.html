Turn 12 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

I have read a bit of Graham Greene; I am happy to confirm that. I did English literature at university—I dare say that the noble and learned Lord did law. Indeed, if he did, I would say that the law curriculum should be updated to include a little bit more Graham Greene. On the serious point that the noble and learned Lord makes, of course the Government will look at ways in which we can address the criminal behaviours here. We have done things in the online safety space; we have created new offences there. However, I think it is fair to say that no one feels that we are in a comfortable place, and the Brand case illustrates some aspects of sexual exploitation and abuse that we need to think about more carefully—and we will. If necessary, we will legislate. It is an appalling case, as my right honourable friend the Prime Minister said, and we will do all that we can.

Turn 14 · Lord Lemos (Lab)

Yes, I agree with my noble and learned friend that it is an important discussion for us to have. I think the scenario that she describes is deeply troubling to all of us, and we should absolutely discuss it—and if necessary, we will legislate. She referenced the Law Commission’s work. We have accepted all the recommendations of the noble Baroness, Lady Casey, and we have legislated in the Crime and Policing Act to create new offences covering rape and other penetrative sexual activity with a child under 16 by an adult. I agree with my noble and learned friend that there is no doubt more that we can do, and we must come back and look at that. What she describes should concern us all.
