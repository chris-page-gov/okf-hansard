Turn 89 · Speaker unlabelled

I pay every level of respect to revenue protection officers working on the railways, who go around seeking to catch people. They do great work; they face difficult and occasionally dangerous situations, and sometimes they need the back-up of the British Transport Police. However, they are spread very thin. I think that most of us would agree that the situation is not satisfactory. In this amendment, we simply call for a licence condition that would give due prominence to that in the Great British Railways licence, asking it to spell out exactly what it is doing in that regard.

Turn 92 · Lord Berkeley (Lab)

My Lords, it has been a very interesting short debate on fares. Reading the amendments in the name of the noble Lord, Lord Moylan, made me ask a question. It is quite simple to see how it would work in places such as London, where Transport for London runs the trains and sets the fares, but what about an open access line to London, to Edinburgh, or anywhere else, where there are two competing services, or more than two? One of them would be run possibly by GBR and the others by what are, in effect, private sector operators. Does the scope for dealing with fares extend beyond GBR-operated trains to open access ones? If so, how will GBR—presumably GBR—control fares on open access trains if that is part of the deal? I would be grateful to hear my noble friend’s response, or even that of the noble Lord, Lord Moylan, if he has a view.
