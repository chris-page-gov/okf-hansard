Turn 50 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

This Government, in reforming the growth and skills levy, have already increased incentives for small businesses to take on apprentices, are now fully funding the cost of an apprenticeship for small businesses and, through the funding made available for local apprenticeship services, will enable small businesses to get the advice and links to young people who want to be apprentices who I know those businesses are keen to get. In all those ways, we are ensuring that young people are getting the type of training, alongside a job, that supports not only them but the employer to bring on an enthusiastic young person for their business.

Turn 51 · Baroness O’Grady of Upper Holloway (Lab)

My Lords, does my noble friend the Minister agree that, as well as concern about the scarring impacts of unemployment on the young, we must also take account of the scarring impacts of insecure, low-paid work? Young people are twice as likely to be trapped in that revolving door of low-paid, insecure work as older workers. Does she agree that ensuring we have well-paid, quality employment opportunities for young people is as important as ensuring that they get off unemployment?

Turn 52 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

My noble friend is absolutely right and, of course, the two are linked. We make no apology, therefore, as I said, for wanting young people to start their working lives in decent, secure jobs. Young people are among those who stand to benefit most from stronger employment rights, because they are, as my noble friend says, disproportionately represented in low-paid, insecure work. Giving people greater certainty over their hours and income can make work more attractive and give young people the confidence and stability they need to stay in work. Our employment rights legislation is not only morally but economically right.
