Turn 81 · Speaker unlabelled

Paragraph 11 gives recipients the right to appeal to the appropriate tribunal. Paragraph 12 provides an important safeguard where the daily penalties described in paragraph 8 are appealed. Once an appeal is brought, the daily penalties stop accruing until the appeal has been finally determined. We are aware that enforcement brings additional cost. Paragraph 13 therefore allows authorities to use penalty proceeds to fund the administrative and legal costs of carrying out their functions under the Bill. Any proceeds not used for that purpose must be paid to the Secretary of State or, where the penalty was imposed by an authority in Wales, to the Welsh Ministers. Paragraph 14 determines the terms used in the schedule. Schedule 3 supports firm but fair enforcement, protecting recipients’ rights while giving authorities the tools to drive compliance.

Turn 83 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 103 will make sure that where a body commits an offence, individuals responsible for that wrongdoing can also be prosecuted. It applies where they consented to or connived in the offence, or where it resulted from their neglect. The same principle applies to the financial penalties introduced by the Bill. The clause allows for a relevant individual to be fined alongside or instead of the body where the breach occurred with their consent or as a result of their neglect. This encourages those in senior positions to take their responsibilities seriously and actively support compliance, while ensuring that individuals can be held personally responsible and accountable where their actions or neglect contribute to non-compliance.

Turn 84 · Speaker unlabelled

Given the potential consequences for individuals, it is important that personal liability is subject to robust safeguards. An individual can be held personally liable only where their consent or neglect is established. Where they are prosecuted or issued with a financial penalty, that person and that personal responsibility must be established beyond reasonable doubt. A person should not face both a financial penalty under the Bill and a criminal conviction for the same conduct. Clause 104 provides that safeguard.

Turn 85 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 105 prevents landlords from passing on the cost of their non-compliance to leaseholders through service charges. The protection applies to the relevant leases of the premises that consist of or include a dwelling in a building covered by the relevant building safety legislation. This covers parts 1 to 3 of the Bill and regulations made under section 123 of the Building Safety Act 2022 and section 114 of the Building Safety (Wales) Act 2026.

Turn 86 · Speaker unlabelled

The protection is deliberately broader than the financial penalty itself. It covers costs arising from financial penalties or other enforcement action against the landlord. This can include associated costs, legal costs or court costs. It also covers the costs of complying with the building ownership information notice under part 3 of the Bill. There is limited exception for leaseholder-controlled companies, such as right-to-manage companies and residential management companies. Where a company incurs an excluded cost, it can recover the cost from the leaseholders who are members of or entitled to become members of the company. Where only some leaseholders are members of or entitled to become members of a leaseholder- controlled company, the full amounts cannot be recovered under the leases. Any remaining shortfall can be shared equally between those leaseholders.

Turn 87 · Speaker unlabelled

Leaseholder-controlled companies, such as right-to-manage companies and residential management companies, are often run by volunteer leaseholders. They typically have access to fewer resources, expertise and professional support than commercial freeholders or professional landlords. Clause 106 recognises the particular challenges faced by leaseholder-controlled companies. It allows the Secretary of State to issue guidance to enforcement authorities on how those circumstances should inform enforcement decisions. Enforcement authorities must have regard to that guidance when deciding whether to impose a financial penalty. This is to make sure that enforcement remains effective, while avoiding financial penalties falling on leaseholders where another route could secure the same outcome.
