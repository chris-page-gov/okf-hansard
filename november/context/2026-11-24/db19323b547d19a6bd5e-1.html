Turn 2 · Jim Shannon (Strangford) (DUP)

First, I thank the hon. Lady for telling the story of Tina. It was almost impossible to listen to it. The Commissioner for Older People for Northern Ireland reports that domestic abuse incidents against the elderly have steadily increased over the past 20 years. Does the hon. Member agree that we must ensure that older victims are not left feeling that the prospect of loneliness or of losing family connections is worse than remaining in an abusive relationship? We must recognise that age makes abuse no less significant and that perpetrators should still be held to account.

Turn 3 · Sonia Kumar

I thank my hon. Friend for that intervention—I say “friend” as we have had many interactions. As people get older, their situation gets really complex, given their housing, their relationships and the fact they have been with someone for such a long time. Leaving someone might make them really dependent on themselves. They are truly unfortunate because they are stuck. They do not want to be lonely, isolated and away from family and friends. That is such a complex web to come out of when there is abuse in an older life. I will talk about that later. The figures that I gave are likely to represent only a fraction of the true scale of the problem because domestic abuse in later life remains profoundly under-reported, according to Hourglass. Domestic abuse may have become hidden within family dynamics. Professionals and even family members may simply stop seeing it for what it is.

Turn 5 · Sonia Kumar

Hourglass has said that £128 million is lost through economic abuse of older people. The financial tie between and individual and their abusive partner means they are so interlinked that it is hard to pull away and realise how to get support. That is really isolating. Sometimes abuse is overlooked because it has been going on for too long. Sometimes it is simply dismissed as a family, or private, matter. Sometimes it is hidden behind assumptions about ageing, illness and dependency. Sometimes older people themselves feel unable to come forward. We should imagine how frightening the prospect of that is. Imagine someone in their 70s or 80s contemplating leaving the person with whom they have shared a home for 40 or 50 years.
