Turn 57 · Alistair Strathern (Hitchin) (Lab)

It has been heartbreaking to see the frankly impossible position that too many of my local civil service pension holders have been put in by Capita’s complete mismanagement of the scheme. It should clearly never have been given the contract by the last Government. I am grateful for the Minister and her Department’s support in driving forward some improvement, but it is clear that there is more to do. As we rightly prioritise getting more of my constituents their long overdue pensions, how can we ensure lessons are learned, so that we do not see future failed outsourcing that leaves my constituents picking up the pieces again?

Turn 63 · Liz Saville Roberts (Dwyfor Meirionnydd) (PC)

I thank the Minister for her thorough answers and evident dedication. Jill Hughes MBE of Nefyn received a civil service pension until her death last December. Some 11 months later, her widowed husband David has yet to receive the dependant’s pension, even though Capita admits the case is not complex. Mr Hughes is now in a care home and he is at a financial loss directly because of Capita’s inaction. It appears to be civil service pension and UK Government policy not to pay interest on delayed payments to bereaved dependants for a number of months. Does she agree, therefore, that this looks very much like an incentive for a profiteering organisation to delay payments, even in non-complex cases?
