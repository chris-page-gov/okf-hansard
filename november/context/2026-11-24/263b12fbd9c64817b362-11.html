Turn 81 · Perran Moon (Camborne and Redruth) (Ind)

I was deeply disappointed that there was no agreement at COP31, and I find the official Opposition’s luddite attitude simply baffling. I mean, change tack, man—there aren’t enough luddites out there for you to win. I was also deeply disappointed to hear about a possible dilution of the ZEV mandate to 50%; with existing flexibilities, that would increase demand for petrol to the equivalent of double the annual output of Rosebank, and would almost certainly lead to the breaking of a manifesto commitment. I ask the Secretary of State to confirm that she is still committed to the banning of pure petrol and diesel vehicles by 2030—not hybrids by 2035, but pure petrol by 2030.

Turn 82 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

I thank my hon. Friend for raising this important point. We are committed to zero emission vehicles by 2035. As he knows, we are going through a consultation at the moment, and we are looking at options for the trajectory. Irrespective of that trajectory, though, we know that EVs are hugely cost-effective for consumers—it is between £800 and £1,400 cheaper to have an EV, so the market is booming. We are seeing record EV sales because consumers can see the opportunity. We will do our bit to ensure that we continue to support that trajectory, but, candidly, I think the market is going to outstrip us.

Turn 85 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

I thank my hon. Friend for ending with that question. I genuinely believe that there is hope. What gave me hope was sitting opposite countries from across the world and seeing that, for all the disagreements and the rancour, there is absolute commitment to the Paris agreement, and resolve that, ultimately, for many communities this is existential and we have to act collectively. We have the technology and the pathway, and we know what needs to be done, so I am hopeful. I am also very clear that the UK has a fundamental leadership role to play, and we are determined to play it.
