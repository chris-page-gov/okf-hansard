Turn 2 · The Secretary of State for Health and Social Care (Yvette Cooper)

The Prime Minister set out a vision for a national care service and Baroness Casey’s independent commission will recommend next summer how that should be taken forward. In the meantime, we are already taking important steps to strengthen support for unpaid carers and the social care workforce. Last week, as a down payment on the national care service, I announced how neighbourhood support teams would start coming together to support the most frail and vulnerable people in our communities.

Turn 4 · Yvette Cooper

The Prime Minister has set out a plan for free personal care, and that should have alongside it an adjustment to the triple lock, where pensioners will still see their pensions go up in line with inflation or by 2.5%. Pensions will also track wages. That is the right thing to do. The adjustment will also allow us to provide a better deal for elderly people, who currently face such huge pressures from social care bills in old age.

Turn 6 · Yvette Cooper

The health service does need to respond to different communities and different conditions, in rural Britain and in coastal areas. As the hon. Member will know, that was reflected in the chief medical officer’s recent report, and we take it extremely seriously. We want more devolution so that we can ensure services are most effectively designed for the communities where people live. Neighbourhood support teams will be providing support at home rather than expecting people to travel long distances for care or health support.
