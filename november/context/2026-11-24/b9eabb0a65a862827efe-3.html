Turn 17 · Yvette Cooper

We will produce the first action plan on maternity services and reforms before Christmas. We will then continue with further work, including around the modern services framework and new standards for national maternity and neonatal care, because that is important. We are also investing in new capital funding for maternity units. I will gently point out to the hon. Member that her party was in government when the previous national framework of standards on maternity care was cancelled.

Turn 18 · Helen Morgan

Let me focus on the issue of safe staffing, because it remains a critical problem. Just last week, the General Medical Council released a report outlining the pressures on obs and gynae trainees, who are more likely to report heavy workloads, feel short of sleep and have a higher risk of burnout. Our written parliamentary question has revealed that the number of midwives quitting early in their career has climbed, as 1,669 midwives under the age of 35 left the profession last year alone. We are still waiting for the workforce plan, which was originally promised for summer 2025. When will we receive it?

Turn 19 · Yvette Cooper

We already have an increased number—in fact, a record number—of midwives working in the NHS this year. That is important, so that we increase care and support for women during pregnancy. We are also providing investment for an additional 1,000 temporary roles to help newly qualified midwives join the NHS, to ensure we improve care for mums and babies at that critical time.
