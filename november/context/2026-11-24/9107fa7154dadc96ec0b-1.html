Turn 2 · The Secretary of State for Health and Social Care (Yvette Cooper)

It is important that people get the highest quality of healthcare closer to where they live. That starts with improving access to GP care. As a result of the work we have done, there are now 24 million additional GP appointments a year compared with before the election, and we have now announced neighbourhood care reforms, including new neighbourhood support teams, to help the frailest of patients stay well and safe at home.

Turn 4 · Yvette Cooper

The hon. Member is absolutely right about this: we know that some people end up in emergency care because they could not get early enough support and care at home. That is why we are giving GPs £40 million this winter to identify the frailest patients and put in place home care plans, and from next April, we will roll out new neighbourhood support teams to help the frailest 1% of the population across the country. This is a fundamental change in the way that we provide NHS care, and will provide those patients with support and care in their own homes.

Turn 6 · Yvette Cooper

I thank the right hon. Member for his advocacy and campaigning on prostate cancer, which affects so many men across the country. As he will know, the Government will roll out England’s first targeted prostate cancer screening programme, as recommended by the UK national screening committee. We have asked the committee to keep the programme under continual review. Men who are not covered by the screening programme and who are concerned can also talk to their GP about getting a prostate-specific antigen test based on informed choice. He is right that raising awareness about prostate cancer is critical, and many campaigning organisations do an amazing job on that.
