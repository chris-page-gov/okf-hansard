Turn 49 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

I do not expect any increase in energy bills as a result of this deal. Going forward, we should see the costs reduce. We know that energy bills have gone up significantly, largely because of what has been happening in the Strait of Hormuz. If we look at the trading standards agreement and, going forward, discussions on electricity, we should be able to move forward on both costs and energy security. I do not expect costs to increase as a result of this.

Turn 50 · Lord Neri (Lab)

My Lords, I declare my interests as CEO of the Ivors Academy and as director of UK Music. I welcome the Prime Minister’s commitment to dismantling the touring barriers faced by British artists, performers and musicians. The Brexit deal omitted creative exemptions and rejected visa waivers. It cost our sector over £208 million and we lost 2,500 jobs. This Government recognise music as our finest export. Will my noble friend the Leader of the House outline a timetable for concrete relief on carnets, cabotage and work permits?

Turn 52 · Lord Mohammed of Tinsley (LD)

My Lords, does the noble Baroness the Leader of the House find it ironic that the people who gave us the Boris wave, where millions came to this country, are now trying to pull up the drawbridge on 100,000 of our young people being able to go to our nearest neighbours to learn, live and work? It is interesting that the Leader cited her visit to Southend. I think many schools up and down this country would give her exactly the same experience, as would my son and daughter, who did not have a say in 2016. How are we going to engage with those young people to let them decide their future, and will that include a referendum on rejoining the EU?

Turn 53 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

I cannot give the noble Lord an absolute guarantee on that last point. As the Prime Minister said yesterday, if a decision is taken that it is in the UK’s interest to return to the EU then it would be inconceivable that it would be undertaken without a referendum. Europe changes: we are in a different world from the one we were in in 2016. The world is more insecure and we have to work together more. We do not know what the future holds. However, it is certainly rather sad for those aged 16 to 20, who do not have the same opportunities that I had when I was younger. I got a sense not just of disappointment but almost of bitterness from some young people that they feel they have been denied those opportunities that others took advantage of.
