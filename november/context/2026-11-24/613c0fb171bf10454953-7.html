Turn 49 · Andy McDonald (Middlesbrough and Thornaby East) (Lab)

I thank the Minister for her assiduous attention—she has been quite superb—and I declare my interest as a member of the PCS parliamentary group. In last week’s Westminster Hall debate on the Department for Work and Pensions’ Synergy contract, the Under-Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, the hon. Member for Nottingham South (Lilian Greenwood), cited the costs and risks of reversing existing outsourcing arrangements, but today’s events show the costs and risks of continuing with these outsourced contracts. I know that the priority is getting people paid, but surely the Minister must now task the insourcing unit with examining both the civil service pension scheme and the Synergy contract and developing plans to rebuild the skills, expertise and operational capacity within the civil service necessary to bring these essential functions back in-house.

Turn 52 · Sally Jameson

As I have set out in previous answers, the Procurement Act 2023 allows Government to take into account previous performance. I have met Capita executives on multiple occasions since I became the Minister for this portfolio, and I made clear in no uncertain terms that their performance is wholly unacceptable. The chief executive officer has now gone and my office is currently arranging for me to meet the new CEO in the coming days. I will be repeating exactly the same to them: improved performance is absolutely imperative, and if there is any sign that they decide to take their foot off the pedal in relation to resolving the civil service pension scheme and improving the service, I will continue to ensure that there are consequences.
