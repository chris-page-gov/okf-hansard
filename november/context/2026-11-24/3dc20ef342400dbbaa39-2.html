Turn 14 · Lord Kamall (Con)

My Lords, I pay tribute to the noble Lord, Lord Touhig, and my noble friend Lady Browning for their persistence on this issue. It is very important that we are constantly reminded about the need for a renewed autism strategy. Earlier this month, the Health and Social Care Committee’s independent expert panel highlighted serious shortcomings in the transition from children’s services to adult services. We know it is not only confined to autism; it happens in other health services as well. Is the Minister in a position to commit to ensuring that the new autism strategy includes measures to provide young autistic people with an individual transition plan and a named professional to oversee their continuing support as they go from children’s services to adult services?

Turn 16 · Baroness Fraser of Craigmaddie (Con)

My Lords, I associate myself with the comments of my noble friend Lord Kamall about the work that the noble Lord, Lord Touhig, and my noble friend Lady Browning have done on autism. I also pick up on my noble friend’s point that the transition from childhood to adulthood is not simply an issue that affects people with autism. I declare an interest here as chief executive of Cerebral Palsy Scotland, because this is a clear crisis point for people with cerebral palsy. Can the Minister say what threshold a condition has to reach before it gets a strategy and before it gets people to take accountability for what should and should not happen for these people? In the world of cerebral palsy, we have NICE guidelines for children and for adults and standards, but nobody is responsible for their implementation.
