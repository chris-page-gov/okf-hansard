Turn 1 · The Minister for Social Security and Disability (Sir Stephen Timms)

With permission, I would like to make a statement on the final report of the Timms review of the personal independence payment. PIP provides vital support to deaf and disabled people and people with long-term health conditions. It contributes to the additional costs they face, and aims to help them to live independently, to participate in their communities and often to work. PIP was introduced 13 years ago. Since then, lots has changed, but PIP has never been fully reviewed, and it has not kept pace, so the Timms review was launched in summer last year. The review was co-produced with disabled people, the organisations that represent them and other experts. It is the first time that the UK Government have co-produced a review of this scale. Turn 7 · Sir Stephen Timms

I am grateful to my hon. Friend, the Chair of the Work and Pensions Committee, for her question. She is absolutely right to say that we need a more personalised approach than the one we had in the past. All of us will know constituents who have significant extra costs as a result of their disability, and this benefit is there to contribute to those costs. She is also absolutely right to highlight—the review is very clear about this—that PIP is for people who are in work, as well as those who are out of work. Indeed, one of the points that was made to us in the debate last summer by a lot of people claiming PIP is that they need it in order to afford the cost of getting to work. It is very important that that support is maintained, and the review is determined that it shall be.
