Turn 65 · Lloyd Hatton (South Dorset) (Lab)

First it was the £90 million contract with the Ministry of Justice, which failed to deliver interpretation services in our courts. Then it was the £1.3 billion contract with the Ministry of Defence, which failed every year to meet recruitment targets in the Army. Now it is the civil service pension scheme fiasco, costing the Cabinet Office some £239 million. Wherever we look, the previous Government signed up to public contracts that effectively fleece the taxpayer, so my question is very simple: what will the Minister do to ensure that this track record of failure, which sits specifically with Capita, is ended once and for all?

Turn 72 · Sally Jameson

I believe that I should wish the hon. Member a very happy birthday. [Hon. Members: “Hear, hear!”] I completely understand the concerns that she raises on behalf of her constituents. I can provide her with the assurance that, as with all strategic suppliers, the Government work across all Departments to ensure that we have good contingency plans in place for any eventuality. That is not just with Capita, but with all strategic suppliers, so Capita is no different. As she will know from my previous statement, we are already looking at contingency because of the state that the pension scheme was in, but the priority is making Capita continue to improve on its service so that we can get money to people as quickly as possible.
