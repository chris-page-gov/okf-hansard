Turn 6 · Sir John Hayes (South Holland and The Deepings) (Con)

The Minister is showing responsiveness and flexibility, and I think he understands that, although the Bill does enjoy cross-party support, there is a need to get it right. Part of that is recognising that this is a highly dynamic environment and that technology moves very fast. What is probably in the their lordships’ minds is that that requires a very regular reappraisal of the appropriateness of the application of policy. To that end—I raised this point last week when we debated artificial intelligence—I wonder whether the Minister might look at the whole issue of interoperability and interconnectivity. My fear is that we are concentrating risk, and when we review these matters we might want to consider that in a cross-departmental way.

Turn 8 · Sir John Hayes

When I was Security Minister, I set up the National Cyber Security Centre and developed the strategy. I was very conscious then that the dilemma for policymakers, Government and the House is that our processes, by their nature and their thoroughness, require time for proper scrutiny, as the Minister is making clear. However, the world that we are trying to legislate for is moving rapidly. There is sometimes a mismatch between those two things. It may well be that the Bill has to be sufficiently flexible to allow for that kind of dynamism. I think he recognises that, and I am grateful to him.
