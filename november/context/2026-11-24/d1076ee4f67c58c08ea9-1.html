Turn 3 · The Minister for Public Health and Patient Safety (Dame Diana Johnson)

The support that people get to have a baby should not depend on where in the country they live, but variations in access to NHS-funded fertility services have been a problem for many years. Our women’s health strategy commits to ensuring that every woman can access those services. That means we expect such services to be commissioned by integrated care boards solely on the basis of patient needs and the relevant National Institute for Health and Care Excellence guidelines. We are currently assessing the level of provision in each ICB to ensure that it lives up to those commitments.

Turn 6 · Navendu Mishra

Similarly, the NHS in Greater Manchester has reduced NHS-funded IVF provision to just one full cycle, despite NICE guidelines recommending up to three cycles for eligible patients. The NHS in Greater Manchester pressed ahead with the change despite 74% of consultation respondents opposing it. The consultation cost the public around £4,000, and it seems to have been entirely ignored. England has a falling birth rate, with fewer than 600,000 babies born last year. We should be making it easier, not harder, for people to start a family. Does the Minister agree that access to fertility treatment should not depend on someone’s postcode or ability to afford it? Will she commit to ending the reduction in NHS-funded IVF provision?
