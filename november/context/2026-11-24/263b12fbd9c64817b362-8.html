Turn 58 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

As I have said, we know that there has to be a balance of projects, and we want a balance of projects, particularly those that will benefit different parts of the country. In thinking about the balance in that suite of projects, we are trying to manage their ability to play a role in our clean energy drive, and about their affordability and cost. That is a difficult balance, but when we open our auctions we are asking for projects that can meet the test of both delivering the clean energy we need and, critically, of doing it at the least cost. Candidly, consumers would expect nothing less from us.

Turn 60 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

My right hon. Friend has been a clear and passionate advocate of this agenda, and she is absolutely right that we must work with partners. We will work with partners in the EU, but also with those across the least developed countries as well as island states at the very forefront of this. There is an IPCC piece that is the science foundations of everything we do, and critical work needs to be done to ensure that the science leads climate action. There is also work we must do to ensure that a coalition of the willing is up for acting. Irrespective of COP action, there is a huge amount that we must come together to progress, and we are determined to do so.

Turn 62 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

My hon. Friend the Minister for Climate Transition met the organisers of the national emergency briefing, and we had a Westminster Hall debate on this critical question. Engagement and recognition of what climate change means for our communities are absolutely critical. We know that we cannot impose this transition on people, so we need a conversation with the public about what this means. There is also an opportunity if we get this right—because there is a downside, but there is also an upside—and we as a Government are absolutely committed to having that conversation, engaging with our communities, working with our local leaders, who will be critical to such a conversation, and driving consensus for the scale of ambition that we all know we need.

Turn 64 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

Let me reassure my hon. Friend that we are clear both about our historical responsibility and about the partnership we need to move forward. Countries are following the UK’s example: we pioneered the Climate Change Act 2008 and now 76 countries have taken it on; we were the first G7 country to phase out coal; and we were one of the first major economies to ban new exploration for oil and gas licences. We are leading and others are following. We take that responsibility to heart.
