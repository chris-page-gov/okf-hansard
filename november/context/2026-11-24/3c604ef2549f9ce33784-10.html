Turn 76 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government’s analysis of FOS case data found that only 11% of cases over 10 years old were upheld in favour of the consumer, and cases over 10 years old were more likely to be abandoned and withdrawn. The new statutory time limit will operate alongside existing time limits set in FCA rules, which provide that complaints must be brought to the FOS within six years of the act or omission being complained about or, if later, within three years of when the complainant became aware or ought reasonably to have become aware of their cause to complain.

Turn 77 · Speaker unlabelled

The 10-year limit acts as a backstop, such that complaints cannot be brought about acts or omissions that are more than 10 years old. None the less—this is important—the Government recognise that there may be circumstances where the 10-year time limit may not be appropriate, such as for some longer term products like mortgages or pensions. The Bill therefore gives the FCA the power to make rules creating limited exceptions to the time limit. That will ensure that in cases where knowledge of the cause for complaint may not emerge until a significant amount of time after the event, there is still a route.
