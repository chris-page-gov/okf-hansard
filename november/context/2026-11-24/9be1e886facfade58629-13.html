Clause 103 exists for a simple reason: for years, owners of unsafe buildings have hidden behind shell companies’ complex corporate structures while the directors who make decisions have walked away untouched. Making directors and senior managers personally liable where failures result from their consent, connivance or neglect is one of the most important parts of this Bill, and it could really change behaviour if people at the top are held liable in this way. Removing that power would let the very people who have dragged their feet for years off the hook and leave residents waiting in unsafe homes while penalties fall on companies that can be wound up.

Turn 99 · Speaker unlabelled

In contrast, we recognise the issues of unpaid volunteer directors and we support amendment 56, which would protect unpaid resident volunteers from being held liable under clause 103. Across the country, leaseholders have stepped up to run their own buildings through right to manage. Resident management companies are unpaid and give their time voluntarily. We heard from witnesses who gave evidence to the Committee that this is a real concern: Lord Roe, chair of the Building Safety Regulator, told the Committee that using the powers in the Bill against resident management companies could be “punching down” and would be completely inappropriate. The Deputy Mayor of Manchester also warned that, despite the mitigations in the Bill, such residents ultimately face the risk of criminal sanctions.
