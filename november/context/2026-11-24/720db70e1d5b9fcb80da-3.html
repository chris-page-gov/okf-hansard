Turn 19 · Sir Stephen Timms

The intention of the review is precisely to ensure that those who have additional costs as a result of disability continue to receive this benefit, and this will go forward in a fair and sustainable way. The hon. Member absolutely rightly makes the point about the obligation that is placed on people, far too often, to give the same information time and again in different assessments in different bits of the system. One proposal in the review is that we should introduce a digital profile, which starts to be populated at the start of the process, so that if people want to pass on that information to other bits of the system, they can do so without having to answer the same questions all over again.

Turn 23 · Sir Stephen Timms

There are indeed projections of that. I thank the right hon. Member for his kind remarks. As I have said, I think the key is to have an assessment that correctly establishes whether people have additional costs arising from their impairment, whether it is a mental health problem or a physical health problem. At the moment, the assessment does not ask that question at all. The benefit has always been intended to contribute to additional costs, but the assessment does not ask about them, and we want an assessment that does.
