Turn 89 · Speaker unlabelled

Alongside new reporting requirements introduced by clause 8, the referral mechanism will provide greater understanding of the FCA’s rules and the standards firms must meet and is, as we have discussed, designed to give both consumers and firms greater confidence in the landscape and greater certainty. I should also be very clear about what this mechanism does not do. It does not create a new route of appeal against decisions by the FOS, nor does it give the FCA a role in determining individual complaints. Resolving complaints based on individual circumstances will remain the role of the FOS. The purpose of the referral mechanism is to ensure that the FCA remains responsible for setting and interpreting regulatory standards.

Turn 90 · Speaker unlabelled

On Second Reading, Members asked whether the referral mechanism could slow down the ultimate resolution of FOS complaints. The Government strongly agree that a customer should not face unnecessary delays when a particular matter is referred to the FCA, so the Bill allows the Government to set a statutory timeframe for the FCA to respond on matters of ambiguity. Our intention is that that will be set at 30 days for most cases.

Turn 91 · Speaker unlabelled

We have worked closely with the FCA and the FOS to ensure that the mechanism can be operationalised effectively, including through learning lessons from a trial that is currently in effect. Our best estimate is that there will be around 25 referrals relating to rule ambiguity a year, primarily focused on new and emerging issues to ensure that a consistent approach can be taken from the outset. One referral may cover an issue which will be applicable to a significant number of complaints before the ombudsman, allowing each of these complaints to be resolved quickly and consistently after the original referral. Should the volume of referrals prove to be significantly higher than expected, and if that is leading to operational issues for the FCA and the FOS, the Government are willing to consider whether it is necessary to adjust the timeframe that will be set in secondary legislation.

Turn 94 · Speaker unlabelled

First, the amendments make clear that the FOS should make a referral only where doing so is necessary for it to determine a complaint. Secondly, they help to clarify that the FCA’s role is provide its view on the interpretation of the relevant rules rather than to give a view on the individual circumstances or outcome of a particular complaint. That has always been the policy intention, but I just want to make that very clear. The FCA will not be asked to resolve cases or to apply its rules to the individual facts of a complaint. It will resolve any areas of genuine ambiguity in the rules and it will remain the role of the FOS to apply the rules to individual cases.

Turn 95 · Speaker unlabelled

Thirdly, amendment 12 removes the requirement for the FOS to make rules, allowing parties to ask it to consider making a referral. That will address concerns that that process could generate large volumes of unnecessary requests, with the potential to cause operational challenges for the FCA and delay the resolution of complaints. We are also making a small technical change so that the FCA’s responses to these referrals sit outside the remit of the Financial Regulators Complaints Commissioner. That will ensure that referrals are treated consistently with comparable FCA functions, including the issuing of general guidance and FCA rule making. As rule making itself falls outside the scope of the Complaints Commissioner, it makes sense for interpretations of those rules to be treated in the same way.
