Turn 65 · Shockat Adam (Leicester South) (Ind)

I was at COP31, too. The strapline was “dialogue, consensus, action”. We saw lots of dialogue. We even saw a consensus between two camps—the polluting countries and the polluted countries—but what we did not see was action. In particular, the Minister spoke about the lack of action on adaptation, which is obviously not just a problem abroad. The Minister told this House that 5,258 people in the UK died from the heat this year alone. Will she commit to a properly funded adaptation plan here at home, and also for the frontline countries?

Turn 66 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

My right hon. Friend the Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs will be coming forward with our responses, but the hon. Member is right to raise adaptation. Climate change is here and all countries now need to ensure that we are scaling up adaptations. We are investing a record amount—£10 billion—in flood defences. We are investing in upgrading our schools and hospitals, and we are investing in upgrading our homes. We understand that there is a huge amount of work to do, but we are committed to making sure that we play our part, because ultimately this comes down to protecting our communities, which is the first duty of Government.

Turn 67 · Richard Burgon (Leeds East) (Lab)

COP31 was a disaster in our battle against climate breakdown. Let us be clear: the world is being held hostage by a fossil fuel mafia hellbent on using its wealth and power to stop necessary action to maintain its profits. It even kept the words “fossil fuels” out of the draft text. As the United Nations Secretary-General said, “fossil fuel interests keep making a killing while killing our planet.” Is it not time that Governments around the world confronted the fossil fuel giants and put humanity’s future before their dirty, obscene profits? Does that not need to include a rejection of Rosebank?

Turn 69 · Carla Lockhart (Upper Bann) (Ind)

I may be in the minority in this place today, but I believe this needs saying. At COP31 the utter madness of net zero was laid bare. All the leaders flew there in their jets while increasing the tax burden on flyers around the UK. None of them took non-fossil fuel methods of transport to get there. Given that the major global emitters of fossil fuels refused to sign the transition deal and that the UK accounts for less than 1% of global emissions, how can the Government justify punishing others with domestic policies that raise energy bills and risk de-industrialisation?

Turn 72 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

I completely agree with my hon. Friend. We have to be at the forefront. There needs to be a transition, but the thing that we are all grappling with is how we do that transition in a way that works for communities. At the heart of this issue are people—their lives, jobs and livelihoods. That transition is incredibly hard for every country that is going through it, but we make progress by learning from and supporting one another. We are a step ahead of other countries on this journey, and we have a responsibility—whether through technical assistance, financial support, or collaboration on unblocking private investment—to do our bit to help others go on this transition.
