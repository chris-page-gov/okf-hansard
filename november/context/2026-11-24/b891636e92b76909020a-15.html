Turn 115 · Lucy Rigby

The Bank regulates certain types of financial market infrastructure, as we all know. That infrastructure plays a critical role in our economy, supporting financial services by enabling transactions to be cleared, settled and recorded. In carrying out that role, the Bank’s primary objective is to protect and enhance financial stability. Its duties to facilitate innovation, however, vary according to the type of infrastructure it regulates. For central counterparties and central securities depositories, which support clearing and settlement, the Bank has a secondary objective to facilitate innovation in those services. There is currently no equivalent objective for its regulation of payment systems and digital settlement asset services. Turn 116 · Mark Garnier

We welcome the inclusion of an innovation objective in the Bank of England’s payment system functions, in pursuit of its financial stability objectives. Obviously, financial stability is incredibly important, but so is innovation. That means helping to facilitate innovation in things such as the operation of payment systems, services provided by recognised digital settlement asset service providers, and services provided by those connected with payment systems. It is important to recognise that the City of London has been innovative for quite some time—I think it was in 1157 that the Knights Templar first issued receipts to pilgrims and crusaders for valuable assets such as gold. Those receipts could be exchanged in the Holy Land for funds, creating what we now recognise as the first iteration of banknotes.
