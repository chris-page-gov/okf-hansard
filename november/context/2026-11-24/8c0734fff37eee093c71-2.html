Turn 9 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

My noble friend raises an important point. This has been a really interesting process, and I know that my right honourable friend has found it so. The Government will of course reflect on what has been learned from this, both on the specifics and for the future. We are absolutely committed to continuing to engage with disabled people and the organisations that represent them as we develop future policies, and we will ensure that their views are heard and are at the heart of those discussions around the policies that affect them. The Government will carefully consider the report that has been given and will continue to engage. We will also be carefully considering the recommendations of the Milburn review in considerable detail and will be bringing forward our proposals in due course.

Turn 15 · Baroness Sherlock (Lab)

I can absolutely reassure my noble friend that we will reflect on each of the recommendations and the whole report. We did not commission the report only to ignore it. We commissioned it to hear from the experienced, to have those conversations and to reflect on them. The review was very clear that too many people have experienced a system that can be complex, burdensome and, at times, simply dehumanising. It would be wrong just to dismiss those experiences. It is important to learn from them and to improve the system to make it fair and fit for the future. None of that needs to be separate from making sure that the system is appropriately robust and seeks appropriate evidence based on the eligibility criteria without dehumanising those who are involved. We have to listen to that and make sure that our reforms improve the situation.
