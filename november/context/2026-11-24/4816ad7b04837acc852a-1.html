Turn 3 · John Slinger

And I wish you a very happy birthday, Ms Jardine—that is the end of my speech [Laughter.] I am grateful to the Backbench Business Committee for granting me the debate. As I said when I pitched the idea, it is incredible that, to my knowledge, the topic has never been debated as a stand-alone issue in the House of Commons. I appreciate that I have been an MP for only two and half years, but that is long enough to know that the role is not quite fit for purpose. Academics and writers have addressed the issue, but it has remained on the fringes of our national debate. The role of an MP is ill-defined, often misunderstood, poorly resourced, underpaid and under-powered. It lacks professional structure, has insufficient authority and is, sadly, widely disrespected. That fuels a mismatch between public expectations and MPs’ powers to deliver. The status quo means Parliament does not do its job …
