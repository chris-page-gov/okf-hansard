Turn 9 · Lord Davies of Gower (Con)

My Lords, perhaps I might press the Minister on his Answer. The “neither confirm nor deny” principle is an accepted position on certain matters of national security. However, the issue here is that the Government broke that principle when they briefed to the press that the technical capability notice regarding Apple did in fact exist. So, can the Minister say whether the briefing to the press on the existence of the technical capability notice was approved by Government Ministers or not? If so, which Minister approved the briefing?

Turn 14 · Lord Collins of Highbury (Lab)

I hope that I have been clear in my answers to the questions so far that these are important matters that protect us all. The term “back door” has been used in some of the press comment on this issue, which suggests a means for the Government to bypass security and gain direct access to data. This is not something that our law permits. Every request for the content of a person’s communication is double-locked by the Secretary of State and the Independent Judicial Commissioner. If the request is deemed necessary and proportionate, it is sent to the company to provide only the data requested.
