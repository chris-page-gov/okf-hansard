Turn 3 · Speaker unlabelled

“With permission, I wish to update the House on Thursday’s coalition of the willing meeting in Berlin and Friday’s UK-EU Summit in Brussels. Before I do so, let me say that it is deeply disappointing that consensus was not reached at COP 31. This moment—after the summer the world saw—called for ambition. Instead, we were presented with a deal taking backwards steps on the climate, and we could not support it. I attended and addressed COP 31 because I wanted to leave no doubt about the UK’s commitment to our 2050 target. Shifting to clean, homegrown power is a solution to the climate and cost of living crises, as well as bolstering energy independence and national security. It is vital to the UK’s and the world’s long-term interests, and we will now consider, as we take on the presidency of the G20 next year, how the UK can rebuild global leadership on climate.

Turn 5 · Speaker unlabelled

The meeting was unanimous on two points. First, there must be no discussion about Ukraine’s security without Ukraine in the room, and no discussion about Europe’s without Europe. Secondly, a successful conclusion of the former, and a ceasefire, must be in place before any decisions about the latter. The meeting heard directly from President Trump on his journey home from China. He pointed to loss of life on a devastating scale, particularly on the Russian side, and the need to build on progress made by the Abu Dhabi talks. In summary, the message out of Berlin was undeniable: Europe’s resolve to defend Ukraine and defend itself is strengthening, and the coalition of countries will stand with them for as long as it takes.
