Turn 17 · Speaker unlabelled

A further area where I have the chance to make progress is on touring musicians and artists. I am particularly pleased that we have negotiated language that may make it easier in future for British bands, performers and artists to tour in Europe. Music is an area we are in danger of taking too much for granted. It is arguably our finest export—British music is known and loved around the world. Brexit made it harder to tour, but now we have the chance to explore mutually beneficial solutions. We still have work to do, but let me say directly to all our musicians: I fully intend to take this opportunity.

Turn 18 · Speaker unlabelled

I will also take the opportunity to negotiate greater ability to control our borders. Small boat crossings across the channel increased hugely post Brexit. By losing access to intelligence and the ability to return people, it has become harder to stop these crossings. I pay tribute to my right honourable friend the Home Secretary, whose determination to do so is delivering results: crossings are down nearly 40% on this time last year, and we are on course to record the fewest since 2020.

Turn 19 · Speaker unlabelled

But we need to go much further, and this agreement opens up that opportunity. In June, the European Commission adopted a new channel action plan—a framework to tackle the gangs behind the small boats and illegal immigration. The agreement paves the way for joint EU-UK activities under the plan and, crucially, information and data exchange. These are real wins. I would like to put on record my thanks to the Minister for European Relations for his skilful diplomacy throughout the negotiations. He has illustrated what our new approach to Europe can deliver—not pointlessly adversarial, like some, but constructive—and the result is a win-win for the UK and for the EU.

Turn 20 · Speaker unlabelled

His Majesty’s Treasury estimates that the win for the British economy is up to 0.5%—or £17 billion—on UK GDP a year. It makes the Bill we will introduce on Thursday the single biggest growth measure this House could pass in this Parliament. The Bill will represent the extent of the major changes that could be achieved in this Parliament, but as part of the 10-year plan process it is right that we consider the range of options for our long-term relationship with our largest market. We will say more next month when we publish the plan for Britain about how we move forward, but the initial agreement we have reached with the commission is that it will be an informal exchange of ideas, with the results to be presented around the time of the next UK-EU summit.

Turn 22 · Speaker unlabelled

What I am very clear about is that this deal is in the long-term interests of our young people. The agreement restores some of the freedom that many Members of this House will have taken advantage of when growing up to travel, study and work in Europe. Brexit closed down some of those opportunities, and the impact of Covid has meant that young people have been among the hardest hit in the last decade. This agreement will lift the lives of many young people. It is right that they are at the heart of it, and I hope that it leaves them with a greater sense of hope for the future on the horizon.
