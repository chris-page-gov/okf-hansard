Turn 2 · Sarah Dyke (Glastonbury and Somerton) (LD)

I beg to move, That this House has considered railway infrastructure in the South West. It is a pleasure to serve with you in the Chair, Mr Turner. In Somerton and Langport, trains run right through the heart of our communities but not one of them stops. The Prime Minister wants growth in every postcode, but there will be no growth in postcodes that cannot be reached. Oxford Economics has found that people with better transport links have better access to services and are more likely to take part in community life, while places that have lost their railway have been left with poorer access to jobs and services.

Turn 4 · Sarah Dyke

I entirely agree with my hon. Friend, and I will come to the impacts of the Beeching cuts later in my speech. Lack of investment and planning has left rural areas such as Glastonbury and Somerton cut off from the railway and unable to share in the growth and opportunity that urban areas enjoy. City regions make up just 9% of our land area but deliver 63% of our economic growth. That productivity gap between cities and the countryside is a constant drag on national growth, and poor transport connectivity makes it worse. The prize is enormous. The Rail Delivery Group estimates that the railway delivers more than £26 billion of benefits to our economy, environment and society every year and that a 40% growth in rail use by 2035 could increase that to £46 billion. Rail passengers also spend £98 billion a year in the communities they travel through. Rural areas should not be shut out of that …
