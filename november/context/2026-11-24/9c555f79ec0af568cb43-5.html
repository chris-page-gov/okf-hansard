Turn 33 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

The noble Lord is right. All these issues hang together with the opportunities we give to young people. I think I am right in saying that in, every other European country, unemployment, or the number of NEETs, is lower than it is in our own country. This is a reciprocal arrangement. In ensuring that our young people can go to live, study and work abroad, there is a reciprocal arrangement for other European young people. There are similar arrangements in place with Australia, New Zealand and other parts of the world, and no one complains about that impacting on youth unemployment here. It is important to address the point my noble friend makes: it is a reciprocal arrangement that will greatly benefit our young people. From talking young people on Friday, I know this is something that they will certainly welcome.

Turn 35 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

I probably cannot give anything on timing. We have discussed the professions issue in this House previously—I remember when we were discussing the Brexit deals, it was one of the concerns we raised—and a number of professions are being discussed. Discussing how long negotiations take is never an easy thing to do, as the noble Earl knows, but it is something we are very mindful of, as we are of the impact on different professions and the need for mutual recognition. That is something we are working towards. As soon as I have more information, I will come back to the House, but it is something we would like to have in place as soon as possible.

Turn 36 · Lord Clarke of Nottingham (Con)

My Lords, is it not the case that what the Prime Minister is really doing is negotiating a Brexit deal of the sort that should have been negotiated six years ago when we first left? Will the Minister confirm that the Government will continue that sort of process on things such as professional qualifications so that we wind up with a situation that is perfectly satisfactory for non-members such as Norway and Switzerland, whose economies are thriving much better than ours at the moment?

Turn 37 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

The noble Lord, with his experience, hits the nail on the head. It is interesting that the party opposite is the only party not happy with the Prime Minister’s arrangements, perhaps because their wonderful negotiation skills were unable to deliver such a deal at the time. He is right that there is more progress to be made. A number of issues still have to be resolved—we can think of bands, musicians and performing artists, and mutual recognition and education issues. It is a pragmatic and common-sense process of where we can make progress in the interests of the UK. It inevitably will involve trade-offs from time to time, but at the very heart of what we are debating is what is in the British interest.

Turn 38 · Lord Smith of Ranmoor (Lab)

My Lords, the Trade and Agriculture Commission, which I chaired, foresaw that both food and drink exporters and those who relied on imports of hard-to-source non-UK produce had no conceivable way of filling the void created by Brexit. No new trade deals did that. Most EU standards were originally ours, and neither farmers, producers nor consumers wanted us to turn the clock back on food safety, animal welfare or competitiveness. Does my noble friend the Leader agree, as all retailers do, that this agreement provides a much-needed reset that will benefit every household and add to our supply chain resilience?

Turn 39 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

I am grateful to the noble Lord, particularly for the experience that he brings to this issue. He is right to highlight an issue that has not been mentioned before—supply chain resilience—which is crucial for food supplies. I find it disappointing that when we talk about having mutual recognition and a level playing field on standards, there is this assumption that, somehow, we could have lower standards. He is absolutely right that when we joined the EU we took our food standards with us and raised the standards, and our higher animal welfare standards remain. That is what we negotiated. It is the right way forward. I am quite shocked at the vehemence with which the party opposite thinks that the deal it did back in 2016 could not be bettered and should have stayed in perpetuity.

Turn 40 · Lord Fox (LD)

My Lords, a thread running through the NATO Parliamentary Assembly over the last four days was the need for Europe to work much more effectively together in design, building and procurement for our defence needs. I welcome some of the issues in this report, but following up from my noble friend, it seems that defence co-operation is being negotiated alongside all the other issues. Does the Leader share my thoughts that this should be decoupled and that there should be a bespoke process for defence co-operation? Will she undertake to make sure that when we make progress on defence, it is not linked to other issues? We need this to happen now and not wait for all the other negotiations to be completed.
