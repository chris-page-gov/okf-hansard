Turn 29 · Mark Garnier (Wyre Forest) (Con)

The clause will modernise the Financial Conduct Authority’s powers to establish consumer redress schemes. Currently, the trigger simply requires of the FCA that consumers have suffered or might suffer loss or damage as a result of a failure to comply with the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The clause will change that by requiring a consumer redress scheme to meet three conditions: first, that it appears that the relevant firms may have failed to comply with the requirements, secondly that consumers have suffered or may suffer loss or damage, and thirdly that the FCA considers that it is desirable to secure redress—this is in reference to mass redress schemes. Turn 31 · Lucy Rigby

The clause will give the FCA an important new power to pause complaints while it investigates a potential recurring or systemic issue. Where a large number of complaints arise from the same underlying issue, the FCA may need time to understand what happened and how many consumers are affected by particular conduct or alleged conduct. It will probably also want time to consider what type of response might be appropriate. Without this power, individual complaints could continue to be decided by the FOS while work by the FCA is under way, with the risk that similar complaints may be decided in different ways before the FCA has established a consistent approach.
