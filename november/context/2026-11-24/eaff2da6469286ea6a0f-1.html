Turn 2 · The Minister for Secondary Care (Karin Smyth)

I have met a number of Members to discuss the issue of maternity services at St Helier hospital, where we are committed to ensuring safe, high-quality care. The board at the hospital is considering the safest option to address the water and estates challenges. Of course, maintaining patient safety and staff safety must be the highest priority. I understand that the trust board is making a decision at the beginning of December.

Turn 3 · Luke Taylor

I thank the Minister for meeting me and colleagues at very short notice to discuss ways to keep maternity services accessible for my constituents due to further failures at St Helier hospital. She knows that the water safety issues in the women’s health block mean the trust is facing difficult and expensive decisions and that, at the same time, lift failure has closed the paediatrics block and the re-ablement unit has been closed due to structural failures. I know the Minister understands the urgency of our pleas, but it would be remiss of me not to take every opportunity to ask that she redoubles efforts to work with the Treasury so that we can bring forward our new hospital, which will secure women’s health and maternity services in Sutton and deliver a brand-new, world-leading hospital for our patients.

Turn 4 · Karin Smyth

The hon. Member is quite right: it would be remiss of him not to advocate on behalf of his local trust, as many Members do. Further to the Secretary of State’s comments, the state of the estate that we inherited after the last 14 years is well documented. We have put the new hospital programme on a sustained footing, and we have ensured there is money allocated to it, which was not the case when we came into power. The capital budget is on a longer-term, sustainable footing. I am happy to continue to meet Members across the House to get these hospitals built quickly and on time for the patients they are designed to serve.
