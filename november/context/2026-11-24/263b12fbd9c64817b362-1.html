Turn 1 · The Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero (Miatta Fahnbulleh)

With permission, Madam Deputy Speaker, I would like to make a statement following the conclusion of the COP31 climate summit. This year’s climate conference sought to confront a hard truth: that the world is on course to exceed 1.5° of warming by around 2030. We all know what this will mean for communities at home and abroad. At around 1.4° of warming today, we are already seeing the impacts. Here in Britain, this summer brought record heatwaves, droughts and wildfires, and it was people’s homes, their livelihoods and the communities that they love that were impacted. Turn 6 · Bill Esterson (Sefton Central) (Lab)

As the shadow Minister mentioned Russia, let us talk about Russia. Russia had a booklet at COP advocating what it called the benefits of global warming. In Russia, of course, climate change includes the disastrous release of billions of tonnes of methane from the melting of the permafrost in Siberia. Russia was also instrumental at COP in blocking the reaffirmation of collective commitments to the transition away from fossil fuels in the final agreement. Does the Secretary of State agree that those in this place who want to delay or reverse the energy transition should think long and hard about the fact that they are not only supporting the damage done by climate change, but putting themselves on the same side as Putin’s murderous regime?

Turn 7 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

I could not agree more with my hon. Friend. In the end, we are standing alongside countries across the European Union and the global south that are on the side of change. Whether it is action on reducing methane, which we must take, or recognition that we must transition away from fossil fuels, on which we had collective global agreement at COP28, everyone can see the direction of travel that is required for the climate, and to protect our communities and ensure our energy security. We Government Members know which side we are on. I caution the Opposition; they should to reflect on whether they want to be standing on the same side as Putin.
