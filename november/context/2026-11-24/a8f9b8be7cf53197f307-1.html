Turn 2 · Madam Deputy Speaker (Ms Nusrat Ghani)

Before we come to the ten-minute rule motion, I should inform the House that the case relating to Elkin and Bell Funerals has been referred to the Court of Appeal by the Attorney General under the unduly lenient sentence scheme, and it is therefore sub judice. Mr Speaker has granted a waiver to allow the case to be referred to during this debate. The facts of the case and the convictions may be referred to, but Members may not discuss the sentencing review or matters that might influence sentencing decisions, such as the seriousness of the offences or the length of the sentences originally imposed.

Turn 3 · Dame Caroline Dinenage (Gosport) (Con)

I beg to move, That leave be given to bring in a Bill to require the Secretary of State to prepare and maintain a register of funeral directors, private mortuaries, direct cremation services and certain other persons who transport or hold the bodies of deceased persons; and for connected purposes. It has been three years since I first met Ministers to register my horror, because I had just discovered that anybody—no matter their background, expertise or resources—could set up business as a funeral director. Since then, there have been numerous meetings and numerous Ministers; the Government have said a lot and done almost nothing. The disturbing fact remains that in the funeral industry, there is no minimum qualification, no registration, no licensing, no inspection, no formal regulation and, crucially, no dedicated legislation to fall back on when things go wrong.
