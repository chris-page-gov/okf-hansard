Turn 33 · Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Con)

My Lords, my Amendment 138A is, in essence, a probing amendment about varying the recall petition threshold. It is pretty straightforward, and it is worth noting that it would not affect petitions already under way when the section would come into force. I hope that there are not many petitions for the recall of Members of Parliament should this amendment, unlikely as it may be, be incorporated into the Bill. This amendment would increase the proportion of eligible registered electors who must sign a recall petition for it to be successful from 10% to 15%. There have been six recall petitions since the Recall of MPs Act 2015 came into force: four succeeded, one failed and one ended early when the Member of Parliament resigned. Turn 35 · Speaker unlabelled

If we look at the case of Peter Bone in 2023, we see that he was removed with a recall petition with 10,505 signatures. In the 2019 election, he polled 62% of the vote—32,277 votes. One has to be mindful of the fact that 10% is a low threshold. In an increasingly divided and acrimonious political world, it is quite easy via social media to generate 10% of the electorate who, let us be honest, hate you, dislike you, loathe your party and want to kick you out of office, irrespective of your record in office working for them and others. My noble friend Lord Hayward says, “No way”, but I will not debate the subject with him as a former Member of the other House.

Turn 38 · Speaker unlabelled

My amendment, which is a probing amendment, seeks a middle way to raise my concerns about the use of recall petitions. I believe that they undermine the sanctity of the relationship between a Member of Parliament and his or her constituents and give rise to political activism, which is quite destructive. Let us be fair, it is also pretty expensive to run a recall petition.
