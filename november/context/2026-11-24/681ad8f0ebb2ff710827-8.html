Turn 57 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 77, in the name of my noble friend Lord Blunkett and spoken to by my noble friend Lady Royall, the Government agree that, if our democracy is to remain strong and trusted, our elected representatives must meet the high standards of conduct that the electorate expects of them. However, it is not clear what purpose such a declaration as proposed in this amendment would serve. If dismissal is not itself a disqualification from election, requiring every candidate to declare that they have not previously been dismissed risks implying that it is relevant to their eligibility, when in law it is not. What is more, it would create significant additional burdens for returning officers without offering any meaningful additional safeguard.

Turn 58 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 102, in the name of the noble Lord, Lord Elliott, seeks to remove rule 30 from Schedule 1 to the Representation of the People Act 1983, relating to Northern Ireland. This would remove the appointment procedures for polling and counting agents from elections in Northern Ireland. Polling agents play an important role in maintaining the safe and secure delivery of elections in Northern Ireland, and these roles help to uphold a fair and transparent democracy.

Turn 59 · Speaker unlabelled

Still on Northern Ireland, there is a second set of amendments that I have tabled in my name. These four amendments are minor and technical corrections to references in the Bill. They update incorrect references relating to completed corresponding number lists. One amendment also provides for that correction to have a retrospective effect. The intention of the amendments is to improve legal clarity by ensuring that the legislation reflects current policy and operational arrangements. The amendments simply ensure that the law refers to correct documents and is an accurate reflection of the procedure undertaken by presiding officers on the close of a poll.

Turn 60 · Speaker unlabelled

I thank the noble Lord, Lord Jackson, for Amendment 138A relating to MP recall petitions, and for his interest in this important process. Recall petitions are an essential feature of our parliamentary system, allowing electors the opportunity to hold their representative to account where a serious offence or misconduct has occurred. While I understand that some may have a preference for a higher threshold, there is no evidence to suggest that the current process is not working as intended. Therefore, the Government have no plans currently to amend it.
