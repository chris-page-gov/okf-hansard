Turn 126 · Speaker unlabelled

New clause 29 would allow for the appeal of FOS determinations to a new chamber of the first-tier tribunal. That would create a more formal, legalistic and adversarial system of dispute resolution, which is not what the FOS is intended to be. It is not intended to be a hugely adversarial or legalistic system, but a simple alternative to the courts, where consumers can access redress in a quick and fair way. A more legalistic approach would not be the right outcome for consumers. That is why the approach taken through the Bill preserves the core strengths and benefits of the ombudsman model, which is quick, informal and accessible, and delivers improvements alongside that.

Turn 127 · Blake Stephenson

Does the Minister accept that a gap exists and that new clause 29, tabled by my hon. Friend the Member for Wyre Forest, seeks to close it? To summarise, if a firm thinks the ombudsman has got a decision badly wrong, its only route today is judicial review. The creation of a new chamber of the first-tier tribunal would allow a proper appeal on the merits in a specialist forum, as is available for things such as tax disputes. That is a clear way to deal with the gap that exists. This is not just the view of industry; Mr Frank Stratford—I do not know him, but I have read his written evidence—has suggested that this would help ordinary people to deal with the costs of judicial review.
