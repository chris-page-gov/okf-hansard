Turn 50 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to Amendment 71. The Government agree that it is premature to consider proposals for compulsory voting. We are committed to encouraging participation in our democracy and ensuring that every eligible elector is able to vote. That is why we are undertaking a broad programme of action to remove barriers to participation. It is important that we allow the measures to take effect and assess their impact before reaching conclusions about whether further steps might be required.

Turn 53 · Speaker unlabelled

The Government already intend to undertake robust monitoring and evaluation following implementation, building on the MHCLG’s published evaluation strategy. This is expected to examine the impact on electors and electoral administration, how the reforms have been implemented in practice and whether their benefits outweigh the costs. Findings will be published in a timely manner, informed, where relevant, by the Electoral Commission’s statutory reports on elections. For those reasons, while we agree with the importance of evidence-led evaluation, we do not believe that the proposals in the noble Lord’s amendment would be suitable for this mechanism.

Turn 54 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendment 269, the City of London has distinct electoral arrangements which reflect its unique residential and commercial character. The current franchise provides representation both for residents and for the substantial business community which occupies premises and employs people in the City, and the Government do not consider it appropriate to seek to recast it.
