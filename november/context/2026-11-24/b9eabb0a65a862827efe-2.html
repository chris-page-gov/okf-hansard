Maternity readmissions within six weeks of birth have risen by more than a third in just three years, and data shows that at least one in five baby deaths last year may have been preventable. In June, the Government promised a maternity and neonatal commissioner; nearly six months on, no one has been appointed. How many avoidable tragedies have to occur before this Secretary of State gets a grip on maternity services?

Turn 16 · Helen Morgan (North Shropshire) (LD)

The Conservatives drove maternity centres to breaking point, but the Labour Government have been in power for more than two years, and I am afraid that they are not moving fast enough. The national maternity action plan is delayed until next year, and the recommendations of previous inquiries are outstanding. Today’s statistics show that rates of postpartum haemorrhage continue to rise and, worst of all, the budget for maternity services has effectively been cut by £80 million because of the removal of the ringfence. That funding would have paid for much-needed nurses, midwives and consultants, and dedicated safety funding has been gutted as a result. Will the Secretary of State restore that funding?
