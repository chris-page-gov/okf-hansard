Turn 55 · Florence Eshalomi

Clause 87 clarifies the definition of a relevant building. The Government consider that people should not be excluded from seeking redress simply because they live in a building containing only one dwelling. The clause therefore clarifies access to the construction product liability regime and ensures a more consistent and equitable approach. Clause 88 is a technical amendment to the limitation framework that supports construction product liability claims. The existing legislation refers to claims brought under section 149 of the Building Safety Act 2022. However, in practice, some claims may rely on rights arising from section 149 while being brought through a different legal route. This clause clarifies that the relevant limitation periods apply to both claims brought under section 149, and to other claims that rely on the rights provided by section 149. Turn 56 · The Chair

With this it will be convenient to consider the following: Clauses 90 to 94 stand part. New clause 7—Remediation contribution orders: consequential losses— “(1) Section 124 of the Building Safety Act 2022 is amended as follows. (2) In subsection (2) at end insert ‘or losses consequential on a relevant defect.’ (3) In subsection (2A)— (a) after the first ‘costs’ insert ‘or losses’ (b) at end insert— ‘(d) losses incurred or to be incurred as a result of— (i) the existence of a relevant defect; (ii) the failure to remedy a relevant defect; or (iii) the remedying of a relevant defect.’” This new clause would allow remediation orders against landlords and developers to be made so as to cover losses incurred as a result of building defects as well as the cost of remediation.
