Turn 18 · Baroness Morgan of Drefelin (Lab)

My Lords, I welcome the work the Government are doing with the voluntary sector through the covenant. However, I am concerned about the real challenges faced by the infrastructure bodies in the voluntary sector. For example, on 1 September Reach Volunteering announced that it was closing after 45 years, and the Directory of Social Change also announced that it is going into insolvency. What more can the Government do to ensure that we make the most of the commitment of the sector to work with government and drive forward positive outcomes for young people?

Turn 19 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

I understand the concerns expressed by my noble friend about the infrastructure organisations, and that was the thinking behind the setting up of the civil society covenant. It is the reason why civil society organisations have a direct route to government, through the Civil Society Council, to represent concerns about their own existence and health, as well as to inform the development of policy. They were able to do that at the most recent meeting, where, for example, youth unemployment was one of the issues discussed. I have already outlined some of the ways the Government are supporting the funding position of civil society organisations, and I know it is something that colleagues across government will continue to think hard about.

Turn 20 · Baroness Bull (CB)

My Lords, poor numeracy is both impacting economic growth and holding young people back. The recent evaluation of Multiply indicated the importance of civil society organisations, charities and community organisations in supporting young people who have been failed by formal education. Of course, this country now has no adult numeracy programme. Will the Government commit to working with the civil sector to re-establish a programme that has all benefits that Multiply brought?
