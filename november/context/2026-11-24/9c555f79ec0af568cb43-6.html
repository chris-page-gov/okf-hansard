Turn 41 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

I think we have shown by these negotiations that we can negotiate some issues and then move on to others. On defence, defence procurement and security, we have lost out—we have not had access to information that would have helped us tackle illegal migration, organised crime and crime across borders in a way that we should have. Particularly on defence, it is important that those discussions, not only through the EU but through the coalition of the willing and NATO, work at pace. We know how important that is and have seen it just this week. Ukraine was top of the agenda when the coalition of the willing met, and the Prime Minister also spoke about it. Ukraine’s safety and security are very much linked to our own.

Turn 46 · Baroness Bull (CB)

My Lords, while I welcome the warm words on artists touring in both the joint statement and the PM’s Statement, I share the disappointment of the creative sector that there has been no concrete progress. There is clear recognition across the Parliamentary Partnership Assembly of the mutual loss, artistic and economic, as well as repeated recommendations by the Partnership Council to get on and address this. Given this mutual appetite, can the Leader of the House say what the PM’s words mean when he says that he fully intends to seize “the chance to explore mutually beneficial solutions”? Does she agree that now is the time for a separate, stand-alone, formal dialogue that would unlock the benefits of enabling cultural exchange and touring?

Turn 47 · Baroness Smith of Basildon (Lab)

This is part of the discussion the Prime Minister is having. The noble Baroness will have heard his enthusiasm yesterday to address this. She will also have heard one Labour MP say that her niece, when touring across Europe, had to return to the UK, while the rest of the company could carry on, so that she did not fall foul of the rules and regulations. It is something the Prime Minister is personally committed to. I cannot say that it will be a separate stream, because lots of different streams of negotiations and discussions are going on. I can give the noble Baroness an assurance that he is well aware of the damage that has been done—not just to the music industry, which he has a particular interest in, but to dance, performing and cultural exchanges. We can do better than this and we really want to, so it is a particular priority of the Prime Minister to address it.
