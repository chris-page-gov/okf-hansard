Turn 17 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

It is remiss of me not to have thanked my noble friend Lord Touhig and the noble Baroness, Lady Browning, for their work in this field, so I add my thanks to them at this point. Your Lordships’ House is full of experts, and I am sure that there are things close to all our hearts that every one of us would want to see a specific strategy on. This Government are about taking action, in many cases cross-governmentally, to improve people’s lives. That is particularly true for DHSC. There is no point in having myriad strategies if we do not take action and implement them. I will take the noble Baroness’s comments back to the Minister, particularly those about the condition she raises, but I am not sure that a plethora of strategies is going to help us tackle some of the most pressing issues facing this country.

Turn 19 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

We recognise that waits for both ADHD and autism assessments remain too long, and that there are significant impacts on individuals and families because of this. The Government have set out expectations, via NHS England, for ICBs and for providers to reduce those long waits, and to improve access, experience, quality and productivity over the next three years. We are also moving to a needs-led support model, so that people can have earlier access to information, support and appropriate interventions, based on individual needs, before they receive any formal diagnosis.

Turn 21 · Baroness Hyde of Bemerton (Lab)

We are committed to ensuring that anybody who wants to work can work, and that also applies to people with autism and ADHD. Through DWP-funded workshops, organisations such as ACAS have been involved in helping employers understand what a neurodivergent person might need in terms of interview and through the employment journey. The Timms review is out today, and later there will be an opportunity to discuss it, how people might access work and how to balance it with the findings in the Milburn review. There has been £1 billion through the Connect to Work programme. There are many ways in which this Government are working, through DWP and through businesses and other NGOs, to ensure that employers who want to be proactive in hiring neurodivergent people are well equipped.
