Turn 89 · Speaker unlabelled

On Amendments 64 and 65, my preference is for noon deadlines. I always think that noon is a good time for a deadline, because if you have an IT problem or some other difficulty in the run-up to the deadline, it is during normal working hours—you do not have to get the IT team out of their beds late at night or early in the morning to fix something. It also means that when there is immediate follow-up work to do after the deadline, there are several hours remaining of the normal working day.

Turn 90 · Speaker unlabelled

However, despite my preference for noon as a deadline, I concede that the evidence is thoroughly against me on this particular point, raised by my noble friend Lord Rennard in his amendment. He made reference to the data kindly provided by the Minister in answer to one of my Written Questions earlier this year. It is worth highlighting in particular the data from April this year: the calendar month just before the big round of elections that we had. Between midnight and noon, in the first half of the day, 28% of the online requests to register to vote came in. A further 32% came in between noon and 5 pm, and 40% came in between 5 pm and midnight. The 5 pm to midnight window comes up again and again in the data as being very popular and very heavily used—I say that somewhat regretfully, given my preference for noon deadlines.

Turn 91 · Speaker unlabelled

Of course, some people will always leave things until the last moment. Therefore, whatever the deadline is, there is likely to be a certain spike in applications just before it, but we have good evidence from things such as the DWP’s digital systems about what time of day people find the space, the headspace and the ability to sit down at a computer or with their phone to do something online and engage with a public service or the Government online. Those late hours in the day, particularly for people with caring responsibilities for young children, are often the time when they can finally get round to doing something important such as that. Therefore, my noble friend Lord Rennard has made a very compelling case about the advantages of a midnight deadline.

Turn 95 · Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Con)

My Lords, this has been a very interesting debate. I was going to keep my remarks quite short—and I still will—until the noble Lord, Lord Pack, mentioned, slightly disobligingly, that the Conservatives want to register people who are foreigners and not British people. It occurs to me that the internet never forgets. The worst case of vote stacking and manipulation of proxy votes in British history took place in the Northwold ward in Hackney between 1994 and 1998, where individuals were registering under-18s, foreign nationals, people living in derelict properties, et cetera. The individuals were eventually found guilty in 2001 of conspiracy to defraud and other election offences, the main individual being Councillor Zev Lieberman of Northwold ward. It would be remiss of me not to remind the Committee that he was a Liberal Democrat.
