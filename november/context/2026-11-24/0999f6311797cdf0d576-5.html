Turn 33 · Lord Norton of Louth (Con)

Turn 34 · Baroness Pinnock (LD)

My Lords, I will speak mainly to Amendment 251 in the names of my noble friends Lord Pack and Lord Wallace, which asks the Government to think about the efficacy of the different voting systems that are already in use across the nations of the UK. So far, the debate has focused entirely on parliamentary elections, but local elections are also affected by this Bill. Turn 36 · Speaker unlabelled

On the matter of democratic representation, which I hope is the noble Baroness’s main intention in tabling this amendment, I remind noble Lords that the square mile is home to only 8,500 people, while further serving a working population of over 600,000. While it is paramount that the interests and voting rights of residents are protected, as they currently are, I am struggling to understand how the complete removal of the business franchise would make the corporation more representative of the extraordinary breadth of the true community that exists in the City.

Turn 38 · Baroness Bennett of Manor Castle (GP)

My Lords, I just want to address the policing point because the noble Lord questioned how much I was aware of this. Is he aware of what the report by the noble Lord, Lord Lisvane, says about the governance of the policing of the City of London? It says of the Police Authority Board: “It is of a reasonable size; but the opaque method of appointment of members … has the same characteristics as elsewhere”— the noble Lord, Lord Lisvane, is speaking about committees across the City of London. He goes on to say that “there is no sure means of securing appointment of members with relevant skills and experience, nor of achieving the mix of skills appropriate to a high-functioning Board”. I wonder whether the noble Lord, Lord Udny-Lister, was aware of that.
