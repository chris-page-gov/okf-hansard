Turn 57 · Speaker unlabelled

At the heart of the Bill is the armed forces covenant: our promise to those who serve that, just as they stand ready to defend us, we will stand behind them. I am really proud that we are delivering on our commitment to put the covenant fully into law, but this must be the beginning and not the end of our work. Putting the covenant into law will not automatically remove the disadvantages associated with military life. Yes, it will mean that more questions are asked of all government processes and at all levels, but now we must focus on putting the covenant into practice so that our armed forces community can really feel the difference in their everyday lives. That is no easy task, but it is our duty to follow through on it.

Turn 58 · Speaker unlabelled

On reserve forces and cadets associations, covered in Lords amendments 11 and 25, I am pleased to see that the Government have listened and tightened up this provision. It has been long agreed that change should come, but I really hope that the Government recognise the service, heritage and commitment of our reserve forces and cadets associations. In particular, I pay tribute to the south-east reserve forces and cadets association, which is based in my constituency. I see the fantastic work it does on a weekly basis in our community, bringing together our armed forces, employers, schools, volunteers and the wider community.

Turn 59 · Speaker unlabelled

That brings me to my last, broader point about the importance of place in our national defence. Everything we have talked about contributes to the lives of our serving personnel, but place also really matters, as the Government have rightly recognised through their defence growth deals in places such as Barrow and Plymouth. These are downpayments on the contributions that these towns and cities are making to our national endeavour, recognising that investing in our defence industries must mean investing in the communities that sustain them. I believe that we need to apply that same thinking to the towns and cities that our armed forces call home. In the increasingly uncertain world in which we live, the strength of those communities is itself a strategic asset.

Turn 60 · Speaker unlabelled

We need our military towns to be places that people are proud to call home—places with thriving town centres, good public services, modern infrastructure, and opportunities for the whole family. That matters to morale, recruitment and retention, but it also matters to our wider national resilience. We cannot expect our communities to be at their strongest in times of crisis if we have not invested in them in the years beforehand. I saw the strength of that community spirit when Aldershot hosted the national Armed Forces Day celebrations this summer. What stayed with me was the enormous sense of pride across our towns, and what we achieve by bringing our military and civilian communities together. We need to build on that.

Turn 61 · Speaker unlabelled

The Bill rightly recognises the importance of the people and families behind our armed forces, but I want the Government to go further and recognise that places stand behind them too. Just as we are making a downpayment on the future of our defence industrial communities, we must make one on the future of our armed forces communities. Investing in the places that are home to those who defend our country is not simply a matter of local regeneration; it is an investment in our national security.
