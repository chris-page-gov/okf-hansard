Turn 5 · The Minister of State, Department for Education and Department for Work and Pensions (Baroness Smith of Malvern) (Lab)

My Lords, civil society is an important partner in delivering opportunity and growth. Through the civil society covenant, we are strengthening collaboration between government and the sector, nationally and locally. We are putting this into practice through Youth Matters, the enrichment framework and Skills England’s employability skills framework, working with civil society organisations to help young people build skills, confidence and connections to education, training and employment.

Turn 6 · Baroness Pitkeathley (Lab)

My Lords, I thank my noble friend for that reply. Civil society is a significant contributor to the development of skills, economic growth and employment, although its contribution is often overlooked. Will the Government consider Alan Milburn’s recommendation that charities and other non-profit organisations should be eligible to operate youth opportunity services where they can meet the required standards? Would the Minister perhaps be willing to meet the NCVO and other civil society organisations to discuss ways in which civil society can play a greater role in supporting and helping to grow the economy?

Turn 7 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

My Lords, certainly my noble friend does sterling work to make sure that civil society is not overlooked. We have committed to take forward Alan Milburn’s recommendation for a new locally led youth service, bringing employment, skills, health and wider support together around young people. Civil society has an important role to play, and we will of course consider how charities and other non-profit organisations can contribute as we develop the detailed delivery model for the new service. In doing that, I would be happy to meet the NCVO and other civil society organisations, as my noble friend asked.

Turn 8 · Lord Mohammed of Tinsley (LD)

My Lords, for the last 25 years I have worked around charities and youth services. In my experience, the best-placed people are from local youth clubs and TARAs, which employ staff from within those communities. They are often the ones who are successful in reaching those hard-to-reach young people who are not in education, employment or training. How are we going to ensure that those small organisations, which often get only one or two years’ grant aid, are supported in delivering what Milburn identified as very important last week?
