Turn 49 · Monica Harding (Esher and Walton) (LD)

The point at which the UK lost its leadership on climate change was under a Conservative Government. Today the UK Health Security Agency reports that more than 5,000 deaths in England this year were linked to the extreme summer heat, after our hottest summer on record. The climate crisis is not some way off; it is now. Does the Secretary of State agree with Al Gore that it is outrageous that a handful of petrostates have a veto power on any mention of fossil fuels in the COP31 agreement? What will the UK Government do to keep the multilateral consensus-based process on track, or does she think there is a different way for states to tackle the climate crisis collectively?

Turn 50 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

I agree with the hon. Lady and Al Gore that it is outrageous that we did not reach a consensus on the scale of ambition. It is a process, and ultimately countries have gone on this journey, painful as it is. At some point, all countries signed up to the transition away from fossil fuels. There is a process, and we will work with all parties to ensure that we are moving forward collectively, because this is a collective action problem. It is not enough for us to act if we are not acting in concert with others, so we will work with all sides, including those that are a bit more resistant, in order to move forward.

Turn 52 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

My hon. Friend is a very passionate advocate for climate action. Of course we need to adjust our economy in response to climate change, but as a country we have delivered a 54% reduction in carbon emissions at the same time as growing our economy by 85%. I believe that the two are possible, but it requires the Government to be intentional about it. It requires deliberate policies to think about how we can ensure that we do the transition in a way that is good for our economy and, critically, that we do it in a way that is good for our communities.

Turn 56 · Miatta Fahnbulleh

We are very clear that there is an economic opportunity from AI, but we need to ensure that we shape it so that it works for the benefit of our communities. That is a combination of focusing on strategic AI projects that have impacts on our wider supply chain and, critically, of ensuring that, where there are data centres, they have a very clear community benefit, whether in what we extract from the companies to invest in communities that host AI centres or wider impacts for the supply chain, chip manufacturing and other things that may come out of this. My hon. Friend is right to raise caution, and I know that communities are worried about AI, but the Government are committed to ensuring that this is AI for good and AI that fundamentally works for our communities.
