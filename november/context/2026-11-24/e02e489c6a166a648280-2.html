Turn 11 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

Over the last 10 to 15 years, civil society certainly has suffered from some of the impacts of austerity, for example. That is why this Government have already taken action in a range of ways: lifting some of the burdens from the Charity Commission, which is estimated to have given back approximately £47 million to those charities; launching the local covenant partnerships programme with £11.5 million of investment to support civil society organisations so they can collaborate more effectively with local authorities and public service providers in those 15 areas; and supporting, with investment through the civil society covenant, civil society organisations to better enter into competitions and tendering in public sector supply chains. All those things are helping civil society to play its crucial role in delivering for young people.

Turn 16 · Baroness Cash (Con)

My Lords, we are all concerned about the number of NEETs and how to help young people. But since the Government’s national insurance rise, a third of the charities surveyed by the Charity Finance Group have made staff redundant, and the National Youth Agency has found that the share of youth workers in permanent jobs has fallen to 67%. Last Tuesday, the Government endorsed Alan Milburn’s report, which we welcome, and the calls for every major labour market decision to be tested against its effect on young people. But on Friday, they agreed to let 100,000 young Europeans come to live and work here for up to three years. Did they carry out the test recommended by Alan Milburn before they signed that agreement, and if not, how do they explain such a contradictory act?
