Turn 9 · Speaker unlabelled

The agreement will also improve economic security here at home. Friday was a landmark moment. There is a clear consensus among the British public: almost three-quarters of people want closer ties with Europe. In the last decade, the existing Brexit deal has had a negative impact on UK growth and added to cost of living and cost of business pressures. It has become harder for our businesses to export to the EU. People were promised more control over immigration, but it has not materialised, as the rise of the small boats crisis in the years after Brexit demonstrates.

Turn 12 · Speaker unlabelled

Ahead of last Friday, I set three tests, all of which I said would need to be met before agreeing to any deal. First, the agreement must be consistent with the red lines set out in our manifesto. Secondly, it must improve the current Brexit deal and bring us closer to what people thought they were voting for in 2016. Thirdly, it must pass what I call the Makerfield test by making life better for people here at home, particularly in communities that have been neglected for too long. The deal agreed on Friday passes all three tests and will deliver real benefits for Britain.

Turn 13 · Speaker unlabelled

Under the current Brexit deal, British food exporters have had to pay for over 1 million export certificates to trade with the EU. This has hit British businesses with millions of pounds in costs and thousands of hours of administration. Over 16,000 businesses that used to export to Europe have stopped doing so. According to the National Farmers’ Union, agricultural exports to the EU have fallen by over 30%, with some of our produce banned from being sent to the continent altogether.

Turn 16 · Speaker unlabelled

More positively, by linking our emissions trading scheme with the EU’s, big industry in Britain will benefit from being released from burdensome paperwork and the threat of taxes on £6.5 billion of exports. There could be further progress on energy costs, too. The possibility opened up by the agreement to link together our electricity markets could help to reduce bills in the long term, and particularly so in Northern Ireland, if we can agree a good, balanced deal that is in our national interests. This is one area where the agreement will pave the way for further discussions. Another is the question of tuition fees for UK universities. We will keep the House updated on the progress of these separate negotiations.
