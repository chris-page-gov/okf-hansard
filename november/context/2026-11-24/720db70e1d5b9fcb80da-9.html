Turn 65 · Sir Stephen Timms

My hon. Friend speaks from the heart and he is absolutely right. When changes were proposed in the summer of last year, a lot of people claiming PIP made the point to us, as I have mentioned, that they used their PIP to get into work. That raises the question of what more we can do in PIP to remove barriers that hold disabled people back at the moment. There is also lots of other work going on, such as the Connect to Work programme, which is now rolled out across England and Wales. We are seeing encouraging results from that, but there is more to be done. I agree with my hon. Friend about the great importance of opening up those opportunities.

Turn 67 · Sir Stephen Timms

There is a variety of different kinds of services: some may be health services, while others may be services provided locally by a charity, for example, which people can be made aware of. I take the hon. Lady’s point that the picture varies in different parts of the country. At the moment we have the Motability service, where people choose to forgo their cash benefit in order to access, say, a wheelchair-adapted car. That system works well. There might be other kinds of equipment that could be made available on a similar basis, whereby people would choose to take the equipment or the service and forgo their cash in response. As her question hints at, the picture will be different in different places, and the Government are, of course, working to improve services in every part of the country.

Turn 69 · Sir Stephen Timms

Alan Milburn’s report is clear that support for additional costs for young people will continue to be in place. However, the absolute overriding ambition in his report is to ensure that disabled young people around the country have the opportunity for work, many of whom have not had that opportunity up until now as barriers have been in the way. We need to see those barriers removed and improve the Government’s support, and we are determined that that should happen.
