We are taking the final steps to reverse the damaging Lansley reforms by bringing NHS England back into the Department of Health and Social Care, reducing swathes of bureaucracy right across the country. Turn 14 · Damian Hinds

The Government said this reorganisation would eventually save £1 billion a year but cost £1 billion to get there. Guess what? The costs are going up, and the savings are coming down. On top of that, there is now the impact of the Employment Rights Act 2025, because it turns out that the No. 1 user of zero-hours contracts in this country might be none other than the national health service—I say “might be” because it also turns out that the Government do not know. That could cost the NHS a further almost £1 billion a year. Will the Government reorganise away from bank staffing, will they exempt the public sector from the Employment Rights Act, or will they cut something else to cover this spectacular oversight?
