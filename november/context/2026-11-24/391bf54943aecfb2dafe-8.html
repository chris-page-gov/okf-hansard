Turn 58 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 93 addresses remediation contribution orders, which can be used by “interested persons”, including leaseholders, landlords and regulators, to obtain funding to fix building safety defects. They are obtained through a court order requiring liable parties to pay. The clause will require sums secured through remediation contribution orders for a building’s future remediation works to be held in a trust. The trust will protect the funds for remediation of building safety defects and ensure they can only be used for the purposes intended. The trust structure will prevent funds from being distributed to creditors, even if a landlord obtains the funds and then subsequently becomes insolvent. The trustee will be responsible for holding the remediation sums in a trust so that they can only be used for the remediation purposes set out in the order.

Turn 59 · Speaker unlabelled

Clause 94 enables similar arrangements in Wales. It enhances the regulation-making powers already established in the Building Safety (Wales) Act. That means that, in Wales, regulations can be used to protect funds recovered through remediation contribution orders in a trust. The clause will ensure that remediation contribution order payments in Wales are afforded the same level of protection from insolvency as those in England. I commend the clauses to the Committee.
