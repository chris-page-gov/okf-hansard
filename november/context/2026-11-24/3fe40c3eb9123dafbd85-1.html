Turn 2 · The Minister for Social Care and Mental Health (Alison McGovern)

Being able to work can be every bit as vital to our health as any treatment that we receive, which is why, alongside all the work being done by our colleagues in the Department for Work and Pensions, we are supporting access to talking therapies and to the healthcare that matters most for participation in work. Prevention starts with young people. Professor Fonagy’s review showed that there is more to do, and we will soon publish a new mental health strategy responding to the issues that he identified.

Turn 3 · Kim Leadbeater

In 2023, when I sat on the Opposition Benches, I wrote a report called “Healthy Britain”, which set out a framework for a cross-Government, cross-sector approach to the health of the nation, with a particular focus on prevention and early intervention and a holistic approach to physical, mental and social wellbeing. I am delighted that the Labour Government have focused more on this agenda, but what are the Government doing to turbocharge that work so that, when possible, we can prevent people from becoming sick in the first place and build a strong, healthy nation with the many benefits—including, of course, economic benefits—that that brings?

Turn 4 · Alison McGovern

I thank my hon. Friend both for the work that she did in opposition and for her continued championing of this issue. We have much more to do to create a healthier Britain, and she will have heard the comments of the Prime Minister and the Chancellor on the subject of prevention. This matters nowhere more than in mental health, and, as I have said, we will soon publish our strategy, which has been supported by colleagues across Government, including the Department for Education in respect of children’s mental health and the Department for Culture, Media and Sport in respect of participation.
