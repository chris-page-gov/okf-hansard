Turn 69 · Lord Pack (LD)

Any electoral statistic—perhaps other than who is the Prime Minister the day after a general election—is likely to generate relatively limited wider public interest. Earlier, we debated compulsory voting and the ability of people to spoil their ballot papers. The answers to basic questions—such as whether the rate at which people are spoiling their ballot papers is going up over time—would inform such debates as the one that we had earlier. Even for the 2024 general election, you could easily spend a few hours trying to rummage around to gather the data consistently, let alone if you want to go back a few general elections and have a time series over 10 or 20 years. That is an example of the sort of data that would be useful to have, that would help inform future debates, and—who knows —that may encourage a few more future electoral geeks.

Turn 70 · Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Con)
