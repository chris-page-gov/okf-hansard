Turn 122 · Lord Jackson of Peterborough (Con)

I take that point, but it runs up against the data provided by the Electoral Commission, which finds that the amount of people who had intended to vote but were put off by voter ID is tiny compared to the number predisposed to vote. It is even smaller among the overall electorate. I disagree with the noble Baroness even though, on the face of it, I could probably support two of her three amendments, because a report by the Electoral Commission into voter ID would actually find that there is no problem with voter ID. That is why mature western democracies and countries from across the globe have adopted voter ID; it supports the integrity of the electoral system.

Turn 123 · Speaker unlabelled

This is part of why I have put Amendment 96 forward. We have an obligation to protect the integrity of the system not just for the voters for whom it is a sacred duty to vote for their elected representatives and to feel that the process is honest, straightforward and clean, but for the people whom we are asking to sit for hours on end in a polling station. It is very difficult for them to potentially witness electoral malpractices and the specific offence, under Section 60 of the Representation of the People Act 1983, of personation. That is why I have brought forward this amendment.
