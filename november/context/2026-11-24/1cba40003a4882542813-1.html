Turn 4 · Lord in Waiting/Government Whip (Lord Wilson of Sedgefield) (Lab)

My Lords, there will be no VAT lost as a result of these proposals. The £90,000 minimum platform threshold and the exclusion for second-hand goods do not change existing VAT obligations on businesses. This measure is designed to strengthen VAT compliance by ensuring that VAT already owed is collected. The reforms are expected to raise approximately £1 billion in additional revenue over the next five years, which is new money that will be invested in the high street.

Turn 6 · Lord Wilson of Sedgefield (Lab)

In 2001, the Government removed VAT relief for imports of up to £15 and made online marketplaces liable for VAT on goods sold by overseas businesses. This was highly successful and raised £8 billion, and it continues to raise about £1.8 billion a year. Customs de minimis relief on parcels up to the value of £135 will be removed by 2028 at the latest, raising a further £500 million per annum. Non-UK sellers will also be required to appoint a UK-based fiscal representative, who would assume joint and several liability for customs debts. The Government’s approach has been welcomed by online platforms such as Amazon and eBay and will continue to be monitored after implementation. As I said, the proposals will raise an additional £1 billion for the high street. Of course the Minister will accept a meeting with the noble Lord.
