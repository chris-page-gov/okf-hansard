VAT Exemptions: Retailers and Online Marketplaces

Lords · 2026-11-24 · Turns 9–16 of 24 in this section.

Hansard speaking order. A reviewed question or reply has its own link. Open Hansard for the full wording of any excerpt.

Turn 9 · Lord Fox (LD)

My Lords, I am less surprised than the noble Baroness, because I have sat through many different Bills in which online marketplaces have been given an advantage over bricks and mortar sellers in this country. Whether it is in business rates, product safety or, most recently, late payment, online marketplaces are consistently treated differently. The Product Regulation and Metrology Act specifically started to look at online retailers and their responsibility on safety, but it is very slow. Where have we got to on the consultation that started last year? When will we see online marketplaces treated in the same way as bricks and mortar shops? At the moment, there is a huge bias against our high streets.

Turn 10 · Lord Wilson of Sedgefield (Lab)

The noble Lord raises an important point. Obviously, we all want to see the result of that consultation. On the online sector as compared with the physical sector of the economy, we now have the 15% global rate that is being imposed on online organisations and platforms around the world, which will bring in additional revenue, and the 2% digital services tax is also being brought in. We are starting to treat the online and physical worlds of the economy, so to speak, as equally as we can, but it is a work in progress. We need to wait for the consultation that the noble Lord mentioned, but this is work that we will continue to do until we get it right.

Turn 14 · Lord Wilson of Sedgefield (Lab)

I thank my noble friend for that question. Given the significant levels of VAT non-compliance that the measures are intended to address, the Government felt obliged to act quickly on this issue, and that is why we are implementing it when we are. The Government will continue to engage with online marketplaces and affected businesses ahead of implementation in October next year, and HMRC will publish guidance in advance to support preparations.
