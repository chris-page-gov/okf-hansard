Turn 90 · Speaker unlabelled

But we do not believe it is necessary for a separate report on children’s services. Turn 91 · Speaker unlabelled

With regard to Amendment 279N, tabled by the noble Baroness, Lady Pidgeon, those who are facing fertility problems deserve consistent, evidence-based care, as she described. While we expect ICBs to have regard to relevant NICE guidelines in their decisions, I recognise that some ICBs are not offering what NICE recommends. Through NHS England, we are in the process of gathering data to understand what ICBs are currently commissioning, and we will continue working to close the gaps to make sure that everyone gets fair access. Of course, in future, because of the Bill, Ministers will be able to directly hold ICBs to account for their commissioning choices.

Turn 93 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, on Amendment 54, in the name of the noble Lord, Lord Lansley, which the noble Earl, Lord Howe, also spoke to, regarding eligibility for continuing healthcare, I say that it depends on primary health needs. Assessing this will consider the nature, intensity, complexity and unpredictability of the condition, including the challenges created by fluctuating needs. So, eligibility will not depend on deterioration, nor on terminal status. Therefore, we believe that this amendment is not necessary, although we understand the intent, as patients with serious, chronic conditions are already eligible for continuing healthcare, and it is not linked to whether they have a named, particular diagnosis or condition.
