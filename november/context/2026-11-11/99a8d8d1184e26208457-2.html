Turn 9 · Baroness Doocey (LD)

My Lords, the director-general of the National Crime Agency has described how criminals are chemically bonding cocaine in everyday materials such as plastic and glue to evade detection. Given that the Government’s number one priority is to reduce the harm that drug does, does the Minister feel we have the right balance in the use of resources between pursuing people who have drugs for their own personal use and dismantling the ever-more sophisticated drug networks that are actually supplying the drugs?

Turn 10 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

We have to take on the organised criminals who are supplying the drugs. In 2025-26, the National Crime Agency seized 189 tonnes of class A drugs. The quantity of illicit drugs seized at the border in 2024-25 was the largest, at 148 tonnes, recorded since 1973. So there are efforts to stop organised crime, but obviously, drug barons are looking at a whole range of methods, and the areas the noble Baroness has mentioned are extremely important. We need to keep on top of that, and that is why we have also put in some £3.4 billion over the next three years on drug and alcohol treatment as a whole, to ensure that we look at who the users of drugs are and how we can help support them.

Turn 11 · Lord Pickles (Con)

My Lords, so far nobody has mentioned those who die as a result of drug use, particularly in road traffic accidents, but who themselves have never taken drugs in their lives. One of the great successes of the last 30 years has been the reduction in the number of people who die on the roads as a result of alcohol use. By the end of this decade, the number who die on the roads because of illicit drug use is likely to exceed the number who die because of alcohol use. There is variation from force to force in terms of the equipment used and the practices, so will the noble Lord consider getting police authorities together and getting best practice established, so that we can start to push down on drug use on the roads, as we have pushed down on drink?

Turn 16 · Lord Hanson of Flint (Lab)

It is extremely important that we send messages out throughout the whole of the age range that using drugs has potential difficulties and can cause death. It is important that we do that in schools and raise education for those involved in drug use. Going back to the noble Baroness’s question earlier, it is important that we do that when people are in prison as well, to help them leave prison in a way which improves their behaviour and gives them a better chance in life outside. Those are important issues and not ones the Government will shirk.
