Turn 129 · Speaker unlabelled

We are not too late, but it is two years since we proposed these measures, and that is two years of wasted time, nursing dependency and failing to think strategically about how our most valuable technological asset could be a source of strength. It is two years in which this House has repeatedly warned, as the noble Lord, Lord Tarassenko, said, that this is the window in which to act. We are reaching a critical moment, and it cannot be that the Government seek to be at the forefront of AI and at the same time continue to undervalue the assets they already hold.

Turn 130 · Speaker unlabelled

AI is only as good as the data it has, and the health data of the NHS is unparalleled. In fact, during last year’s debate on copyright, I had a call with one of the most senior global leaders in tech who, while conciliatory on the question of copyright itself, changed his tone entirely when I raised the question of NHS data. He said, “The Kenyans have kidneys too”. Of course they do, but they do not have the NHS’s extraordinary accumulation of longitudinal health data, built up over generations and representing such a broad and valuable resource for medical research and AI. It was a chilling moment, revealing the extent to which the unique value of our national data assets can be dismissed by those seeking access to them. This is a rich national inheritance, not the Government’s to give away foolishly but to be held in trust for us. We are undervaluing data in every part of our economy, …
