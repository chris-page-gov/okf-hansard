Turn 25 · The Minister for the Future of Work (Kate Dearden)

I beg to move, That the Committee has considered the draft Employment Rights Act 2025 (Unfair Dismissal) (Removal of Limit on Compensation) (Consequential Amendments) Regulations 2026. It is a pleasure to serve under your chairmanship, Sir Alec. The draft regulations, which were laid before the House on 12 October, will make necessary consequential amendments arising from the Employment Rights Act 2025. Commencement regulations have been signed to confirm that, from 1 January 2027, the statutory cap on compensation awards for unfair dismissal will be removed. A number of other employment law provisions do not contain their own stand-alone compensation limits; instead, they cross-refer to the unfair dismissal cap. As I have said, this change does not reset how tribunals calculate awards. Tribunals have well-established ways to calculate compensation that might be awarded for particular losses, and they will continue to base those awards on evidenced actual and projected financial losses. We are committed to ensuring that all employees receive fair compensation when they have been unfairly dismissed. We are talking about unfair dismissal here. As I have said, the provisions concern claims for detriment brought by workers. Our long-standing approach is that such workers should receive neither more nor less compensation than they would have received as employees for bringing an unfair dismissal claim.
