Turn 10 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

We do believe that we are on track for a start-up in 2031, and of course we work with international partners. The UK is working closely with European partners to develop a secure and resilient supply, which will play an important role in also producing medical radioisotopes. We work closely with the G7 to remain close partners in discussions on how we build supply chains free of Russian influence. I am sure noble Lords will be aware that the UK has already implemented a direct ban on Russian uranium, in May 2026. This also restricts UK persons or entities from facilitating Russian uranium supply to third countries. We have a close working partnership with the United States on nuclear policy. I attended the G20 Ministers’ meeting, and in that dimension, there was good co-operation.

Turn 11 · Lord Ravensdale (CB)

My Lords, I declare my interest as a chief engineer working for AtkinsRéalis. Within the plutonium stockpile at Sellafield, there is something of a goldmine: £60 billion worth of an isotope called americium. The economic benefit could be much wider than that, because if we can capture the full value chain of this isotope in using it for things such as nuclear batteries and space and defence applications, we could capture even more value in a new industry for the UK. To unlock private capital and private investment, we need clear assurances from the Government and the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority that they support this programme, to allow that investment to proceed. Can the Minister say when the Government will be in a position to provide those assurances?

Turn 12 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

I have had a discussion previously with the noble Lord about americium, and I appreciate the briefing that he has given me. The Government have supported the UK’s National Nuclear Laboratory to extract americium from the UK’s civil separated plutonium inventory at Sellafield for the European Space Agency’s ENDURE programme. We recognise the potential importance of americium for other advanced technologies, including space power systems. We are currently working with the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority to explore opportunities for collaboration with industry. I should say to the noble Lord that no decisions have been made at this stage, but discussions are ongoing, and I have a funny feeling that he will ask for those further discussions to include him, which I am happy to do.

Turn 14 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

I thank the noble Lord for that question—he never fails to surprise me with some of the questions that he asks. I can commit to the domestic supply chain. It is important because, as I have said before from the Dispatch Box, we want to get a double hit out of our investment: not only is it about creating that supply chain, it is about the benefits that come with the supply chain and the skills and the jobs that that will mean for Britain. I guarantee to the noble Lord that that is a vital part of this programme. It is a success story for Britain that we should be proud of and that we should focus on. On his wider point about Europe, I am always willing to work with European partners and support the electricity market of Great Britain, and will continue to do so.

Turn 16 · Baroness Curran (Lab)

I do not have direct details on that specific project so I will write to the noble Lord and make sure that I can address those issues. As he will know, the Government’s SMR programme is backed by £2.6 billion across the spending review period and will create 3,000 skilled jobs onsite during peak construction and thousands more across the supply chain. The Government’s ambition is for 70% of SMR supply chain products to be British built, which is shown by over 70% of our £900 million invested into supply chain contracts going into UK-registered companies. As I say, I want this investment to work twice, once in supporting our energy security mission and once for British jobs and British business.
