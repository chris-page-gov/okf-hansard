Turn 41 · Speaker unlabelled

Amendment 224 would place a duty on all Ministers—not only the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care—to consider health outcomes and the promotion of public health when exercising their duties. Public health is possibly one of the most neglected, misunderstood and underfunded functions of public services, but it is also one of the most important, especially to prevent ill health. Directors of public health now work for local authorities and they have regularly had their funding cut over recent years. I am grateful for the support from the noble Lord, Lord Young, for prevention and his key questions in this area. It is an area that the noble Baroness, Lady Walmsley, really feels needs to be at the heart of the Bill.

Turn 43 · Speaker unlabelled

In this day and age, people rightly feel that they should be able to make informed decisions about their own and their children’s health. However, good decisions rely on good-quality information being available. Although various agencies, such as the NHS website and the UK Health Security Agency, provide good-quality, accurate information, it is swamped by the overwhelming mass of simply wrong information from other less reliable and unqualified sources, whether social media or other websites. Often when you search for information from reliable sources, the social media algorithms put in front of you unrequested and inaccurate information from people who are simply not qualified to advise.

Turn 48 · Speaker unlabelled

Finally, I turn to Amendments 279QA and 53A, tabled by the noble Lord, Lord Rennard. On Amendment 53A, the Government share the noble Lord’s ambition for a smoke-free country, but we do not believe that the amendment is necessary. The recent Tobacco and Vapes Act provides the legislative framework for a smoke-free generation and wider tobacco control. We are cracking on with implementing that Act, considering consultation responses on extending smoke-free places to certain outdoor settings and investing in local authority stop smoking services, with funding weighted towards areas with higher smoking prevalence. The Act already requires the Secretary of State to review its operation and report to Parliament. Together, these measures provide an accountable route to progress without the need for another statutory road map and annual report.
