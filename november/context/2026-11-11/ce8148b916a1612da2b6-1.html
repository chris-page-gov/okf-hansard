Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Secretary, HM Treasury (Lord Pitt-Watson) (Lab)

Local government, property owners and occupiers can work together to improve high streets. I start by recognising that my noble friend has been at the forefront of the business improvement district movement, which the Government are keen to promote. Innovations such as the Camden Collective have provided vital incubation spaces for creative start-ups. However, the evidence suggests that shifting rates liability to landlords would be administratively cumbersome, and may disrupt funding for local services, so the Government are not considering this proposal at this time.

Turn 6 · Lord Pitt-Watson (Lab)

There is quite a long list of things to help. In the Budget alone, there is a 20% reduction for pubs and venues, 70,000 shops which will have no rates at all, and other leisure and retail outlets are frozen in real terms. There is the high streets strategy, with £210 million for the community assets programme, £125 million for derelict and vacant properties, and support for the extension of business improvement districts, including property owners outside of London being involved in the business improvement districts—I could go on. It is a broad programme to support the things that my noble friend would like to see happening.

Turn 7 · Baroness Kramer (LD)

My Lords, with small and high street businesses under so much pressure, surely this is the time for the fundamental reform of business rates to, I suggest, a commercial landowner levy. I think we are past the point of fiddling at the margins. Turn 8 · Lord Pitt-Watson (Lab)

I reply to that in the same way as I did to my noble friend Lord Pitkeathley. The evidence suggests that shifting rates liability to landlords would be administratively quite cumbersome and would make it difficult for the funding of certain local services that want to go to particular types of shops and venues. That is why it is not being considered at this time.
