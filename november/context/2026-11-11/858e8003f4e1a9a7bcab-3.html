Turn 21 · Speaker unlabelled

I turn to the Government’s Keep Britain Working review. They have found that someone absent from work for four to six weeks has a 96% chance of returning. After being absent for a year, that falls to less than 50%. Once they have been economically inactive for health reasons for much longer, their probability of moving back into employment within a year is just 4%. Those statistics emphasise and reinforce many of the points that noble Lords have made during this debate.

Turn 24 · Speaker unlabelled

Given that there are already a number of wider duties upon ICBs—improving services, reducing inequalities and considering broader consequences of their decisions —there might be some merit in establishing a similar expectation that ICBs should, where appropriate, consider how the provision of healthcare could help people to enter, remain in or return to suitable employment. We cannot divide the two and say that it is purely for the economy and we are not really thinking about the individual. In these cases, surely individuals, their families and their communities, as well as the wider community, all benefit. That is a win-win that we should be looking at.
