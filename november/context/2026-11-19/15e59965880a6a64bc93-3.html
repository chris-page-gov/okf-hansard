Turn 17 · Lord Kamall (Con)

My Lords, building on the question from the noble Lord, Lord Mohammed, regarding concerns about access to trials for people from ethnic backgrounds, there are also concerns that access to clinical trials often depends on where a patient happens to live or what hospital they attend. Given this, what is the Minister’s department doing to ensure that patients who live away from major teaching hospitals, particularly those in rural and underserved areas, have a genuine and feasible opportunity to participate in clinical trials?

Turn 18 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I referred earlier to the research delivery network. I have been looking at where the commercial research delivery centres are: there are 35 of them and they have been established to support the delivery of commercial trials across the UK. That includes 14, for example, in primary care sites. The point the noble Lord makes is absolutely right. As I said, that is why it is hard-wired throughout all the changes that we have made to make the improvements that we have.

Turn 20 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

When I have met people who have come forward and, indeed, been encouraged to take part in clinical trials, there are several reasons that they have come forward: one of them is the point made by the noble Lord about access to possibilities that they could not otherwise access; for others, their motivation is that they just want to help others in the future. Both motivations are legitimate, and we ensure that they are discussed with individuals.
