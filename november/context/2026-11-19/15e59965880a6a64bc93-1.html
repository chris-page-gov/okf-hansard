Turn 4 · The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, Department of Health and Social Care (Baroness Merron) (Lab)

My Lords, we have fast-tracked clinical trials to give patients quicker access to more innovative treatments. In the first three months of this year, all eligible commercial clinical trials were set up in less than 150 days, meeting our Life Sciences Sector Plan target. We have achieved this by cutting bureaucracy and increasing NHS accountability, and we are investing in research infrastructure to support participation in all types of clinical trials across the country.

Turn 6 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

I am glad that the noble Baroness recognises the progress, because this is a success story in the making. We have more to do, of course. On non-commercial trials, we want all eligible studies to be set up within 150 days or fewer; therefore, the things that we apply in the commercial setting also apply. I should say to the noble Baroness that the data suggests that actions to accelerate commercial trials have also improved set-up times, but we will be publishing an update on this early next year.

Turn 8 · Baroness Merron (Lab)

We work closely with the devolved Administrations so that we can extend this UK-wide. As the noble Baroness will know, the national cancer plan includes a dedicated chapter for action on rare and less common cancers, including how we will make them a research priority and implement this year’s Rare Cancers Act to give more patients access to clinical trials in respect of rare cancers. There are two complementary roles: we have Dr Metcalf driving clinical improvement and delivery through the national cancer board, as the national cancer lead, but we also have Dr Stoneham, who has been appointed as the NIHR national specialty lead for rare cancers, and that will provide dedicated leadership.
