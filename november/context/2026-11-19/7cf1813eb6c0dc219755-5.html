Turn 36 · Gideon Amos

I listened carefully to the Minister, but this amendment simply requires that the register includes information on any building for which an external assessment has concluded that fire safety risks are tolerable without physical remediation or cladding removal having been completed. This amendment does not seek a huge amount of detail; it is simply telling people in these buildings, “This is the situation.” I am struggling to think of reasons why we should not be doing that.

Turn 38 · Gideon Amos

The hon. Gentleman highlights the exact point underlying this amendment. The Bill does not go down the line that Dame Judith Hackitt and other witnesses suggested, which was a clear, objective standard. We proposed the building regulations standard—as exists for new homes—but there may be other approaches. The hon. Gentleman is absolutely right that the example from Australia was just such an objective test. We do not have that. We have a different test where owners can conclude, on the basis of reports from fire safety assessors that they have appointed and paid for, that risks are tolerable and that flammable cladding should remain. All this amendment asks is that those buildings are listed publicly—nothing else. On that basis, I intend to press this to a vote.
