Turn 9 · Lord Spellar (Lab)

My Lords, we rightly give praise to those who keep our deterrent running around the clock, 365 days a year, but, frankly, they should not have to spend 217 days under the ocean maintaining it. The reason they have to is the disgraceful and disastrous decision of the coalition Government at the behest of the Liberal Democrats not to order the Dreadnought submarine programme. They ran down the ships and the workforce at Barrow, which is still having to be reconstituted. Can I have the assurance of the Minister that this Labour Government will never repeat that disgraceful mistake?

Turn 10 · Lord Coaker (Lab)

My noble friend makes an excellent point. I give that assurance, because it is important. We are investing nearly £64 billion in the renewal and replacement of Dreadnought submarines. That is taking place over the next few years. We expect the Dreadnought class of submarines to be introduced into service through the 2030s. Certainly, this Labour Government, and I am sure most future Governments, will not play around with our nuclear deterrent. It is fundamental to the security of Europe and the world as a whole.

Turn 11 · Lord Wallace of Saltaire (LD)

My Lords, I remind the House that in 2007 a Labour Defence Minister assured us that nuclear spending goes within the defence budget and not in a separate compartment—there is a historical myth on that. What impact has unilateral conventional disarmament had on Britain’s ability to defend itself over the last 10 years? General Barrons was quoted in August as saying that expenditure on nuclear capital investment is squeezing conventional capital spending out of the defence budget, leaving us with too few surface ships, not enough drones and the smallest Army we have had since the Napoleonic war. That is a problem. How are the Government going to tackle that?

Turn 12 · Lord Coaker (Lab)

It is a fundamental mistake to put nuclear deterrence against conventional forces and pay for one with the other. The Government are paying for the renewal of our nuclear deterrent through the Dreadnought programme. We are also investing in conventional forces. That is important. The noble Lord also asked about unilateral disarmament. The Government’s pursuit of nuclear non-proliferation goes through the nuclear non-proliferation treaty. We were at that conference in New York a couple of months ago and we were very close, with many others, to having a statement which talked about how, notwithstanding the fact that we retain our right to have the deterrent, we do all that we can to reduce the threat from that.

Turn 14 · Lord Coaker (Lab)

The noble Lord is absolutely right. He will know from his experience as a Minister but also from the work that he continues to do in various capacities that having the nuclear deterrent is a significant point for this country. We are a P5 member of the Security Council. That counts enormously across the world. It brings responsibility with it. We have hard power and we will retain that, but alongside that we recognise the need to negotiate and discuss. Those who seek to do us harm should know that we have a nuclear deterrent and we keep it there in case of the ultimate threats we may face.
