Turn 9 · Lord Mohammed of Tinsley (LD)

Alan Milburn was right to point out that we need to invest in our young people at an early stage. One of his recommendations is that we should invest £500 million in 2027-28. Will the Government be backing that recommendation rather than simply reshuffling existing funding? This extra money is needed by local authorities for those early interventions that Milburn says are so important.

Turn 10 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

The Prime Minister has been clear and has asked the Cabinet to ensure that the resources are available for us to fulfil what we have already said we will do, which is to move forward in setting up the youth opportunity service. To be clear, that will not be funded from local or strategic authorities alone; the point of it is to bring together at a local level employment support, skills, health provision and other support that will be necessary for young people. We will ensure that all those who need to contribute to the local youth opportunity service have the necessary resources.

Turn 11 · Baroness Bull (CB)

My Lords, the recent Making it Count report from your Lordships’ Numeracy for Life Committee noted the importance of a strong foundation in numeracy as a gateway to education, apprenticeships and employment. It also noted, however, that one in four students do not achieve the benchmark level 4 at GCSE. Other qualifications are available, of course, but they are not widely understood and recognised. Does the Minister agree with the report’s recommendation that the Government should produce a post-16 numeracy framework that sets out the target learners, the progression routes and the detail of those other qualifications, so that employers, parents and students understand what they are and that functional skills are widely understood as an alternative to GCSE maths.

Turn 12 · Baroness Smith of Malvern (Lab)

I appreciated the opportunity to give evidence to the committee, and we are looking carefully at the enormously helpful report it produced. I agree with the noble Baroness that there is an important issue for those young people who do not reach grade 4 by the age of 16. That is why we have already said that we will provide additional support post-16 for further education colleges to continue supporting young people to reach that level. On the point she makes about qualifications, we will introduce a new level 1 stepping-stone qualification that will enable young people to build the foundations of maths understanding that they will need for lifelong success and in gaining that GCSE grade 4-level qualification.

Turn 13 · Baroness McIntosh of Hudnall (Lab)

My Lords, my noble friend has already acknowledged that capabilities such as self-confidence in dealing with other people—that is, social skills and oracy—are an important part of the skill set young people need to be employable. Will she acknowledge the role that arts organisations across the country perform in bringing confidence strategies for learning those skills to young people who might not otherwise be able to acquire them? In addition, will she look into how those programmes can be better funded and made more widely available?
