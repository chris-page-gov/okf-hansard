Turn 70 · Sir John Hayes

As my hon. Friend knows, I admire him in many ways. We agree on much, but we do not agree on much on this issue. By the way, I find his naive faith in technological change charming. I ask him to consider the closest parallel that we have, which is the internet. When the internet emerged, we were never warned about the ills that now prevail; we were told that it was a wondrous new thing. Someone said to me at the time that everyone will be a publisher—that is the problem! This is not a fear of technology; this is wisdom based on bitter experience.

Turn 71 · Dr Spencer

My right hon. Friend will know that the Online Safety Act 2023 was built around regulating harms, not technologies. I do not believe that there is a single misuse case—beyond a very few narrow exceptions —that is not already covered by UK domestic law. Let me return to my previous point. The Government do not build AI; business does. During covid, we learned the hard lesson that when we focus on immediate risks, we forget to fully consider the long-term impact of the decisions we make. As this House considers the risks of artificial intelligence, I urge the Government not to make the same mistake, and I urge everyone to take pause and reflect. The biggest risk of AI is that we fail to embrace the opportunities that AI innovation presents, the world keeps on spinning and the UK gets left behind.
