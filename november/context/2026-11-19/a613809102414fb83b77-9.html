Turn 66 · Jon Pearce (High Peak) (Lab)

I declare an interest as a member of GMB. Recently, Jo Taylor, in my High Peak office, celebrated 25 years of working for Labour Members, which is a tremendous achievement. Jo has dedicated her career to making the everyday lives of people better and protecting their rights. Now is a good time for us to look to protect the rights of all staff like Jo, which is why I am backing GMB’s “One Parliament One Employer” campaign. The Independent Parliamentary Standards Authority writes the contracts, sets the job descriptions and the pay bands, and pays the salaries, yet the legal liability sits with 650 individual MPs. Will the Leader of the House join me in congratulating Jo on her 25 years of public service, and make time in Government time for a debate on making Parliament the single employer for its staff?

Turn 68 · Tessa Munt (Wells and Mendip Hills) (LD)

The Department for Education’s keeping children safe in education accreditation scheme is voluntary and does not automatically apply to permanent online-only schools. Ofsted visits only those providers who choose to join it as a quality assurance body, rather than under the usual inspection regime. As of August 2026, only 17 providers in England are accredited. Safeguarding children should not rely on whether their education provider opts in, so will the Leader of the House ask the Education Secretary to look again at bringing all full-time and part-time online education providers within a statutory framework?

Turn 71 · Sir Alan Campbell

I will indeed join my hon. Friend in congratulating Jim and the Sair Inn, as well as the Royal Oak and its publican, on being recognised as among the best pubs in the United Kingdom. I have said it before and I will say it again: local pubs are the beating heart of our towns and villages. We are committed to supporting them, including through cutting business rates, and there was help in the Budget. They have gone through a tough time—7,000 closed during the period of the last Government. We are doing everything we can and looking at what more we can do to ensure our pubs thrive, because if they thrive, it helps our communities to thrive.
