Clause 73 establishes the scope of part 4, making it clear which authorities, functions and buildings are covered by the co-operation agreements. That matters because responsibility for fire safety in residential buildings is shared across several statutory regimes. Local housing authorities, fire and rescue authorities and the Building Safety Regulator each have important but different roles to regulate fire safety. In some cases, more than one authority may be able to act in relation to the same building or defect. Co-operation between regulatory bodies is therefore essential. Turn 99 · Gareth Bacon

I beg to move amendment 57, in clause 74, page 42, line 36, at end insert— “(3A) Where co-ordination in exercising the relevant functions between relevant authorities is unable to progress beyond 3 months, the Building Safety Regulator will make the final decisions on a relevant building.” This amendment ensures that disagreements between Local Authorities, Local Fire Authorities and the Building Safety Regulator regarding areas of co-operation can be resolved by giving the Building Safety Regulator the ability to decide the final relevant action within a specified timeframe.

Turn 102 · Darren Paffey

I support the clauses in this group, because they tackle an issue that every Member with such buildings in their constituencies has encountered: residents simply do not know who is in charge. In Southampton, three bodies have a legitimate role in fire safety in residential building: Southampton city council, Hampshire and Isle of Wight fire and rescue service and, for the tallest buildings, the Building Safety Regulator. Each has different powers under different regulations, and each has its own priorities and pressures.
