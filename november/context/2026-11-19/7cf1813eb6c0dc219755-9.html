Clause 57 will establish a financial penalty regime for non-compliance with part 2 registration and information provision duties. It will allow enforcing authorities to impose financial penalties when a registration duty holder fails to meet key duties, such as by failing to register an occupied building and/or provide the required information, by failing to pass the information to a new duty holder, or by failing to tell the registrar when the duty holder changes. When an enforcing authority is satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that a duty holder has breached such a duty, the authority may impose an initial penalty of up to £7,000. Clause 60 will give people affected by certain registrar decisions the right to ask for a review. This is needed so that those decisions can be challenged fairly but effectively. It will give the registrar an opportunity to reconsider the decision before an affected person pursues a formal appeal. The clause covers decisions to add a building to, or remove it from, the register under clause 52. For those decisions, the affected person is the registration duty holder. Clause 62 makes local housing authorities responsible for enforcing part 2 of the Bill. The registration duties need to be backed by effective enforcement, so that duty holders have a strong incentive to comply and action can be taken where they do not. Local housing authorities are well placed to perform that role. They already have responsibilities and enforcement powers for housing conditions and safety in their areas, and hold important knowledge and intelligence about the local building stock. The registrar is likely to be a different body from the local housing authority responsible for enforcement.
