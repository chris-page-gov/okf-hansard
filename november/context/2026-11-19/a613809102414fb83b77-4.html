Turn 25 · Ian Lavery (Blyth and Ashington) (Lab)

Today is World Toilet Day. It sounds quite amusing, but this is not an amusing issue for the train drivers and other transport workers who are required to work for 11 hours with only one 20-minute break, with a total lack of clean, accessible toilets. Unbelievably, train drivers have had to resort to extreme measures: changing sanitary products in the cab; leaving the train to urinate alongside the railway; or using bottles and lunchboxes. Despite ASLEF’s campaign and the Office of Rail and Road charter, the industry is still not meeting the required standards. Will my right hon. Friend use his offices to secure a meeting with the relevant Ministers to discuss what can be done to provide the dignity that those workers so rightly deserve?

Turn 31 · Sir Alan Campbell

My hon. Friend is right to raise that. In our water system, shareholders appear never to lose and customers never to win. We have announced a series of measures to go further in implementing public control. In September, the Prime Minister announced that we would bring forward a strengthened water Bill when parliamentary time allows. I do not think my hon. Friend will have long to wait before that can be confirmed.

Turn 32 · Dave Doogan (Angus and Perthshire Glens) (SNP)

I am currently assisting 28 constituents—the number goes up and down—with civil service pension problems. There is a particular irony in the fact that the people affected are civil servants, who have given their career to the bureaucracy that is now so badly letting them down. Capita recently demonstrated that there is not even uniformity in the level and manner of its incompetence: it seems to be finding new ways to let civil servants down all the time. Can we have a debate in Government time to allow right hon. and hon. Members to bear witness to this utter disaster on behalf of their constituents, and allow the Cabinet Office to explain away why there is such institutional lack of grip on the issue?
