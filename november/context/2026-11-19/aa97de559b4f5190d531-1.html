Turn 4 · Helen Hayes (Dulwich and West Norwood) (Lab)

I am grateful to the Backbench Business Committee for granting time for this statement on the Education Committee’s fifth special report, “Reading for Pleasure: Government Response”. Select Committee work is a privilege. We hear hard evidence about systems that have failed and opportunities that have been lost. However, scrutiny is not only about finding fault, but about finding what works and ensuring it travels further. This inquiry gave us that chance, and it offered something that is rarer than it should be in public policy: joy. Witness after witness in our inquiry described reading as something that brings joy to life. Former children’s laureate Frank Cottrell-Boyce told us that in education, we can teach children all the steps, “but the important thing is that they dance.” He urged us to encourage policymakers to “prioritise the pleasure before you get into the learning”. Turn 7 · Helen Hayes

I thank my hon. Friend for his question and for the contribution he made as a member of the Select Committee to our inquiry on reading for pleasure. I join him in paying tribute to the late, great Dolly Parton. It is due to her work that every child in my constituency has access to books from birth through her Imagination Library. It has been a transformative programme for millions of children. All of us owe her a great debt of gratitude for that work.

Turn 8 · Jim Shannon (Strangford) (DUP)

I thank the Education Committee and its Chair for their contribution. As the grandfather of six children, I am very aware of the importance of books. When I was small, we had maybe one or two books, whereas today’s child has maybe a dozen or 20 books. The hon. Member for Bracknell (Peter Swallow) referred to Dolly Parton. In the world we live in, partnership and how we can do things together is incredibly important. Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library has donated 7 million books to the United Kingdom, and I think almost 300,000 to London alone and 300 million across the world. Is there not an opportunity here to work with the Imagination Library to collectively and collaboratively do something that can benefit all our children? Dolly Parton wanted that, the hon. Member for Dulwich and West Norwood (Helen Hayes) wants that, and so do I.
