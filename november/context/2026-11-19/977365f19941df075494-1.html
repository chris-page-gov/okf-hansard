In the last two months, the cost of living taskforce has delivered interventions ranging from the £100 household energy payment for up to 20 million households to the extension of 0% VAT on electricity bills and energy debt relief—and that is just the start.

Turn 3 · Danny Beales

I welcome the work of the taskforce to date. For many families in constituencies like Uxbridge and South Ruislip, a car is not a luxury but a necessity to get to work, school and vital appointments. In a cost of living crisis, insurers are taking advantage of that by charging exorbitant annual percentage rates on monthly insurance policies—some as high as 53%, more than a credit card interest rate—costing consumers hundreds of pounds extra a year compared with annual insurance premiums. Will the First Secretary of State bring together colleagues across Government to address this poverty premium and protect consumers from unfair monthly charges?

Turn 4 · Louise Haigh

I congratulate my hon. Friend on the work he is doing to highlight this important issue, particularly the issue of the poverty premium, which is so clear in motor insurance. Car insurance is an essential, not a luxury; it is vital to accessing economic opportunities. That is why, as Transport Secretary, I established the motor insurance taskforce with the Treasury. I welcome continued action to bring down monthly APRs, but there is more to be done, so I have asked the cost of living taskforce to work across Government to explore possible interventions to tackle disproportionately high costs.

Turn 8 · Lisa Smart (Hazel Grove) (LD)

Again, this week, I received an email from a constituent—this time in Offerton—who told me they are struggling to make ends meet at the sharp end of the cost of living crisis. We all know that the best way to tackle that is to grow the economy. We also know that the biggest lever the Government could pull is to go beyond Labour’s self-imposed red lines and really strengthen our relationship with the EU. The Conservatives’ Brexit deal, cheered on by their Reform chums, left a £90 billion hole in the public finances—money that could be used to cut taxes or invest in our communities. Families pay for that at the till. Has the cost of living taskforce assessed what a new growth and defence partnership, including joining the single market and negotiating a new UK-EU customs union, would do to prices in the UK—and if not, will the First Secretary ask it to do so?
