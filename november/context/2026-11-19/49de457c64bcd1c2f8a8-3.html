Turn 17 · Baroness Hayman (CB)

My Lords, there was considerable discussion during the passage of the Financial Services and Markets Bill in this House about the risks that climate change posed for householders, the insurance industry and the housebuilding industry. Those risks were not only flooding but drought and coastal erosion. Is the Minister’s department in close contact with the Treasury and the Financial Conduct Authority, both of which were given responsibility in this area to see that risk was properly analysed and that some of the severe possible consequences, for example in the insurance industry, were being properly managed?

Turn 20 · Baroness Hayman of Ullock (Lab)

The noble Earl is absolutely right. The Government have no plans to extend Flood Re to businesses. It is difficult because that model does not work for businesses, as I am sure noble Lords are aware, because the scheme is funded through a levy on UK household insurers. If we were to extend the scheme, it would require a new levy on businesses, which could mean that businesses and their customers end up subsidising profit-making organisations in flood-risk areas. We have looked at this in detail and it is complicated. Having said that, it is really important that we continue to work with BIBA, the British Insurance Brokers’ Association, to look at how we can best support businesses to get the flood cover that they need. From living in Cockermouth, where the high street has flooded badly a number of times, I am very aware of the challenges for small businesses in flood-risk areas.
