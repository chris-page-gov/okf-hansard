Turn 17 · Lord Addington (LD)

My Lords, as I listened to this debate, I was astounded by the degree of harmony that had broken out and by the fact that every original point I had has been picked off one by one. I will give just a few thoughts. When I look at GCSEs and A-levels, I am not that bothered by the figures, but I am particularly interested in the skills that come with the person who teaches them. If you are going to encourage people even to do this as a hobby, we need to remember what we have learned from sports—I think the noble Lord, Lord Doyle, mentioned this—which is that anybody who partakes in sports generally has better results in education. The same is true of anything else that gives them that degree of positiveness and that reassurance that opens them up to learning. Turn 18 · Baroness Spielman (Con)

My Lords, this is a valuable debate but time is brief. Arts and creative subjects are building blocks of individual fulfilment, economic success and civilisation itself, so it is right that in compulsory schooling every child should have a broad and rich curriculum, including in arts and creative subjects. Your Lordships can find me on record on this over many years. I have checked the source data. The good news is that art is in rude health in schools. GCSE entries dipped between 2000 and 2010, but they recovered and in 2026 were as strong as in 2000. I see art rooms everywhere in constant use, and we should celebrate that. Elsewhere, the picture is more mixed—by the way, some of the same trends are also visible in Scotland and Wales.
