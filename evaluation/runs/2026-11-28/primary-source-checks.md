## P1-Q2 · Current rights and proposed changes

In England a parent or young person can appeal specified local authority decisions to the First-tier Tribunal under section 51 of the Children and Families Act 2014. These include refusal to assess or reassess, refusal to issue a plan after assessment, specified plan contents (needs, special educational provision, named institution or type, or absence of a named institution), refusal to amend or replace after review or reassessment, and a decision to cease a plan. Section 55 generally requires a mediation adviser’s certificate and provides an exception for placement-only appeals. This is general information. The Department for Education consulted on setting-led individual support plans, local complaints and disagreement resolution, and tribunal appeals on specified specialist-package decisions. The consultation and the November reply do not enact those changes. The revised XML read on 28 November gives 29 October 2026 as its document validity date. Its section-specific version lists end on 1 September 2014. A separate request for section 51 as at 16 November returned HTTP 500.

## P3-Q2 · What can be established about the outage

On 16 November Ian Murray reported that the 12–13 November outage had disrupted email and other services, including public services, and that 999 remained operational. He attributed the infrastructure account and absence of indications of malicious activity to Microsoft. Hansard establishes what the minister told the Commons. Independent technical confirmation would require incident evidence. In two written corrections the next day he amended the wording to say that 14% of server capacity was also down and that two NHS trusts had declared and then called off critical incidents. The earlier wording about one server accounting for 14% and about incident response units should not be repeated as the corrected account.
