Hansard is the official edited report of parliamentary debates. Start with one statement and see who spoke, which organisations were mentioned and where the same subject came up before. The public example and reader open without a sign-in.

Follow the Microsoft 365 statement

  1. Open the Microsoft 365 companion view. Select Read the original statement in Hansard to see Ian Murray’s statement in the Commons on 16 November 2026.
  2. Under People, find Ian Murray and the ministerial role recorded for him at the time. Under Organisations, open Three recent examples for Microsoft or the Cabinet Office to see where else that organisation was named.
  3. Under Earlier mentions of Microsoft 365, follow any of the 19 results to Hansard. The dates and Houses help you find the surrounding debates. The results match a name or alias, so some concern other parliamentary business.

Browse the week

  1. Return to A week in Parliament and select Open in OKF Explorer. The bundle can take a moment to load.
  2. Use a facet, which is a filter. Start with Sitting date or House, then try People, Organisations or Legislation.
  3. Open a result and follow its source link to check the wording and context in Hansard.

OKF+ packages the relationships between records and the evidence behind them. Explorer is the browser interface. If you want to reuse the data in another tool, the homepage offers JSON and the YAML-LD graph.

Ask a chat or voice assistant

Give the assistant this short plain-text evidence file as a link if it can read web pages, or paste the text into the chat. Ask aloud if your chat supports voice. Keep the companion view open to inspect the people, organisations and source links.
